# Section E — Human Clinical Evidence (KPV)

> **Bottom line (read first):** There is **essentially no human clinical efficacy evidence for KPV (Lys-Pro-Val, α-MSH(11-13))**. As of June 2026, a thorough search of ClinicalTrials.gov, EU/EudraCT, and PubMed/PMC returns **zero registered, completed, or results-posted human clinical trials** of KPV — no RCTs, no cohort studies, no open-label efficacy trials — for IBD/colitis, dermatology (atopic dermatitis, psoriasis), wound healing, or any other indication. The entire KPV evidence base is **preclinical** (rodent models, cell culture) plus **ex vivo human-tissue pharmaceutics** (transdermal-delivery studies on excised human skin, which are NOT clinical trials and enrolled no patients). The same is true for the analog **KdPT** (a KPV derivative): no human trials, only animal/in vitro work. Human use of KPV today is entirely off-label/unapproved (compounding-pharmacy and "research peptide" channels); the U.S. FDA has not approved KPV for any indication.

**E2.** A systematic search of EU clinical-trial registries (EudraCT / EU CTIS) and general web/registry searches for **KdPT** in IBD, atopic dermatitis, or psoriasis returns **no registered or completed human trial**. [2, regulatory] (NCT/EudraCT: none.)

**E3.** PubMed indexes ~50+ KPV publications spanning two decades; **none are human clinical trials**. The therapeutic studies are rodent colitis/IBD models, peritonitis/inflammation models, ocular and wound-healing animal models, and nanoparticle/oral-delivery formulation work in mice — plus in vitro cell studies (keratinocytes, bronchial epithelium, HepG2). No patient-enrolling efficacy or safety trial appears. [3, anecdote_aggregate→null] [4, regulatory] (The foundational IBD mechanism paper, Dalmasso 2008 PMID 18061177, is a **mouse DSS/TNBS colitis** study, not human.) [5, anecdote_aggregate→null]

**E4.** The widely cited "KPV across **human skin**" paper (Pawar et al., *J Pharm Sci* 2017, PMID 28343991) is an **ex vivo transdermal-permeation pharmaceutics study** using dermatomed/excised human skin in Franz diffusion cells. It enrolled **no living subjects, measured no clinical outcome, and is not a clinical trial** — it tests iontophoresis + microneedle delivery of KPV through skin tissue. [6, regulatory] (Design: ex vivo human skin; n = skin samples, not patients; outcome = permeation flux, not therapeutic effect.)

**E5.** For the analog **KdPT** (Lys-D-Pro-Thr, an α-MSH(11-13) derivative distinct from KPV), the principal study (Bettenworth/Böhm et al., 2011, PMC3157275) reports protection from intestinal inflammation and barrier maintenance in **animal/in vitro models only**; subsequent KdPT dermatology work (psoriasis-like disease) is in **mouse models and human-skin equivalents/biopsy-derived cells in vitro**, not in patients in a controlled trial. **No completed human KdPT clinical trial exists** for IBD, atopic dermatitis, or psoriasis. [7, anecdote_aggregate→null] [8, regulatory]

**E6.** "Human evidence" cited by commercial peptide vendors and clinics (eczema, acne, hidradenitis suppurativa, gut health) is **marketing/anecdotal**, not derived from any registered or peer-reviewed human trial; KPV is **not FDA-approved** for any indication and is used off-label via compounding/"research" channels with **no published human efficacy or long-term safety data**. [9, anecdote_aggregate] [10, anecdote_aggregate]

**KPV-vs-KdPT note:** KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) and KdPT (Lys-D-Pro-Thr) are **different molecules** — KdPT was engineered as a more stable/non-pigmenting α-MSH(11-13) derivative. **Neither** has a human clinical trial. Do not let KdPT preclinical dermatology/IBD papers be miscited as human KPV evidence.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits (BE EXPLICIT about the human-evidence state)

1. **There is no human clinical efficacy evidence for KPV. Full stop.** No RCT, no cohort, no open-label trial, no registered protocol, no results posting — in any indication (IBD/colitis, atopic dermatitis, psoriasis, acne, hidradenitis suppurativa, wound healing, metabolic). Any claim of KPV "human efficacy" should be treated as unsupported.

2. **No human safety dataset either.** There is no published Phase I or formal human safety/PK trial of KPV. Tolerability claims circulating in commercial sources are extrapolated from animal studies and uncontrolled clinical anecdote, not from trial data.

3. **The "human skin" citation is a trap.** Pawar 2017 (PMID 28343991) uses *excised* human skin in diffusion cells — a pharmaceutics delivery study with no patients and no clinical endpoint. It must not be reported as a clinical trial or as human efficacy evidence. Similarly, in vitro studies on "human keratinocytes/bronchial cells/peripheral-blood T cells" are cell-culture, not trials.

4. **KdPT ≠ KPV, and KdPT also has no human trial.** KdPT (Lys-D-Pro-Thr) preclinical IBD/psoriasis data are sometimes conflated with KPV. Both are α-MSH(11-13)-related, both are preclinical-only in humans. Keep them distinct and do not transfer KdPT animal findings onto KPV human claims.

5. **Registry search limits.** ClinicalTrials.gov and EU registries were queried by drug name/term; a trial registered under a proprietary code, formulation name, or a sponsor-internal alias could in principle be missed. However, no such trial surfaced through name, indication, sponsor (Böhm/Luger/Münster group), or web channels — the convergent null across registry + literature + web makes a hidden completed trial unlikely. If a sponsor code is later identified, this section should be re-queried.

6. **Foundational efficacy is rodent.** The strongest KPV efficacy signal (intestinal anti-inflammation, PepT1-mediated uptake; Dalmasso 2008) is mouse colitis. Translation to humans is unestablished; oral-bioavailability/stability challenges (addressed only via experimental nanoparticle/delivery systems in animals) are an additional unresolved barrier to any future human program.

7. **Regulatory state.** KPV is not an FDA-approved drug for any indication; current human exposure is off-label compounded or grey-market "research peptide" use, outside any trial framework — meaning no systematic adverse-event capture exists.
