# Section A — Identity, Chemistry, PK (KPV)

KPV = Lys-Pro-Val, the C-terminal tripeptide α-MSH(11-13). Section scope: identity/chemistry, relation to α-MSH, melanocortin-receptor dependence vs independence, stability, PK/absorption (oral via PepT1; topical; systemic), and the KPV vs KdPT distinction.

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; species+n on animal; route tags)

### Identity & chemistry

1. KPV is the tripeptide L-lysyl-L-prolyl-L-valine (H-Lys-Pro-Val-OH; one-letter K-P-V). Canonical molecular formula **C16H30N4O4**, average molecular weight **342.43 g/mol**, **CAS 67727-97-3**, PubChem **CID 125672** (catalogued under the synonym "MSH (11-13)"). IUPAC name: (2S)-2-[[(2S)-1-[(2S)-2,6-diaminohexanoyl]pyrrolidine-2-carbonyl]amino]-3-methylbutanoic acid. [6, regulatory]
   - Note: some vendor pages quote MW "~400 Da" — this is incorrect for the free tripeptide and likely reflects an acetate/TFA salt or a transcription error; the database value 342.43 g/mol is authoritative. [9, vendor_label] (cited only to flag the discrepancy; not used to ground the number)

2. KPV corresponds exactly to **residues 11–13 (the C-terminus) of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH)**, a proopiomelanocortin (POMC)-derived tridecapeptide (13 residues, N-terminally acetylated, C-terminally amidated in the native hormone). Most of α-MSH's anti-inflammatory activity localizes to this C-terminal tripeptide, while KPV lacks the pigmentary (melanogenic) action of the parent hormone. [5, mechanism_review]

### Relation to α-MSH & receptor dependence (KEY FINDING)

3. **KPV's peripheral anti-inflammatory effect is, in the best-characterized models, melanocortin-receptor INDEPENDENT.** In murine crystal (urate/MSU)-induced peritonitis, the KPV anti-migratory (reduced PMN accumulation) effect was NOT blocked by melanocortin receptor antagonists, persisted in MC1R-deficient (recessive-yellow, e/e) mice, and KPV — unlike core α-MSH peptides — did not raise cAMP; the authors concluded KPV does not act through melanocortin receptors and more likely acts by inhibiting IL-1β function. [1, animal][1, in_vitro] (mouse; recessive-yellow e/e and wild-type; macrophage cultures in vitro) [route-extrapolation: intraperitoneal model]

4. Independence is corroborated at the cellular/gut level: in intestinal epithelial (Caco2-BBE) and immune cells, KPV is taken up by the PepT1 di/tripeptide transporter and inhibits NF-κB and MAP-kinase signaling and cytokine secretion at nanomolar concentrations; the protective effect was attributed to PepT1-mediated uptake rather than melanocortin receptors (Caco2-BBE cells lacked MC3R/MC5R, and α-MSH did not reproduce the effect comparably). [2, animal][2, in_vitro] (mouse colitis in vivo + human-derived epithelial cell lines in vitro)
   - Honest nuance: α-MSH itself signals through melanocortin receptors (MC1R–MC5R), and broader reviews note α-MSH/related tripeptides exert anti-inflammatory action through BOTH receptor-dependent (central/neurogenic and on MCR-expressing peripheral cells) and direct intracellular mechanisms; "receptor-independent" is the established conclusion for KPV in the peritonitis and gut-epithelial models, not a blanket claim across every cell type. [5, mechanism_review]

### Stability / chemistry of degradation

5. As a small, linear, unmodified tripeptide KPV is a substrate for peptidases and (administered systemically) is expected to have a short plasma half-life typical of small peptides; no admissible primary source providing a quantified KPV serum/plasma half-life or aqueous-solution stability constant was retrieved. The functional consequence — that intact intestinal/colonic delivery depends on carrier-mediated (PepT1) uptake rather than diffusion — is documented [2], and that topical KPV does not cross intact skin by passive diffusion is documented [3]. (Quantitative stability = gap; see Coverage gaps.)

### PK / absorption — ORAL

6. **KPV is orally/lumenally absorbable via the proton-coupled oligopeptide transporter PepT1** (SLC15A1), which carries most di- and tripeptides and is normally small-intestinal but is induced in colon during inflammation. PepT1 has high affinity for KPV, transporting it into epithelial and immune cells even at low (nanomolar) luminal concentrations. [2, in_vitro][2, mechanism_review]

7. Oral KPV is biologically effective in rodent colitis: oral administration reduced the incidence/severity of DSS- and TNBS-induced colitis with decreased pro-inflammatory cytokine expression (effective at ~100 µM in the drinking-water/lumenal exposure paradigm; ~10 nM effective in cell uptake studies). [2, animal] (mouse; DSS and TNBS colitis models) [route: oral/lumenal]

### PK / absorption — TOPICAL / TRANSDERMAL

8. **KPV does not cross intact human skin by passive diffusion** (permeation below limit of detection, LOD 0.01 µg/mL). Physical enhancement is required: microneedle microporation gave ~4.4 µg/cm²/h; iontophoresis added an ~8-fold increase and combined microporation + iontophoresis a ~35-fold increase over microneedles alone. [3, in_vitro] (excised/dermatomed human skin, in vitro Franz-cell permeation) [route: transdermal]

### PK / absorption — SYSTEMIC

9. No admissible primary human PK study (plasma concentration–time, Cmax, AUC, clearance, bioavailability by any route) for KPV was retrieved; systemic/parenteral PK parameters in humans are unestablished in the literature found. (gap)

### KPV vs KdPT distinction

10. **KdPT (Lys-D-Pro-Thr) is a different tripeptide from KPV**, not a salt or formulation variant. It is a derivative related to the α-MSH C-terminal region in which Pro is the D-enantiomer and Val is replaced by Thr; KdPT's L-form sequence is homologous to residues 193–195 of IL-1β, motivating an IL-1β-interaction hypothesis. [4, animal][5, mechanism_review]

11. KdPT, like KPV, protects in experimental colitis (multiple murine models), promotes colonic epithelial proliferation, wound closure, and barrier function (transepithelial electrical resistance, tight-junction protein preservation after IFN-γ/TNF-α), and does NOT affect melanogenesis in vitro; notably KdPT acted **independently of IL-1 receptor type I in vivo**, distinguishing its mechanism from a simple IL-1RI antagonism. [4, animal][4, in_vitro] (mouse colitis models in vivo + colonic epithelial cells in vitro)

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **No quantitative stability data**: no admissible primary source gave a numeric KPV serum/plasma half-life, in-solution degradation rate, or storage-stability constant. Claims about "short half-life" are mechanistic inference from peptide chemistry, not a cited measured value (Claim 5).
- **No human/systemic PK**: no plasma concentration–time, Cmax, AUC, clearance, or absolute bioavailability data for KPV by any route in humans (Claim 9). Oral evidence is rodent efficacy + cell-uptake kinetics (PepT1), not human PK.
- **Oral "absorption" is carrier-mediated, model-derived**: the ~100 µM oral-efficacy figure [2] is a dosing/exposure parameter in mice, not an absolute oral bioavailability fraction.
- **Receptor-independence is model-specific**: established for KPV in MSU peritonitis [1] and gut epithelium [2]; the parent α-MSH does signal through melanocortin receptors, and the literature does not exclude MC1R involvement for KPV in every cell type [5]. Stated as model-qualified, not absolute.
- **Topical data are in-vitro human skin** [3] (route-extrapolation to in-vivo human topical efficacy not established here).
- **Tier-3/vendor sources** (peptidepedia, apex, vendor labels) surfaced in searches were NOT used to ground any number; the only vendor citation [9] is an explicit discrepancy flag.
- KdPT pharmacology is included only to the extent of distinguishing it from KPV (scope); full KdPT efficacy/PK belongs to other sections if covered.
