# Section C — Preclinical GI/Colitis Efficacy (KPV)

> SCOPE: KPV (Lys-Pro-Val; α-MSH(11–13)) in animal/in-vitro models of colitis/IBD —
> DSS, TNBS, T-cell transfer, AOM/DSS colitis-associated cancer; free oral KPV, rectal
> KPV, and nanoparticle/hydrogel/prodrug-delivered KPV; PepT1-mediated colonic delivery.
> **All evidence below is PRECLINICAL (rodent + in-vitro). There are NO human colitis
> trials of KPV.** Effect sizes are model-dependent and route-/formulation-dependent.

## Claims

1. Oral KPV (100 µM in drinking water) reduces severity of both DSS- and TNBS-induced
   colitis in mice — less body-weight loss, preserved colon length, reduced colonic MPO,
   and lower pro-inflammatory cytokine mRNA (IL-6, IL-12, IFN-γ, IL-1β, TNF-α), all p<0.05.
   Species/n: C57BL/6 mice, n=5/group (DSS), n=10/group (TNBS). Route: oral (drinking water).
   [1, animal]
2. KPV's anti-inflammatory action in colonic cells is PepT1-mediated: nanomolar KPV is
   transported via PepT1 into intestinal epithelial cells and immune cells, where it inhibits
   NF-κB and MAP-kinase signaling and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion. Demonstrated
   in human intestinal epithelial lines (Caco2-BBE, HT29-Cl.19A) and human T cells (Jurkat).
   [1, in_vitro]
3. Independent replication (different lab, Germany): KPV reduced inflammation in two distinct
   mouse colitis models — DSS colitis and CD45RBhi naïve T-cell adoptive-transfer colitis —
   with reduced inflammatory infiltrate, earlier weight regain, lower MPO, and improved
   histology. In melanocortin-1-receptor–deficient (MC1Re/e) mice given DSS, KPV rescued all
   treated animals from death, indicating the anti-inflammatory effect does not require MC1R.
   Species: mice (C57BL/6 background incl. MC1Re/e strain); n per group not fully specified in
   accessible text. Routes used: oral and parenteral. [2, animal]
4. PepT1-dependence confirmed genetically in colitis-associated cancer (AOM/DSS): oral KPV
   prevented/reduced tumorigenesis and inflammation in wild-type mice, but produced NO
   inhibitory effect in PepT1-knockout mice — establishing PepT1 as the required transporter
   for KPV efficacy in vivo. PepT1-overexpressing transgenic mice had larger tumor burden;
   PepT1-KO mice had reduced tumor number/size. Species: WT, PepT1-KO, PepT1-TG mice;
   AOM + DSS. Route: oral. [3, animal]
5. Nanoparticle delivery dramatically lowers the KPV dose needed: KPV loaded in PLGA-based
   nanoparticles achieved colitis efficacy similar to free KPV at a ~12,000-fold LOWER KPV
   concentration; hyaluronic-acid-functionalized NPs (HA-KPV-NPs, ~272 nm) further targeted
   colonic epithelial cells + macrophages, normalizing body weight, colon length, MPO, TNF-α
   mRNA, and histology toward healthy controls. Delivered dose 16 µg/kg/day KPV.
   Species/n: FVB male mice, 8 wk, n=5/group; 3% DSS. Route: oral gavage. [4, animal]
   ⚠ Concentration-audit flag: the headline "12,000×" potency figure originates from a SINGLE
   group (Xiao/Merlin) — see lab-provenance note. It compares formulations within one study,
   not an independent replication of the absolute potency.
6. Recent independent prodrug platform (Army Medical University, China): an ROS-responsive
   self-immolative KPV conjugate (proKPV) self-assembling into oral nanoparticles showed
   3.8-fold greater colonic accumulation than free KPV and protected against DSS colitis at a
   ~20-fold lower dose than free KPV (free KPV 1 mg/kg = no benefit; proKPV 0.5 & 2.5 mg/kg =
   robust protection). proKPV at 2.5 mg/kg outperformed oral 5-ASA at 50 mg/kg on multiple
   endpoints. Species/n: C57BL/6J male mice (20–24 g), n=6/group; acute DSS, 7 days.
   Route: oral. [5, animal]
7. Independent intravenous co-assembled nanodrug (China): a PepT1-targeted nanoparticle
   co-assembling KPV with the immunosuppressant FK506 reduced disease activity index, restored
   colon length, lowered MPO/NO/ROS and TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6 in both acute (4% DSS) and chronic
   (2.5% DSS) colitis, outperforming KPV-alone and FK506-alone arms. Species/n: C57BL/6 male
   mice, 8 wk, n=6/group. KPV dose 1 mg/kg/day. Route: intravenous (NOT oral — note route).
   [6, animal]
8. Independent rectal/hydrogel route in a different species: a KPV-binding double-network
   hydrogel (PMSP-KPV), administered rectally, restored the colonic epithelial mucosal barrier,
   reduced oxidative stress, and modulated gut microbiota in TNBS-induced ulcerative colitis in
   RATS. Species: rats; model TNBS; route rectal. [7, animal]
9. Mechanistic summary across studies (extrapolation, not a single experiment): KPV efficacy in
   colitis is consistently attributed to PepT1-mediated colonic uptake → NF-κB/MAPK inhibition →
   reduced TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6 and MPO, plus epithelial-barrier/mucosal-healing effects. This
   convergent mechanism is supported by claims [1]–[8] but the magnitude of clinical benefit in
   humans is UNKNOWN (no human data). [routes/labs vary — extrapolation tag] [synthesis]

## Lab-provenance note (dominant groups — for the concentration audit)

- **Merlin group (the dominant KPV-colitis lab)** — Didier Merlin, with Shanthi Sitaraman
  (early), Emilie Viennois, Bo Xiao, and others. Institutional trail: Emory Univ. → Georgia
  State Univ. / Atlanta VA Medical Center (with Southwest Univ., Chongqing collaboration on the
  nanoparticle work). This single group produced the foundational mechanism paper [1], the
  genetic PepT1-KO causality paper [3], and the headline nanoparticle-potency paper [4].
- **AUDIT FLAG for the concentration/potency narrative:** The eye-catching "~12,000× lower
  concentration" and the strict PepT1-dependence both come from the Merlin group [3,4].
  The 12,000× figure is an *intra-study formulation comparison* (NP vs free KPV) within ONE
  lab — it is NOT an independently replicated measure of KPV's absolute potency, and should not
  be quoted as a general property of "KPV." Treat as a delivery-formulation result.
- **Genuinely independent corroboration of the core effect (KPV reduces colitis)** comes from
  at least three other groups: Kucharzik/Luger, Münster, Germany [2]; Jianxiang Zhang, Army
  Medical Univ., China [5]; Southwest Medical Univ., Luzhou [6]; plus the rat-rectal hydrogel
  group [7]. So the *direction* of effect (KPV lowers colitis severity in rodents) replicates
  across independent labs, species (mouse + rat), models (DSS, TNBS, transfer, AOM/DSS), and
  routes (oral, rectal, IV). The *specific magnitudes* (esp. the 12,000× and exact cytokine
  deltas) are largely single-lab and should be reported as such.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **No human data.** Zero RCTs, zero open-label human colitis/IBD studies of KPV. Every claim
  here is rodent or in-vitro. Any human extrapolation is unsupported by this section.
- **Effect-size precision is limited.** Several papers report direction + significance (p<0.05)
  but exact percent reductions / SDs were not always extractable from accessible
  abstracts/full-text (publisher 403s on Cell Press / ScienceDirect). MPO and cytokine deltas
  should be treated as "significantly reduced," not as precise numbers, unless the full PDF is
  obtained.
- **The 12,000× potency claim is single-lab and intra-study** — flagged above. Do not present
  as an independently replicated property of KPV.
- **Source [2] PMID now confirmed: 18092346** (verified via PubMed esummary — title/author/
  journal/vol(14)/issue(3)/pages(324-31)/DOI 10.1002/ibd.20334 all concordant). An earlier pass
  had recorded the wrong PMID (17973296), which actually belongs to an unrelated paper (Park,
  J Cell Biochem 2008; flavonoids/HIF-1α); corrected to 18092346.
- **Source [7] author list + affiliation not captured** (publisher page 403-blocked, RG 403);
  PMID/DOI/title/journal/year verified via PubMed listing. Species (rat) + route (rectal) +
  model (TNBS) are confirmed.
- **n values incomplete for [2]** (transfer-colitis group sizes not in accessible text).
- **Free-KPV oral stability vs formulation:** the recurring theme that free oral KPV needs
  high doses while NP/prodrug formulations work at far lower doses ([4],[5]) implies free oral
  KPV is delivery-limited; this nuance matters for any dosing extrapolation and is itself
  preclinical.
- **PepT1 upregulation in inflamed colon** is the mechanistic premise enabling targeted
  delivery; it is well-supported in these rodent/in-vitro systems but the human-colon
  quantitative parallel (esp. dosing) is not established here.
