# Section D — Preclinical Skin/Wound/Antimicrobial/Other (KPV)

> Scope note: KPV = Lys-Pro-Val = the C-terminal tripeptide of α-MSH, i.e. **α-MSH(11-13)** (α-MSH = SYSMEHFRWG**KPV**). (The prompt's "α-MSH(11-13)" framing is correct; some vendor pages loosely call it "C-terminal" — same residues.) Where the direct evidence is on the proteolytically-stabilized analogue **KdPT** (Lys-D-Pro-Thr) or the dimer **(CKPV)₂** rather than KPV itself, this is flagged explicitly in-line. Vendor and consumer-blog summaries (e.g. "topical KPV closes wounds 40% faster," "KPV in NC/Nga atopic-dermatitis mice lowers IgE") were screened and are **NOT** carried as claims because no retrievable primary source supports them — see Coverage gaps.

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; species+n; route tags)

### Antimicrobial / antifungal — direct activity

1. KPV (α-MSH 11-13), together with α-MSH(1-13) and α-MSH(6-13), exerts direct antimicrobial activity against *Staphylococcus aureus* and the yeast *Candida albicans* in vitro: the peptides significantly inhibited *S. aureus* colony formation and reduced *C. albicans* viability and germ-tube formation; effects spanned a broad concentration range down to the physiological picomolar range. [1, in_vitro] [8, mechanism_review]

2. The candidacidal effect of these α-MSH peptides is attributed to increasing fungal intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP) — cAMP was elevated in peptide-treated yeast and the adenylyl-cyclase inhibitor dideoxyadenosine partially reversed killing — a mechanism that mimics α-MSH's cAMP-elevating action in mammalian cells. [1, in_vitro] [8, mechanism_review]

3. Crucially, α-MSH peptides retained antimicrobial activity **without** impairing host defense: they did **not** reduce killing of *C. albicans* or *S. aureus* by human neutrophils (in vitro), distinguishing them from conventional anti-inflammatory drugs that blunt microbial clearance. [1, in_vitro]

4. **Replication conflict (integrity flag):** A later independent study synthesizing Ac-KPV-NH₂ (and α-/ε-glycoalkylated analogues) found **no antimicrobial activity** for capped KPV under a variety of standard microbiology assay conditions, while the glycoalkylated forms gained proteolytic stability. The KPV antimicrobial result is therefore **not robustly reproduced** and appears assay-condition-dependent. [13, in_vitro]

5. Consistent with [4], a review from the originating group acknowledges that native α-MSH/KPV-type melanocortins have only **weak** antimicrobial activity in standard growth medium, motivating the design of more rigid synthetic analogues (substituting Gly10 in an MSH(6-13) scaffold) that achieved broad-spectrum activity against *Candida* spp. and Gram-positive/negative bacteria — i.e. the strong antimicrobial promise sits with engineered analogues, not unmodified KPV. [15, in_vitro] [8, mechanism_review]

6. A palmitoylated α-MSH(11-13) analogue [Pal-α-MSH(11-13)] conjugated to gold nanoparticles showed measurable antibacterial activity against methicillin-sensitive and methicillin-resistant *S. aureus* in vitro (MIC ~18 µM for the conjugate, ~12 µM for free Pal-peptide) with biofilm inhibition and reduced cytotoxicity vs the free peptide — again a *modified* KPV-derived construct, not native KPV. [14, in_vitro]

### Antifungal in a whole-animal infection model — via the (CKPV)₂ dimer

7. The KPV-containing synthetic dimer **(CKPV)₂** [Ac-Cys-Lys-Pro-Val-NH₂ ×2] cleared experimental *Candida albicans* vaginitis in rats: intravaginal (CKPV)₂ at 2 mg/kg/day drove vaginal *C. albicans* survival to ~12% by treatment day 11 (vs ~44.7% for miconazole 0.5 mg/kg), dose-dependently inhibited *C. albicans* colony formation in vitro, and acted partly via MC1R-mediated cAMP induction and macrophage M1→M2 polarization (↓TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6, ↑arginase/IL-10). [9, animal] (rat, female Sprague-Dawley, n=60 across groups; route=intravaginal gel)

### Wound healing / mucosal repair

8. KPV accelerates epithelial wound closure. In a chemotherapy- (5-FU) and acid-induced **oral mucositis** model, a mucoadhesive hydrogel releasing KPV promoted re-epithelialization (↑CK10, ↑PCNA) within 3-5 days, improved body-weight and food-intake recovery, and enhanced LPS-stimulated human oral keratinocyte (HOK) migration in scratch-wound assays in vitro. [4, animal] (rat, male Sprague-Dawley 180-200 g, n=6/group; route=topical mucosal hydrogel, 100 µg/mL, 200 µL twice daily ×5 d) [4, in_vitro]

9. The same KPV hydrogel showed direct antibacterial action in vivo: residual MRSA at the wound zone was ~3.1-fold lower with the optimized KPV hydrogel vs the non-optimized formulation, and KPV inhibited *S. aureus* in vitro comparably to amoxicillin — i.e. a combined antibacterial + repair effect at an infected mucosal wound. [4, animal] (rat; route=topical) [4, in_vitro]

10. Orally delivered KPV (hyaluronic-acid-functionalized nanoparticles) promoted **mucosal/wound healing** in DSS ulcerative colitis: in vitro it dose-dependently accelerated recovery of wounded Caco2-BBE monolayers, and in vivo treated colonic tissue was histologically near-normal vs DSS controls, alongside reduced TNF-α mRNA and myeloperoxidase activity. [10, animal] (mouse, FVB male, 8 wk, n=5/group; route=oral gavage, KPV 16 µg/kg/day ×5-6 d) [10, in_vitro]

11. **KdPT (analogue) — epithelial wound/barrier:** In colonic epithelial cells, KdPT increased proliferation, accelerated wound (scratch) closure, raised transepithelial electrical resistance after IFN-γ/TNF-α challenge, and preserved tight-junction protein expression and barrier function in vivo; it protected against DSS colitis and in IL-10⁻/⁻ mice, acting independently of IL-1RI and without melanogenic effect. Direct wound/barrier-repair data are stronger for KdPT than for native KPV. [5, animal] (mouse, DSS + IL-10 gene-deficient; route=oral) [5, in_vitro]

### Skin / dermatologic inflammation

12. The parent C-terminal tripeptide α-MSH(11-13) = KPV inhibits **contact sensitivity** and acute skin/paw inflammation: α-MSH and α-MSH(11-13) significantly reduced acute ear inflammation, mouse-paw edema, and contact-sensitivity responses. This is the foundational in-vivo evidence for KPV in cutaneous inflammation. [12, animal] (mouse; route=systemic; rabbit capillary-permeability data also reported) 

13. **Acne (KdPT analogue):** In human SZ95 sebocytes (in vitro model of acne), KdPT potently suppressed IL-1β-induced IL-6 and IL-8 expression by reducing IκBα degradation, p65 nuclear accumulation and NF-κB DNA binding, lowering intracellular ROS, and decreasing IL-1β surface binding; molecular docking predicted KdPT binds IL-1R type I. KdPT did **not** bind MC-1R (so no pigment/lipid induction), addressing the limitation that makes α-MSH itself unsuitable for acne. [6, in_vitro]

### Mechanism / receptor independence (cross-model, anti-inflammatory)

14. KPV's anti-inflammatory action is **melanocortin-receptor-independent** and likely operates via IL-1β/NF-κB inhibition rather than cAMP/MCR signaling: in crystal-induced peritonitis, systemic KPV reduced PMN accumulation, its effect was not blocked by the MC3/4R antagonist SHU9119, KPV did **not** raise cAMP (unlike MTII), and KPV remained anti-inflammatory in IL-1β-induced peritonitis and in mice with nonfunctional MC1R. [11, animal] (mouse; route=systemic/i.p.) [11, in_vitro]

15. KPV's intestinal anti-inflammatory uptake is **PepT1-mediated**: nanomolar KPV (~10 nM) inhibited NF-κB and MAP-kinase signaling and pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion in human intestinal epithelial (Caco2-BBE, HT29-Cl.19A) and Jurkat T cells; orally administered KPV (100 µM in drinking water) reduced DSS and TNBS colitis severity (↓weight loss, ↓histologic damage, ↓cytokine mRNA). [2, in_vitro] [2, animal] (mouse, C57BL/6; DSS n=5/group, TNBS n=10/group; route=oral)

16. KPV is anti-inflammatory across two independent IBD models, with receptor-independence confirmed: KPV produced earlier recovery, stronger body-weight regain, reduced histologic infiltrate and reduced colonic MPO in DSS colitis and in CD45RB^hi transfer colitis, and remained effective in mice expressing a nonfunctional MC1R (MC1R^e/e). [16, animal] (mouse; DSS + CD45RB^hi transfer + MC1R^e/e; route=systemic/oral)

### "Other inflammation" — gout/crystal arthritis (KPV-derivative (CKPV)₂)

17. In monosodium-urate (MSU) crystal arthritis (gout) inflammation, melanocortins inhibit phagocyte activation: α-MSH and the KPV-dimer **(CKPV)₂** reduced MSU-crystal-stimulated monocyte→neutrophil priming, neutrophil chemotaxis, reactive-oxygen production, CD11b/TLR2/TLR4 surface expression, and IL-1β/IL-8/TNF-α/caspase-1 release. Evidence is in vitro on human primary monocytes/neutrophils; the active agents tested were α-MSH and (CKPV)₂ (not KPV monomer). [17, in_vitro]

## Lab-provenance note

Three independent research lineages dominate Section D, which is good for triangulation but also explains where claims cluster:
- **Catania / Lipton / Cutuli lineage (Milan / UT-Southwestern)** — origin of the KPV/α-MSH *antimicrobial* and cAMP-mechanism claims [1,8,17] and the engineered-analogue work [15]. The strongest antimicrobial claims trace to this single group; the one **independent** antimicrobial test (Songok/Taylor, LSU) [13] failed to reproduce activity for capped KPV. Treat native-KPV antimicrobial potency as **single-lineage + one failed replication** = LOW confidence.
- **Münster melanocortin/dermatology group (Luger, Böhm, Maaser, Kucharzik, Brzoska)** — KPV/KdPT IBD, barrier, sebocyte/acne work [5,6,16]. Internally consistent; the skin/acne and epithelial-wound data lean heavily on the *KdPT analogue*, not KPV itself.
- **Merlin lab (Emory/Georgia State)** — PepT1 mechanism and oral/HA-nanoparticle delivery in colitis with mucosal-wound readouts [2,10]. Robust, mechanistically detailed, but colitis/mucosal — not skin.
- Independent corroboration for KPV anti-inflammation comes from the Perretti lab (London) [11] and the historical Hiltz & Lipton skin work [12].

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **No direct KPV atopic-dermatitis / NC/Nga / IgE primary exists.** Vendor and blog pages assert KPV lowers clinical severity, scratching, epidermal thickening and serum IgE in NC/Nga atopic-dermatitis mice; targeted database searches returned **no** retrievable primary study for this. These claims are **unsupported** and excluded. The only verifiable KPV skin-inflammation in-vivo data are the older contact-sensitivity/paw-edema results [12].
- **The "40% faster wound closure with topical KPV" figure** circulating in blogs is **not traceable** to any retrievable primary; excluded. Real KPV wound/repair evidence is mucosal (oral mucositis [4]; colonic [10]) or KdPT-based [5], not dermal skin wounds.
- **Native-KPV antimicrobial activity is fragile.** The flagship positive result [1] is single-lineage; an independent group found no activity for capped KPV [13], and the originating group concedes weak activity in standard medium [15]. Antimicrobial promise is real mainly for **engineered/dimerized/conjugated** derivatives ((CKPV)₂ [9], Pal-α-MSH(11-13)-GNP [14], Gly10 analogues [15]).
- **Acne and gout evidence is on analogues/dimers, not KPV monomer:** acne = KdPT [6]; gout/MSU = α-MSH + (CKPV)₂ [17]. KPV-monomer-specific acne/arthritis efficacy is unestablished.
- **No dermal (skin) wound-healing in-vivo model with native KPV** (e.g. excisional/diabetic mouse wound) was retrievable — a notable gap given how heavily KPV is marketed for skin/wound repair.
- **No allergic-contact-dermatitis dose-response for KPV vs KdPT** beyond the qualitative statement (seen only in secondary reviews) that "KdPT suppresses contact dermatitis more effectively than KPV" — no retrievable head-to-head primary; not carried as a claim.
- **No human/clinical (rct/open_label) skin, wound, or antimicrobial data for KPV** were found; entire section is preclinical (animal + in_vitro).
- **Ocular:** searches surfaced KPV/KPV-dimer ophthalmic *patents* (e.g. EP1578435A2) and α-MSH uveitis work, but no peer-reviewed KPV-specific ocular efficacy primary; patents are not admitted as efficacy evidence and no ocular claim is made.
