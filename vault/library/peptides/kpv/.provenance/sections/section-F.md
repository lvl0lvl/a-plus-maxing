# Section F — Safety, Regulatory & Anti-Doping (KPV)

**Scope:** KPV = Lys-Pro-Val = α-MSH(11–13), the C-terminal tripeptide of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone. This section covers (1) safety/AE profile, (2) theoretical risks, (3) FDA/regulatory status, (4) WADA anti-doping status, (5) contraindications & monitoring.

**Headline integrity flags (read first):**
- Human safety data on KPV is **essentially nonexistent.** All quantitative safety evidence is preclinical (animal / in vitro). There are no published RCTs, cohorts, or pharmacovigilance datasets on KPV in humans. AE *rates* in humans cannot be stated; only AE *existence/absence in animals* can be.
- KPV is **NOT explicitly named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List** (verified below). It is nonetheless prohibited in sport by operation of category **S0 (Non-Approved Substances)** because it has no human therapeutic approval by any government health authority.
- KPV was **REMOVED from the FDA 503A interim Category 2 list on 15 April 2026** (its nomination was withdrawn — one of 12 peptides removed in that action, same window as BPC-157/TB-500). KPV is now **UNLISTED** (neither Category 1 nor Category 2) pending PCAC review on **23 July 2026**. **Removal is NOT an approval and does not authorize 503A compounding** — KPV is still not lawfully compoundable. (verified below)

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; species+n; AE rate vs existence distinguished)

1. In the foundational anti-inflammatory characterization, α-MSH(11–13) (KPV) inhibited histamine-induced vasopermeability/swelling in a dose-related fashion; the tripeptide retained the anti-inflammatory "message" of full-length α-MSH. No toxicity/AE was reported at the doses tested (rodent model; sample sizes not specified in retrieved abstract). [1, animal]

2. Oral KPV (100 µM in drinking water) reduced the incidence and severity of DSS-induced colitis (C57BL/6 mice, n=5/group) and TNBS-induced colitis (C57BL/6 mice, n=10/group), measured by body-weight loss, myeloperoxidase activity, histology, and pro-inflammatory cytokine mRNA. **No drug-attributable toxicity or adverse findings were reported** at the doses used (existence-of-AE: none reported; this is NOT a measured human AE rate). [2, animal]

3. The KPV anti-inflammatory effect is mediated by the di/tripeptide transporter **PepT1** (in intestinal epithelial and immune cells) and acts intracellularly via suppression of NF-κB and MAPK signaling — i.e., it is **not** melanocortin-receptor-mediated. Mechanistically this matters for safety: KPV does **not** activate MC1R and therefore does **not** drive melanogenesis/tanning the way full-length α-MSH does. [2, animal][3, mechanism_review] (See claim 7 for the residual melanoma caveat.)

4. Orally delivered KPV via hyaluronic-acid-functionalized nanoparticles (16 µg/kg/day × 5 days) alleviated ulcerative colitis in FVB mice; in vitro the KPV nanoparticles showed **no obvious cytotoxicity** in the cell lines tested, and treated mice showed spleen weight, colon length, and colon histology **not significantly different from healthy controls** (existence-of-AE: none detected; not a human AE rate). [4, animal]

5. Acute-toxicity narratives circulating in the secondary/practitioner literature claim rodent studies failed to identify an LD50 (animals tolerating ≥100 mg/kg without mortality), implying a wide therapeutic window, and that chronic dosing (4–12 weeks) produced minimal adverse effects. **These specific dose figures could not be traced to a verifiable primary toxicology publication and are reported here only as practitioner-tier claims — treat as unverified.** [5, practitioner_protocol][6, vendor_label] (qualitative only — vendor/practitioner sources do not ground numerical AE rates.)

6. Reported real-world side effects in community/practitioner use are described as mild and self-limited: transient injection-site irritation, mild topical redness/itching, and occasional mild GI discomfort. These are **uncontrolled anecdotal observations**, qualitative only, with no denominator. [5, practitioner_protocol][7, anecdote_aggregate]

7. **Residual theoretical melanoma/pigmentation concern:** full-length α-MSH stimulates melanogenesis via MC1R and has been discussed as a theoretical proliferation concern; KPV lacks the MC-receptor pharmacophore and does not stimulate pigmentation, which is generally cited as *reducing* this concern. However, no long-term human carcinogenicity data exist for KPV, so the concern cannot be affirmatively cleared. Practitioner sources nonetheless flag a history-of-cancer precaution. [3, mechanism_review][5, practitioner_protocol]

8. **Theoretical immunomodulation risk:** KPV is an immunomodulator (dampens pro-inflammatory cytokine production). The plausible theoretical risk is blunted host defense if used during active infection, in immunodeficiency, or alongside immunosuppressive drugs. Secondary sources assert KPV "does not suppress the immune system" systemically, but this is an over-strong claim given the absence of human immune-function data; treat the immunosuppression question as **open**, not resolved. [3, mechanism_review][5, practitioner_protocol]

## Risk-floor inputs (contraindications / monitoring / stopping — each cited)

- **No established human safety margin.** KPV is an experimental compound with no human PK, dosing, or safety established by controlled research; this is itself the dominant risk-floor input. [3, mechanism_review][8, regulatory]
- **Pregnancy / breastfeeding:** avoid — no safety data (practitioner precaution). [5, practitioner_protocol]
- **History of / active cancer:** caution / avoid pending clinician input — driven by the α-MSH melanocortin lineage and absence of carcinogenicity data. [5, practitioner_protocol][3, mechanism_review]
- **Active infection / immunodeficiency / concurrent immunosuppressants:** caution — KPV is immunomodulatory; combining immune modulators without monitoring is flagged as risky. [5, practitioner_protocol]
- **Source/purity risk (compounded or research-chemical KPV):** FDA's class-level concern for these peptides includes immunogenicity, peptide-related impurities, and incomplete active-ingredient characterization — meaning product quality, not just the molecule, is a risk vector. [9, regulatory][8, regulatory]
- **Objective monitoring (named assays, not a KPV-specific surrogate):** if KPV were used, objective monitoring would track **hs-CRP** (systemic inflammation; [[biomarkers/hs-crp]]), **fecal calprotectin** (objective gut-inflammation marker, for the IBD/gut use case), and **CBC + CMP** (general safety labs, warranted given KPV's immunomodulatory action). These are standard objective inflammation/safety assays — **NOT** validated surrogates of KPV activity; **no KPV-specific validated biomarker of effect exists.** [3, mechanism_review][2, animal]
- **Stopping rule (inferable, not from a trial):** discontinue and seek care for any signs of infection worsening, allergic/injection-site reaction, or unexpected systemic symptoms. No KPV-specific validated monitoring panel exists; no biomarker-based stopping criterion specific to KPV is established (objective monitoring would use the general assays named above). [3, mechanism_review]

## Regulatory & WADA status (cited; stated clearly where NOT listed)

**FDA (United States):**
- KPV is **not FDA-approved** for any indication and is **not** a listed dietary ingredient; products marketed for human use are unapproved drugs. [8, regulatory]
- **503A status — corrected:** KPV was historically placed in the **interim 503A Category 2** list (bulk drug substances "nominated but with identified safety risks"; it was never in Category 1). However, on **15 April 2026 the FDA REMOVED KPV from Category 2** — its nomination was **withdrawn by the nominator**, and KPV was one of **12 peptides removed in that action** (same window as BPC-157 and TB-500). KPV is therefore **now UNLISTED** (in neither Category 1 nor Category 2), in regulatory limbo, **pending PCAC review (23 July 2026).** [9, regulatory][14, regulatory][15, regulatory]
- **Removal from Category 2 is NOT an approval and does NOT authorize compounding.** Per FDA/legal coverage, "removal from Category 2 does not render these bulk drug substances eligible for compounding under section 503A" — KPV remains **not lawfully compoundable** unless and until it is reclassified into Category 1 (or covered by enforcement discretion). The bottom line is unchanged: KPV is not FDA-approved, not lawfully compoundable for these uses, and has no human data. [14, regulatory][15, regulatory]
- FDA's class-level rationale historically cited for these peptides includes **immunogenicity, peptide-related impurities, characterization complexity, and lack of sufficient human safety/exposure data.** This rationale is class-level; a KPV-specific FDA safety statement was not located in the retrieved primaries. [9, regulatory]
- **PCAC review scheduled 23 July 2026** (Day 1, alongside BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c; Day 2 covers DSIP/Emideltide, Semax, Epitalon). The committee will consider whether to recommend these peptides for inclusion on the **503A bulks list**. PCAC recommendations are **non-binding**; even a favorable vote requires notice-and-comment rulemaking (>1 yr typical) before 503A compounding could be lawful. **Do not characterize the July 2026 review as approval.** [9, regulatory][10, regulatory][14, regulatory]

**EU / other:** No EMA marketing authorization for KPV was identified; topical/cosmetic use of KPV-type peptides exists in the unregulated-supplement/cosmetic gray market. EU regulatory status not affirmatively established in retrieved sources — coverage gap.

**WADA Anti-Doping — VERIFIED:**
- **KPV / Lys-Pro-Val is NOT explicitly named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List.** Verification method: (a) the official WADA 2026 Prohibited List and the change-summary coverage of it do not name KPV, any tripeptide, melanocortin, or α-MSH fragment among additions/listed substances [11, regulatory][12, regulatory]; (b) anti-inflammatory tripeptides are not in the named S2 peptide-hormone/growth-factor entries.
- **However, KPV is prohibited at all times under S0 (Non-Approved Substances).** S0 covers "any pharmacological substance which is not approved by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use" (e.g., drugs under preclinical/clinical development, discontinued, designer, or veterinary/research-only). Because KPV has no human therapeutic approval anywhere, an athlete who used it and tested positive would fall under S0. [11, regulatory][13, regulatory]
- **Contrast with growth-factor peptides:** unlike BPC-157 (which anti-doping commentary names directly under S0) or true growth factors (S2), KPV is caught by the *catch-all* S0 mechanism, not by being individually enumerated. The practical anti-doping outcome (prohibited) is the same; the legal route differs. [13, regulatory]
- Athletes should obtain written guidance from their National Anti-Doping Organization before any use.
