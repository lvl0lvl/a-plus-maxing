# Section B — Mechanism of Action (KPV)

KPV is Lys-Pro-Val, the C-terminal tripeptide (residues 11–13) of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH, SYSMEHFRWGKPV). This section covers its anti-inflammatory mechanism. **Bottom line up front:** KPV's anti-inflammatory action is mechanistically well-characterized at the cell/animal level — it converges on inhibition of NF-κB (and MAP kinase) signaling, it is transported into cells by the PepT1 (SLC15A1) di/tripeptide transporter, and its effect is **independent of melanocortin receptors** (intracellular/receptor-independent). All mechanism evidence is in vitro and in animal models; there are no human RCTs establishing these mechanisms in vivo in humans. Mechanism→human is therefore extrapolation.

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; model/cell-line + n)

1. KPV is the C-terminal tripeptide Lys-Pro-Val (α-MSH 11–13) and retains the anti-inflammatory activity of the full α-MSH hormone despite being only 3 of its 13 residues [1, mechanism_review][6, mechanism_review]. The smallest-active-fragment observation traces to early work showing C-terminal α-MSH tripeptide retains anti-inflammatory activity (reviewed in [6, mechanism_review]).

2. At nanomolar concentrations KPV inhibits activation of the NF-κB and MAP-kinase inflammatory signaling pathways and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine/chemokine secretion [2, in_vitro]. Specifically, in human intestinal epithelial models (Caco2-BBE, HT29-Cl.19A) and human Jurkat T cells, ~10 nM KPV suppressed NF-κB activation (luciferase reporter), MAP-kinase phosphorylation (ERK1/2, JNK, p38), and IL-8 expression/secretion [2, in_vitro].

3. KPV's molecular mechanism on NF-κB is intracellular and post-receptor: KPV translocates into the cell nucleus, stabilizes the inhibitor IκBα in TNF-α–stimulated cells, and competitively blocks the interaction between the p65/RelA subunit of NF-κB and the nuclear-import carrier importin-α3 (Imp-α3), thereby suppressing nuclear translocation of p65 [3, in_vitro]. Directly tested on KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) in immortalized human bronchial epithelial cells (16HBE14o-) at 1–10 µg/mL [3, in_vitro].

4. KPV's anti-inflammatory effect is **independent of melanocortin receptors** (MC1R–MC5R). KPV lacks the core sequence motif (His-Phe-Arg-Trp, α-MSH 6–9) required for melanocortin-receptor binding [6, mechanism_review]. In the Caco2-BBE and Jurkat models neither MC3R nor MC5R was expressed and KPV did not raise intracellular cAMP (unlike α-MSH controls), yet it remained anti-inflammatory [2, in_vitro]; in bronchial epithelium the effect was likewise concluded to be "independent of the melanocortin receptor signalling system" and intracellular [3, in_vitro]. In vivo, KPV reduced colitis even in MC1R-mutant (MC1Re/e) mice, indicating effects "at least partially independent of MC1R signaling" [4, animal]. (Note: full-length α-MSH also signals MC-receptor-dependently via cAMP — the dual α-MSH mechanism is documented separately [5, in_vitro]. The receptor-independent arm is what KPV isolates.)

5. KPV enters cells via the proton-coupled oligopeptide transporter PepT1 (SLC15A1), expressed in small-intestinal enterocytes, in colonic epithelium during inflammation, and in colonic/peripheral immune cells; this transporter-mediated uptake is required for KPV's intracellular anti-inflammatory action [2, in_vitro][7, animal]. KPV uptake by PepT1 was confirmed by competitive inhibition and kinetic uptake assays in epithelial and immune cells [2, in_vitro]. PepT1 is up-regulated in inflamed colon (normally low/absent there), which plausibly targets KPV to inflamed tissue [2, in_vitro].

6. PepT1 dependence is causally demonstrated in vivo: in a murine colitis-associated-cancer model, KPV given to wild-type mice reduced inflammation and tumor burden, but the same KPV gave **no benefit in PepT1-knockout mice** — establishing PepT1 transport as necessary for KPV's anti-inflammatory/anti-tumorigenic effect [7, animal]. Murine CAC model, WT vs PepT1-KO [7, animal].

7. KPV is anti-inflammatory in vivo in multiple rodent colitis models. Oral or intrarectal KPV reduced DSS- and TNBS-induced colitis with decreased pro-inflammatory cytokine expression (C57BL/6 mice, DSS 3% or TNBS 150 mg/kg, ~5–10/group, KPV ~100 µM in drinking water) [2, animal]; in an independent lab, KPV reduced disease activity, colon shortening, MPO activity, histologic inflammation and cytokines in DSS and in CD45RBhi T-cell-transfer colitis [4, animal].

8. KPV has direct antimicrobial activity (a mechanism distinct from immune modulation): α-MSH and its C-terminal tripeptide KPV inhibited Staphylococcus aureus colony formation and reduced viability and germ-tube formation of Candida albicans, over a broad concentration range including picomolar; the proposed mechanism is peptide-induced elevation of microbial cAMP [8, in_vitro]. In vitro only; against S. aureus and C. albicans [8, in_vitro].

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **No human data for mechanism.** Every mechanistic and efficacy claim derives from in-vitro (human cell lines + mouse macrophage/Jurkat) or rodent colitis/cancer models. NF-κB/PepT1 mechanisms are **not** demonstrated in living humans; mechanism→human is extrapolation. No RCT, open-label, or regulatory source exists for KPV mechanism.
- **Receptor-independence is well-supported but nuanced.** KPV itself is repeatedly shown MC-receptor-independent ([2],[3]) and lacks the binding motif ([6]); however the *parent* α-MSH has a genuine MC-receptor/cAMP arm ([5]), so "KPV = α-MSH mechanism" is an over-simplification. KPV isolates the intracellular/receptor-independent arm.
- **The importin-α3 / p65 mechanism rests primarily on one KPV-specific study** ([3], 16HBE14o- bronchial epithelium). It is directly tested on KPV (not merely extrapolated from other α-MSH fragments), but independent replication of the exact importin-α3 step is limited; treat the precise molecular target (Imp-α3 competition) as plausible-but-single-lab while the upstream IκBα-stabilization/NF-κB-suppression phenotype is multiply supported ([1],[3]).
- **PepT1 expression on immune cells** is asserted across the literature; the strongest causal PepT1 evidence is the KO-loss-of-effect in the colitis-cancer model ([7]) and epithelial uptake kinetics ([1]). Quantitative human enterocyte/immune-cell uptake parameters for KPV specifically are thin.
- **Antimicrobial mechanism is in-vitro only** ([8], S. aureus + C. albicans, proposed cAMP route); no in-vivo or human antimicrobial efficacy. Distinct from the anti-inflammatory mechanism.
