# KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 non-English-literature survey for **KPV (Lys-Pro-Val, α-MSH(11–13))**, companion to `vault/library/peptides/kpv/research-report.md`. Non-English peer-reviewed sources are admissible under the SAME tier rules as English; primary publication language is noted on every entry. Tools: WebSearch (US-only) + WebFetch. Tavily not connected — sanctioned substitution. Every citation below was checked for a retrievable identifier (PMID / DOI / stable journal/database URL). No citation is fabricated; items found only as secondhand mentions with no locatable record are reported as "none located," not cited.

**Critical admissibility distinction enforced throughout.** A study **authored in China/Italy/Russia but published in English** (PubMed/DOI-indexed English article) is NOT a "genuinely non-English source" — it is already reachable by the English layer and is already in the KPV corpus. Only works whose **primary publication language is Russian, Chinese, or Italian** are counted here as genuine non-English primaries.

**Headline result: ZERO genuinely non-English admissible KPV primaries located** across all three required tracks. Russian = vendors + one α-MSH (full-hormone) review that is not a KPV study. Chinese = vendor/encyclopedic pages + English-published Chinese-group work already captured in the corpus (Merlin/Xiao/Southwest U; Army Medical U; Wenzhou Medical U; China Pharmaceutical U). Italian (originator country) = the originator lineage (Catania/Lipton/Cutuli; Drago/Bonfiglio) published in English; Italian-language hits are commercial vendor pages, not primaries. This is a clean, well-characterized null — the non-English KPV footprint is consumption/marketing of the English corpus, not independent non-English-language primary research.

## Italian / originator-country / other (note)

**Result: NO Italian-language KPV primary located — and this is the EXPECTED valid "none," because the originator lineage published in English.**

KPV's α-MSH-fragment origin lineage is **US (James M. Lipton) + Italian (Anna Catania, with the Drago group at the University of Catania)**. The dispatch flagged the correct outcome up front: where the originator literature is English, "none in Italian" is a valid finding. That is exactly the case here:

- **Cutuli M, Cristiani S, Lipton JM, Catania A.** "Antimicrobial effects of α-MSH peptides." *J Leukoc Biol* 2000;67(2):233–239. **PMID 10670585; DOI 10.1002/jlb.67.2.233.** Affiliation: **3rd Division of Internal Medicine, IRCCS Ospedale Maggiore, Milano, Italy** — an **Italian** group, but **English-language** publication (already corpus ref, the contested antimicrobial primary). Not a non-English source.
- **Drago/Bonfiglio** corneal-epithelial wound-healing work, **University of Catania, Italy** (Bonfiglio 2006, *Exp Eye Res*, PMID 16965771) — **Italian** group, **English** publication (corpus P5). Not a non-English source.
- The foundational Lipton/Catania α-MSH(11–13) anti-inflammatory "message" papers and US composition patents (e.g. US5157023 antipyretic Lys-Pro-Val; US6894028B2 KPV for dermatological disorders, Zengen) are English. Not non-English sources.
