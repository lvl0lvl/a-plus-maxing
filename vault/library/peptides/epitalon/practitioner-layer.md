# Epitalon — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how Epitalon is actually accessed and dosed in practice* — the dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions that circulate in grey-market dosing aggregators, biohacker communities, and named longevity clinics, plus the regulatory and compounding status that governs whether any legitimate clinical prescribing pathway exists. It is **not** an efficacy document. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (Tier 3), and `vendor_label` / `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4) sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) purity/identity/reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** As of mid-2026, Epitalon (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly; AEDG synthetic tetrapeptide) is **not an FDA-approved drug for any indication**. It was removed from the FDA 503A Category 2 bulk drug substances list effective April 22, 2026 (seven calendar days after the April 15, 2026 notice; Federal Register 91 FR 20465, Docket FDA-2025-N-6895). **Removal from Category 2 does not authorize compounding.** FDA explicitly stated: "removal of a bulk drug substance from Category 2 does not, on its own, authorize use of that substance in compounding" and that the agency has not stated it will exercise enforcement discretion with respect to the substance. Epitalon is scheduled for PCAC review July 23–24, 2026, to evaluate its potential inclusion on the 503A bulks list. Until and unless it is placed on the bulks list (Category 1), **503A compounding of Epitalon remains unlawful.** [1, regulatory]
>
> **Epithalamin ≠ Epitalon — the regulatory distinction that pervades this document.** Two chemically distinct preparations share overlapping names in clinical and commercial use. **Epithalamin** is the heterogeneous crude low-molecular-weight polypeptide fraction extracted from bovine pineal gland tissue. Epithalamin has Russian Federation Ministry of Health registration and a defined clinical history of use in Russian geriatric medicine. **Epitalon (AEDG)** is a defined synthetic tetrapeptide developed by Khavinson and colleagues to reproduce the proposed biological activity of Epithalamin. The Russian MoH registration applies only to Epithalamin; it does not extend to the synthetic AEDG tetrapeptide. No Western, EU, or US regulatory authority recognizes either compound. Consumer products marketed under the names "Epitalon," "Epithalon," and "Epithalamin" may contain the synthetic tetrapeptide, the crude extract, or mislabeled mixtures — and standard peptide CoA testing (HPLC purity) cannot distinguish them. The entirety of human clinical outcome evidence (mortality reduction, cardiovascular outcomes, circadian rhythm restoration) was generated with Epithalamin, not with synthetic AEDG. This distinction is the most consequential interpretive point in any evaluation of efficacy claims and is not resolved by grey-market commerce.

## Regulatory and compounding status in detail

**FDA 503A position:** Epitalon was placed on the FDA 503A Category 2 interim list — substances "nominated but not yet determined suitable for compounding" — and compounded widely under that nominal coverage. On April 16, 2026, the FDA published Federal Register notice 91 FR 20465 (Docket FDA-2025-N-6895) announcing that nominations for Epitalon (free base and acetate) and six other peptides (BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTs-C, Emideltide/DSIP, and Semax) had been withdrawn from Category 2, with removal effective seven calendar days later. The notice simultaneously announced the July 23–24, 2026 PCAC meeting to evaluate whether those substances should be added to the 503A bulks list. The National Law Review's legal analysis of this action is explicit: "removal of a bulk drug substance from Category 2 does not, on its own, authorize use of that substance in compounding or bring it within FDA's interim enforcement discretion policy for substances in Category 1." A 503A pharmacy that compounds Epitalon before any Category 1 placement is operating outside settled regulatory authorization. [1, regulatory] [2, regulatory]

**The Epithalamin Russia distinction:** Epithalamin has Russian MoH registration as a pharmaceutical agent derived from pineal gland tissue for correction of endocrine function in geriatric practice. This registration represents real governmental health authority approval — for Epithalamin, in Russia. It does not extend to: (a) the synthetic AEDG tetrapeptide, (b) non-Russian jurisdictions, or (c) any non-geriatric indication. Any clinical data cited in favor of epitalon that draws on Russian MoH authorization is drawing on Epithalamin approval, not synthetic tetrapeptide approval. [3, regulatory]

**EU/UK/Canada/Australia:** Epitalon (synthetic AEDG tetrapeptide) is not authorized for clinical use in any Western jurisdiction. It is not listed on any EMA or MHRA approved-product register. In these jurisdictions it exists exclusively as an unregulated research chemical with no prescribing pathway.

**WADA Prohibited List (2026):** Epitalon is prohibited in sanctioned sport in-competition and out-of-competition under WADA 2026 Prohibited List Section S0 (Non-Approved Substances), which captures pharmacological entities lacking current governmental regulatory health authority approval. No individual listing by name is required; absence of approval is sufficient. [4, regulatory]

## Safety as the dominant practitioner concern

**Telomerase activation — the oncogenic tension.** Epitalon's proposed primary mechanism is induction of telomerase (hTERT) activity, resulting in telomere elongation and delay of replicative senescence. Telomerase reactivation is simultaneously the central hallmark of malignant cell immortalization. This is not a coincidental overlap — it reflects the same underlying biology. The theoretical concern is that systemic telomerase induction could enable or accelerate existing subclinical malignancy. This concern is biologically coherent and has not been resolved by the available literature. Animal studies from the Khavinson group and the Anisimov group at the N.N. Petrov Research Institute of Oncology report oncostatic (not pro-oncogenic) effects, but these are same-institutional findings with structural COI and have not been independently replicated. The 2025 Brunel University replication (Al-Dulaimi et al., *Biogerontology*) found that in cancer cell lines, hTERT was downregulated and telomere elongation proceeded via Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres (ALT) rather than canonical telomerase — a mechanistic discrepancy that deepens rather than resolves the oncogenic concern. No independent long-term carcinogenicity study of synthetic AEDG in any species has been published. [6, in_vitro] [7, mechanism_review]

**The extract-vs.-synthetic evidence confound as a safety issue.** The human mortality and cardiovascular outcome data — the most clinically significant safety-and-benefit information available — were generated using Epithalamin (the crude bovine pineal extract), not synthetic AEDG. Epithalamin contains multiple peptide fragments, hormones, and bioactive molecules in addition to AEDG. The safety profile of Epithalamin over 6–15 years of observation in elderly populations does not transfer mechanistically to synthetic AEDG. Any toxicity unique to the synthetic tetrapeptide, or any benefit absent in the synthetic that was provided by other Epithalamin components, remains entirely uncharacterized. This is not a footnote — it is the principal epistemic gap in the compound's human evidence record. [8, rct] [9, open_label]

**Active or prior malignancy as primary contraindication.** Given the telomerase-oncogenic tension, active cancer is the primary absolute contraindication for Epitalon use. TrufaMED (Surfside, FL) independently names this: the clinic states Epitalon is "not appropriate for patients with active malignancy without oncology clearance." A practitioner administering Epitalon to a patient with active or recent cancer would be doing so without any published human safety data for that population and against the mechanistic grain of the dominant known biological action. [10, practitioner_protocol]

**Stereoisomeric uncertainty.** Epitalon exists in eight possible stereoisomeric forms (all combinations of L/D configuration at each of the four amino acid positions). Only the all-L form has been studied in any published research. Grey-market synthetic peptides manufactured without rigorous quality control may contain off-target stereoisomers. The pharmacology and toxicology of non-native stereoisomers are entirely uncharacterized. Unlike FOXO4-DRI (where D-chirality is the intentional active form), for Epitalon the all-L form is the studied form and any L/D mixtures represent contamination or manufacturing error, not a deliberate design. [11, mechanism_review]

**No human PK data for any route in any jurisdiction.** No published study in any language has measured plasma concentration, half-life, volume of distribution, or route-bioavailability for synthetic AEDG administered subcutaneously, intramuscularly, or orally in humans. Extrapolation of any dose across routes is without pharmacokinetic anchor.

**Practical contraindication summary for any clinical or grey-market context:**

1. Active or recent malignancy — primary absolute contraindication; telomerase oncogenic mechanism
2. Personal or strong family history of hereditary cancer syndromes (BRCA, Lynch, Li-Fraumeni, etc.) — high-caution; no safety data
3. Pregnancy — no reproductive toxicology data; absolute contraindication
4. Athletes in sanctioned sport — WADA S0 prohibited, in- and out-of-competition [4, regulatory]
5. Pediatric use — no data; no biological rationale for telomere intervention in non-aged somatic cells
6. Immunocompromised individuals — no safety data; theoretical risk from peptide-mediated immune modulation described in Khavinson-group literature but not independently characterized

These are mechanism-derived and regulatory-derived constraints. No clinical guidelines exist because no regulatory body recognizes Epitalon for any indication.

## Grey-market dose/cycle/route conventions

**There is no human dose-finding trial for synthetic AEDG epitalon in any jurisdiction.** Published clinical data — the Khavinson group's 6-8 year mortality cohort and the Korkushko 15-year cardiovascular follow-up — used **intramuscularly administered Epithalamin** at institutional dosing protocols, not subcutaneous synthetic tetrapeptide. Every figure in this section is a *convention* extrapolated by grey-market communities and longevity-aggregator sites from animal protocols or from the Epithalamin clinical programs via imputed equivalence. These figures carry no evidentiary weight for synthetic AEDG; they are documented because they are real and because practitioners and patients encounter them.

**Clinic convention vs. grey-market convention:** The TrufaMED clinic (physician-supervised) uses 10 mg/day for 10–20 days, 2–4 cycles/year [10, practitioner_protocol]. Huemn clinic uses 1–5 mg/day without a publicly stated cycle length [12, practitioner_protocol]. These are higher-dose-shorter-cycle vs. lower-dose-longer-cycle interpretations of the same absence of data.

**The Epithalamin IM-dose disconnect.** Khavinson and Morozov 2003 — the most-cited human study — used Epithalamin administered intramuscularly at institutional protocols. The route, dose, bioavailability, and pharmacokinetics of subcutaneous synthetic AEDG bear no established relationship to the intramuscularly administered crude extract that generated the mortality data. The community dosing conventions are inferentially extrapolated from Epithalamin IM protocols by imputed equivalence — a step that has not been validated in any published study.

## Reconstitution / route notes (admin math only)

Reconstitution conventions are admin bookkeeping (Tier-4 vendor/aggregator math; cannot ground efficacy or safety).

**Standard reconstitution (Epitalon, 10 mg vial):**

| Vial | + Bac. water | Concentration | 5 mg dose | 10 mg dose |
|------|-------------|---------------|-----------|------------|
| 10 mg | 2.0 mL | 5.0 mg/mL | 1.0 mL (100 U on U-100) | 2.0 mL (200 U on U-100) |
| 10 mg | 1.0 mL | 10 mg/mL | 0.5 mL (50 U on U-100) | 1.0 mL (100 U on U-100) |

Source: [14, anecdote_aggregate] for reconstitution targets.

- **Reconstitution technique:** Inject bacteriostatic water slowly down the vial wall; swirl gently (do NOT shake — agitation stresses peptide bonds); inspect for clarity and label with preparation date
- **Storage:** Lyophilized powder at −20°C; reconstituted at 2–8°C; use within 2–4 weeks per aggregator convention (more conservative: within 5–7 days); avoid freeze-thaw cycles; protect from light
- **Route:** Subcutaneous; abdomen, thigh, or upper arm; rotate injection sites; insert at 45–90° angle with U-100 insulin syringe (27–31G, 0.5–1.0 mL)
- **Oral framing:** Some clinics (Huemn) mention oral formulations. Oral bioavailability of a tetrapeptide is subject to gastric and intestinal protease degradation; no human or animal pharmacokinetic study has characterized oral AEDG bioavailability. Oral route conventions are not supported by published data.

**Stereoisomer caveat:** Grey-market Epitalon is manufactured with varying levels of quality control. The all-L stereoisomeric form is the only form with any published research. Synthesis errors or deliberate cost-cutting can produce L/D mixtures. Standard HPLC CoA cannot resolve stereoisomeric purity in a tetrapeptide without chiral HPLC columns and methods not typically offered in research-peptide certificates of analysis. Purchased material may or may not be the all-L configuration. [11, mechanism_review]

## Monitoring framework (experimental / off-label context only)

No published clinical guideline, society recommendation, or validated monitoring protocol exists for synthetic AEDG Epitalon in humans. If a clinician were tracking a patient in an explicitly experimental capacity under full informed consent, the following is a mechanistic scaffold derived from the telomerase-oncogenic tension, the Epithalamin immunomodulatory literature, and basic safety monitoring:

**Baseline (before starting):**

| Domain | Tests | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Malignancy clearance | Age- and sex-appropriate cancer screening current; oncology clearance if ANY active or recent cancer or hereditary syndrome | Telomerase-oncogenic tension; primary absolute contraindication [6, in_vitro] |
| Circadian / melatonin baseline | Serum melatonin (AM/PM); questionnaire sleep quality | Claimed melatonin-restoration mechanism; baseline required before inferring change |
| Immune function | CBC with differential, NK cell activity if accessible | Khavinson-group Epithalamin literature reports immune modulation; characterize baseline |
| Hepatic | ALT, AST, GGT | No hepatic safety data for synthetic AEDG; standard safety panel |
| Renal | BUN, creatinine, eGFR | No renal clearance data for AEDG; standard safety panel |
| Cancer biomarkers (age/risk-appropriate) | PSA (men ≥50), CA-125 (women with risk), CEA if applicable | Baseline oncologic surveillance before telomerase activator; establish pre-treatment reference |

**Follow-up (30 days post-first cycle):**
- Repeat cancer biomarkers established at baseline
- CBC with differential
- Hepatic panel
- Sleep diary / circadian symptom review

**Follow-up (annually if continuing):**
- Full age-appropriate cancer screening
- CBC, hepatic, renal
- Assess ongoing risk-benefit given evolving regulatory status

**Stopping rules (mechanism-derived, not validated):**
- Any new cancer diagnosis or suspicious finding during use → stop; oncology evaluation before any re-challenge
- ALT or AST > 3× upper limit of normal at any on-cycle measurement
- Rapid unexplained rise in a cancer biomarker (PSA, CEA, CA-125) from baseline
- Systemic immunogenic reaction (rash, urticaria, angioedema, anaphylaxis)
- Persistent injection-site nodule, induration, or abscess (>2 weeks)

**Honest limit:** This framework is a mechanistic extrapolation. No human study has applied it. Given the absolute absence of human PK, human safety, or human efficacy data for synthetic AEDG administered by any route, any application should be accompanied by formal adverse-event reporting and ethics-board-level informed-consent documentation.
