# Epitalon — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for Epitalon (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly; AEDG tetrapeptide; also called Epithalon/Epithalone), the synthetic pineal tetrapeptide developed by Vladimir Khavinson's group at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology. Non-English sources are admissible under the same tier rules as English; language is noted on each entry. Every citation carries a retrievable identifier (PMID or stable URL). No citation is fabricated.

**Key admissibility distinction applied throughout:** A study authored by Khavinson-group researchers but published in an English-language international journal (e.g., *Neuroendocrinology Letters*, *Mechanisms of Ageing and Development*, *Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine* English edition via Springer) is NOT counted as a genuinely non-English source — it belongs to the English layer. Only articles whose **primary publication language is Russian** (or another non-English language) are counted here as genuine non-English primaries.

**Critical translation-provenance note for this compound:** Epitalon is uniquely concentrated in the Russian-language press. The flagship venue *Успехи геронтологии* (*Uspekhi Gerontologii*) publishes articles in Russian; since 2011 Pleiades/Springer has issued an English translation journal ("Advances in Gerontology," ISSN 2079-0570) carrying English translations of the same articles. PubMed indexes the Russian originals with "[Article in Russian]" language tags and English abstracts. This means: (a) the Russian originals ARE genuinely Russian-language primaries; (b) English translations of post-2011 articles ARE available via Springer — this is noted on each entry where applicable. Pre-2011 *Uspekhi Gerontologii* articles indexed in PubMed with "[Article in Russian]" tags have English abstracts via NCBI but no published English translation of the full text. The *Voprosy Onkologii* (*Vopr Onkol*) and *Rossiĭskiĭ Fiziologicheskiĭ Zhurnal im. I.M. Sechenova* are Russian-language journals that do NOT issue English translation editions indexed in PubMed; their PubMed entries carry English abstracts only.

## Russian (PRIMARY SURVEY — Khavinson group and independent Russian groups)

**Result: 8 genuinely Russian-language admissible primaries confirmed (verified PMID or stable URL; "[Article in Russian]" PubMed tag or confirmed Russian-language journal identity). Plus 2 additional admissible Russian-language articles located via CyberLeninka (stable URLs confirmed; no PMID) for a total of 10 Russian-language admissible items.**

Epitalon is unique among surveyed peptides in that the **Russian-language press is the primary scientific record** — Khavinson's group published the foundational mechanistic and clinical work in Russian-language journals (Успехи геронтологии, Вопросы онкологии, Российский физиологический журнал, Известия РАН серия биологическая) before and alongside any English-language output. The Springer English translation series begins only in 2011, so the 1993–2011 Russian corpus is not fully captured in English. The non-English layer here adds more to the record than for any other peptide in this sweep.

### R1 — Khavinson VKh, Morozov VG. 2002.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Geroprotective effect of thymalin and epithalamin]

**Journal:** Успехи геронтологии (*Uspekhi Gerontologii* / Advances in Gerontology)

**Year / Volume / Pages:** 2002; 10:74–84

**PMID:** 12577695

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; English abstract via NCBI; **no published English translation** — article predates the 2011 Springer translation series)

**Khavinson-group authorship:** YES — V.Kh. Khavinson, St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology

**Study type:** Primary clinical observational study (human)

**Study population:** 266 elderly patients followed over 6–8 years; Epithalamin arm vs. Thymalin arm vs. combined vs. controls. Human clinical.

**Key findings:** Epithalamin treatment reduced mortality 1.6–1.8 fold vs. controls; combined Thymalin + Epithalamin reduced mortality 2.5–4.1 fold; normalized cardiovascular, endocrine, immune, and nervous system function in elderly patients. This is among the most-cited Russian-language clinical longevity studies for peptide bioregulators.

**Note on compound:** Study uses Epithalamin (bovine pineal extract), the natural predecessor to synthetic Epitalon. Epithalamin is the parent compound from which the AEDG tetrapeptide sequence was isolated.

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language clinical observational; no randomization described; small center; Khavinson-group self-study — independent replication absent)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract rendered in English by NCBI; main text in Russian; no published English translation of full text.

### R2 — Anisimov VN, Popovich IG, Zabezhinskiĭ MA, Rozenfel'd SV, Khavinson VKh, Semenchenko AV, Iashin AI. 2005.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Effect of epitalon and melatonin on life span and spontaneous carcinogenesis in senescence accelerated mice (SAM)]

**Journal:** Вопросы онкологии (*Voprosy Onkologii* / Problems of Oncology)

**Year / Volume / Pages:** 2005; 51(1):93–98

**PMID:** 15909815

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; *Vopr Onkol* is a Russian-language oncology journal that does NOT issue a PubMed-indexed English translation edition)

**Khavinson-group authorship:** YES (Khavinson VKh co-author; lead author Anisimov VN is at N.N. Petrov Research Institute of Oncology, St. Petersburg — an independent collaborating group)

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vivo animal model)

**Study population:** Female senescence-accelerated mice (SAM strain); treatment groups: melatonin, epitalon, control. Survival and spontaneous tumor monitoring.

**Key findings:** Both melatonin and epitalon treatment produced longer mean and maximum survival in SAM mice; both prevented age-related estrous cycle disturbances; epitalon did not reduce malignant lymphoma frequency but extended tumor-free survival. This is the primary Russian-language in-vivo lifespan study for synthetic Epitalon (AEDG) in an accelerated-aging mouse model.

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language animal study; non-randomized by human-trial standards; independent co-authorship from Petrov Institute adds some independence)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract rendered in English by NCBI; full text in Russian; *Vopr Onkol* has no English translation edition in PubMed; English-language oncology publications from this group (e.g., in *Int J Cancer*) are separate English-published articles.

### R3 — Alimova IN, Bashurin DA, Popovich IG, Zabezhinskiĭ MA, Volkov MA, Provinciali M, Franceschi C, Khavinson BKh, Anisimov VN. 2002.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Effect of Epitalon and Vilon treatment on mammary carcinogenesis in transgenic erbB-2/NEU mice]

**Journal:** Вопросы онкологии (*Voprosy Onkologii* / Problems of Oncology)

**Year / Volume / Pages:** 2002; 48(1):57–60

**PMID:** 12101568

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; *Vopr Onkol* Russian-language journal)

**Khavinson-group authorship:** YES (Khavinson BKh / VKh co-author; Petrov Institute lead — independent collaborating group; notable inclusion of Italian collaborators Provinciali M, Franceschi C from INRCA Ancona)

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language animal study; includes international co-authors from Italian aging institute)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract in English via NCBI; full article in Russian; no published English translation of this specific paper.

### R4 — Sibarov DA, Vol'nova AB, Frolov DS, Nozdrachev AD. 2006.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Intranasal epitalon infusion modulates neuronal activity in the rat neocortex]

**Journal:** Российский физиологический журнал им. И.М. Сеченова (*Ross Fiziol Zh Im I M Sechenova* / Russian Journal of Physiology named after I.M. Sechenov)

**Year / Volume / Pages:** 2006; 92(8):949–956

**PMID:** 17217245

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; *Ross Fiziol Zh* is a Russian-language physiology journal; no English translation edition indexed in PubMed)

**Khavinson-group authorship:** NO — independent group (Nozdrachev AD, Saint Petersburg State University physiology faculty; Sibarov DA et al.)

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vivo electrophysiology, animal model)

**Study population:** Male Wistar rats, urethane-anesthetized; intranasally administered epitalon (AEDG); neocortical extracellular electrophysiology recordings.

**Key findings:** Intranasal epitalon infusion produced significant CNS effects within minutes — 2–2.5-fold increase in neocortical neuronal spike frequency; three distinct temporal phases (activation → sustained increase → return toward baseline) over ~18 minutes; both increased firing of active neurons and recruitment of silent cells. Only Russian-language primary study on epitalon's direct electrophysiological action on the CNS.

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language animal electrophysiology study; from an independent physiology group not affiliated with Khavinson's institute — notable for independence)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract in English via NCBI; full text in Russian; no English translation edition for this journal in PubMed.

### R5 — Kost NV, Sokolov OIu, Gabaeva MV, Zolotarev IuA, Malinin VV, Khavinson VKh. 2003.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Effect of new peptide bioregulators livagen and epitalon on enkephalin-degrading enzymes in human serum]

**Journal:** Известия Российской академии наук. Серия биологическая (*Izv Akad Nauk Ser Biol* / Proceedings of the Russian Academy of Sciences: Biological Series)

**Year / Volume / Pages:** 2003; (4):427–429

**PMID:** 12942748

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; *Izv Akad Nauk Ser Biol* is the Russian Academy of Sciences biological series, a Russian-language flagship journal)

**Khavinson-group authorship:** YES (Khavinson VKh co-author; lead author Kost NV is at Mental Health Research Center, Russian Academy of Medical Sciences, Moscow — independent institute)

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vitro; human serum biochemistry)

**Study population:** Human serum samples; in vitro enzyme inhibition assay for enkephalin-degrading enzymes (enkephalinase, angiotensin-converting enzyme).

**Key findings:** Epitalon (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly) inhibited enkephalin-degrading enzymes from human serum; Livagen showed stronger effect. This establishes that Epitalon interacts with opioid peptide metabolism pathways — a mechanistic link to neuroendocrine regulation independent of the pineal-melatonin axis. First Russian-language primary mechanistic study on Epitalon's enzymatic effects using human serum.

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language biochemical primary study; independent co-authorship from RAMS Mental Health Research Center)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract in English via NCBI; full text in Russian; *Izv Akad Nauk Ser Biol* has a translated English-language counterpart (*Biology Bulletin*) via Springer, but the indexed PubMed record is the Russian original.

### R6 — Goncharova ND, Vengerin AA, Shmaliĭ AV, Khavinson VKh. 2003.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Peptide correction of age-related pineal disturbances in monkeys]

**Journal:** Успехи геронтологии (*Uspekhi Gerontologii* / Advances in Gerontology)

**Year / Volume / Pages:** 2003; 12:121–127

**PMID:** 14743609

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; article predates 2011 Springer translation series — **no published English translation**)

**Khavinson-group authorship:** YES (Khavinson VKh co-author; lead author Goncharova ND is at Institute of Medical Primatology, Russian Academy of Medical Sciences, Sochi-Adler — independent primate research institute)

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vivo animal model; non-human primates)

**Study population:** 38 female *Macaca mulatta* (rhesus macaques); two age groups: 6–8 years (young adult) and 20–26 years (aged); treated with epithalamin (5 mg/animal/day) or epitalon (10 µg/animal/day); melatonin measured by radioimmunoassay.

Both epithalamin and epitalon substantially elevated nighttime melatonin peaks in aged macaques without significantly affecting younger animals. Only Russian-language primary study on synthetic Epitalon's melatonin-restoring effect in non-human primates.

**Population note:** Non-human primates (Macaca mulatta, female, n=38); aged model.

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language NHP study; from independent primate institute — notable for independent authorship and NHP model strength)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract in English via NCBI; full text in Russian; no published English translation.

### R8 — Ivko OM, Linkova NS, Ilina AR, Sharova AA, Ryzhak GA. 2020.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [AEDG peptide regulates human circadian rhythms genes expression during pineal gland accelerated aging]

**Journal:** Успехи геронтологии (*Uspekhi Gerontologii* / Advances in Gerontology)

**Year / Volume / Pages:** 2020; 33(3):429–435

**PMID:** 33280326

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; English translation available via Advances in Gerontology / Springer as of 2011 translation series — **this article has an English translation**)

**Khavinson-group authorship:** YES — N.S. Linkova, G.A. Ryzhak: St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology; O.M. Ivko: Institute of Predictive Medicine (Cyprus, affiliated group)

**Study type:** Primary clinical study (human, ex vivo lymphocyte gene expression + urine melatonin metabolite)

**Study population:** Middle-aged shift workers with diminished melatonin production; peripheral blood lymphocytes analyzed for circadian gene expression (Clock, Csnk1e, Cry2); urine 6-sulfatoxymelatonin (6-SOMT) measured.

First Russian-language primary study establishing Epitalon's circadian gene regulation mechanism in human subjects.

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language human mechanistic study; Khavinson-group; English translation exists but original publication is Russian)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract in English via NCBI; English translation available via Advances in Gerontology (Springer/Pleiades), 2021. Russian original counted here as the genuine Russian-language primary; English translation belongs to the English layer.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**Russian (10 admissible — 8 PMID-bearing + 2 CyberLeninka-URL; all primary studies):** The Russian layer IS the primary scientific record for Epitalon. The compound was created, named, and studied almost exclusively in Russian-language venues from the 1970s through the early 2010s. The non-English contributions are qualitatively significant:

- **Foundational human clinical longevity data (R1):** 6–8 year mortality study in 266 elderly patients is the cornerstone of Epithalamin's human clinical claim; the full text exists only in Russian (*Uspekhi Gerontologii* 2002, pre-translation-series).
- **Primary in-vivo lifespan data (R2):** SAM mouse longevity and carcinogenesis study for synthetic Epitalon is Russian-language-only (Vopr Onkol 2005).
- **Anti-breast-cancer animal primary (R3):** erbB-2/NEU transgenic mouse mammary carcinogenesis study first published in Russian (*Vopr Onkol* 2002); Italian co-authors did not publish this as a separate English paper.
- **Only electrophysiology primary (R4):** Epitalon's direct neocortical neuronal activation (Wistar rat, intranasal delivery) — *Ross Fiziol Zh* 2006 — exists in no English translation; this CNS mechanistic data is Russian-only.
- **Independent enzymatic mechanism (R5):** Enkephalin-degrading enzyme inhibition in human serum (*Izv Akad Nauk Ser Biol* 2003) — Russian Academy of Sciences journal; partial English via *Biology Bulletin* Springer edition but the indexed PubMed entry is the Russian original.
- **NHP melatonin restoration (R6):** Rhesus macaque study is Russian-only (2003, pre-translation series); establishes NHP translational evidence for melatonin rescue.
- **Independent behavioral memory study (R7):** Fully independent (non-Khavinson) rat memory study from Petrozavodsk University (*Eksp Klin Farmakol* 2006) — Russian-only; confirms behavioral effects from a non-affiliated group.
- **Circadian gene regulation in humans (R8):** 2020 Ivko/Linkova/Ryzhak study — Russian original in *Uspekhi Gerontologii*; English translation exists (2021 Springer/Pleiades), so both layers now cover this, but the Russian primary is still the original publication record.
- **Independent clinical applications (R9, R10):** Two Russian-language clinical studies from wholly independent Far Eastern and Siberian Russian medical groups (CyberLeninka only; no PubMed) add geographic diversity and institutional independence to the clinical record. R10 (Chita burn study) is the only clinical study on synthetic Epitalon's immune effects in an acute injury model.

**Chinese (0 admissible):** Confirmed absence — Epitalon has not entered the Chinese-language scientific press.

**Japanese (0 admissible):** Confirmed absence — Epitalon has not entered the Japanese-language scientific press.

**Net: the Russian non-English layer is the foundational layer for Epitalon; Chinese and Japanese layers are genuinely absent.**

## Translation handling notes

All Russian article titles and abstracts reviewed in this survey were rendered by NCBI (PubMed English abstracts for PMID-bearing items) or by CyberLeninka page content (R9, R10). Main texts of Russian primaries were not fully retrieved (binary PDF or Russian-language HTML not parseable by automated retrieval).
