## Section B — Efficacy (Phase 2)

**INVESTIGATIONAL-COMPOUND BANNER.** Retatrutide (formerly LY3437943) is a once-weekly, single-molecule **triple agonist** of the GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors. As of this writing it is **investigational and NOT approved by the FDA, EMA, or any regulator for any indication.** Every efficacy figure in this section comes from **Phase 1 or Phase 2 trials** — early- to mid-stage studies that establish proof-of-concept and inform dose selection, but do **not** constitute confirmatory evidence. None of the magnitudes below has been confirmed in a peer-reviewed Phase 3 outcome publication. They should be read as **promising but unconfirmed** signals, and they cannot be assumed to predict Phase 3 results, long-term durability, or safety at the population scale. (See note on Phase 3 / TRIUMPH status at the end of this section.)

### Defining efficacy fact (Phase 2, not confirmed)

In its pivotal Phase 2 obesity trial, retatrutide produced a **least-squares mean weight reduction of about −24% (−24.2%) at 48 weeks on the 12 mg dose** in adults with obesity and **no diabetes** — the **largest mean weight loss reported to date for any incretin-based pharmacotherapy in a clinical trial.** This is the headline result, and it must be carried with two qualifiers attached: it is **Phase 2 (mid-stage, dose-finding)** and **investigational/not approved.** [1]

### Receptor pharmacology and proof of concept (Phase 1)

Retatrutide's mechanism distinguishes it from dual (GLP-1/GIP) agonists such as tirzepatide: it adds **glucagon-receptor (GCGR) agonism**, which is thought to increase energy expenditure, while the GLP-1 and GIP components reduce caloric intake. In the discovery-to-proof-of-concept report, the molecule was characterized as having **balanced GCGR and GLP-1R activity with greater GIPR activity**, and a single dose reduced body weight in healthy participants with the effect persisting **up to day 43**, supporting once-weekly dosing. `[type: rct/PK-PD]` [2]

A **Phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose trial in people with type 2 diabetes** (N=72; doses 0.5, 1.5, 3 mg, escalated 3/6 mg, and escalated 3/6/9/12 mg, vs. placebo and dulaglutide 1.5 mg) reported **placebo-adjusted HbA1c reductions up to ~−1.6%** and **body-weight reduction up to −8.96 kg** in the highest-escalation arm, with a **terminal half-life of ~6 days**. Gastrointestinal events were the most common adverse events. These are **Phase 1b, small-N, hypothesis-generating** findings. `[type: rct]` [3]

### Obesity — Phase 2 (the headline trial)

**Jastreboff AM, Kaplan LM, et al. *N Engl J Med* 2023 (PMID 37366315; NCT04881760).** `[type: rct]`

- **Design / population:** Phase 2, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. **N=338** adults (51.8% men) with **obesity (BMI ≥30), or overweight (BMI 27 to <30) plus ≥1 weight-related condition**, **without type 2 diabetes** as the studied population. Duration **48 weeks**; primary endpoint = percentage change in body weight at **24 weeks**. [1]
- **Doses:** 1 mg, 4 mg (initial 2 or 4 mg), 8 mg (initial 2 or 4 mg), 12 mg (initial 2 mg), or placebo, once weekly. [1]
- **Primary endpoint — LS-mean weight change at 24 weeks (Phase 2, not confirmed):** 1 mg **−7.2%**; 4 mg (combined) **−12.9%**; 8 mg (combined) **−17.3%**; 12 mg **−17.5%**; **placebo −1.6%**. [1]
- **Weight change at 48 weeks (Phase 2, not confirmed):** 1 mg **−8.7%**; 4 mg (combined) **−17.1%**; 8 mg (combined) **−22.8%**; 12 mg **−24.2%**; **placebo −2.1%**. A clear **monotonic dose-response** is evident, and at 48 weeks the higher doses had **not reached a weight-loss plateau**, which the investigators flagged as notable. [1]
- **Responder thresholds at 48 weeks (4 / 8 / 12 mg doses; Phase 2):** ≥5% body-weight loss **92% / 100% / 100%**; ≥10% **75% / 91% / 93%**; ≥15% **60% / 75% / 83%**. The trial reported that on 12 mg, roughly **a quarter of participants lost ≥30%** of body weight — an effect size in the range previously associated only with bariatric surgery, but here observed in a **single mid-stage trial**. [1]
- **Tolerability:** Most common adverse events were **gastrointestinal (nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation)**, **dose-related**, mostly **mild-to-moderate**, and partially mitigated by a lower starting dose. Detailed safety is covered in Section C. [1]

### Type 2 diabetes — Phase 2

**Rosenstock J, Frias J, et al. *Lancet* 2023 (PMID 37385280; NCT04867785).** `[type: rct]`

- **Design / population:** Randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, **placebo- and active-comparator (dulaglutide 1.5 mg) controlled**, parallel-group Phase 2 trial conducted in the USA. **N=281** randomized (275 in efficacy analyses). Adults **18–75 y with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c 7.0–10.5%, BMI 25–50**, on diet/exercise or metformin ≥1000 mg/day. Mean age 56.2 y; mean diabetes duration 8.1 y; 56% female. Duration **36 weeks**; primary endpoint = HbA1c change at **24 weeks**. [4]
- **Doses:** retatrutide 0.5 mg; 4 mg (with and without escalation); 8 mg (slow and fast escalation); 12 mg (escalation); plus dulaglutide 1.5 mg and placebo. [4]
- **HbA1c change at 24 weeks — LS-mean (Phase 2, not confirmed):** placebo **−0.01%**; **dulaglutide 1.5 mg −1.41%**; retatrutide 0.5 mg **−0.43%**; 4 mg (escalation) **−1.39%**; 4 mg (no escalation) **−1.30%**; 8 mg (slow) **−1.99%**; 8 mg (fast) **−1.88%**; **12 mg (escalation) −2.02%**. The top retatrutide doses **numerically exceeded the active comparator dulaglutide** within this single trial. [4]
- **Weight change at 36 weeks (% change; Phase 2):** placebo **−3.00%**; dulaglutide **−2.02%**; retatrutide 0.5 mg **−3.19%**; 4 mg (escalation) **−7.92%**; 4 mg (no escalation) **−10.37%**; 8 mg (slow) **−16.81%**; 8 mg (fast) **−16.34%**; **12 mg −16.94%** — i.e., **up to ~−17%** in a T2D population, where weight loss is typically blunted relative to non-diabetic populations. [4]
- **Tolerability:** Mild-to-moderate GI adverse events in **35%** (67/190) of retatrutide participants vs **13%** placebo and **35%** dulaglutide; **no severe hypoglycemia and no deaths** during the study. [4]

### Liver fat / MASLD — Phase 2a substudy

**Sanyal AJ, Kaplan LM, Frias JP, et al. *Nat Med* 2024 (PMID 38858523).** `[type: rct]`

A Phase 2a substudy in **N=98** participants with **metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)** and ≥10% liver fat (doses 1, 4, 8, 12 mg vs placebo over 48 weeks) reported **relative liver-fat reductions at 24 weeks** of **−42.9% / −57.0% / −81.4% / −82.4%** (1/4/8/12 mg) vs **+0.3%** placebo (all active doses P<0.001), with **normal liver fat (<5%) achieved in 27% / 52% / 79% / 86%** of dose groups vs **0%** placebo. These are **Phase 2a, surrogate-endpoint (imaging) findings**, not histology-confirmed outcomes, and remain investigational. [5]

### Cross-class comparison — stated carefully

The retatrutide Phase 2 obesity signal of **~−24% at 48 weeks (12 mg)** is **numerically larger** than the headline mean weight losses reported in the pivotal trials of approved incretin agents — semaglutide 2.4 mg (~−15% over 68 weeks, STEP-1) and tirzepatide 15 mg (~−21% over 72 weeks, SURMOUNT-1). **This comparison must be treated as cross-trial and indirect**, not a head-to-head: the trials differ in **duration (retatrutide 48 wk vs the comparators 68–72 wk), populations, escalation schemes, and design**, and **retatrutide remains Phase 2 while the comparators have completed Phase 3 and are approved.** No randomized head-to-head trial of retatrutide against semaglutide or tirzepatide has been published. The accurate framing is: **in a mid-stage trial, retatrutide produced the largest mean weight loss yet seen for an incretin agent, a finding that is striking but unconfirmed.** `[type: rct, cross-trial/indirect]` [1]

### Phase 3 / TRIUMPH status (integrity note)

**No peer-reviewed Phase 3 outcome publication for retatrutide existed at the time these efficacy data were collected**, and the body of confirmatory evidence remains the Phase 3 **TRIUMPH** program (multiple parallel pivotal trials plus a cardiovascular outcomes trial). Lilly issued a **topline press-release announcement of TRIUMPH-1** (obesity without diabetes) reporting large weight reductions; however, **press-release topline data are NOT a Tier-1 admissible source**, have **not been peer-reviewed or published in full**, and are explicitly **excluded from this section's grounding numbers**. All efficacy magnitudes cited above are **Phase 1/Phase 2** and remain **investigational and unconfirmed** pending full peer-reviewed Phase 3 publication and regulatory review. `[type: regulatory]`
