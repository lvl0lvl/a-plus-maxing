## Section F — Practitioner-practice + non-English

> **Defining frame (read first):** Retatrutide (LY3437943) has **NO approved label and NO standard clinical dose anywhere in the world** as of June 2026. It is an investigational triple agonist (GIP/GLP-1/glucagon) owned by Eli Lilly, in Phase 3 (TRIUMPH program) but not yet cleared by FDA, EMA, PMDA, or NMPA. The **only** dosing reference that exists is the titration schedule used inside Lilly's own clinical trials — that is a trial protocol, **not** a prescribing label, and is reproduced below strictly as trial-derived context. There is **no FDA-approved product, therefore no legitimate compounding pathway** (compounders may not compound a drug that has never been approved; the §F.2 gray-market channel is not compounding in any regulated sense). Any clinical or self-administered use today is off-investigational and is especially premature relative to other peptides in this wiki, because there is **no approved comparator product at all**. Per section rules: practitioner and vendor sources below **never** ground efficacy or adverse-event-rate claims — those live only in the Tier-1 Phase-2 trial evidence in §B/C/D.

### Dosing reference (trial-derived, not a label)

The closest thing to a "dose" is the Phase 2 trial titration. In the Jastreboff et al. Phase 2 trial (NEJM 2023; registration **NCT04881760**), 338 adults with obesity were randomized to subcutaneous retatrutide **once weekly for 48 weeks**, using slow dose escalation to manage GI tolerability `[1][2]`:

- Dose arms: **1 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, and 12 mg** target maintenance doses `[1][2]`. *(regulatory / trial-derived)*
- Starting (initial) doses were **2 mg or 4 mg**, with escalation toward the assigned target — encoded in the trial arms as "4 mg [initial 2 mg]," "4 mg [initial 4 mg]," "8 mg [initial 2 mg]," "8 mg [initial 4 mg]," and "12 mg [initial 2 mg]" `[2]`. *(regulatory / trial-derived)*
- Escalation was gradual (stepwise over the early weeks of the 48-week course) specifically to mitigate dose-dependent nausea/vomiting `[1][2]`. *(regulatory / trial-derived)*
- Route/frequency: **subcutaneous, once weekly, same day each week** `[1][2]`. *(regulatory / trial-derived)*

The Phase 3 TRIUMPH program subsequently uses a refined titration that includes an intermediate **6 mg** step and treats 4 mg / (up to) 12 mg as maintenance targets depending on arm `[3]`. *(regulatory / trial-derived)* This is reported only to characterize the trial dose ceiling; it is **not** a recommended schedule for anyone outside a trial. There is no validated dose for any indication, no approved package insert, and no established dose-conversion or maintenance guidance for non-trial use. **The dose numbers above must not be read as a how-to.**

### Gray-market / access

Despite having no approved product, retatrutide is widely sold by research-chemical and "peptide" vendors, which is the channel through which essentially all current non-trial users obtain it. This is reported here as **practice context only** — vendor labels carry no efficacy or safety weight.

- Online retatrutide is **unregulated, unapproved, and sold "for laboratory research purposes only / not for human consumption,"** language used to exploit a legal gray area; true retatrutide remains investigational `[4]`. *(vendor_label / journalistic-context)*
- The sequence is public in Lilly's patent, so overseas chemists synthesize a molecule they claim is identical — but insiders note the developed drug "is more than just the molecule," and there is **no guarantee gray-market material is safe, pure, or correctly dosed** `[4]`. *(vendor_label / journalistic-context)*
- The trade is large and crypto-settled: peptide gray-market crypto flows reached a **$100M+ annual run rate by mid-2026** (up from ~$1M/quarter in 2024), with banks refusing to process non-FDA-approved compounds `[5]`. *(journalistic-context)*
- **Sterility is a documented failure mode, not a theoretical one:** at least one Chinese supplier's retatrutide batch shipped with a "glowing" mass/purity COA yet **failed independent sterility testing** — injecting non-sterile material carries severe, life-threatening risk `[5]`. *(vendor_label COA / journalistic-context)* A separately reported consumer sterility submission also failed `[4]`. This means a vendor COA showing high "purity" tells you nothing about sterility or endotoxin.
- A registered dietitian / diabetes specialist writing for clinicians (S. Hormachea, RD) states plainly that gray-market retatrutide has **"no standardized dosing schedule, no prescription oversight, and no medical supervision,"** that products "labeled 'for research purposes only' operate entirely outside regulatory oversight," and advises steering patients to approved therapies rather than experimental compounds `[6]`. *(practitioner_protocol — venue: Hormachea practitioner blog, Dec 2025; note: this is practitioner *opinion/advice*, not an efficacy/safety dataset)*

**Cost (qualitative, gray-market):** because supply is unregulated research-chem material sourced largely from China and priced per-milligram of powder, gray-market retatrutide is typically **far cheaper per milligram than any branded incretin therapy** — but the price reflects an unapproved, unverified, potentially non-sterile product with no clinical support, so low cost is a risk signal, not a value signal `[4][5]`. *(vendor_label / journalistic-context — qualitative only; no specific price quoted as none was verified from a primary vendor page opened here.)*

**Safety-monitoring caveats (reported as convention, not as endorsement of use):** the glucagon-receptor component drives a **dose-dependent resting heart-rate increase** (peaking around week 24 and declining thereafter in the trial) `[1][2]`, so heart-rate/blood-pressure monitoring is the most-cited cardiovascular caution. As with all high-efficacy incretin therapies, attention to **lean-mass / muscle preservation** (adequate protein and resistance training during rapid weight loss) is the standard convention discussed for this drug class. These are conventions of cautious practice; none substitutes for the absence of an approved product and supervised protocol.

### Non-English literature

Honest assessment: **there is no distinct non-English-language primary trial literature for retatrutide.** The pivotal evidence is Lilly-sponsored and **English-published** (NEJM, Lancet, NCT registrations), and the global Phase 3 TRIUMPH program — though it runs international sites and includes a dedicated **Phase 1 study in Japanese adults** to support PMDA submission — reports its results in English `[3]`. *(regulatory / trial-derived)*

What *does* exist in other languages is **secondary**:

- **Japanese:** clinician explainer articles and a yodosha/J-GLOBAL ecosystem entry exist, but the J-GLOBAL "Japanese" record I opened is explicitly a **JST/Kyoto-University machine translation of an English review** ("Retatrutide: a triple incretin receptor agonist for obesity management," *Expert Opinion on Investigational Drugs*, 2023) — i.e., a translated index of an English secondary source, not original Japanese scholarship `[7]`. *(secondary — machine-translated index)* Japanese clinic blogs (e.g., obesity-clinic explainers) discuss the same English trial data for a lay/clinician audience and add no independent primary data.
- **Chinese:** despite heavy Chinese involvement on the *supply* side (gray-market synthesis), targeted searches surfaced **no original Chinese-language primary trial or distinct review** on retatrutide as of this pass — only English reviews and the gray-market trade `[5]`. *(none distinct found)*
- **Other languages (Spanish/LATAM, Russian, etc.):** no distinct non-English primary literature surfaced; regional discussion tracks the English trial data and regulatory-timeline speculation.

**Conclusion: non-English literature = none distinct.** All substantive primary evidence is English-language and Lilly-sponsored; non-English material is translated or derivative secondary content and must not be treated as independent evidence.
