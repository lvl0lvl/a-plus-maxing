## Section D — Safety & adverse effects

**Scope and evidentiary status.** Retatrutide (LY3437943) is an **investigational** once-weekly triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist. As of this writing, its entire human safety record derives from **Phase-2 trials** [1][2][3] and supporting post-hoc analyses [4]; there is **no long-term safety dataset, no cardiovascular-outcomes trial, no approved label, and no human carcinogenicity/thyroid dataset.** Every adverse-event (AE) figure below is grounded in the registrational Phase-2 trials and must be read as short-duration (24–48 week) data in modest sample sizes. The added GLUCAGON-receptor component distinguishes retatrutide's safety profile from the GLP-1 (semaglutide) and GIP/GLP-1 (tirzepatide) drugs, contributing the signature dose-dependent heart-rate effect and a retatrutide-specific cutaneous-sensitivity signal. `[regulatory]` `[rct]`

### Gastrointestinal adverse events (dominant, dose-dependent, titration-mitigated)

GI events were the most common AEs in every Phase-2 trial, were dose-related, were mostly mild-to-moderate, and were partially mitigated by a lower starting dose (2 mg vs 4 mg) [1]. In the Phase-2 obesity trial (Jastreboff 2023, N=338), event rates rose steeply with dose; figures below are exact from the trial's AE table, given as placebo → highest-dose (12 mg) values: `[rct]`

- **Nausea:** 11% (placebo) vs 14% (1 mg) up to **60% (8 mg, initial-dose 4 mg) and 45% (12 mg)** [1].
- **Vomiting:** 1% (placebo) vs up to **26% (8 mg ID-4 mg) and 19% (12 mg)** [1].
- **Diarrhea:** 11% (placebo) vs up to **20% (8 mg groups) and 15% (12 mg)** [1].
- **Constipation:** 3% (placebo) vs up to **16% (12 mg)** [1].
- **Decreased appetite:** 9% (placebo) vs up to **31% (8 mg) and 29% (12 mg)** [1] — an on-target effect rather than a toxicity.

Severe GI events were uncommon (0% placebo; up to 6% in the 12-mg group) [1]. The Phase-2 type-2-diabetes trial (Rosenstock 2023) reported the same GI-dominant, dose-related, titration-sensitive pattern, with events concentrated during rapid dose escalation [2] `[rct]`, and the MASLD Phase-2a trial (Sanyal 2024) likewise found transient, mostly mild-to-moderate GI events that were more frequent in the 8-mg and 12-mg groups [3] `[rct]`.

**Discontinuations.** AE-driven discontinuation in the obesity trial occurred in **0% (placebo) vs 7%–16% across retatrutide groups** (16% in the 12-mg group), most often for GI events [1]. `[rct]`

### Glucagon-component effects

**Heart-rate increase (the signature tri-agonist concern).** Resting/ambulatory heart rate rose in a **dose-dependent** manner with retatrutide, peaked at **week 24, then declined thereafter** despite continued dosing — consistent with physiological adaptation [1]. The primary trial reports the dose-dependent pattern and peak timing directly [1]; secondary clinical summaries of the trial place the mean peak increase at roughly **5–10 bpm**, with the largest mean rise (~6–7 bpm) in the 12-mg group at week 24 [5]. Reported cardiac arrhythmias (supraventricular arrhythmias / conduction events) were captured as an AE of special interest in **6% overall** (up to 11–14% in the 8/12-mg groups vs 3% placebo), and were mild-to-moderate except for one severe prolonged-QT event in a participant who was also receiving ondansetron [1]. `[rct]` Because heart-rate elevation is plausibly tied to glucagon-receptor agonism and is the most consistent cardiovascular signal, it is the key cardiac caution for an investigational agent without CV-outcome data. `[mechanism_review]`

**Glucagon–glucose effect / transient glucose excursions.** Glucagon-receptor agonism is intrinsically glucose-raising, creating a **theoretical** early-hyperglycemia concern. In practice, the Phase-2 trials did **not** show net glycemic harm: in the T2D trial, HbA1c **fell** by up to ~2.2 percentage points at the 12-mg dose and fasting glucose declined, because concurrent GLP-1R/GIPR agonism offsets glucagon-driven glucose elevation [2][6]. **No clinically significant (level-2/level-3) hypoglycemia** was reported in the obesity trial [1], and no severe hypoglycemia occurred in T2D participants who were not on a sulfonylurea or insulin [2]. The transient glucagon-glucose effect is therefore best framed as a **monitoring point during titration / in insulin-treated or sulfonylurea-treated patients**, not as a demonstrated net-hyperglycemia harm. `[rct]` `[mechanism_review]`

**Lipid / hepatic.** Transient ALT elevations to >3× the upper limit of normal occurred in **1%** of retatrutide recipients, and mean ALT/AST were unchanged or decreased by week 48; **no hepatotoxicity signal** was identified in the obesity trial or in the MASLD substudy through 48 weeks [1][3]. Amylase/lipase increases were generally asymptomatic; one serious acute-pancreatitis AE occurred (12-mg group) [1]. Biliary events (cholecystitis/cholelithiasis) occurred in a small number of participants (≤6% in the higher-dose groups) [1]. `[rct]`

### Phase-2 signals to report honestly

**Renal — transient, dose-dependent eGFR decrease that recovers.** A pooled post-hoc kidney analysis of the two Phase-2 trials (Heerspink 2025) found an **initial decrease in creatinine-based eGFR at week 12** in the retatrutide groups — statistically significant vs placebo for the 8-mg and 12-mg doses [4]. In the T2D trial this early dip returned to baseline by weeks 24–36; in the obesity trial it reversed to a **net dose-dependent eGFR increase at week 48** (+5.3 mL/min/1.73 m² at 8 mg [95% CI 1.9–8.7]; +8.5 at 12 mg [95% CI 4.9–12.1]), with the increase partially reversing after a 4-week washout [4]. Urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio fell (e.g., −31.5% vs placebo at 12 mg in obesity; −37.0% at 12 mg in T2D) [4]. The honest read: **an early transient eGFR decline (haemodynamic, hyperfiltration-correction pattern) followed by recovery/improvement**, observed only over ≤48 weeks. `[rct]`

**Cutaneous hyperesthesia / skin sensitivity (retatrutide-specific signal).** Cutaneous hyperesthesia and skin-sensitivity events were reported in **7% of retatrutide recipients vs 1% of placebo** in the obesity trial, and were **dose-dependent** (the "hyperesthesia or related AE" category ran from ~1% at 1 mg up to **14% at 8 mg and 13% at 12 mg**, vs 1% placebo) [1]. **None were severe or serious, none were associated with overt skin findings, and none led to discontinuation** [1]. This signal is notably absent from the pure GLP-1 / GIP–GLP-1 agonists, making it a distinctive retatrutide (plausibly glucagon-linked) Phase-2 finding rather than a class effect. `[rct]`

**Other AEs of special interest.** Hypersensitivity (9% overall) and injection-site reactions (2% overall; up to 8% at 12 mg) were captured; anti-drug antibodies appeared in 9% overall with no clear efficacy/safety correlate [1]. One death occurred (drowning), adjudicated as undetermined and assessed by the investigator as unrelated to retatrutide [1]. `[rct]`

### What the Phase-2 data does NOT establish (limits and class-extrapolated risks)

- **No long-term safety data** — maximum exposure is 48 weeks (obesity/MASLD) or 36 weeks (T2D). `[rct]`
- **No cardiovascular-outcomes data** — the dose-dependent heart-rate increase has not been linked to hard CV endpoints; whether it is benign over years is unknown. `[rct]`
- **No human thyroid/C-cell data.** No medullary thyroid carcinoma or C-cell hyperplasia was reported in Phase-2 [1], but the GLP-1-class rodent C-cell tumor concern is a **theoretical class extrapolation** carried over from approved GLP-1 agonists; it is **not** demonstrated for retatrutide in humans and should be flagged as extrapolated, not measured. `[mechanism_review]` `[regulatory]`

### Contraindications, precautions, monitoring, and stopping (precautionary — investigational)

Because retatrutide is unapproved, there is **no formal contraindication list**; the following are precautionary and class-/mechanism-derived: `[regulatory]` `[mechanism_review]`

- **Precautions:** pregnancy/breastfeeding (no human reproductive-safety data; weight-loss agents are generally avoided in pregnancy); pre-existing tachyarrhythmia or conditions where a sustained heart-rate rise is hazardous (given the dose-dependent HR signal); history of pancreatitis or symptomatic gallbladder disease; concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea (hypoglycemia risk on titration); class-precautionary caution in personal/family history of MTC or MEN-2 (extrapolated from GLP-1-class labeling).
- **Monitoring:** heart rate (the signature glucagon-component effect); glucose (especially early and in those on insulin/secretagogues); renal function/eGFR (transient early dip); body weight and nutritional adequacy during rapid loss; symptoms of pancreatitis/biliary disease; injection-site and cutaneous-sensitivity symptoms.
- **Stopping:** intolerable GI AEs (the leading cause of discontinuation), suspected pancreatitis, marked or symptomatic heart-rate elevation/arrhythmia, or pregnancy.

### Bottom line

Retatrutide's Phase-2 safety profile is dominated by **dose-dependent, titration-mitigated GI events**, with a **distinct glucagon-attributable heart-rate increase peaking at week 24**, a **transient early eGFR decrease that recovers (net improvement by 48 weeks)**, and a **retatrutide-specific dose-dependent cutaneous-hyperesthesia signal (7% vs 1% placebo)**. There is **no net hyperglycemia** despite the glucagon component (HbA1c fell), **no clinically significant hypoglycemia**, and **no hepatotoxicity signal** in Phase 2. All of this is short-term data only — **no long-term, cardiovascular-outcome, or human thyroid safety information exists**, and the C-cell concern is class-extrapolated. `[rct]`
