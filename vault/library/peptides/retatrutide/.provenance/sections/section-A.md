## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### Identity and regulatory status

Retatrutide (development code **LY3437943**) is a single synthetic, acylated peptide engineered by Eli Lilly to act as a **triple agonist at three incretin/glucagon-family receptors simultaneously: the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR), the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R), and the glucagon receptor (GCGR)** [1][3]. It is designed for once-weekly subcutaneous (SC) administration [1][2]. As of this writing it is **INVESTIGATIONAL and not approved by any regulator** for any indication; the most advanced published evidence is phase 2 (obesity, type 2 diabetes, and metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease), with phase 3 programs ongoing [3][5] `[regulatory]` `[mechanism_review]`.

The single most important defining fact: retatrutide adds **glucagon-receptor (GCGR) agonism** to the GIP + GLP-1 activity that other incretin agents already possess. This is the differentiator versus **tirzepatide**, a *dual* GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, and versus **semaglutide**, a *GLP-1-only* agonist — neither of which engages the glucagon receptor [4] `[mechanism_review]`. The added glucagon arm is the pharmacological rationale for retatrutide's distinct emphasis on energy expenditure and hepatic fat handling (detailed below) [1][5] `[mechanism_review]`.

### Molecular structure

Retatrutide is a **synthetic peptide built on an incretin/glucagon-family backbone (derived from GIP and glucagon sequence elements) rather than from GLP-1** — a different design lineage from GLP-1-backbone agents such as semaglutide [4] `[mechanism_review]`. The molecule carries **non-coded (non-natural) amino-acid substitutions** that stabilize the N-terminus and tune receptor balance. Cryo-electron-microscopy structural work on retatrutide bound to each of its three receptors reports the use of **α-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib) at position 2 and α-methyl-L-leucine (αMeL) at position 13** within the peptide backbone [4] `[mechanism_review]`. The Aib substitution at the N-terminal region is the canonical modification used across this drug class to **resist dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) cleavage**, protecting the bioactive N-terminus from rapid proteolysis [4] `[mechanism_review]`.

Half-life extension is achieved by **acylation**: a long-chain **fatty-diacid (lipid) moiety is attached, via a linker, to a lysine residue on the peptide**, which lets the molecule bind reversibly to serum albumin [4] `[mechanism_review]`. Albumin binding slows renal clearance and proteolysis, producing the long plasma residence that supports once-weekly dosing [2][4] `[mechanism_review]`. The structural analysis notes that the position of the acylated lysine in retatrutide differs from that used in semaglutide and tirzepatide, consistent with retatrutide's distinct backbone-engineering strategy [4] `[mechanism_review]`.

### Receptor pharmacology — a deliberately balanced tri-agonist

In vitro, retatrutide does not simply maximize potency at all three receptors; it is tuned to a particular balance. Relative to the native endogenous hormones, retatrutide is reported to be **more potent at GIPR (by a factor of ~8.9) and less potent than the native ligands at GCGR (~0.3×) and at GLP-1R (~0.4×)** [4] `[mechanism_review]`. The discovery paper characterizes the in-vitro profile as showing **balanced GCGR and GLP-1R activity with comparatively greater GIPR activity** [1] `[mechanism_review]`. This "balanced triagonist" tuning is the central design idea: each receptor arm contributes a complementary metabolic effect, and the relative weighting is chosen so that glucagon agonism augments — rather than destabilizes — glucose control.

Structurally, the balanced agonism is explained by retatrutide **maintaining conserved interactions shared across GLP-1R, GIPR and GCGR (e.g., conserved salt bridges and aromatic-stacking contacts in the receptor binding pocket) while accommodating receptor-specific residues through variable contacts, predominantly in the upper half of the transmembrane-domain pocket and in extracellular loop 1 (ECL1)** [4] `[mechanism_review]`. In other words, a single peptide can productively dock into and activate three related class-B G-protein-coupled receptors by combining a conserved binding mode with receptor-selective accommodation.

### Mechanism of action — what each receptor arm does

The therapeutic effect is the integration of three signaling arms `[mechanism_review]`:

1. **GLP-1R agonism** — enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppresses inappropriate glucagon secretion, slows gastric emptying, and acts centrally to **reduce appetite/food intake and promote satiety** [1][3].
2. **GIPR agonism** — also augments glucose-dependent insulin secretion and contributes (in this molecule's design) to the **reduction of caloric intake**; in animal work the GIP and GLP-1 arms together drive the food-intake-lowering component of weight loss [1].
3. **GCGR (glucagon-receptor) agonism** — the differentiating arm. Glucagon-receptor activation **increases energy expenditure** and promotes **hepatic lipid mobilization/fat oxidation**, adding a "calorie-out" and liver-fat-clearing mechanism on top of the "calorie-in" suppression from the GLP-1/GIP arms [1][5].

The discovery study made this division explicit in obese mice: **"Body weight loss was augmented by the addition of GCGR-mediated increases in energy expenditure to GIPR- and GLP-1R-driven calorie intake reduction"** [1] `[animal]` (mouse; diet-induced-obese models). Thus the mechanistic rationale for triple agonism is that combining **reduced energy intake (GLP-1 + GIP) with increased energy expenditure (glucagon)** can produce weight and metabolic effects beyond what dual GIP/GLP-1 agonism achieves [1] `[mechanism_review]`.

A theoretical hazard of adding glucagon agonism is that glucagon classically *raises* blood glucose (it is the counter-regulatory hormone to insulin). The balanced design — relatively strong GIP/GLP-1 insulinotropic activity offsetting the comparatively weaker glucagon arm — is what allows net **glycemic improvement** rather than glycemic worsening; in the phase 1b type 2 diabetes trial, placebo-adjusted HbA1c fell significantly at the higher dose groups, indicating the insulin-promoting arms dominate glycemic control in practice [2] `[rct]` (phase 1b, multiple-ascending-dose, T2D).

### The hepatic-fat rationale (glucagon arm in the liver)

The glucagon-receptor arm is also the basis for retatrutide's pronounced effect on liver fat. The liver is rich in glucagon receptors, and GCGR agonism promotes **hepatic fatty-acid oxidation and reduced hepatic lipid storage** [5] `[mechanism_review]`. In the phase 2a trial in metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), 24 weeks of retatrutide produced large relative reductions in liver fat content at the higher doses, with a high proportion of participants achieving resolution of steatosis [5] `[rct]` (phase 2a, randomized, placebo-controlled). This combination of systemic weight loss (GLP-1/GIP) plus a direct hepatic fat-oxidation signal (glucagon) is the mechanistic argument for why the triple agonist is being pursued specifically in steatotic liver disease, beyond what GLP-1-class drugs alone offer [5] `[mechanism_review]`.

### Pharmacokinetics and dosing

Retatrutide's albumin-binding acylation produces a long half-life. In the phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose study in people with type 2 diabetes, the **pharmacokinetics were dose-proportional and the half-life supported once-weekly subcutaneous dosing** [2] `[rct]` (phase 1b, T2D). Consistent with this, in early human single-dose work referenced by the discovery paper, a **reduction in body weight persisted up to day 43 after a single dose**, illustrating the prolonged exposure that underpins the weekly schedule [1] `[mechanism_review]`. The class-typical adverse-event signal in these trials was **dose-related, mostly mild-to-moderate, transient gastrointestinal events (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), predominantly during dose escalation** [2][3] `[rct]` — a tolerability profile broadly comparable to other GLP-1/GIP-based therapies [3] `[mechanism_review]`.

### Summary of the contrast (enumerated)

1. **Semaglutide** — single-receptor: **GLP-1R only** (GLP-1 backbone) [4].
2. **Tirzepatide** — dual-receptor: **GIPR + GLP-1R** (no glucagon activity) [4].
3. **Retatrutide (LY3437943)** — triple-receptor: **GIPR + GLP-1R + GCGR**; the glucagon arm adds increased energy expenditure and hepatic fat oxidation, and is the defining mechanistic distinction of this class [1][3][4][5].
