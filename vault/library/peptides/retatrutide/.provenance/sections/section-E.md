## Section E — Regulatory & sport status

### Approval status: NOT APPROVED anywhere — investigational only

Retatrutide (development code **LY3437943**; Eli Lilly and Company) is an **investigational** once-weekly triple hormone-receptor agonist (GLP-1 / GIP / glucagon) and is **not approved by the FDA, the EMA, or any regulatory agency** in any jurisdiction. There is **no marketed product** anywhere in the world. `[regulatory]` `[1]` Eli Lilly states plainly on its own corporate site: "Retatrutide is an investigational once-weekly triple hormone receptor agonist," "It has not been approved by any regulatory agency," and "Retatrutide is an investigational molecule available only to participants in Lilly's clinical trials." `[regulatory]` `[1]`

The compound is in **Phase 3** development. Lilly's pivotal program is **TRIUMPH** (plus the **TRANSCEND-T2D** type-2-diabetes program). As an example of an active Phase 3 record, the clinicaltrials.gov entry **NCT05882045** (TRIUMPH-3, retatrutide in participants with obesity and cardiovascular disease) confirms: Phase 3; lead sponsor "Eli Lilly and Company"; intervention "retatrutide" (LY3437943) vs. placebo; status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING; started 30 May 2023, estimated completion May 2026. `[regulatory]` `[2]` Per Lilly's own statement, retatrutide is "not yet available for public use" and "is legally available only through Lilly's clinical trials." `[regulatory]` `[1]` No Biologics License or New Drug Application approval exists as of this writing (June 2026).

**Bottom line on approval (enumerated):**
1. FDA — **not approved** (no NDA/BLA approval). `[regulatory]` `[1]`
2. EMA — **not approved** (no marketing authorisation). `[regulatory]` `[1]`
3. Any other agency — **not approved**; "not approved by any regulatory agency." `[regulatory]` `[1]`
4. Marketed product — **none**; available only inside Lilly clinical trials. `[regulatory]` `[1]`

### FDA designations: Fast Track NOT verified

The prompt flagged a possible **Fast Track** designation for retatrutide. **This could not be verified against any primary source.** Eli Lilly's own retatrutide page does **not** mention Fast Track or any FDA designation. `[regulatory]` `[1]` The only Fast Track designation confirmable in this family is for **tirzepatide** (a different Lilly molecule, the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist marketed as Mounjaro/Zepbound), which the FDA granted Fast Track for obesity/overweight in October 2022 per Lilly's own press release. `[regulatory]` `[3]` **No public, primary-source confirmation of a Fast Track or Breakthrough Therapy designation for retatrutide was found.** This claim should be treated as **unverified / not established**, not asserted as fact. `[regulatory]` `[1]` `[3]`

### Gray-market "research-chemical" retatrutide — premature and high-risk

Despite having **no approved product and only Phase-2/early Phase-3 efficacy and safety data**, retatrutide is sold on the gray market by peptide vendors and "research chemical" sites, frequently mislabeled "for research purposes only" or "not for human consumption" while in fact being sold to consumers for self-injection with dosing instructions. The FDA has addressed this directly. On its drug-alert page **"FDA's Concerns with Unapproved GLP-1 Drugs Used for Weight Loss"** (content current as of 15 June 2026), the agency states: "FDA has warned companies that have illegally sold unapproved drugs containing semaglutide, tirzepatide or retatrutide that are falsely labeled 'for research purposes' or 'not for human consumption.' These products have been sold directly to consumers for human use with dosing instructions." `[regulatory]` `[4]`

Critically for any reader considering gray-market product, the FDA states that retatrutide is **not a lawful supplement, not a compoundable drug component, and not an approved-drug ingredient**: "Retatrutide and cagrilintide cannot be used in compounding under federal law. Additionally, these are not components of FDA-approved drugs and have not been found safe and effective for any condition." `[regulatory]` `[4]` The agency has issued enforcement actions against (enumerated): (1) telehealth companies marketing unapproved retatrutide, including direct-to-consumer marketing; (2) active-pharmaceutical-ingredient distributors selling retatrutide to compounders; and (3) outsourcing facilities repackaging retatrutide. `[regulatory]` `[4]` The FDA has backed this with warning letters to specific vendors marketing peptides (including retatrutide) as research chemicals — e.g., warning letters to peptide/GLP-1 sellers issued across 2024–2026. `[regulatory]` `[5]`

The FDA's general stance is that **unapproved versions "do not undergo FDA's review for safety, effectiveness and quality before they are marketed,"** and it urges consumers "to be vigilant when purchasing drugs online and only purchase from state-licensed pharmacies." `[regulatory]` `[4]` Layered on top of the absence of FDA review is the specific problem that **retatrutide has no pharmacopeial (USP/EP) monograph or reference standard** — its identity, purity, and potency cannot be independently verified by the buyer, and its **long-term safety in humans is unknown** because the Phase 3 outcome trials are still ongoing (e.g., NCT05882045 not yet complete). `[regulatory]` `[1]` `[2]` `[4]` Using gray-market retatrutide is therefore **especially premature**: it combines an unapproved molecule, no quality control, unknown long-term safety, and no lawful supplement/compounding pathway.

*(Mechanistic/clinical efficacy and adverse-event data belong to Sections B–D and are not duplicated here.)* `[regulatory]`

### WADA / sport status: NOT prohibited (monitored only via class markers)

Retatrutide is **NOT on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List.** This was verified directly against the official WADA documents. In the **2026 Prohibited List** (International Standard, in force 1 January 2026), the relevant section **S4.4 "Metabolic Modulators"** enumerates only: S4.4.1 (AMPK / PPARδ-type agonists), **S4.4.2 Insulins and insulin-mimetics**, S4.4.3 Meldonium, and S4.4.4 Trimetazidine. **GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon-receptor agonists are not listed**, and retatrutide is **not named anywhere** in the document (including S0 Non-Approved Substances, S2 Peptide Hormones, and S4). `[regulatory]` `[6]`

However, the broader incretin class is **monitored**. The **WADA 2026 Monitoring Program** lists, under item 6, **"Markers of Semaglutide and Tirzepatide"** — flagged **"In and Out-of-Competition."** `[regulatory]` `[7]` Two points of accuracy: (a) the Monitoring Program is **explicitly not the Prohibited List** — monitored substances are tracked to detect patterns of misuse and are **not prohibited**; and (b) the monitored entry names **semaglutide and tirzepatide markers specifically**, not retatrutide. So as of the 2026 list, retatrutide is **neither prohibited nor individually named in the Monitoring Program**; the closest secondary signal is that its drug class (GLP-1-based metabolic agents) is on WADA's monitoring radar via the semaglutide/tirzepatide markers. `[regulatory]` `[6]` `[7]`

**Sport-status summary (enumerated):**
1. Prohibited List 2026 — **NOT prohibited** (retatrutide and GLP-1/GIP/glucagon agonists absent from S0/S2/S4). `[regulatory]` `[6]`
2. Monitoring Program 2026 — class signal only; **markers of semaglutide and tirzepatide** monitored In/Out-of-Competition; **retatrutide not individually named.** `[regulatory]` `[7]`
