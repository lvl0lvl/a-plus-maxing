- **manufacturer / development stage:** **Eli Lilly and Company.** Administered by **once-weekly subcutaneous injection**. **INVESTIGATIONAL — not approved by the FDA, EMA, or any regulator for any indication anywhere in the world**; the most advanced published evidence is **Phase 2**, with the Phase 3 **TRIUMPH** program ongoing [3][13].
- **evidence_tier: B** — the efficacy/safety base is a **strong, well-conducted Phase-2 RCT program** (Jastreboff 2023 NEJM, obesity; Rosenstock 2023 Lancet, type 2 diabetes; Sanyal 2024 Nat Med, MASLD), which is better than the unapproved no-RCT peptides elsewhere in this wiki — **BUT** there is **no regulatory approval, no peer-reviewed Phase-3 outcome publication** (the TRIUMPH program is ongoing, with only sponsor topline press releases), and the evidence is **100% single-sponsor** (Eli Lilly), with **zero independent replication** [3][6][5][7][8][10][11]. ## TL;DR

Retatrutide is the first mid-stage member of the **triple-agonist** class — one synthetic acylated peptide that activates the GIP, GLP-1, **and glucagon** receptors, the last of which is precisely the feature that separates it from dual (tirzepatide) and GLP-1-only (semaglutide) agents [1][4]. Its weight-loss signal is the **largest yet documented for any incretin agent in a trial** — a least-squares-mean **−24.2% at 48 weeks (12 mg)** in adults with obesity and without diabetes — but that figure is **Phase 2 (mid-stage, dose-finding) and the molecule is INVESTIGATIONAL and NOT APPROVED anywhere** [3]. Every efficacy magnitude in this entry is **Phase 1/Phase 2** and must be read as **promising but unconfirmed**; the confirmatory Phase 3 **TRIUMPH** program is **ongoing**, and the only Phase 3 readouts are **sponsor topline press releases** that are not peer-reviewed and are explicitly excluded from this report's verified numbers [10][11]. The glucagon arm is the mechanistic differentiator — increased energy expenditure and hepatic fat oxidation, the basis for a Phase-2a MASLD substudy in which liver fat fell **−86% at 48 weeks on imaging** with steatosis resolved in 86% at 12 mg [1][5] — but it also produces a **dose-dependent heart-rate increase** that peaks at week 24, the signature cardiac caution for a drug with no CV-outcome data [3]. Other Phase-2 signals to read honestly: a **retatrutide-specific dose-dependent cutaneous hyperesthesia** (~7% vs 1% placebo), and a **transient early eGFR decrease that recovers** (net improvement by 48 weeks) [3][12]. The dominant adverse effects are **dose-related, titration-mitigated gastrointestinal events**, and despite the glucagon arm there was **no net hyperglycemia** (HbA1c fell up to ~2 points in T2D) and **no clinically significant hypoglycemia** [3][6]. The whole evidence base is **single-sponsor (Eli Lilly), pre-publication on Phase 3, and investigational** — no independent replication, no approved indication, no hard-outcome (CV/renal) or histologic-MASH benefit supportable today [5][7]. Regulatory: **not approved** by FDA/EMA/any agency; **gray-market use is especially premature** (no product, no compounding pathway, documented sterility failures) [13][15][20]; **WADA does not prohibit it** (the 2026 Monitoring Program names only semaglutide/tirzepatide markers) [17][18]. Net: a high-quality but **early-phase, single-sponsor, investigational** signal of unprecedented size — **evidence_tier B, risk_tier experimental**.

## 1. Identity, Structure & Mechanism

### 1.1 What retatrutide is

Retatrutide (development code LY3437943) is a single synthetic, acylated peptide engineered by Eli Lilly to act as a **triple agonist at three incretin/glucagon-family receptors simultaneously: the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR), the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R), and the glucagon receptor (GCGR)** [1][3]. It is designed for **once-weekly subcutaneous administration** [1][2]. As of this writing it is **INVESTIGATIONAL and not approved by any regulator** for any indication; the most advanced published evidence is **Phase 2** (obesity, type 2 diabetes, and MASLD), with Phase 3 programs ongoing [3][5].

The single most important defining fact: retatrutide adds **glucagon-receptor (GCGR) agonism** to the GIP + GLP-1 activity that other incretin agents already possess. This is the differentiator versus **tirzepatide**, a *dual* GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, and versus **semaglutide**, a *GLP-1-only* agonist — neither of which engages the glucagon receptor [4]. The added glucagon arm is the pharmacological rationale for retatrutide's distinct emphasis on energy expenditure and hepatic fat handling (detailed below) [1][5].

### 1.2 Molecular structure

Retatrutide is a **synthetic peptide built on an incretin/glucagon-family backbone (derived from GIP and glucagon sequence elements) rather than from GLP-1** — a different design lineage from GLP-1-backbone agents such as semaglutide [4]. The molecule carries **non-coded (non-natural) amino-acid substitutions** that stabilize the N-terminus and tune receptor balance. Cryo-electron-microscopy structural work on retatrutide bound to each of its three receptors reports the use of **α-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib) at position 2 and α-methyl-L-leucine (αMeL) at position 13** within the peptide backbone [4]. The Aib substitution at the N-terminal region is the canonical modification used across this drug class to **resist dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) cleavage**, protecting the bioactive N-terminus from rapid proteolysis [4].

Half-life extension is achieved by **acylation**: a long-chain **fatty-diacid (lipid) moiety is attached, via a linker, to a lysine residue on the peptide**, which lets the molecule bind reversibly to serum albumin [4]. Albumin binding slows renal clearance and proteolysis, producing the long plasma residence that supports once-weekly dosing [2][4]. The structural analysis notes that the position of the acylated lysine in retatrutide **differs from that used in semaglutide and tirzepatide**, consistent with retatrutide's distinct backbone-engineering strategy [4].

### 1.3 Receptor pharmacology — a deliberately balanced tri-agonist

In vitro, retatrutide does not simply maximize potency at all three receptors; it is tuned to a particular balance. Relative to the native endogenous hormones, retatrutide is reported to be **more potent at GIPR (by a factor of ~8.9) and less potent than the native ligands at GCGR (~0.3×) and at GLP-1R (~0.4×)** [4]. The discovery paper characterizes the in-vitro profile as showing **balanced GCGR and GLP-1R activity with comparatively greater GIPR activity** [1]. This "balanced triagonist" tuning is the central design idea: each receptor arm contributes a complementary metabolic effect, and the relative weighting is chosen so that glucagon agonism augments — rather than destabilizes — glucose control.

Structurally, the balanced agonism is explained by retatrutide **maintaining conserved interactions shared across GLP-1R, GIPR and GCGR (e.g., conserved salt bridges and aromatic-stacking contacts in the receptor binding pocket) while accommodating receptor-specific residues through variable contacts, predominantly in the upper half of the transmembrane-domain pocket and in extracellular loop 1 (ECL1)** [4]. In other words, a single peptide can productively dock into and activate three related class-B G-protein-coupled receptors by combining a conserved binding mode with receptor-selective accommodation.

### 1.4 Mechanism of action — what each receptor arm does

The therapeutic effect is the integration of three signaling arms:

1. **GLP-1R agonism** — enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppresses inappropriate glucagon secretion, slows gastric emptying, and acts centrally to **reduce appetite/food intake and promote satiety** [1][3].
2. **GIPR agonism** — also augments glucose-dependent insulin secretion and contributes (in this molecule's design) to the **reduction of caloric intake**; in animal work the GIP and GLP-1 arms together drive the food-intake-lowering component of weight loss [1].
3. **GCGR (glucagon-receptor) agonism** — the differentiating arm. Glucagon-receptor activation **increases energy expenditure** and promotes **hepatic lipid mobilization/fat oxidation**, adding a "calorie-out" and liver-fat-clearing mechanism on top of the "calorie-in" suppression from the GLP-1/GIP arms [1][5].

The discovery study made this division explicit in obese mice: **"Body weight loss was augmented by the addition of GCGR-mediated increases in energy expenditure to GIPR- and GLP-1R-driven calorie intake reduction"** [1] (mouse; diet-induced-obese models). Thus the mechanistic rationale for triple agonism is that combining **reduced energy intake (GLP-1 + GIP) with increased energy expenditure (glucagon)** can produce weight and metabolic effects beyond what dual GIP/GLP-1 agonism achieves [1].

A theoretical hazard of adding glucagon agonism is that glucagon classically *raises* blood glucose (it is the counter-regulatory hormone to insulin). The balanced design — relatively strong GIP/GLP-1 insulinotropic activity offsetting the comparatively weaker glucagon arm — is what allows net **glycemic improvement** rather than glycemic worsening; in the Phase 1b type 2 diabetes trial, placebo-adjusted HbA1c fell significantly at the higher dose groups, indicating the insulin-promoting arms dominate glycemic control in practice [2].

### 1.5 The hepatic-fat rationale (glucagon arm in the liver)

The glucagon-receptor arm is also the basis for retatrutide's pronounced effect on liver fat. The liver is rich in glucagon receptors, and GCGR agonism promotes **hepatic fatty-acid oxidation and reduced hepatic lipid storage** [5]. In the Phase 2a trial in metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), retatrutide produced large relative reductions in liver fat content at the higher doses, with a high proportion of participants achieving resolution of steatosis (detailed in §3) [5]. This combination of systemic weight loss (GLP-1/GIP) plus a direct hepatic fat-oxidation signal (glucagon) is the mechanistic argument for why the triple agonist is being pursued specifically in steatotic liver disease, beyond what GLP-1-class drugs alone offer [5].

### 1.6 Pharmacokinetics and dosing

Retatrutide's albumin-binding acylation produces a long half-life. In the Phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose study in people with type 2 diabetes, the **pharmacokinetics were dose-proportional and the half-life supported once-weekly subcutaneous dosing**, with a reported **terminal half-life of ~6 days** [2]. Consistent with this, in early human single-dose work referenced by the discovery paper, a **reduction in body weight persisted up to day 43 after a single dose**, illustrating the prolonged exposure that underpins the weekly schedule [1]. The class-typical adverse-event signal in these trials was **dose-related, mostly mild-to-moderate, transient gastrointestinal events (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), predominantly during dose escalation** [2][3] — a tolerability profile broadly comparable to other GLP-1/GIP-based therapies [3].

### 1.7 The mechanistic contrast (enumerated)

1. **Semaglutide** — single-receptor: **GLP-1R only** (GLP-1 backbone) [4].
2. **Tirzepatide** — dual-receptor: **GIPR + GLP-1R** (no glucagon activity) [4].
3. **Retatrutide (LY3437943)** — triple-receptor: **GIPR + GLP-1R + GCGR**; the glucagon arm adds increased energy expenditure and hepatic fat oxidation, and is the defining mechanistic distinction of this class [1][3][4][5].

### 1.8 Coverage gaps (Section 1)
- The energy-expenditure / hepatic-fat-oxidation attribution to the glucagon arm rests on **mechanism review plus animal (mouse) data** [1][4][5]; the GCGR contribution is not directly isolated in a human dismantling study in the corpus.
- The single-dose "weight loss persisted to day 43" detail and the ~6-day half-life are from sponsor-run early human work [1][2]; no independent PK replication is carried.
- Receptor-potency ratios (~8.9× GIPR, ~0.3× GCGR, ~0.4× GLP-1R) are from a single cryo-EM/pharmacology study [4].

## 2. Efficacy (Phase 2 — investigational, not confirmed)

> **INVESTIGATIONAL-COMPOUND BANNER.** Every efficacy figure in this section comes from **Phase 1 or Phase 2 trials** — early- to mid-stage studies that establish proof-of-concept and inform dose selection but do **not** constitute confirmatory evidence. None of the magnitudes below has been confirmed in a peer-reviewed Phase 3 outcome publication. They should be read as **promising but unconfirmed** signals, and they cannot be assumed to predict Phase 3 results, long-term durability, or safety at the population scale (see the Phase 3 / TRIUMPH status in §3) [3][6][5].

### 2.1 Defining efficacy fact (Phase 2, not confirmed)

In its pivotal Phase 2 obesity trial, retatrutide produced a **least-squares mean weight reduction of about −24.2% at 48 weeks on the 12 mg dose** in adults with obesity and **no diabetes** — the **largest mean weight loss reported to date for any incretin-based pharmacotherapy in a clinical trial** [3]. This is the headline result, and it must be carried with two qualifiers attached: it is **Phase 2 (mid-stage, dose-finding)** and **investigational/not approved** [3].

### 2.2 Receptor pharmacology and proof of concept (Phase 1)

Retatrutide's mechanism distinguishes it from dual (GLP-1/GIP) agonists such as tirzepatide: it adds **glucagon-receptor agonism**, thought to increase energy expenditure, while the GLP-1 and GIP components reduce caloric intake. In the discovery-to-proof-of-concept report, the molecule was characterized as having **balanced GCGR and GLP-1R activity with greater GIPR activity**, and a single dose reduced body weight in healthy participants with the effect persisting **up to day 43**, supporting once-weekly dosing [1].

A **Phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose trial in people with type 2 diabetes** (N=72; doses 0.5, 1.5, 3 mg, escalated 3/6 mg, and escalated 3/6/9/12 mg, vs. placebo and dulaglutide 1.5 mg) reported **placebo-adjusted HbA1c reductions up to ~−1.6%** and **body-weight reduction up to −8.96 kg** in the highest-escalation arm, with a **terminal half-life of ~6 days** [2]. Gastrointestinal events were the most common adverse events. These are **Phase 1b, small-N, hypothesis-generating** findings [2].

### 2.3 Obesity — Phase 2 (the headline trial)

**Jastreboff AM, Kaplan LM, et al. *N Engl J Med* 2023 (PMID 37366315; NCT04881760).**

- **Design / population:** Phase 2, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. **N=338** adults (51.8% men) with **obesity (BMI ≥30), or overweight (BMI 27 to <30) plus ≥1 weight-related condition**, **without type 2 diabetes** as the studied population. Duration **48 weeks**; primary endpoint = percentage change in body weight at **24 weeks** [3].
- **Doses:** 1 mg, 4 mg (initial 2 or 4 mg), 8 mg (initial 2 or 4 mg), 12 mg (initial 2 mg), or placebo, once weekly [3].
- **Primary endpoint — LS-mean weight change at 24 weeks (Phase 2, not confirmed):** 1 mg **−7.2%**; 4 mg (combined) **−12.9%**; 8 mg (combined) **−17.3%**; 12 mg **−17.5%**; **placebo −1.6%** [3].
- **Weight change at 48 weeks (Phase 2, not confirmed):** 1 mg **−8.7%**; 4 mg (combined) **−17.1%**; 8 mg (combined) **−22.8%**; 12 mg **−24.2%**; **placebo −2.1%**. A clear **monotonic dose-response** is evident, and at 48 weeks the higher doses had **not reached a weight-loss plateau**, which the investigators flagged as notable [3].
- **Responder thresholds at 48 weeks (4 / 8 / 12 mg doses; Phase 2):** ≥5% body-weight loss **92% / 100% / 100%**; ≥10% **75% / 91% / 93%**; ≥15% **60% / 75% / 83%**. The trial reported that on 12 mg, roughly **a quarter of participants lost ≥30%** of body weight — an effect size in the range previously associated only with bariatric surgery, but here observed in a **single mid-stage trial** [3].
- **Tolerability:** Most common adverse events were **gastrointestinal (nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation)**, **dose-related**, mostly **mild-to-moderate**, and partially mitigated by a lower starting dose. Detailed safety is in §5 [3].

### 2.4 Type 2 diabetes — Phase 2

**Rosenstock J, Frias J, et al. *Lancet* 2023 (PMID 37385280; NCT04867785).**

- **Design / population:** Randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, **placebo- and active-comparator (dulaglutide 1.5 mg) controlled**, parallel-group Phase 2 trial conducted in the USA. **N=281** randomized (275 in efficacy analyses). Adults **18–75 y with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c 7.0–10.5%, BMI 25–50**, on diet/exercise or metformin ≥1000 mg/day. Mean age 56.2 y; mean diabetes duration 8.1 y; 56% female. Duration **36 weeks**; primary endpoint = HbA1c change at **24 weeks** [6].
- **Doses:** retatrutide 0.5 mg; 4 mg (with and without escalation); 8 mg (slow and fast escalation); 12 mg (escalation); plus dulaglutide 1.5 mg and placebo [6].
- **HbA1c change at 24 weeks — LS-mean (Phase 2, not confirmed):** placebo **−0.01%**; **dulaglutide 1.5 mg −1.41%**; retatrutide 0.5 mg **−0.43%**; 4 mg (escalation) **−1.39%**; 4 mg (no escalation) **−1.30%**; 8 mg (slow) **−1.99%**; 8 mg (fast) **−1.88%**; **12 mg (escalation) −2.02%**. The top retatrutide doses **numerically exceeded the active comparator dulaglutide** within this single trial [6].
- **Weight change at 36 weeks (% change; Phase 2):** placebo **−3.00%**; dulaglutide **−2.02%**; retatrutide 0.5 mg **−3.19%**; 4 mg (escalation) **−7.92%**; 4 mg (no escalation) **−10.37%**; 8 mg (slow) **−16.81%**; 8 mg (fast) **−16.34%**; **12 mg −16.94%** — i.e., **up to ~−17%** in a T2D population, where weight loss is typically blunted relative to non-diabetic populations [6].
- **Tolerability:** Mild-to-moderate GI adverse events in **35%** (67/190) of retatrutide participants vs **13%** placebo and **35%** dulaglutide; **no severe hypoglycemia and no deaths** during the study [6].

### 2.5 Cross-class comparison — stated carefully

The retatrutide Phase 2 obesity signal of **~−24% at 48 weeks (12 mg)** is **numerically larger** than the headline mean weight losses reported in the pivotal trials of approved incretin agents — semaglutide 2.4 mg (~−15% over 68 weeks, STEP-1) and tirzepatide 15 mg (~−21% over 72 weeks, SURMOUNT-1). **This comparison must be treated as cross-trial and indirect**, not a head-to-head: the trials differ in **duration (retatrutide 48 wk vs the comparators 68–72 wk), populations, escalation schemes, and design**, and **retatrutide remains Phase 2 while the comparators have completed Phase 3 and are approved** [3]. No randomized head-to-head trial of retatrutide against semaglutide or tirzepatide has been published. The accurate framing is: **in a mid-stage trial, retatrutide produced the largest mean weight loss yet seen for an incretin agent, a finding that is striking but unconfirmed** [3].

### 2.6 Enumerated efficacy trial set (5 trials)

1. **Coskun T, Urva S, et al.** *Cell Metab* 2022 — discovery + Phase 1 first-in-human proof of concept [1].
2. **Urva S, Coskun T, Loh MT, et al.** *Lancet* 2022 — Phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose in T2D [2].
3. **Jastreboff AM, Kaplan LM, et al.** *NEJM* 2023 — Phase 2 obesity (headline; −24.2% at 48 wk, 12 mg) [3].
4. **Rosenstock J, Frias J, et al.** *Lancet* 2023 — Phase 2 T2D (HbA1c −2.02%, weight −16.94% at top dose) [6].
5. **Sanyal AJ, et al.** *Nat Med* 2024 — Phase 2a MASLD liver-fat substudy (detailed in §3) [5].

### 2.7 Coverage gaps (Section 2)
- All efficacy magnitudes are **Phase 1/Phase 2**; none is confirmed by a peer-reviewed Phase 3 outcome publication.
- The cross-class comparison vs semaglutide/tirzepatide is **indirect/cross-trial**, not head-to-head; no randomized comparator trial exists.
- Every trial is Lilly-sponsored (see §4); no independent efficacy replication is carried.

## 3. Beyond-Weight Efficacy & Phase 3 Pipeline

> **DEFINING INTEGRITY FRAME.** Retatrutide is an **INVESTIGATIONAL** triple agonist with **NO marketing approval** from FDA, EMA, or any regulator as of June 2026. Nothing in this section describes an approved indication. Every population is annotated; every efficacy claim is tagged to the trial and population in which it was generated.

### 3.1 MASH / MASLD — the strongest beyond-weight efficacy signal

The principal beyond-weight evidence is the dedicated hepatic-fat substudy of the Phase 2 obesity program, reported by **Sanyal AJ, Kaplan LM, Frias JP, et al., *Nature Medicine* 2024;30(7):2037–2048 (PMID 38858523; PMCID PMC11271400; DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-03018-2)** [5].

- **Design and population (annotated):** randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled **Phase 2a** trial in **98 adults** with obesity/overweight and **MASLD/MASH defined by ≥10% liver fat**, *explicitly excluding type 2 diabetes*. Once-weekly subcutaneous retatrutide at **1, 4, 8, or 12 mg vs placebo** for **48 weeks** (primary analysis at 24 weeks). Liver fat measured by MRI-PDFF (the imaging gold standard) [5].
- **Primary endpoint (relative liver-fat change, MRI-PDFF, 24 weeks):** −42.9% (1 mg), −57.0% (4 mg), −81.4% (8 mg), −82.4% (12 mg) vs **+0.3% placebo** (P < 0.001 for all active doses vs placebo) [5].
- **At 48 weeks** relative reductions deepened to approximately −51.3%, −59.0%, −81.7%, and **−86.0%** for the 1/4/8/12 mg arms respectively [5].
- **Resolution of steatosis (<5% liver fat, 24 weeks):** 27% (1 mg), 52% (4 mg), 79% (8 mg), **86% (12 mg)** vs **0% placebo** [5].
- **Interpretation discipline:** this is an **MRI-PDFF imaging surrogate** in a **Phase 2a** trial of only 98 participants without diabetes. It is a steatosis (liver-fat) endpoint, **not** a histological MASH-resolution-with-fibrosis endpoint and **not** a hard outcome (cirrhosis, decompensation). The magnitude is large, but the evidence tier remains early-phase and investigational. A registrational MASH program would be required before any liver claim could be made [5].

### 3.2 The Phase 3 program — TRIUMPH (enumerated, with verified registrations)

The registrational program is described in the design/rationale paper **Giblin K, Kaplan LM, Somers VK, et al., *Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism* 2026 (PMID 41090431; DOI 10.1111/dom.70209)** — a **design/rationale paper that reports NO efficacy outcomes** [7]. Trial-level facts below are taken from ClinicalTrials.gov primary records [8][9]. Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and knee osteoarthritis are studied as **nested sub-protocols within the master/basket trials**, not as separate standalone obesity trials.

**Enumerated core TRIUMPH trials (n = 4 master/registrational + 1 outcomes trial; all sponsor = Eli Lilly and Company):**

1. **TRIUMPH-1 — NCT05929066.** Phase 3 master protocol, adults **without T2D** with obesity/overweight + ≥1 weight-related complication (with nested OSA/knee-OA sub-protocols); ~2,339 randomized to retatrutide 4 / 9 / 12 mg vs placebo, 80 weeks (104-week extension for BMI ≥35). **Status: Completed** (primary completion April 2026) [8].
2. **TRIUMPH-2 — NCT05929079.** Phase 3 master protocol in adults **with type 2 diabetes** and obesity/overweight (nested OSA sub-protocol); ~1,000 participants; retatrutide 4 / 9 / 12 mg vs placebo. **Status: Active, not recruiting** (primary completion ~May 2026) [8].
3. **TRIUMPH-3 — NCT05882045.** Phase 3, adults with **severe obesity and established cardiovascular disease**; ~1,946 participants; retatrutide vs placebo. **Status: Active, not recruiting** (primary completion April 2026) [8][13].
4. **TRIUMPH-4 — NCT05931367.** Phase 3, adults with obesity/overweight **and knee osteoarthritis, without diabetes**; 445 participants randomized 1:1:1 to retatrutide 9 mg, 12 mg, or placebo, 68 weeks. **Status: Completed** (primary completion Nov 2025) [8].
5. **TRIUMPH-Outcomes (cardiovascular/kidney outcomes trial) — NCT06383390.** Phase 3, event-driven CVOT in adults with **BMI ≥27 plus atherosclerotic CVD and/or chronic kidney disease**; estimated **~10,000** participants; primary completion estimated **February 2029**. **Status: Active, not recruiting.** This is the trial carrying the **renal (MAKE) and hard cardiovascular (MACE) outcome** endpoints [9].

> **NCT-resolution note (integrity).** Several secondary outlets mis-assigned NCTs across Lilly's metabolic portfolio. In particular **NCT05869903 is *ATTAIN-1*, a Phase 3 trial of orforglipron — a *different* Lilly drug — not a TRIUMPH/retatrutide trial** [8]; one TRIUMPH-4 press release reproduced that NCT in error. The registrations listed above were each opened on ClinicalTrials.gov and confirmed to name retatrutide/LY3437943.

### 3.3 Phase 3 efficacy status — what is and is NOT published (PRESS-RELEASE ONLY)

As of June 2026, **no TRIUMPH trial has a peer-reviewed primary-outcome publication.** Two readouts exist only as **sponsor topline/press-release announcements** (regulatory/corporate tier, *not* peer-reviewed, not yet presented in full at a scientific congress). Per Lilly, full data for both are slated for "a future medical meeting" and subsequent peer-reviewed publication:

## 5. Safety & Adverse Effects

> **Scope and evidentiary status.** Retatrutide's entire human safety record derives from **Phase-2 trials** [3][5][6] and a supporting post-hoc pooled analysis [12]; there is **no long-term safety dataset, no cardiovascular-outcomes trial, no approved label, and no human carcinogenicity/thyroid dataset.** Every adverse-event figure below is short-duration (24–48 week) data in modest sample sizes. The added GLUCAGON-receptor component distinguishes retatrutide's profile from the GLP-1 (semaglutide) and GIP/GLP-1 (tirzepatide) drugs, contributing the signature dose-dependent heart-rate effect and a retatrutide-specific cutaneous-sensitivity signal.

### 5.1 Gastrointestinal adverse events (dominant, dose-dependent, titration-mitigated)

GI events were the most common AEs in every Phase-2 trial, were dose-related, were mostly mild-to-moderate, and were partially mitigated by a lower starting dose (2 mg vs 4 mg) [3]. In the Phase-2 obesity trial (Jastreboff 2023, N=338), event rates rose steeply with dose; figures below are exact from the trial's AE table, given as placebo → highest-dose values:

- **Nausea:** 11% (placebo) vs 14% (1 mg) up to **60% (8 mg, initial-dose 4 mg) and 45% (12 mg)** [3].
- **Vomiting:** 1% (placebo) vs up to **26% (8 mg ID-4 mg) and 19% (12 mg)** [3].
- **Diarrhea:** 11% (placebo) vs up to **20% (8 mg groups) and 15% (12 mg)** [3].
- **Constipation:** 3% (placebo) vs up to **16% (12 mg)** [3].
- **Decreased appetite:** 9% (placebo) vs up to **31% (8 mg) and 29% (12 mg)** [3] — an on-target effect rather than a toxicity.

Severe GI events were uncommon (0% placebo; up to 6% in the 12-mg group) [3]. The Phase-2 T2D trial (Rosenstock 2023) reported the same GI-dominant, dose-related, titration-sensitive pattern, with events concentrated during rapid dose escalation [6], and the MASLD Phase-2a trial (Sanyal 2024) likewise found transient, mostly mild-to-moderate GI events that were more frequent in the 8-mg and 12-mg groups [5].

**Discontinuations.** AE-driven discontinuation in the obesity trial occurred in **0% (placebo) vs 7%–16% across retatrutide groups** (16% in the 12-mg group), most often for GI events [3].

### 5.2 Glucagon-component effects

**Heart-rate increase (the signature tri-agonist concern).** Resting/ambulatory heart rate rose in a **dose-dependent** manner with retatrutide, **peaked at week 24, then declined thereafter** despite continued dosing — consistent with physiological adaptation [3]. The primary trial reports the dose-dependent pattern and peak timing directly [3]. *(A specific mean ~bpm magnitude that circulated in a secondary vendor/anecdote summary has been DROPPED here as not Tier-1-admissible; only the dose-dependent rise and the week-24 peak — grounded in the Jastreboff 2023 trial — are carried.)* Reported cardiac arrhythmias (supraventricular arrhythmias / conduction events) were captured as an AE of special interest in **6% overall** (up to 11–14% in the 8/12-mg groups vs 3% placebo), and were mild-to-moderate except for one severe prolonged-QT event in a participant who was also receiving ondansetron [3]. Because heart-rate elevation is plausibly tied to glucagon-receptor agonism and is the most consistent cardiovascular signal, it is the key cardiac caution for an investigational agent without CV-outcome data [3].

**Glucagon–glucose effect / transient glucose excursions.** Glucagon-receptor agonism is intrinsically glucose-raising, creating a **theoretical** early-hyperglycemia concern. In practice, the Phase-2 trials did **not** show net glycemic harm: in the T2D trial, HbA1c **fell** by up to ~2 percentage points at the 12-mg dose and fasting glucose declined, because concurrent GLP-1R/GIPR agonism offsets glucagon-driven glucose elevation [6]. **No clinically significant (level-2/level-3) hypoglycemia** was reported in the obesity trial [3], and no severe hypoglycemia occurred in T2D participants who were not on a sulfonylurea or insulin [6]. The transient glucagon-glucose effect is therefore best framed as a **monitoring point during titration / in insulin-treated or sulfonylurea-treated patients**, not as a demonstrated net-hyperglycemia harm [3][6].

**Lipid / hepatic.** Transient ALT elevations to >3× the upper limit of normal occurred in **1%** of retatrutide recipients, and mean ALT/AST were unchanged or decreased by week 48; **no hepatotoxicity signal** was identified in the obesity trial or in the MASLD substudy through 48 weeks [3][5]. Amylase/lipase increases were generally asymptomatic; one serious acute-pancreatitis AE occurred (12-mg group) [3]. Biliary events (cholecystitis/cholelithiasis) occurred in a small number of participants (≤6% in the higher-dose groups) [3].

### 5.3 Phase-2 signals to report honestly

**Renal — transient, dose-dependent eGFR decrease that recovers.** A pooled post-hoc kidney analysis of the two Phase-2 trials (Heerspink 2025) found an **initial decrease in creatinine-based eGFR at week 12** in the retatrutide groups — statistically significant vs placebo for the 8-mg and 12-mg doses [12]. In the T2D trial this early dip returned to baseline by weeks 24–36; in the obesity trial it reversed to a **net dose-dependent eGFR increase at week 48** (+5.3 mL/min/1.73 m² at 8 mg [95% CI 1.9–8.7]; +8.5 at 12 mg [95% CI 4.9–12.1]), with the increase partially reversing after a 4-week washout [12]. Urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio fell (e.g., −31.5% vs placebo at 12 mg in obesity; −37.0% at 12 mg in T2D) [12]. The honest read: **an early transient eGFR decline (haemodynamic, hyperfiltration-correction pattern) followed by recovery/improvement**, observed only over ≤48 weeks [12].

**Cutaneous hyperesthesia / skin sensitivity (retatrutide-specific signal).** Cutaneous hyperesthesia and skin-sensitivity events were reported in **7% of retatrutide recipients vs 1% of placebo** in the obesity trial, and were **dose-dependent** (the "hyperesthesia or related AE" category ran from ~1% at 1 mg up to **14% at 8 mg and 13% at 12 mg**, vs 1% placebo) [3]. **None were severe or serious, none were associated with overt skin findings, and none led to discontinuation** [3]. This signal is notably absent from the pure GLP-1 / GIP–GLP-1 agonists, making it a distinctive retatrutide (plausibly glucagon-linked) Phase-2 finding rather than a class effect [3].

**Other AEs of special interest.** Hypersensitivity (9% overall) and injection-site reactions (2% overall; up to 8% at 12 mg) were captured; anti-drug antibodies appeared in 9% overall with no clear efficacy/safety correlate [3]. One death occurred (drowning), adjudicated as undetermined and assessed by the investigator as unrelated to retatrutide [3].

### 5.4 What the Phase-2 data does NOT establish (limits and class-extrapolated risks)

- **No long-term safety data** — maximum exposure is 48 weeks (obesity/MASLD) or 36 weeks (T2D) [3][5][6].
- **No cardiovascular-outcomes data** — the dose-dependent heart-rate increase has not been linked to hard CV endpoints; whether it is benign over years is unknown [3].
- **No human thyroid/C-cell data.** No medullary thyroid carcinoma or C-cell hyperplasia was reported in Phase-2 [3], but the GLP-1-class rodent C-cell tumor concern is a **theoretical class extrapolation** carried over from approved GLP-1 agonists; it is **not** demonstrated for retatrutide in humans and should be flagged as extrapolated, not measured.

### 5.5 Contraindications, precautions, monitoring, and stopping (precautionary — investigational)

Because retatrutide is unapproved, there is **no formal contraindication list**; the following are precautionary and class-/mechanism-derived:

- **Precautions:** pregnancy/breastfeeding (no human reproductive-safety data; weight-loss agents are generally avoided in pregnancy); pre-existing tachyarrhythmia or conditions where a sustained heart-rate rise is hazardous (given the dose-dependent HR signal); history of pancreatitis or symptomatic gallbladder disease; concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea (hypoglycemia risk on titration); class-precautionary caution in personal/family history of MTC or MEN-2 (extrapolated from GLP-1-class labeling) [3].
- **Monitoring:** heart rate (the signature glucagon-component effect); glucose (especially early and in those on insulin/secretagogues); renal function/eGFR (transient early dip); body weight and nutritional adequacy during rapid loss; symptoms of pancreatitis/biliary disease; injection-site and cutaneous-sensitivity symptoms [3][12].
- **Stopping:** intolerable GI AEs (the leading cause of discontinuation), suspected pancreatitis, marked or symptomatic heart-rate elevation/arrhythmia, or pregnancy [3].

### 5.6 Bottom line

Retatrutide's Phase-2 safety profile is dominated by **dose-dependent, titration-mitigated GI events**, with a **distinct glucagon-attributable heart-rate increase peaking at week 24**, a **transient early eGFR decrease that recovers (net improvement by 48 weeks)**, and a **retatrutide-specific dose-dependent cutaneous-hyperesthesia signal (7% vs 1% placebo)**. There is **no net hyperglycemia** despite the glucagon component (HbA1c fell), **no clinically significant hypoglycemia**, and **no hepatotoxicity signal** in Phase 2. All of this is short-term data only — **no long-term, cardiovascular-outcome, or human thyroid safety information exists**, and the C-cell concern is class-extrapolated [3][5][6][12].

- **No long-term, CV-outcome, or human thyroid/C-cell safety data** exist; the C-cell concern is rodent-class-extrapolated, not measured.
- Prior-pancreatitis patients' risk cannot be characterized from the Phase-2 set; one serious pancreatitis AE was observed.

- A specific mean heart-rate-increase magnitude (~bpm) was **dropped** as non-Tier-1; only the dose-dependent pattern/week-24 peak is carried [3].

### 8.1 Dosing reference (trial-derived, NOT a label)

The closest thing to a "dose" is the Phase 2 trial titration. In the Jastreboff Phase 2 trial (NEJM 2023; NCT04881760), 338 adults with obesity were randomized to subcutaneous retatrutide **once weekly for 48 weeks**, using slow dose escalation to manage GI tolerability [3]:

- Dose arms: **1 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, and 12 mg** target maintenance doses [3].
- Starting (initial) doses were **2 mg or 4 mg**, with escalation toward the assigned target — encoded as "4 mg [initial 2 mg]," "4 mg [initial 4 mg]," "8 mg [initial 2 mg]," "8 mg [initial 4 mg]," and "12 mg [initial 2 mg]" [3].
- Escalation was gradual (stepwise over the early weeks of the 48-week course) specifically to mitigate dose-dependent nausea/vomiting [3].
- Route/frequency: **subcutaneous, once weekly, same day each week** [3].

The Phase 3 TRIUMPH program subsequently uses a refined titration that includes an intermediate **6 mg** step and treats 4 mg / (up to) 12 mg as maintenance targets depending on arm [11]. This is reported only to characterize the trial dose ceiling; it is **not** a recommended schedule for anyone outside a trial. There is no validated dose for any indication, no approved package insert, and no established dose-conversion guidance. **The dose numbers above must not be read as a how-to.**
