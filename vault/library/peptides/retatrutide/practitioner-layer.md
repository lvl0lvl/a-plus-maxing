# Retatrutide — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how retatrutide is referenced for dosing and how it is accessed in practice*. Unlike an approved drug, retatrutide has **NO approved label and NO standard clinical dose anywhere in the world** as of June 2026 — so there is **no authoritative `regulatory` prescribing source**. The only dosing reference that exists is the titration schedule used inside Eli Lilly's own clinical trials, reproduced strictly as **trial-derived context** (`regulatory / trial-derived`), never as a label or recommendation. The gray-market/access and cost items are reported as **practice/regulatory context** and are admissible **only** for access/route/dose conventions. **None of them grounds an efficacy or adverse-event-rate claim** — those live in the retatrutide research report (§2/§3/§5) as Tier-1 Phase-2 trial evidence and are unaffected by anything below.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** Retatrutide is **INVESTIGATIONAL** — not approved by the FDA, EMA, PMDA, NMPA, or any agency, in Phase 3 (TRIUMPH program) but with no cleared product anywhere. Because it has **never been approved, there is no legitimate compounding pathway** (compounders may not compound a drug that has never been approved, and retatrutide is not a component of any FDA-approved drug). Any clinical or self-administered use today is off-investigational and is **especially premature** relative to other peptides in this wiki, because there is **no approved comparator product at all**.

## Dose reference (TRIAL-DERIVED, NOT a label)

The closest thing to a "dose" is the Phase 2 trial titration — a trial protocol, not a prescribing schedule. In the Jastreboff Phase 2 trial (NEJM 2023; registration **NCT04881760**), 338 adults with obesity were randomized to subcutaneous retatrutide **once weekly for 48 weeks**, using slow dose escalation to manage GI tolerability `regulatory / trial-derived`:

- Dose arms: **1 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, and 12 mg** target maintenance doses `regulatory / trial-derived`.
- Starting (initial) doses were **2 mg or 4 mg**, with escalation toward the assigned target — encoded in the trial arms as "4 mg [initial 2 mg]," "4 mg [initial 4 mg]," "8 mg [initial 2 mg]," "8 mg [initial 4 mg]," and "12 mg [initial 2 mg]" `regulatory / trial-derived`.
- Escalation was gradual (stepwise over the early weeks of the 48-week course) specifically to mitigate dose-dependent nausea/vomiting `regulatory / trial-derived`.
- Route/frequency: **subcutaneous, once weekly, same day each week** `regulatory / trial-derived`.

The Phase 3 **TRIUMPH** program subsequently uses a refined titration that includes an intermediate **6 mg** step and treats 4 mg / (up to) 12 mg as maintenance targets depending on arm `regulatory / trial-derived`. This is reported only to characterize the trial dose ceiling; it is **not** a recommended schedule for anyone outside a trial.

**Framing discipline.** There is **no validated dose for any indication, no approved package insert, and no established dose-conversion or maintenance guidance** for non-trial use. The numbers above are the *trial protocol* only and **must not be read as a how-to**. They are reproduced as trial-derived context to characterize what dose magnitudes the human evidence covers — nothing here is a clinical dosing recommendation.

## Gray-market / access (practice context only — NOT efficacy/AE)

Despite having **no approved product**, retatrutide is widely sold by research-chemical and "peptide" vendors, which is the channel through which essentially all current non-trial users obtain it. This is reported as **practice context only** — vendor labels carry no efficacy or safety weight, and nothing below grounds an efficacy or AE-rate claim.

- Online retatrutide is **unregulated, unapproved, and sold "for laboratory research purposes only / not for human consumption,"** language used to exploit a legal gray area; true retatrutide remains investigational `vendor_label / journalistic-context`.
- The sequence is public in Lilly's patent, so overseas (largely Chinese) chemists synthesize a molecule they claim is identical — but insiders note the developed drug "is more than just the molecule," and there is **no guarantee gray-market material is safe, pure, or correctly dosed** `vendor_label / journalistic-context`.
- The trade is large and crypto-settled: peptide gray-market crypto flows reached a **$100M+ annual run rate by mid-2026** (up from ~$1M/quarter in 2024), with banks refusing to process non-FDA-approved compounds `journalistic-context`.
- **Sterility is a documented failure mode, not a theoretical one:** at least one Chinese supplier's retatrutide batch shipped with a "glowing" mass/purity COA yet **failed independent sterility testing** — injecting non-sterile material carries severe, life-threatening risk `vendor_label COA / journalistic-context`. A separately reported consumer sterility submission also failed `journalistic-context`. A vendor COA showing high "purity" therefore tells you nothing about sterility or endotoxin.
- A registered dietitian / diabetes specialist writing for clinicians (S. Hormachea, RD) states plainly that gray-market retatrutide has **"no standardized dosing schedule, no prescription oversight, and no medical supervision,"** that "for research purposes only" products "operate entirely outside regulatory oversight," and advises steering patients to approved therapies rather than experimental compounds `practitioner_protocol` (opinion/advice, NOT an efficacy/safety dataset).

**No lawful compounding pathway.** Per the FDA's "Concerns with Unapproved GLP-1 Drugs Used for Weight Loss," retatrutide **cannot be compounded under federal law** and is **not a component of any FDA-approved drug**; the agency has taken enforcement action against telehealth marketers, API distributors, and outsourcing facilities. The gray-market channel is **not compounding in any regulated sense** `regulatory`.

**Cost (qualitative, gray-market).** Because supply is unregulated research-chem material sourced largely from China and priced per-milligram of powder, gray-market retatrutide is typically **far cheaper per milligram than any branded incretin therapy** — but the price reflects an unapproved, unverified, potentially non-sterile product with no clinical support, so **low cost is a risk signal, not a value signal** `vendor_label / journalistic-context` (qualitative only; no specific price is verified from a primary vendor page).

**Especially premature, stated plainly.** Combining an unapproved molecule, no quality control (no USP/EP monograph or reference standard), unknown long-term safety (Phase 3 unfinished), documented sterility failures, and no lawful supplement/compounding pathway makes gray-market retatrutide use **especially premature** relative to the rest of this library — there is no approved comparator product at all.

## Named-prescriber / guideline protocols

**None admissible.** Because retatrutide is an **investigational, unapproved drug**, there is no FDA/EMA label, no compounding-pharmacy clinical data sheet, no practitioner reference-text protocol, and no CME/society-stated prescribing protocol that constitutes a legitimate dosing authority for it. The only "schedule" in existence is the trial titration above, which is a research protocol, not a prescribing protocol. The one practitioner voice located (Hormachea, RD) explicitly **advises against** non-trial use rather than supplying a protocol. This absence is reported **honestly rather than fabricated** — no named-prescriber dose for retatrutide was located or invented.
