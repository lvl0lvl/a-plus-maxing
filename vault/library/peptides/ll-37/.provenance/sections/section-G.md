## Concentration audit (dominant labs; estimated single-lab share + method)

**Headline finding: single-lab concentration is LOW. LL-37 is an exceptionally broad, multi-decade, globally distributed literature with no dominant in-vivo efficacy lab. Estimated single-lab share of in-vivo efficacy primaries is well under ~20%** (i.e., far below the 70% concern threshold). The risk this audit screens for — a peptide whose "evidence base" is really one small group recycling itself — is **NOT present** for LL-37. The opposite is true: it is one of the most widely independently studied antimicrobial peptides in existence.

**Method.** I sampled the efficacy-relevant primary literature across the main in-vivo claim domains (wound healing, skin/MRSA infection, gut/colitis, the venous-leg-ulcer clinical program) and recorded corresponding-author group + institution + country for each. I cross-checked originators against the discovery papers. Because LL-37 is an *endogenous human peptide* discovered in 1995, the literature is intrinsically distributed across immunology, dermatology, microbiology, and structural-biology communities worldwide; there is no single patent-holder lab gatekeeping the molecule.

**Originators (Sweden, Karolinska Institutet).** LL-37 was first characterized as "FALL-39" by **Agerberth, Gunne, Odeberg, Kogner, Boman & Gudmundsson, PNAS 1995** [1]. Gudmundsson and Agerberth's Karolinska group remain foundational, but their share is dominated by *discovery/expression/mechanism* work, not the bulk of downstream in-vivo efficacy studies.

**Independent in-vivo efficacy groups (representative, distinct institutions/countries):**
- **Spain — CIEMAT, Madrid** (Carretero, Larcher, Del Río et al., *J Invest Dermatol* 2008): in-vivo wound-healing / re-epithelialization, adenoviral LL-37 transfer in ob/ob mice [2].
- **Italy + Poland — Polytechnic University of Marche (Ancona) with University/Medical University of Gdańsk** (Simonetti, Cirioni, Offidani, W. Kamysz et al., *Antibiotics* 2021): MRSA wound-infection mouse model, topical/systemic LL-37 [3].
- **USA / UCLA** cathelicidin-in-colitis line (e.g., Koon, Cheng, Pothoulakis groups) — endogenous vs exogenous cathelicidin in TNBS/DSS colitis models [6]; a separate community from the Swedish and dermatology groups.
- **Sweden — Promore Pharma / academic collaborators** (Grönberg et al. 2014; Mahlapuu et al. 2021) ran the *clinical* venous-leg-ulcer program [4][5] — this is the one place a single sponsor concentrates, but it is the clinical (not preclinical) track, and the Phase IIb result was negative on the full population (see Sourcing/caveats).

**Conclusion of audit.** Dominant *originator* lab = Gudmundsson/Agerberth (Karolinska, Sweden). Dominant *single sponsor of clinical trials* = Promore Pharma (Sweden), but only ~2 trials. Across the in-vivo efficacy primary literature as a whole, no single lab approaches even a plurality; the body of work spans Sweden, Spain, Italy, Poland, the USA, the UK, China, Japan and more. Estimated single-lab share: **LOW / diverse (<~20%)**. This is a positive provenance signal, not a flag.

## Sourcing landscape (gray-market injectable; purity caveats)

1. **No approved exogenous LL-37 product exists.** Exogenous LL-37 is **NOT an FDA-approved (or otherwise approved) drug** for any indication. The most advanced clinical attempt — topical LL-37 for hard-to-heal venous leg ulcers — was promising in a small Phase I/II [4] but **failed its primary efficacy endpoint on the full population in the Phase IIb HEAL trial** [5]. There is therefore no licensed injectable, oral, or topical LL-37 therapeutic on the market.

2. **Gray-market "research chemical" vials are the dominant supply.** LL-37 is widely sold by research-peptide vendors as lyophilized powder, typically **2 mg, 5 mg, or 10 mg vials**, "≥98–99% purity (HPLC)", explicitly labeled **"research use only / not for human or veterinary use."** [7][8][9] These labels are vendor_label tag: usable ONLY for purity/reconstitution/identity math, never for efficacy. The research-use-only disclaimer is legally load-bearing — these are not pharmaceuticals and carry no regulatory purity/sterility guarantee despite "third-party COA" marketing claims.

3. **Identity / purity caveats.** LL-37 is a 37-residue cationic peptide (sequence LLGDFFRKSKEKIGKEFKRIVQRIKDFLRNLVPRTES). It is a relatively long synthetic peptide; long peptides have higher risk of truncation/deletion sequences, deamidation, and counter-ion/TFA salt content that inflate apparent mass. "99% purity" claims on vendor pages are not independently verifiable and the genoracle "doctor sheet" PDF [10] (a vendor data sheet) could not be parsed to confirm its stated formula — treat its specs as unverified vendor_label. Reconstitution conventions cited by vendors/dosing pages: add bacteriostatic water (commonly 2 mL to a 5 mg vial → 2.5 mg/mL), refrigerate 2–8 °C after reconstitution, store lyophilized at −20 °C [9] — administration/handling info only.

4. **Cosmetic channel.** LL-37 / "cathelicidin" also appears in the topical cosmetic / "skincare peptide" space (and in atopic-dermatitis literature endogenously); this is a separate, lower-dose, non-injectable channel and is not the gray-market injectable concern. No verified cosmetic regulatory approval for exogenous LL-37 as an active was located.

## Prescribing-practice conventions (name+venue+date) [practitioner_protocol]

Tagging note: items below are **practitioner_protocol / dosing-guide** sources — captured for **dose/cycle/route conventions ONLY**, never as efficacy evidence. Several lacked a verifiable author/venue/date and are flagged as such (does not meet the name+venue+date bar; recorded as convention-aggregate, not as an attributable protocol).

- **Convention range (aggregated across peptide dosing guides):** subcutaneous injection, commonly **100–400 mcg/day** (some guides ~125 mcg/day; others 200–400 mcg/day), often cycled **~4 weeks on / 2 weeks off**, or **200 mcg/day for 5–10 days** during/after acute infection [9][11]. Reconstitute lyophilized powder in bacteriostatic water; refrigerate after reconstitution. These are anecdotal/community conventions — dosing pages themselves concede "no scientific data supports the safety or effectiveness of such suggestions" [11].

- **Gut/biofilm convention:** some guides describe LL-37 ~100 mcg AM (sometimes stacked with BPC-157) for ~3–4 week "gut repair" cycles, taken fasted [11]. Convention only; not efficacy-grounded.

- **William A. Seeds, MD — *Peptide Protocols, Volume One* (self-published, 2020)** [12] is the canonical practitioner reference frequently cited in this space. VERIFIED to exist (real book, real author/venue/date). However, I could **NOT verify a specific LL-37 dose/cycle inside the Seeds text** from retrievable sources — search returned the book's existence but not its LL-37 page. Per integrity rule I will NOT fabricate a Seeds LL-37 protocol; recorded as "named source exists; specific LL-37 dose unverified."

- **Clinic-marketed use:** peptide/longevity clinics market LL-37 for chronic/recurrent infection, biofilm, gut/IBD support, and immune resilience [9][11]. Specific clinic dose pages (e.g., Beverly Hills Rejuvenation Center) could not be fetched (redirect loop) — not citing dose figures I could not retrieve.

- **MANDATORY SAFETY FLAG (autoimmunity).** Practitioners marketing LL-37 for "immune support" should weigh that **LL-37 is itself a documented autoantigen**: native and citrullinated LL-37 drive autoreactive Th17 and T-follicular-helper responses in **psoriasis/psoriatic arthritis** [13][14] and in **systemic lupus erythematosus**, where ~45% of SLE patients have LL-37-reactive T cells correlating with anti-LL-37 antibodies and disease activity [15]. LL-37 complexed with self-DNA/RNA licenses plasmacytoid-dendritic-cell type-I-interferon autoreactivity [16]. Exogenous LL-37 in patients with (or predisposed to) autoimmune disease is a real theoretical hazard that the consumer dosing guides largely omit.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **Single-lab share is an estimate, not a citation count.** I sampled claim-domain corresponding authors rather than performing an exhaustive bibliometric of all LL-37 in-vivo papers. The qualitative conclusion (broad, multi-country, no dominant lab; <~20% single-lab) is robust given the spread observed, but a precise percentage was not computed.
- **[6] colitis line not individually PMID-verified** — used only as provenance-diversity evidence, deliberately not as an efficacy source.
- **Vendor specs unverifiable.** Peptide Sciences page 403'd; genoracle PDF unparseable; "99% purity / third-party COA" claims taken as marketing, not verified. No independent HPLC/MS on any specific gray-market lot was obtainable.
- **Seeds LL-37 dose not located.** The canonical practitioner book exists and is cited, but I could not retrieve its specific LL-37 dose page; per integrity rules I did not fabricate one. A copy of the text would close this gap.
- **Some clinic dose pages unreachable** (redirect loops) → I declined to cite figures I could not retrieve.
- **Cosmetic regulatory status thin** — no authoritative source on exogenous-LL-37-as-cosmetic-active approval was found; reported as "none located," not "none exists."
