# Section C — Preclinical Antimicrobial/Wound/Infection (LL-37)

Scope: human cathelicidin LL-37 (and engineered/truncated analogues) — direct
antimicrobial efficacy (broad-spectrum, biofilm, antiviral), wound-healing
models, and in vivo anti-infective efficacy (sepsis, local/systemic infection).
DEEP mode. Effect sizes, species+n, and route are given for every animal claim.
In-vitro-vs-in-vivo status is flagged explicitly throughout.

---

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; species+n every animal claim; route tags)

### Antimicrobial efficacy — biofilm and MDR pathogens (in vitro)

1. Against 24-h *Staphylococcus aureus* biofilms grown on cobalt-chromium discs
   (CDC biofilm reactor), LL-37 produced **>4 log CFU reduction at 10 µM**
   (sub-MIC), with **3.2 log reduction after only 5 min exposure**; the planar
   MIC was 32 µM. By contrast, gentamicin, vancomycin, rifampin, and silver
   nanoparticles each gave <1 log reduction at up to 1000 µM
   (gentamicin+rifampin combination = 1.71 log). LL-37 was thus far more potent
   against established biofilm than conventional antibiotics in this model.
   [1, in_vitro]

2. Against multidrug-resistant *Acinetobacter baumannii* clinical isolates,
   LL-37 MIC ranged **16–32 µg/mL**, and LL-37 inhibited and dispersed biofilm
   at **32 µg/mL** (MBEC assay). Truncated fragments showed varying potency:
   KS-30 MIC 8–16 µg/mL (more potent than parent on a per-mass basis), KR-20
   MIC 16–64 µg/mL; fragments dispersed biofilm at 64–128 µg/mL. [2, in_vitro]

3. LL-37 displays broad-spectrum direct killing against Gram-negative and
   Gram-positive bacteria, fungi, and enveloped viruses via membrane
   permeabilization, with reported activity across dozens of bacterial, fungal,
   and viral species (mechanism review; specific numbers should be sourced from
   primary studies, not from the review). [3, mechanism_review]

### Antiviral efficacy (in vitro and in vivo)

4. **In vivo influenza:** In female BALB/c mice (6–8 wk; n=5 per group for
   survival/weight, n=3 for viral titer) infected intranasally with 10 MLD50
   (~350 PFU) of A/Puerto Rico/8/34 (H1N1), **nebulized LL-37** (500 µg/mL in
   200 µL, once daily day −1 to day +7) reduced **lung virus titer ~70–80% at
   day 3** and improved survival to **~60% vs 0%** in scrambled-peptide/vehicle
   controls — protection of similar magnitude to the antiviral drug zanamivir.
   [4, animal] (species=mouse/BALB/c; n=5 survival, n=3 titer; route=nebulized/intranasal)

5. Mechanistically, LL-37 inhibits influenza A primarily by **direct action on
   the virion** (pre-incubation of virus with peptide is required for optimal
   inhibition) and blocks replication at a **post-entry step before viral RNA/
   protein synthesis**; antiviral activity is **reduced by binding to serum HDL**
   — a translational caveat for systemic dosing. [4, animal] (same study, in vitro mechanism arm)

### Wound healing models

6. **Diabetic wound (non-infected mechanism study):** In diabetic mice with
   full-thickness dorsal wounds, LL-37 **accelerated wound closure** by
   activating **TFEB-dependent autophagy** (increased nuclear TFEB
   translocation; increased ATG5, ATG7, beclin-1). The paper reports faster
   closure kinetics in LL-37-treated diabetic wounds vs vehicle. [5, animal]
   (species=mouse, diabetic; n and exact closure % not extractable from
   abstract — flagged as a gap; route=topical/local per model)

7. **MRSA-infected wound (efficacy + healing):** In 6-month-old BALB/c mice
   (80 animals, 8 groups, **n=10 per group**) with full-thickness dorsal wounds
   inoculated with MRSA (*S. aureus* ATCC 43300, 5×10^7 CFU/mL, 200 µL),
   **combined topical + daily intraperitoneal LL-37 (1 mg/kg each route)**
   reduced day-7 wound bacterial load from **7.8×10^7 ± 1.4×10^7 CFU/g
   (untreated) to 6.9×10^2 ± 1.2×10^2 CFU/g (~5 log reduction; p<0.001)** and
   improved histologic healing (re-epithelialization 2.70±0.52, granulation
   2.90±0.74, collagen organization 2.73±0.49) and angiogenesis (micro-vessel
   density 271.8±54.9 vessels/mm²; VEGF+ cells 493.5±68.2 cells/mm²),
   outperforming teicoplanin. [6, animal] (species=mouse/BALB/c; n=10/group;
   route=topical + i.p.)

### In vivo anti-infective (sepsis, pneumonia, systemic infection)

8. **Polymicrobial sepsis (CLP):** In mice (n=8–15 per group) with cecal
   ligation and puncture, a **single intravenous dose of LL-37 immediately
   after surgery** improved survival dose-dependently — reported survival of
   **14.3% (1 µg/mouse) and 36.4% (2 µg/mouse)** — while reducing bacterial
   burden in peritoneal fluid and blood and lowering IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α.
   [7, animal] (species=mouse; n=8–15/group; route=IV)

9. **Sepsis mechanism (CLP):** LL-37 improves survival of CLP septic mice by
   **inhibiting caspase-1 activation and pyroptosis of peritoneal macrophages**
   and modulating inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α), with reduced
   bacterial burden in peritoneal fluid and blood. [8, animal] (species=mouse;
   route=IV; n not specified in accessible text — flagged) — same Juntendo
   group as [7] (provenance note below).

10. **Pseudomonas pneumonia:** In C57Bl/6 wild-type and Camp−/− (cathelicidin-
    knockout) mice (n≥9 per condition at most timepoints), **intranasal human
    LL-37 (10 µg in 50 µL PBS)** significantly **enhanced clearance of
    *P. aeruginosa* PAO1 (3×10^7 CFU intranasal) by 24 h**, acting via an
    **early (~2-fold at 6 h) augmentation of neutrophil recruitment/function**
    independent of endogenous cathelicidin. [9, animal] (species=mouse/
    C57Bl/6 + Camp−/−; n≥9; route=intranasal)

11. **Systemic A. baumannii infection:** In a mouse model of systemic
    *A. baumannii* infection, recombinant LL-37 protein treatment was reported
    to result in **survival of all treated infected mice with no detectable
    bacteria in blood**; in vitro the recombinant peptide gave complete kill at
    1.5 µg/mL after 40 min. (Small/early study; exact n and route not specified
    in accessible abstract — flagged.) [10, animal] (species=mouse; n not
    stated; route not stated)

## Lab-provenance note (dominant groups — for concentration audit)

- **Juntendo University / Nagaoka group (Tokyo)** dominates the **IV-LL-37
  sepsis (CLP)** evidence: refs [7] and [8] are the SAME lab. The two sepsis
  papers should NOT be counted as independent replication of each other; the
  CLP survival benefit rests substantially on one group. Co-affiliations
  (LPS Consulting, Microcoat Biotechnologie) appear on [7] — note for any
  conflict-of-interest audit.
- **Edinburgh MRC Centre for Inflammation Research** appears on both the in
  vivo influenza paper [4] (with CDC) and the Pseudomonas pneumonia paper [9].
  Different pathogens/models, but a shared cathelicidin-in-respiratory-defense
  program — worth flagging when weighting respiratory anti-infective claims.
- **Wound-healing efficacy** [6] (Marche/Gdańsk) and **diabetic autophagy** [5]
  (Shanghai) are independent groups — good cross-group support for the wound
  axis.
- In vitro biofilm/MDR data [1] (West Virginia) and [2] (Walter Reed) are
  independent — good cross-lab support for direct/antibiofilm activity.
- Concentration-audit caution: in vitro potency is reported variably in **µM**
  ([1]: MIC 32 µM) and **µg/mL** ([2]: MIC 16–32 µg/mL). LL-37 MW ≈ 4493 Da,
  so 32 µM ≈ 144 µg/mL — the WVU S. aureus MIC (32 µM) is numerically much
  higher than the Walter Reed A. baumannii MIC (16–32 µg/mL ≈ 3.6–7.1 µM).
  Unit harmonization is required before any cross-study potency comparison.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **Sepsis evidence is single-lab.** Refs [7]+[8] are both Nagaoka/Juntendo.
  The dose-dependent CLP survival numbers (14.3% / 36.4%) are NOT independently
  replicated here; survival of 14.3% even at the higher benefit is modest, and
  the small per-mouse dose (1–2 µg) is unusual — treat as promising, not robust.
- **Diabetic wound-healing numbers are abstract-level only** ([5] full text
  paywalled): mechanism (TFEB/autophagy) and direction (faster closure) are
  documented, but exact n, closure %, and timepoints could not be verified —
  flagged, not fabricated.
- **A. baumannii systemic-infection survival claim** [10] is a small Tier-2
  study (Protein Pept Lett) with "all treated mice survived" but no extractable
  n or route; striking result that needs independent confirmation.
- **Antiviral in vivo rests on one influenza study** [4]; antiviral breadth
  (RSV, others) exists in the literature but was largely in vitro and is not
  fully harvested here.
- **In-vitro-to-in-vivo gap is the central caveat for the whole section.**
  LL-37's direct antimicrobial potency is partially neutralized in vivo by
  serum/HDL binding (documented for the antiviral arm [4]) and by physiological
  salt and protein — in vivo benefit often tracks immunomodulation
  (neutrophil/NET enhancement [9], anti-pyroptosis [8]) as much as direct kill.
- **No human RCT or regulatory source** is included in Section C scope
  (preclinical); a diabetic-foot-ulcer LL-37 cream RCT exists (PMID 37480520)
  and should be carried in the clinical section, not here.
- **Engineered analogues** partially covered (LL-37 fragments KS-30/KR-20/KR-12
  in [2]); a fuller analogue/optimization survey (e.g., KR-12, OP-145, SAAP-148)
  was not exhaustively harvested and is a deliberate scope boundary.
