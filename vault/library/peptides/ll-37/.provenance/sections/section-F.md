# Section F — Safety, Regulatory & Anti-Doping (LL-37)

*Target: LL-37 (human cathelicidin, hCAP-18 C-terminal fragment). DEEP mode. All sources verified retrievable (author+year+title+PMID/DOI/URL). Vendor/anecdote tags are NOT used to ground numerical AE rates.*

---

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; species+n; AE rate vs existence distinguished)

### F.1 — Human trial safety: venous leg ulcer (VLU) topical program

1. In the first-in-man Phase I/IIa VLU trial, synthetic LL-37 was applied topically twice-weekly at 0.5, 1.6, or 3.2 mg/mL vs placebo in **34 patients**; the authors reported **no safety concerns regarding local or systemic adverse events** and described treatment as "safe and well tolerated." This is an **existence-of-safety** statement, not a quantified AE rate. [1, rct]

2. In the same Phase I/IIa trial, the **highest dose (3.2 mg/mL) showed no benefit over placebo** while the two lower doses did — i.e., efficacy is lost at the top dose, consistent with a **narrow therapeutic window** and concentration-dependent cellular toxicity at higher local exposure. [1, rct]

3. In the larger multicentric Phase IIb VLU trial (LL-37 topical, **149 randomized / 148 treated**, 15 sites, Poland + Sweden), **12 non-fatal serious adverse events occurred in 11 patients (7.4% of the study population)**; the reported SAEs were erysipelas (3), cellulitis, cardiac failure, myocardial infarction, femur fracture, lower-limb fracture, increased blood creatinine, cerebrovascular accident, and acute kidney injury. **None were assessed as related to study drug.** [2, rct] — *This 7.4% is a background SAE rate in an elderly chronic-wound population, NOT a drug-attributable AE rate.*

4. In the Phase IIb trial, local reactions were **predominantly mild-to-moderate**; **severe reactions affected ≤5 patients (≤3.4%) per visit**, infection rates were low (2–6% across groups), and no consistent treatment-group differences emerged in laboratory values, vital signs, or physical exams. Drug described as "well tolerated and safe." [2, rct]

### F.2 — Human trial safety: intratumoral melanoma program

5. A Phase 1 trial of **intratumoral LL-37 injection for cutaneous metastatic melanoma (NCT02225366)** was conducted to find a tolerable dose and assess immune activation (plasmacytoid-DC–mediated). [3, regulatory] [4, mechanism_review]

6. **Dermatologic toxicity is a documented adverse event of intratumoral LL-37 in melanoma.** A clinicopathologic case report (Dolkar et al., 2018) detailed cutaneous toxicity arising from intratumoral LL-37 therapy in a melanoma patient (single-case detailed examination), establishing that **local dermatologic adverse reactions occur** — existence, not a population rate. [5, anecdote_aggregate/case-report] *(case report; n=1; cannot ground an AE rate)*

### F.3 — Preclinical cytotoxicity / hemolysis (concentration-dependent membrane toxicity)

7. Native LL-37 **induces hemolysis and is toxic to human leukocytes and T-lymphocytes**, attributed to hydrophobic interactions with eukaryotic-cell membranes — i.e., the same membrane-permeabilizing mechanism that gives antimicrobial activity also damages host cells at sufficient concentration. [6, in_vitro/mechanism_review]

8. LL-37 cytotoxicity is **concentration-dependent**: in human isolated platelets, **1–50 µM showed no toxicity, but 100 µM LL-37 was significantly toxic**. [7, in_vitro] (species: human platelets, in vitro)

9. LL-37 is reported cytotoxic to many human cell types at **~1–10 µM**, and **very high concentrations (~300 µM) are detected in psoriatic lesional skin** — a benchmark for the pathologic-inflammatory range. [8, mechanism_review] *(psoriatic-lesion concentration figure)*

### F.4 — Theoretical / mechanistic risks of EXOGENOUS administration (major for LL-37)

10. **Autoimmunity / psoriasis autoantigen risk.** LL-37 is a **bona fide T-cell autoantigen in psoriasis**: two-thirds of patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis harbor CD4+ and/or CD8+ T cells specific for LL-37; these cells produce IFN-γ (CD4+ also Th17 cytokines), and **circulating LL-37-specific T-cell presence correlates with disease activity**. Exogenous LL-37 plausibly supplies the very autoantigen driving psoriatic autoimmunity. [9, mechanism_review/cohort] (human; T-cell reactivity in a defined patient series)

11. **Pro-inflammatory / type-I-interferon mechanism.** LL-37 complexes with self-DNA/RNA, is taken into plasmacytoid dendritic cells, bypasses TLR9 self/non-self safeguards, and **triggers IFN-α and a self-perpetuating pro-inflammatory cascade** (keratinocyte IL-1/IL-36γ/chemokine release, neutrophil recruitment, NET amplification). This is the central driver of psoriatic innate inflammation and is also implicated in SLE (LL-37/self-DNA in NETs; anti-LL-37 autoantibodies) and rheumatoid arthritis (elevated synovial LL-37). [10, mechanism_review]

12. **Keratinocyte sensitization.** LL-37 enables keratinocytes to respond to TLR9 ligands (CpG/genomic DNA), greatly increasing type-I-interferon production — a distinct keratinocyte-intrinsic route by which LL-37 amplifies skin inflammation, demonstrated at 0.1–10 µM (within the psoriatic physiologic range). [11, in_vitro]

13. **Context-dependent pro-tumor risk.** LL-37 has **dual, tissue-specific effects on cancer**: pro-tumorigenic (proliferation/migration/tumorigenesis) in ovarian, lung, breast, prostate, pancreatic cancers, **melanoma**, and skin SCC; anti-tumor in colon, gastric, hematologic, and oral SCC. Direction is governed by which membrane receptors (EGFR, FPR2, TRPV2, P2X7) the tumor expresses. For exogenous LL-37 this is a genuine, unresolved safety consideration, especially for skin/melanoma exposure. [12, mechanism_review] [4, mechanism_review]

14. **Pro-thrombotic potential.** LL-37 (≤50 µM) **activates human platelets** (aggregation, fibrinogen binding, granule secretion, Ca²⁺ mobilization) via FPR2/ALX and **augments thrombus formation**, shortening bleeding time in mice; LL-37 is elevated in sepsis and psoriasis, linking it to thrombotic risk in inflammatory states. [7, in_vitro + animal] (human platelets in vitro; mouse in vivo, thrombosis model)

## Risk-floor inputs (contraindications / monitoring / stopping — each cited)

**Contraindications / caution (mechanistic, not label-derived — no approved label exists):**
- **Psoriasis or psoriatic arthritis (and known LL-37 autoreactivity)** — LL-37 is the psoriasis T-cell autoantigen and innate-inflammation trigger; exogenous administration is mechanistically contraindicated / flare-risk. [9, mechanism_review/cohort] [10, mechanism_review]
- **Other type-I-IFN / autoimmune conditions (SLE, RA)** — LL-37/self-nucleic-acid complexes and NET amplification implicated; caution. [10, mechanism_review]
- **Active or history of malignancy, particularly melanoma / skin SCC / breast / lung / ovarian / prostate / pancreatic** — context-dependent pro-tumor signal. [12, mechanism_review] [4, mechanism_review]
- **Pro-thrombotic states / antiplatelet considerations** — LL-37 activates platelets and augments thrombosis. [7, in_vitro+animal]
- **High local concentration** — avoid; cytotoxic/hemolytic above ~50–100 µM and efficacy lost at the top tested topical dose (3.2 mg/mL). [1, rct] [7, in_vitro] [6, in_vitro]

**Monitoring — named objective markers (risk-floor inference; no validated monitoring protocol exists for exogenous LL-37):**
- **hs-CRP** and inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-α surrogate where available) — objective inflammation markers, given LL-37's pro-inflammatory/IFN cascade. [10, mechanism_review] *(hs-CRP named as the practical inflammation floor marker; the cited mechanism justifies an inflammatory-marker watch, not a specific published threshold)*
- **CBC / platelet function awareness** and bleeding-time / thrombotic-symptom vigilance — given platelet-activation/thrombosis signal. [7, in_vitro+animal]
- **Skin examination** for psoriasiform/inflammatory eruptions at and beyond injection/application sites. [5, case-report] [9, mechanism_review/cohort]
- **Renal markers (creatinine)** were tracked in the Phase IIb trial as part of safety labs. [2, rct]

**Stopping rules (inferred from observed reversibility / toxicity):**
- Discontinue on new/worsening psoriasiform or inflammatory dermatologic reaction; the Dolkar case documents cutaneous toxicity tied to therapy. [5, case-report]
- Discontinue at concentrations producing local irritation; severe local reactions occurred in ≤3.4% per visit at trial doses. [2, rct]

## Regulatory & WADA status (cited)

**FDA — not an approved drug.** LL-37 has **no FDA marketing approval** for any indication; human use has been investigational (VLU topical program; melanoma NCT02225366). [3, regulatory] [1, rct] [2, rct]

**FDA 503A bulk-substances status (with April-2026 action):**
- Through early 2026, **cathelicidin LL-37 sat in interim Category 2** ("do not compound" — significant-safety-concern bucket) of the §503A bulk drug substances framework.
- **On the April 16, 2026 Federal Register notice (FR Doc. 2026-07361), FDA announced removal of 12 peptide bulk substances from Category 2, and cathelicidin LL-37 IS one of the 12** (alongside BPC-157, TB-500/Tβ4-fragment, KPV, MOTS-c, DSIP/Emideltide, Semax, Epitalon, GHK-Cu injectable, Melanotan II, Dihexa acetate, PEG-MGF). Removal was effective ~7 days after the notice (≈**April 22–23, 2026**). [13, regulatory] [14, regulatory]
- **CRITICAL — removal ≠ legal to compound.** FDA states removal from Category 2 does **not** make these substances eligible for compounding under §503A; they remain outside interim enforcement discretion (which covers only Category 1). LL-37 is in a **regulatory gray zone** pending advisory-committee review and rulemaking. [14, regulatory]
- **PCAC timing — LL-37 is NOT on the July 23–24, 2026 PCAC agenda.** That meeting reviews BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c (July 23) and Emideltide/DSIP, Semax, Epitalon (July 24). **LL-37 is deferred to a separate PCAC convening "before the end of February 2027,"** batched with GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, Dihexa acetate, and PEG-MGF. [15, regulatory] [14, regulatory]

**WADA / anti-doping (2026 Prohibited List):**
- **LL-37 / cathelicidin is NOT explicitly named anywhere on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List** (verified: name absent from the WADA list and from BSCG's 2026 peptide-regulation analysis, which enumerates named peptides — BPC-157 under S0, TB-500 as a growth factor, CJC-1295 under S2.2.4 — but does not list LL-37/cathelicidin). [16, regulatory]
- **However, S0 (Non-Approved Substances) is an all-inclusive catch-all**: any substance not approved by a governmental health authority for human therapeutic use (including drugs in pre-clinical/clinical development) is prohibited at all times. Because LL-37 has no regulatory approval, **its non-investigational use by athletes would fall under S0 by default**, even though it is not named. [16, regulatory]
- *Honest limit:* WADA does not publish a substance-by-substance "S0 includes X" list, so "covered by S0" is a reasoned regulatory inference from the S0 definition, not an explicit WADA naming. The verifiable hard fact is the **absence of LL-37/cathelicidin as a named entry**.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

1. **Melanoma trial outcomes not fully published.** NCT02225366 safety/AE tables are not in a primary peer-reviewed results paper I could retrieve; the toxicity signal rests on a single case report [5] (n=1) + a review [4]. AE *rates* for the melanoma program cannot be stated — only that dermatologic toxicity *exists*.

2. **No quantified drug-attributable AE rate exists for LL-37.** The VLU trials report *no drug-related* SAEs and a background 7.4% SAE rate in an elderly comorbid population [2]; this must not be read as an LL-37 AE rate.

3. **Hemolysis/cytotoxicity numbers are largely review-cited, not always primary.** The strongest primary cytotoxicity figure is the platelet 100 µM threshold [7]; the "~300 µM in psoriatic lesions" and "1–10 µM cytotoxic" figures come from review [8] and would benefit from tracing to the original measurement papers.

4. **WADA "S0 covers LL-37" is inference, not explicit naming.** Verified hard fact = LL-37/cathelicidin is NOT a named entry on the 2026 list [16]; S0 catch-all coverage of unapproved use is a reasoned regulatory inference from the S0 definition, not a WADA-published statement about LL-37 specifically.

5. **Federal Register full text not directly fetchable** (host blocked WebFetch). FR Doc 2026-07361 dated 2026-04-16 is confirmed via the federalregister.gov URL slug + FDA PCAC page + two independent legal-analysis secondaries [14][15]; the granular 12-peptide list and the LL-37-deferral-to-pre-Feb-2027 detail come from those secondaries, corroborated across Frier Levitt, thefdalawblog.com, and Orrick.

6. **No approved label → contraindications/monitoring are mechanistic inferences**, not regulator-issued. hs-CRP and platelet/skin monitoring are reasoned risk-floor markers grounded in the cited inflammation/thrombosis/autoimmunity mechanisms, not a validated LL-37 monitoring protocol.
