# Section E — Human Clinical Evidence (LL-37)

Scope: human studies in which **synthetic/exogenous LL-37 (the 37-residue C-terminal fragment of hCAP18, drug name ropocamptide) is ADMINISTERED to people**, plus a clear separation from the much larger body of **endogenous-LL-37-as-biomarker** observational data. The honest headline (see Claims §1) is that the entire human-administration evidence base for LL-37 is small (four trials; 211 total subjects enrolled across all arms incl. placebo, of whom only ~150-160 were actually LL-37-exposed) and concentrated in topical wound healing; oncology exposure is a single 4-patient dose-finding study.

---

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; per trial NCT/phase/n/status/result)

1. **There IS real human evidence of administered LL-37, but it is limited.** Four trials have dosed humans with exogenous LL-37: two venous-leg-ulcer (VLU) RCTs, one diabetic-foot-ulcer (DFU) RCT, and one melanoma dose-finding trial — totaling 211 subjects enrolled across all arms (incl. placebo), of whom only ~150-160 were actually LL-37-exposed. No Phase III, no regulatory approval, no marketed product. [1, rct][2, rct][3, rct][4, open_label]

2. **First-in-human VLU trial (positive at low dose).** Grönberg et al. 2014 — Phase I/II, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 3-center (Sweden); 3-week open-label placebo run-in then 4-week randomized treatment + 4-week follow-up; sponsor Pergamum/Promore Pharma; n=34. LL-37 0.5 and 1.6 mg/mL vs 3.2 mg/mL vs placebo, twice weekly. Result: healing-rate constants ~6-fold (0.5 mg/mL, p=0.003) and ~3-fold (1.6 mg/mL, p=0.088) higher than placebo; 3.2 mg/mL no better than placebo (inverted/bell-shaped dose response); no safety concerns. [1, rct] PMID 25041740; DOI 10.1111/wrr.12211.

3. **Confirmatory Phase IIb VLU trial (HEAL) MISSED its primary endpoint.** Mahlapuu et al. 2021 — Phase IIb, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter (Sweden + Poland); sponsor Promore Pharma; 149 randomized / 148 treated; arms LL-37 0.5, 1.6 mg/mL, placebo (1:1:1) twice weekly + compression. Primary endpoint (confirmed complete wound closure at 13 wk) was **negative** — closure 26.5% vs 24.7% vs 25.3%, no significant difference. Post-hoc subgroup of large ulcers (≥10 cm²) showed 0.5 mg/mL benefit (healing rate 0.0367 vs 0.0093/day, p=0.0439; closure 28.1% vs 8.1%, p=0.0458). [2, rct] PMID 34687253; DOI 10.1111/wrr.12977; EudraCT 2018-000536-10. (Per Promore press materials this is the trial the company calls "HEAL"; a discrete NCT number was not located on ClinicalTrials.gov — registration was via the EU EudraCT system.)

4. **DFU trial (mixed: granulation yes, wound closure no).** Miranda et al. 2023 — randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; Universitas Indonesia (Jakarta); enrolled/screened 25, randomized 13 LL-37 vs 12 placebo, all 25 analyzed; LL-37 cream 0.5 mg/g twice weekly × 4 wk. Result: significantly greater granulation-tissue index at all timepoints (p≈0.031–0.037) but **no significant difference in wound-area reduction** (−2.35 vs −1.54 cm², p>0.05), and **no significant reduction in bacterial colonization or IL-1α/TNF-α** vs placebo. [3, rct] PMID 37480520; DOI 10.1007/s00403-023-02657-8; registry NCT04098562.

5. **Melanoma dose-finding trial — only 4 patients, oncology efficacy NOT established.** "Induction of Antitumor Response in Melanoma Patients Using the Antimicrobial Peptide LL37," NCT02225366 — Phase 1/2, MD Anderson; intratumoral LL37 weekly up to 8 wk in cutaneous melanoma metastases; **enrollment 4 (actual)**; status Completed (Jul 2015–Nov 2020); results posted Dec 2021. Primary outcome was optimal biological dose / dose-limiting toxicity — i.e., a safety/dosing study, not an efficacy study. Secondary cancer-promotion review describes it as "shown significant potency," but that is an unquantified secondary characterization of an n=4 study and should not be read as demonstrated clinical efficacy. [4, open_label][5, meta_analysis] NCT02225366.

6. **A documented harm signal exists in the melanoma program.** Dolkar et al. 2018 reported the dermatologic/clinicopathologic toxicity of LL-37 melanoma therapy (intratumoral, same MD Anderson program), confirming that intratumoral LL-37 in humans produced real adverse cutaneous reactions. [6, anecdote_aggregate] PMID 29665030.

7. **Endogenous LL-37 as a biomarker is a SEPARATE, larger literature and is NOT evidence that giving LL-37 works.** Multiple observational studies measure native LL-37/hCAP18 levels and correlate them with disease (e.g., Krejner et al. 2016: serum LL-37 — not vitamin D — correlated with VLU healing, n=19 observational). Numerous ClinicalTrials.gov records (NCT04404335, NCT06867250, NCT03270709, etc.) only MEASURE LL-37; they do not administer it. These do not count as LL-37-administration efficacy evidence. [7, cohort]

8. **Mechanistic caution, not human efficacy:** preclinical/translational work indicates LL-37 can also *promote* melanoma cell invasion and activate tumor-associated macrophages — a reason the tiny oncology dataset cannot be taken as net-beneficial. [5, meta_analysis] PMID (review) 36081952; cell-biology PMID set summarized therein.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **EXOGENOUS-administration vs endogenous-biomarker — the critical distinction.** Only refs [1]–[4] (and the safety/mechanism refs [5]–[6]) concern *giving* LL-37 to humans. Ref [7] and a dozen ClinicalTrials.gov records (NCT04404335, NCT06867250, NCT03270709, NCT03369275, NCT02806414, NCT06863415, etc.) merely *measure* native LL-37/hCAP18 as a biomarker. The biomarker literature is suggestive of LL-37's biological role but is **not** evidence that administering LL-37 is efficacious or safe; readers must not conflate the two.
- **No NCT located for the pivotal Phase IIb VLU (HEAL/Mahlapuu 2021).** It was registered in the EU EudraCT system (2018-000536-10), not (locatable) on ClinicalTrials.gov. The 2014 first-in-human VLU trial predates registry norms and has no NCT. Both are nonetheless real, peer-reviewed, PMID-verified.
- **The strongest single result (Grönberg 2014, p=0.003) did NOT replicate** on its primary endpoint in the larger confirmatory Phase IIb (Mahlapuu 2021). The VLU efficacy story therefore rests on a positive small Phase I/II + a negative Phase IIb rescued only by a post-hoc subgroup. This is a genuine efficacy weakness, not a citation gap.
- **Oncology evidence is essentially a case series (n=4).** "Significant potency" appears only as an unquantified phrase in a secondary review; the trial's own primary outcome was dose/toxicity. Combined with documented dermatologic toxicity [6] and pro-tumor mechanisms [5], intratumoral LL-37 in humans is exploratory at best.
- **No trials found** for any other indication (sepsis, respiratory, oral/periodontal, ophthalmic) involving *administered* LL-37 in humans — those appear only as biomarker measurements.
- Wiley full text for [1] returned HTTP 403; its design/n/p-values were corroborated across PubMed (PMID 25041740), the Mahlapuu 2021 paper's introduction, and Promore/Pergamum materials — consistent figures, so the citation stands.

### One-sentence honest human-evidence bottom line
Yes, exogenous LL-37 has been given to humans in four real trials (211 subjects enrolled across all arms incl. placebo; only ~150-160 actually LL-37-exposed) — a positive first-in-human VLU study (Grönberg 2014, p=0.003) that FAILED to confirm on its primary endpoint in the larger Phase IIb HEAL trial (Mahlapuu 2021, rescued only by a post-hoc subgroup), a DFU trial showing better granulation but no wound-closure/antibacterial benefit (Miranda 2023), and a 4-patient melanoma dose-finding study with no established efficacy and documented skin toxicity — so there is genuine but weak, unconfirmed, non-approved human efficacy evidence, concentrated in topical wound healing.
