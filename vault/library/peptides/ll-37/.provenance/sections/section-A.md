# Section A — Identity, Chemistry, PK, Endogenous Biology (LL-37)

Scope: LL-37 (human cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide; the C-terminal antimicrobial
fragment of the hCAP18/CAMP gene product). This section covers molecular identity and
sequence, the hCAP18 precursor and its proteolytic processing, secondary structure,
endogenous expression and the vitamin-D regulation of CAMP, and proteolytic
stability / pharmacokinetics. Therapeutic efficacy, immunomodulatory signaling,
and exogenous dosing belong to other sections.

INTEGRITY NOTE: A specific circulating plasma half-life figure ("90–120 min") surfaced
in search aggregations but could NOT be traced to a verifiable primary or review source
on inspection (it appears only in vendor/aggregator text). It is therefore NOT cited as a
number. Only the qualitative, source-supported statements about rapid proteolytic
degradation and short half-life are made below (Claims 13–15).

## Claims

### Identity & gene / precursor

1. LL-37 is the only cathelicidin (host-defense antimicrobial peptide of the cathelicidin
   family) found in humans; hCAP18 is the sole human cathelicidin precursor. [8, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review]

2. LL-37 is the C-terminal antimicrobial peptide derived from hCAP18 (human cationic
   antimicrobial protein, ~18 kDa), the product of the CAMP gene. [1, in_vitro][2, mechanism_review]

3. The hCAP18 pre-propeptide is organized as a signal peptide (30 aa) + a conserved
   N-terminal cathelin-like domain (~103 aa) + the C-terminal LL-37 (37 aa); LL-37
   corresponds to the C-terminal residues (134–170) of the hCAP18 precursor. [8, mechanism_review][3, in_vitro]

4. LL-37 is a 37-amino-acid peptide with the sequence
   LLGDFFRKSKEKIGKEFKRIVQRIKDFLRNLVPRTES; it is named "LL-37" for its two N-terminal
   leucine residues and 37-residue length, and was originally described as the human
   peptide FALL-39 / FALL39. [3, in_vitro][8, mechanism_review]

5. LL-37 carries a net positive charge of approximately +6 at physiological (neutral) pH
   and is a cysteine-free, cationic peptide. [8, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review]
   (Note: a related computed value of net charge ≈ +5.9 at pH 7.4 is reported in
   structural work [9, in_vitro]; the +6 figure is the commonly cited integer charge.)

6. The mature LL-37 peptide has a molecular weight of approximately 4.49 kDa
   (~4493 g/mol). [9, in_vitro][3, in_vitro]

### Proteolytic processing (precursor → LL-37)

7. hCAP18 is processed extracellularly to the active peptide LL-37 by cleavage with
   proteinase 3 (PR3), a serine protease; in neutrophils this occurs after exocytosis,
   when hCAP18 stored in specific (secondary) granules meets PR3 from azurophil
   granules. [1, in_vitro]

8. Original characterization of the human FALL39/CAMP gene showed the cathelin-containing
   precursor is processed to the antibacterial peptide LL-37 in granulocytes. [3, in_vitro]

9. The processing protease differs by cell type: proteinase 3 is restricted to myeloid
   cells; in skin/epithelium the hCAP18 precursor is processed at the surface by the
   serine proteases kallikrein 5 (stratum corneum tryptic enzyme) and kallikrein 7
   (stratum corneum chymotryptic enzyme), which can generate LL-37 and shorter
   epidermal forms. [10, mechanism_review]

### Secondary structure

10. LL-37 is an amphipathic, α-helical peptide. The monomer is largely unstructured in
    dilute aqueous solution but adopts an α-helix under conditions of higher peptide
    concentration, elevated salt, or in the presence of lipids/detergents (e.g., SDS
    micelles). [2, mechanism_review][9, in_vitro]

11. By solution NMR in lipid micelles, LL-37 adopts a curved amphipathic
    helix-bend-helix motif spanning approximately residues 2–31, with a bend near
    Gly14–Glu16, followed by a disordered C-terminal tail. [9, in_vitro]
    (PDB entry 2K6O.) [11, in_vitro]

### Endogenous expression

12. hCAP18/LL-37 is expressed broadly: it is stored in the specific (secondary) granules
    of neutrophils (the largest reservoir) and is produced by epithelial cells of skin,
    the respiratory tract, and the gastrointestinal tract, as well as by monocytes/
    macrophages and other leukocytes; in healthy skin keratinocyte expression is low and
    is strongly induced by infection or barrier injury. [8, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review][10, mechanism_review]

### Vitamin-D regulation of CAMP

13. The human CAMP gene is a direct transcriptional target of the vitamin D receptor
    (VDR): a consensus vitamin-D response element (VDRE) in the CAMP promoter is bound by
    VDR (heterodimerized with RXR), and 1α,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 (1,25D3) strongly
    up-regulates CAMP/hCAP18 expression in myeloid cells. [5, mechanism_review]

14. The VDRE driving vitamin-D induction of CAMP arose from exaptation of an ancient
    primate-specific AluSx short interspersed element (SINE); consequently this
    vitamin-D → cathelicidin regulation is present in humans and other primates but is
    NOT conserved in mouse, rat, or dog, whose CAMP-orthologue promoters lack the VDRE.
    [6, mechanism_review]

15. In primary human immune cells, 1,25D3 induces CAMP most strongly in macrophages
    (reported up to ~615-fold increase in CAMP mRNA), with weaker induction in dendritic
    cells; induction in macrophages and epithelia by VDR/ligand is a human/primate
    feature. [15, in_vitro]

16. The vitamin-D–cathelicidin axis is physiologically coupled to innate immune
    activation: Toll-like receptor (e.g., TLR2/1) triggering of human macrophages
    up-regulates the VDR and the vitamin-D 1-hydroxylase (CYP27B1), leading to
    1,25D3-dependent induction of cathelicidin (CAMP/LL-37) and antimicrobial activity.
    [7, mechanism_review]

### Proteolytic stability / pharmacokinetics

17. LL-37 is susceptible to proteolytic degradation, which (together with salt
    sensitivity) is a recognized limitation; reviews list rapid enzymatic degradation,
    low proteolytic stability, and short plasma half-life among the chief obstacles to
    therapeutic use, motivating stabilizing modifications (D-amino-acid substitution,
    cyclization via disulfide bridges, terminal acetylation/amidation). [2, mechanism_review]
    [route-extrapolation: these limitations are framed for exogenous/systemic delivery,
    not endogenous peptide]

18. In vitro, synthetic LL-37 (10 µg/mL, 37 °C, 6 h) was completely degraded by the
    serine protease trypsin (≥10 ng/mL), whereas matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9)
    produced no degradation; LL-37 susceptibility to trypsin was markedly reduced in the
    presence of chronic venous-ulcer wound fluid (diluted 10–20%), where no apparent
    cleavage was seen (n = 9 ulcer fluid samples). [13, in_vitro]

19. Bacterial proteases contribute to LL-37 turnover at infection/wound sites: e.g.,
    Pseudomonas aeruginosa elastase degrades LL-37, which can limit the activity of
    exogenous LL-37 in colonized ulcers. [13, in_vitro]

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- Plasma half-life NUMBER unverifiable: A widely repeated "90–120 min plasma half-life"
  figure could not be traced to a citable primary/review source (appears only in vendor/
  aggregator copy). Not cited. Only qualitative "rapid degradation / short half-life"
  (review-supported) is stated. A quantified in vivo human PK half-life for native LL-37
  is, to this retrieval, NOT established in the primary literature — flagged as a genuine
  gap.
- No human in vivo pharmacokinetic study of administered LL-37 (Cmax/Tmax/clearance/
  bioavailability by any route) was located; PK statements are mechanistic/in vitro.
- Tag distribution is in_vitro + mechanism_review only — appropriate for an identity/
  chemistry/endogenous-biology section, but there are no rct/cohort/clinical-grade
  sources here (those would live in efficacy/clinical sections).
- [8] (PMC9368159) and [10] (Exp Dermatol) have PMIDs not independently re-confirmed via
  eutils in this pass; both tier-capped at 2 accordingly. Their factual claims are
  corroborated by Tier-1 sources ([1],[3],[5],[6],[9]).
- Net-charge values (+6 integer vs +5.9 computed at pH 7.4) are both reported; the
  difference is methodological (nominal vs pH-specific calculation), not a contradiction.
- LL-37 self-association/oligomerization state and detailed lipid-binding thermodynamics
  are touched only via the structure source [9] and are largely out of Section A scope
  (mechanism section territory).
