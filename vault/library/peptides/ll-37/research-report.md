# LL-37 (Human Cathelicidin · CAMP/hCAP18)

## Metadata

- **evidence_tier: C** — the LL-37 literature is enormous and decades deep, but it is dominated by **endogenous-biology, mechanism, in-vitro, and animal work**. > **Read this first — the honest evidence state.** LL-37 is the **37-residue C-terminal antimicrobial peptide** of human cathelicidin hCAP18 (gene *CAMP*; a direct, vitamin-D-inducible transcriptional target). It is genuinely **DUAL-NATURED**, and that is the defining fact about it. On one side it is a broad-spectrum antimicrobial, an LPS (endotoxin) neutralizer, a chemoattractant, and a wound-healing/angiogenic factor. On the other side it is a **documented driver of inflammatory and autoimmune DISEASE**: it is a bona fide **psoriasis T-cell autoantigen** and the founding **self-DNA/self-RNA → plasmacytoid-dendritic-cell → type-I-interferon** trigger implicated in **psoriasis, lupus/SLE, rosacea, and atherosclerosis**, and it has **context-dependent CANCER effects** (pro-tumor in ovarian/lung/breast; anti-tumor in colon/gastric). The same biochemistry — high cationic charge plus membrane/nucleic-acid affinity — drives BOTH the good and the bad outcomes. Human evidence for *exogenous administration* is **four small trials**: a positive Phase I/II venous-leg-ulcer study (Grönberg 2014, p=0.003) that **FAILED to replicate on the primary endpoint** of the larger Phase IIb HEAL trial (Mahlapuu 2021); a diabetic-foot-ulcer trial improving only granulation (Miranda 2023); and a 4-patient melanoma dose-finding study — **no Phase III, no approval.** The much larger *endogenous-LL-37-as-biomarker* observational literature is **SEPARATE** from administration evidence and must not be conflated with it. Lab provenance is **DIVERSE / low single-lab share** (≈0.15) — a *positive* signal, the opposite of a peptide whose evidence base is one small recycling group. Regulatory state: not FDA-approved; placed in 503A interim Category 2 (2023), then **REMOVED ~April 2026** as one of twelve peptides dropped from interim Category 2 in a parallel FDA 503A bulks action (FDA Category 2 page) — **removal ≠ approval/compoundable**; LL-37 is **NOT** on the July-2026 PCAC agenda established by FR Doc 2026-07361 (deferred to a separate PCAC by ~Feb 2027). WADA: not individually named, but prohibited at all times under category **S0**. Native LL-37 has a very short half-life (rapid protease degradation); a specific human PK half-life is **not established** (vendor "90–120 min" figures are unverified and not cited as a number). ## TL;DR

LL-37 is the human body's only cathelicidin — the 37-residue antimicrobial tail of the hCAP18 precursor encoded by *CAMP*, switched on by vitamin D. Marketed in the gray market as a clean "infection / biofilm / gut / immune" peptide, it is in reality the most Janus-faced molecule in this library. Its host-defense résumé is real and broad: it permeabilizes microbial membranes, sequesters LPS, recruits immune cells through FPRL1/FPR2, and promotes angiogenesis and wound re-epithelialization. But the *same* peptide is a central character in autoimmune and inflammatory disease — it converts inert self-DNA and self-RNA into potent type-I-interferon triggers in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, it is a T-cell autoantigen in roughly two-thirds of moderate-to-severe psoriasis patients, it drives lupus, rosacea, and atherosclerosis biology, and it promotes some cancers while suppressing others. The human evidence for *giving* LL-37 is thin and unimpressive: a small positive venous-leg-ulcer trial that did not confirm in the larger Phase IIb, a diabetic-foot-ulcer trial that improved granulation but not closure, and a 4-patient melanoma study with documented skin toxicity. There is no Phase III, no approval, and a thin human safety dataset sitting next to a *mechanistic* contraindication (LL-37 is the autoantigen of a common autoimmune skin disease). Provenance is a rare bright spot — the literature is genuinely multi-lab and multi-country, not single-group. Net: LL-37 is a **mechanistically fascinating, dual-natured, clinically unproven** experimental peptide whose dark side deserves equal billing with its host-defense side. Treat all human-use framing as off-label experimentation, and discuss with a clinician.

## 1. Identity, Chemistry, PK & Endogenous Biology

### 1.1 What LL-37 actually is

LL-37 is the **C-terminal antimicrobial peptide derived from hCAP18** (human cationic antimicrobial protein, ~18 kDa), the product of the *CAMP* gene; it is the **only cathelicidin found in humans**, and hCAP18 is the sole human cathelicidin precursor [1, in_vitro][2, mechanism_review][4, in_vitro]. It is a **37-amino-acid peptide** with the sequence **LLGDFFRKSKEKIGKEFKRIVQRIKDFLRNLVPRTES**; the name "LL-37" reflects its two N-terminal leucines and its 37-residue length, and it was originally described as the human peptide **FALL-39 / FALL39** [4, in_vitro][2, mechanism_review]. It carries a net positive charge of approximately **+6** at physiological pH and is a **cysteine-free, cationic** peptide [2, mechanism_review][1, in_vitro]; a related computed value of net charge ≈ **+5.9 at pH 7.4** is reported in structural work [5, in_vitro] (the difference is methodological — nominal integer vs pH-specific calculation — not a contradiction). Its molecular weight is approximately **4.49 kDa (~4493 g/mol)** [5, in_vitro][4, in_vitro].

### 1.2 The hCAP18 precursor and its processing

The hCAP18 pre-propeptide is organized as a **signal peptide (30 aa) + a conserved N-terminal cathelin-like domain (~103 aa) + the C-terminal LL-37 (37 aa)**; LL-37 corresponds to the C-terminal residues (134–170) of the hCAP18 precursor [4, in_vitro][6, in_vitro]. (A note on numbering conventions you will see in the literature: some sources number LL-37 as hCAP-18(104–140), counting from the *mature* protein after the 30-aa signal peptide is cleaved; both span exactly 37 residues and are valid conventions.)

hCAP18 is processed extracellularly to active LL-37 by **proteinase 3 (PR3)**, a serine protease; in neutrophils this occurs *after exocytosis*, when hCAP18 stored in specific (secondary) granules meets PR3 released from azurophil granules [6, in_vitro]. The original characterization of the human *FALL39/CAMP* gene showed the cathelin-containing precursor is processed to the antibacterial peptide LL-37 in granulocytes [4, in_vitro]. The processing protease differs by cell type: PR3 is **myeloid-restricted**, whereas in skin/epithelium the hCAP18 precursor is processed at the surface by the serine proteases **kallikrein 5** (stratum corneum tryptic enzyme) and **kallikrein 7** (stratum corneum chymotryptic enzyme), which generate LL-37 and shorter epidermal forms [7, mechanism_review]. This skin-processing detail is not academic — abnormal kallikrein-5 processing of cathelicidin is central to rosacea (§8).

### 1.3 Secondary structure

LL-37 is an **amphipathic, α-helical** peptide. The monomer is largely unstructured in dilute aqueous solution but adopts an α-helix under higher peptide concentration, elevated salt, or in the presence of lipids/detergents (e.g., SDS micelles) [2, mechanism_review][5, in_vitro]. By solution NMR in lipid micelles, LL-37 adopts a curved **helix-bend-helix** motif spanning approximately residues 2–31, with a bend near Gly14–Glu16, followed by a disordered C-terminal tail (PDB entry 2K6O) [5, in_vitro][8, in_vitro]. This amphipathicity is the structural basis for both its membrane-permeabilizing antimicrobial action and its self-association behavior.

### 1.4 Endogenous expression

hCAP18/LL-37 is expressed broadly: it is stored in the **specific (secondary) granules of neutrophils** (the largest reservoir) and is produced by epithelial cells of skin, the respiratory tract, and the gastrointestinal tract, as well as by monocytes/macrophages and other leukocytes. In healthy skin, keratinocyte expression is **low** and is strongly induced by infection or barrier injury [9, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review][7, mechanism_review]. This induction-on-demand pattern is important context: native LL-37 is normally deployed locally and transiently, which is a different exposure profile from any exogenous, sustained, or systemic administration.

### 1.5 The vitamin-D → cathelicidin axis (a human/primate feature)

The human *CAMP* gene is a **direct transcriptional target of the vitamin D receptor (VDR)**: a consensus vitamin-D response element (VDRE) in the *CAMP* promoter is bound by VDR (heterodimerized with RXR), and 1α,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 (1,25D3) strongly up-regulates *CAMP*/hCAP18 expression in myeloid cells [10, mechanism_review]. The VDRE arose from **exaptation of an ancient primate-specific AluSx short interspersed element (SINE)**; consequently this vitamin-D → cathelicidin regulation is present in humans and other primates but is **NOT conserved in mouse, rat, or dog** [11, mechanism_review]. This is a critical translational caveat: rodent cathelicidin (CRAMP, encoded by *Camp*) is not vitamin-D-inducible the way the human gene is, so a large class of "vitamin D boosts your antimicrobial peptide" inference does not transfer cleanly from mouse to human and, conversely, mouse cathelicidin-knockout work isolates the peptide's role without the human regulatory wiring.

In primary human immune cells, 1,25D3 induces *CAMP* most strongly in macrophages (reported up to **~615-fold** increase in *CAMP* mRNA), with weaker induction in dendritic cells [12, in_vitro]. The axis is physiologically coupled to innate activation: Toll-like-receptor (e.g., TLR2/1) triggering of human macrophages up-regulates the VDR and the vitamin-D 1-hydroxylase (CYP27B1), leading to 1,25D3-dependent induction of cathelicidin and antimicrobial activity [13, mechanism_review].

### 1.6 Proteolytic stability and pharmacokinetics — the short-half-life reality

LL-37 is **susceptible to proteolytic degradation**, which — together with salt sensitivity — is a recognized limitation. Reviews list rapid enzymatic degradation, low proteolytic stability, and short plasma half-life among the chief obstacles to therapeutic use, motivating stabilizing modifications (D-amino-acid substitution, disulfide cyclization, terminal acetylation/amidation) [2, mechanism_review] [route-extrapolation: these limitations are framed for exogenous/systemic delivery, not the endogenous peptide]. Concretely, in vitro, synthetic LL-37 (10 µg/mL, 37 °C, 6 h) was completely degraded by the serine protease **trypsin** (≥10 ng/mL), whereas matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) produced no degradation; LL-37 susceptibility to trypsin was markedly reduced in the presence of chronic venous-ulcer wound fluid (n=9 ulcer-fluid samples) [14, in_vitro]. Bacterial proteases also contribute to turnover: *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* elastase degrades LL-37, which can limit the activity of exogenous LL-37 in colonized ulcers [14, in_vitro].

**The honest PK gap.** A widely repeated **"90–120 min plasma half-life"** figure surfaced in search aggregations but **could not be traced to any verifiable primary or review source** — it appears only in vendor/aggregator text. It is therefore **NOT cited as a number** anywhere in this report. The retrievable, source-supported statements are qualitative: LL-37 undergoes rapid proteolytic degradation and has a short half-life. **No human in-vivo pharmacokinetic study of administered LL-37** (Cmax, Tmax, clearance, bioavailability by any route) was located; a quantified human PK half-life for native LL-37 is, to this retrieval, **not established in the primary literature.** This is a genuine gap, not an omission.

### 1.7 Coverage gaps (Section 1)

- **No human PK and no measured human half-life.** The "90–120 min" figure is vendor-only and is rejected, not cited. Only "rapid degradation / short half-life" (review-supported) is asserted.
- **Vitamin-D induction is human/primate-specific** — a major species caveat for any mouse-to-human inference about the vitamin-D → cathelicidin pathway.
- **Net-charge values (+6 vs +5.9 at pH 7.4)** are both reported; the difference is methodological, not a conflict.
- **Self-association/oligomerization thermodynamics** are touched only via the structural source; detailed lipid-binding parameters are mechanism-section territory (§2).

## 2. Mechanism of Action — The Dual Nature

**Bottom line up front:** LL-37's mechanisms run along **two opposing axes**, and the direction of effect is set by *context* — concentration, timing relative to other signals (especially LPS priming), tissue, post-translational state (citrullination), and binding partner (microbial membrane vs self-DNA/RNA vs tumor-cell receptor). The **same biochemical property — high cationic charge and membrane/nucleic-acid affinity — underlies BOTH the protective and the pathogenic outcomes.** This section marks each direction explicitly. The detailed disease consequences of the pathogenic axis are developed in §8.

### 2.1 PROTECTIVE — direct antimicrobial membrane disruption

LL-37 is **directly bactericidal by physically permeabilizing the microbial cytoplasmic membrane.** Its mechanism is a hybrid showing features of both the **toroidal-pore model** (peptide-lined transmembrane channels with induced positive curvature) and the **carpet model** (surface accumulation to a threshold, then membrane breakdown/lipid extraction) [15, mechanism_review]. Structurally, LL-37 **oligomerizes on membrane mimics**: a crystal structure obtained with detergents resolved a narrow **tetrameric channel** with a strongly charged core, and planar-lipid-membrane electrophysiology demonstrated a small but defined transmembrane conductance, with MD simulations showing a channel that is stable in membrane and passes water — direct structural/biophysical evidence that LL-37 forms defined channels [16, in_vitro]. It is **broad-spectrum**, with reported activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, certain fungi, and enveloped viruses; the cationic peptide is electrostatically drawn to the anionic surfaces (LPS, lipoteichoic acid, anionic phospholipids) that distinguish microbial from host membranes [15, mechanism_review]. (Direction: **PROTECTIVE.**)

### 2.2 PROTECTIVE / anti-inflammatory — LPS neutralization

LL-37 **binds and neutralizes LPS (endotoxin), blunting TLR4-driven inflammation.** In THP-1 human monocytic cells, LL-37 pretreatment (5–10 µg/mL) inhibited *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* LPS-induced IL-8 production by **~90%** and dampened NF-κB activation. Critically, the effect required LL-37 to remain present — removing peptide from the medium before LPS stimulation abolished inhibition — showing the mechanism is largely **extracellular LPS sequestration**, not an intracellular signaling block [17, in_vitro]. (Direction: **ANTI-inflammatory.**)

### 2.3 DUAL — immunomodulation and chemotaxis

LL-37 is a **chemoattractant** for human peripheral-blood neutrophils, monocytes, and T cells, acting through the **formyl-peptide-receptor-like 1 (FPRL1 / FPR2)** G-protein-coupled receptor and inducing calcium mobilization — recruiting innate and adaptive immune cells to sites of microbial invasion [18, in_vitro] (PRO-inflammatory recruitment).

But LL-37 exerts **BOTH** pro- and anti-inflammatory effects depending on microenvironment and timing. **ANTI-inflammatory:** it down-regulates TLR4 signaling by binding LPS (§2.2) and can drive anti-inflammatory mediators (e.g., IL-1RA). **PRO-inflammatory:** when macrophages/monocytes are **primed with LPS *before*** LL-37 exposure, LL-37 enhances TNF-α release and enables inflammasome activation with IL-1β/IL-18 production; and ~10 µg/mL LL-37 present during monocyte→macrophage differentiation down-regulates IL-10 and up-regulates IL-12p40, polarizing toward a pro-inflammatory **M1** phenotype. **The order of signals is the switch** [19, mechanism_review] (explicitly DUAL).

### 2.4 PROTECTIVE — wound healing, re-epithelialization, angiogenesis

LL-37 **promotes wound re-epithelialization.** In vitro it drives migration of human HaCaT keratinocytes (actin remodeling; increased tyrosine phosphorylation of focal-adhesion kinase and paxillin) via **transactivation of EGFR** and induction of the FPRL-1 receptor, engaging Snail/Slug, MMPs, and MAPK and PI3K/Akt signaling; in vivo, adenoviral delivery of LL-37 to excisional wounds in **ob/ob mice** significantly improved re-epithelialization and granulation-tissue formation [20, animal] (in_vitro + animal).

LL-37 is also **directly angiogenic.** Acting through FPRL1 on endothelial cells, LL-37 (~5 µg/pellet) significantly increased vessel formation in the chick chorioallantoic-membrane assay, increased collateral vessel growth in a rabbit hindlimb-ischemia model, and **CRAMP-deficient mice** (the murine LL-37 ortholog) showed significantly reduced wound revascularization [21, animal]. **The double-edge is built in:** this same FPRL1-driven angiogenesis machinery is co-opted by tumors (the ovarian pro-tumor mechanism, §2.6 / §8) — so the protective wound/angiogenesis axis and the pathogenic pro-tumor axis run on shared hardware.

### 2.5 PATHOGENIC — self-nucleic-acid carrier → type-I interferon (the founding "break-tolerance" event)

This is the defining dark-side mechanism. **In normal physiology, plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) do not respond to self-DNA.** LL-37 binds self-DNA released from dying cells into **condensed, aggregated, degradation-resistant complexes** that are delivered to endosomal **TLR9** in pDCs, triggering robust type-I interferon (IFN-α) production. This is the founding mechanism by which an antimicrobial peptide converts inert self-DNA into a potent autoimmune trigger, and it is central to psoriasis pathogenesis [22, mechanism] (human pDCs; psoriatic skin).

The carrier logic extends to **RNA and to systemic lupus (SLE).** LL-37 also protects self-RNA, enabling endosomal **TLR7** activation and IFN-α in pDCs. In SLE, neutrophil-extracellular-trap (NET)-derived DNA externalized with LL-37 forms immune complexes that stimulate pDC TLR9; LL-37 itself becomes a target of pathogenic autoantibodies, and anti-LL-37 antibodies can themselves induce NETs, creating a **self-amplifying inflammatory loop.** LL-37 also enhances dsDNA uptake by monocytes, enabling cytosolic STING/TBK1 sensing [19, mechanism_review] (HARMFUL; psoriasis + SLE). (Direction: **PATHOGENIC.**)

### 2.6 PATHOGENIC — pro-tumor signaling (ovarian and lung)

**Ovarian.** LL-37 (over-expressed in ovarian, breast, and lung cancers) promotes ovarian tumor progression by recruiting bone-marrow-derived multipotent mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs) through **FPRL1**; recruited MSCs secrete pro-angiogenic VEGF, IL-6, and active MMP-2. In human ovarian-tumor xenografts in **SCID mice**, neutralizing-LL-37 antibodies significantly reduced MSC engraftment and tumor weight (P<0.01) and disrupted the fibrovascular network [23, animal]. Note that this is the *same* FPRL1/angiogenesis machinery that is protective in wound healing (§2.4).

**Lung.** Myeloid-cell-derived LL-37 promotes lung-cancer growth by binding **TLR4** on tumor cells → Akt activation → GSK3β(Ser9) inactivation → β-catenin stabilization → Wnt/β-catenin (TCF/LEF) transcription. In patients, serum LL-37 was ~26-fold elevated in NSCLC vs healthy controls (7,312 ± 532 vs 275.6 ± 31 µg/L) and predicted worse survival; **Cramp⁻/⁻ mice** (LL-37 ortholog knockout) given Lewis lung carcinoma showed reduced mortality, fewer tumors, and lower proliferation markers (PCNA, Ki-67, cyclin D1) [24, animal] (HARMFUL). *(An erratum exists for this paper — Theranostics 2023;13(11):3640 — noted before relying on specific figure-level quantities.)*

The cancer story is genuinely bidirectional and is developed fully, with the anti-tumor (colon/gastric) direction, in §8.

### 2.7 Post-translational control of the dark side — citrullination

At inflammatory foci, **peptidyl-arginine deiminases (PAD2/PAD4 — highly active during NETosis)** convert LL-37's arginines to citrulline. Citrullination **abolishes LL-37's bactericidal activity** and alters its immunomodulatory/nucleic-acid-handling functions; a homoarginine-substituted, citrullination-RESISTANT variant retained antimicrobial potency even after PAD4 treatment or NET exposure [25, in_vitro]. This shows the **native vs modified state of the SAME peptide flips its functional output**, and it ties the dark side mechanistically to the NET-rich environments of autoimmune disease.

### 2.8 Amyloid — genuinely double-edged

LL-37's active core (residues 17–29) **self-assembles into thermostable, ribbon-like helical fibrils** that co-localize with bacteria — a functional supramolecular state implicated in antimicrobial action [26, in_vitro]. Conversely, full-length LL-37 can act as an **anti-amyloid chaperone**: it binds islet amyloid polypeptide (IAPP) with **nanomolar affinity** and effectively suppresses IAPP amyloid self-assembly and the associated pancreatic β-cell damage in vitro [27, in_vitro]. So LL-37 **BOTH self-aggregates AND inhibits the aggregation of disease amyloids**, depending on partner and context. (The status of LL-37's own self-assembly as "true cross-α amyloid" is an open question — see §8.)

### 2.9 Coverage gaps (Section 2)

- **Direction is context-dependent, not absolute.** The pro-/anti- labels reflect the dominant reported direction in each cited model; the LL-37 dual nature is the headline finding, not an artifact of source selection.
- **Mechanistic reviews ground qualitative model claims, not numbers** — every numeric value (90% IL-8 inhibition, 26-fold serum elevation, ~5 µg/pellet, ~10 µg/mL) traces to a primary paper.
- **Quantitative n / cell-line specifics are uneven** — several primaries' exact group sizes were not in the fetched abstracts; claims state model/cell-line where available and flag where n was not reported.
- **The amyloid-β (vs IAPP) anti-aggregation extension** is framed as plausible, not separately asserted as verified primary; the verified number is the nanomolar IAPP inhibition.

## 3. Human Clinical Evidence

**State this plainly: there IS real human evidence of administered LL-37 — but it is small, concentrated in topical wound healing, and largely unconfirmed.** Four trials have dosed humans with exogenous LL-37 (drug name *ropocamptide*): two venous-leg-ulcer (VLU) RCTs, one diabetic-foot-ulcer (DFU) RCT, and one melanoma dose-finding trial — totaling **212 randomized / 211 treated across all four trials (all arms, including placebo)** — the one-subject difference is the single Mahlapuu 2021 participant randomized but not treated (149 randomized / 148 treated) — of whom only **~150–160 were actually LL-37-exposed.** There is **no Phase III, no regulatory approval, no marketed product** [28, rct][29, rct][30, rct][31, open_label].

### 3.1 First-in-human VLU trial — positive at low dose (Grönberg 2014)

Grönberg et al. 2014 was a Phase I/II, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 3-center (Sweden) study: a 3-week open-label placebo run-in, then 4-week randomized treatment + 4-week follow-up; sponsor Pergamum/Promore Pharma; **n=34.** LL-37 was applied at 0.5 and 1.6 mg/mL vs 3.2 mg/mL vs placebo, twice weekly. Result: healing-rate constants ~**6-fold** higher than placebo at 0.5 mg/mL (**p=0.003**) and ~3-fold at 1.6 mg/mL (p=0.088); the highest dose (3.2 mg/mL) was **no better than placebo** — an inverted/bell-shaped dose response. No safety concerns were reported [28, rct] (PMID 25041740; DOI 10.1111/wrr.12211). The bell-shaped curve — efficacy lost at the top dose — is itself a signal of a narrow therapeutic window (§9).

### 3.2 Confirmatory Phase IIb VLU trial (HEAL) — MISSED its primary endpoint (Mahlapuu 2021)

Mahlapuu et al. 2021 ("HEAL") was the larger Phase IIb, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter (Sweden + Poland) study; sponsor Promore Pharma; **149 randomized / 148 treated;** arms LL-37 0.5, 1.6 mg/mL, placebo (1:1:1) twice weekly + compression. **The primary endpoint — confirmed complete wound closure at 13 weeks — was NEGATIVE:** closure 26.5% vs 24.7% vs 25.3%, no significant difference. A **post-hoc** subgroup of large ulcers (≥10 cm²) showed a 0.5-mg/mL benefit (healing rate 0.0367 vs 0.0093/day, p=0.0439; closure 28.1% vs 8.1%, p=0.0458) [29, rct] (PMID 34687253; DOI 10.1111/wrr.12977; EudraCT 2018-000536-10). A discrete NCT number was not located on ClinicalTrials.gov — the trial was registered via the EU EudraCT system.

**This is the single most important fact about LL-37's human efficacy:** the strongest result (Grönberg 2014, p=0.003) **did not replicate** on its primary endpoint in the larger confirmatory trial. The VLU story therefore rests on a positive small Phase I/II + a negative Phase IIb rescued only by a post-hoc subgroup. That is a genuine efficacy weakness, not a citation gap.

### 3.3 DFU trial — mixed (granulation yes, closure no) (Miranda 2023)

Miranda et al. 2023 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at Universitas Indonesia (Jakarta): 25 screened, randomized 13 LL-37 vs 12 placebo, all 25 analyzed; LL-37 cream 0.5 mg/g twice weekly × 4 weeks. Result: significantly greater **granulation-tissue index** at all timepoints (p≈0.031–0.037) but **no significant difference in wound-area reduction** (−2.35 vs −1.54 cm², p>0.05), and **no significant reduction in bacterial colonization or IL-1α/TNF-α** vs placebo [30, rct] (PMID 37480520; DOI 10.1007/s00403-023-02657-8; registry NCT04098562).

### 3.4 Melanoma dose-finding — only 4 patients, oncology efficacy NOT established

"Induction of Antitumor Response in Melanoma Patients Using the Antimicrobial Peptide LL37" (**NCT02225366**) was a Phase 1/2 study at MD Anderson: intratumoral LL37 weekly up to 8 weeks in cutaneous melanoma metastases; **enrollment 4 (actual)**; Completed (Jul 2015–Nov 2020); results posted Dec 2021. The primary outcome was **optimal biological dose / dose-limiting toxicity** — i.e., a safety/dosing study, not an efficacy study [31, open_label]. A secondary cancer-promotion review describes it as having "shown significant potency," but that is an **unquantified secondary characterization of an n=4 study** and must not be read as demonstrated clinical efficacy [32, meta_analysis]. A documented **harm signal** exists in this program: Dolkar et al. 2018 reported the dermatologic/clinicopathologic toxicity of intratumoral LL-37 melanoma therapy, confirming real adverse cutaneous reactions [33, anecdote_aggregate] (PMID 29665030). And mechanistically, LL-37 can also *promote* melanoma cell invasion and activate tumor-associated macrophages — a reason the tiny oncology dataset cannot be read as net-beneficial [32, meta_analysis].

### 3.5 The critical distinction — endogenous biomarker ≠ administration evidence

A **much larger, SEPARATE** literature measures *native* LL-37/hCAP18 levels and correlates them with disease (e.g., Krejner et al. 2016: serum LL-37 — not vitamin D — correlated with VLU healing, n=19 observational) [34, cohort]. Numerous ClinicalTrials.gov records (NCT04404335, NCT06867250, NCT03270709, NCT03369275, NCT02806414, NCT06863415, etc.) only **MEASURE** LL-37; they do not administer it. **These do not count as LL-37-administration efficacy evidence.** The biomarker literature is suggestive of LL-37's biological role but is not evidence that *giving* LL-37 is efficacious or safe; the two must never be conflated.

### 3.7 Bottom line on human evidence

Yes, exogenous LL-37 has been given to humans in four real trials (212 randomized / 211 treated across all arms — the 1-subject gap is the lone Mahlapuu 2021 randomized-but-untreated participant; only ~150–160 LL-37-exposed) — a positive first-in-human VLU study (Grönberg 2014, p=0.003) that **FAILED to confirm** on its primary endpoint in the larger Phase IIb HEAL trial (Mahlapuu 2021, rescued only by a post-hoc subgroup), a DFU trial showing better granulation but no wound-closure/antibacterial benefit (Miranda 2023), and a 4-patient melanoma dose-finding study with no established efficacy and documented skin toxicity. There is **genuine but weak, unconfirmed, non-approved** human efficacy evidence, concentrated in topical wound healing.

### 3.8 Coverage gaps (Section 3)

- **The strongest single result did NOT replicate** on its primary endpoint — the central efficacy weakness.
- **No NCT located for the pivotal Phase IIb** (HEAL/Mahlapuu 2021); it was registered in the EU EudraCT system. The 2014 trial predates registry norms (no NCT). Both are real, peer-reviewed, PMID-verified.
- **Oncology evidence is essentially a case series (n=4)** with documented dermatologic toxicity and pro-tumor mechanisms — exploratory at best.
- **No trials found for any other indication** (sepsis, respiratory, oral/periodontal, ophthalmic) involving *administered* LL-37 in humans — those appear only as biomarker measurements.
- **Endogenous-biomarker observational data is separate** and is not administration evidence.

## 5. Concentration / Lab-Provenance Audit (first-class)

This audit is carried **first-class** because single-lab concentration is one of the standard honesty screens for a peptide — but for LL-37 the finding is unusual and *positive*, so it is surfaced honestly rather than buried.

### 5.1 Headline finding — DIVERSE / LOW single-lab share

**Single-lab concentration is LOW.** LL-37 is an exceptionally broad, multi-decade, globally distributed literature with **no dominant in-vivo efficacy lab.** The estimated single-lab share of in-vivo efficacy primaries is **well under ~20%** (largest cluster ≈ **0.15**), far below the 70% concern threshold. The risk this audit screens for — a peptide whose "evidence base" is really one small group recycling itself — is **NOT present** for LL-37. The opposite is true: it is one of the most widely *independently* studied antimicrobial peptides in existence. This is a **positive provenance signal,** as expected for an endogenous human peptide studied worldwide since 1995.

### 5.2 Method

The efficacy-relevant primary literature was sampled across the main in-vivo claim domains (wound healing, skin/MRSA infection, gut/colitis, the venous-leg-ulcer clinical program), recording corresponding-author group + institution + country for each, and cross-checking against the discovery papers. Because LL-37 is an *endogenous human peptide* discovered in 1995, the literature is intrinsically distributed across immunology, dermatology, microbiology, and structural-biology communities worldwide; there is no single patent-holder lab gatekeeping the molecule.

### 5.3 Originators and independent groups

- **Originators (Sweden, Karolinska Institutet).** LL-37 was first characterized as "FALL-39" by **Agerberth, Gunne, Odeberg, Kogner, Boman & Gudmundsson (PNAS 1995)** [40, mechanism_review]. Gudmundsson and Agerberth's Karolinska group remain foundational, but their share is dominated by *discovery/expression/mechanism* work, not the bulk of downstream in-vivo efficacy studies.
- **Spain — CIEMAT, Madrid** (Carretero, Larcher, Del Río et al., *J Invest Dermatol* 2008): in-vivo wound-healing / re-epithelialization, adenoviral LL-37 transfer in ob/ob mice [20, animal].
- **Italy + Poland — Polytechnic University of Marche (Ancona) with University/Medical University of Gdańsk** (Simonetti, Cirioni, Offidani, W. Kamysz et al., *Antibiotics* 2021): MRSA wound-infection mouse model, topical/systemic LL-37 [41, animal].
- **USA / UCLA** cathelicidin-in-colitis line (Koon/Cheng/Pothoulakis groups) — a separate community from the Swedish and dermatology groups [42, animal].
- **Sweden — Promore Pharma / academic collaborators** (Grönberg 2014; Mahlapuu 2021) ran the *clinical* VLU program [28][29] — the one place a single sponsor concentrates, but it is the clinical (not preclinical) track, and the Phase IIb result was negative on the full population.

### 5.4 The largest cluster — and why it does not flag

The largest single cluster is the **Lande/Gilliet psoriasis–SLE mechanism program** (the founding self-DNA/pDC/type-I-IFN work and its SLE extensions). It accounts for roughly **5 of ~24** verifiable bibliography-anchored primaries (**share ≈ 0.15**), and a far smaller fraction of the whole multi-country LL-37 literature. Note the subtlety: this dominant cluster is the cluster establishing the **pathogenic** (autoimmunity) axis, not the efficacy axis — so even the single largest group in this literature is documenting LL-37's dark side, not over-selling its benefits.

### 5.5 Conclusion

Dominant *originator* lab = Gudmundsson/Agerberth (Karolinska). Dominant *single clinical sponsor* = Promore Pharma (Sweden), but only ~2 trials and the pivotal one was negative. Across the in-vivo efficacy primary literature as a whole, no single lab approaches even a plurality; the work spans Sweden, Spain, Italy, Poland, the USA, the UK, China, Japan, and more. **Estimated single-lab share: LOW / diverse (<~20%, largest cluster ≈0.15).** A positive provenance signal, not a flag.

### 5.6 Sourcing landscape (gray-market; purity/identity caveats)

- **No approved exogenous LL-37 product exists** — no licensed injectable, oral, or topical LL-37 therapeutic on the market.
- **Gray-market "research chemical" vials are the dominant supply.** LL-37 is widely sold as lyophilized powder, typically **2 mg, 5 mg, or 10 mg vials**, "≥98–99% purity (HPLC)," explicitly labeled **"research use only / not for human or veterinary use."** These labels are `vendor_label` — usable ONLY for purity/reconstitution/identity math, never for efficacy. The research-use-only disclaimer is legally load-bearing; these are not pharmaceuticals and carry no regulatory purity/sterility guarantee despite "third-party COA" marketing claims [43, vendor_label][44, vendor_label].
- **Identity / purity caveats.** LL-37 is a relatively **long (37-residue) synthetic peptide**; long peptides carry higher risk of truncation/deletion sequences, deamidation, and counter-ion/TFA salt content that inflates apparent mass. "99% purity" claims are not independently verifiable; a vendor "doctor sheet" PDF could not be parsed to confirm its specs and is treated as unverified [45, vendor_label]. Reconstitution conventions cited by vendors (add bacteriostatic water — e.g., 2 mL to a 5 mg vial → 2.5 mg/mL; refrigerate 2–8 °C after reconstitution; store lyophilized at −20 °C) are administration/handling info only [46, vendor_label].
- **Cosmetic channel.** "Cathelicidin"/LL-37 also appears in the topical cosmetic/"skincare peptide" space; this is a separate, lower-dose, non-injectable channel. No verified cosmetic regulatory approval for exogenous LL-37 as an active was located.

## 6. Why this lands at evidence_tier C

The grade is a deliberate, conservative call. LL-37 lands at **C** for converging reasons:

1. **Preclinical-dominant evidence base.** The overwhelming majority of LL-37 evidence is endogenous-biology, mechanism, in-vitro, and animal work. That body is large and high-quality, but it is not human-administration efficacy.
2. **Weak / negative human-administration evidence.** Only four trials have *given* LL-37 to humans; the strongest (Grönberg 2014) **did not confirm** on the primary endpoint of the larger Phase IIb (Mahlapuu 2021); the DFU trial helped only granulation; the oncology data are n=4. No Phase III, no approval (§3).
3. **The dual-nature risk.** Unlike most peptides graded C for *absence* of data, LL-37 carries *positive* evidence of harm: it is a psoriasis autoantigen and a type-I-IFN autoimmunity trigger, with context-dependent pro-tumor signaling (§8). This is a tier-relevant safety consideration, not merely a data gap.
4. **Thin human safety dataset.** No quantified drug-attributable AE rate exists; the human safety record is two small VLU RCTs plus an n=1 melanoma toxicity case report (§9).

What keeps LL-37 at **C** rather than lower is the genuine depth and cross-lab breadth of its biology, the diverse provenance (§5), and the existence of *some* real human-administration trials. What prevents anything higher is the weak/unconfirmed human efficacy plus the documented dark side. **C = mechanistically rich, clinically unproven, and double-edged.**

## 7. Preclinical Efficacy by Domain

All evidence in this section is **preclinical** (in-vitro + animal). Effect sizes, species + n, and route are given for every animal claim, and the in-vitro-to-in-vivo gap is flagged throughout — it is the central caveat for this whole section, because LL-37's direct antimicrobial potency is **partially neutralized in vivo by serum/HDL binding and by physiological salt and protein,** so in-vivo benefit often tracks immunomodulation as much as direct killing.

### 7.1 Antimicrobial / antibiofilm (in vitro)

- **S. aureus biofilm.** Against 24-h *Staphylococcus aureus* biofilms on cobalt-chromium discs (CDC biofilm reactor), LL-37 produced **>4 log CFU reduction at 10 µM** (sub-MIC), with **3.2 log reduction after only 5 min** exposure; the planar MIC was 32 µM. Gentamicin, vancomycin, rifampin, and silver nanoparticles each gave <1 log reduction at up to 1000 µM (gentamicin+rifampin = 1.71 log) — i.e., LL-37 was far more potent against established biofilm than conventional antibiotics in this model [47, in_vitro].
- **MDR *A. baumannii*.** Against multidrug-resistant *Acinetobacter baumannii* clinical isolates, LL-37 MIC ranged **16–32 µg/mL**, and it inhibited and dispersed biofilm at **32 µg/mL** (MBEC assay). Truncated fragments varied: KS-30 MIC 8–16 µg/mL (more potent per-mass than parent), KR-20 MIC 16–64 µg/mL [48, in_vitro].
- **Broad spectrum (review framing).** LL-37 displays broad-spectrum direct killing against Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria, fungi, and enveloped viruses via membrane permeabilization (used for qualitative breadth framing only; specific numbers trace to primaries) [49, mechanism_review].

**Unit-harmonization caution for the concentration audit:** in-vitro potency is reported in **µM** ([47]: MIC 32 µM) and **µg/mL** ([48]: MIC 16–32 µg/mL). At MW ≈ 4493 Da, 32 µM ≈ 144 µg/mL — so the *S. aureus* MIC (32 µM) is numerically much higher than the *A. baumannii* MIC (16–32 µg/mL ≈ 3.6–7.1 µM). Harmonize units before any cross-study potency comparison.

### 7.2 Antiviral (in vitro + one in-vivo study)

- **In-vivo influenza.** In female BALB/c mice (6–8 wk; n=5/group for survival/weight, n=3 for viral titer) infected intranasally with 10 MLD50 (~350 PFU) of A/Puerto Rico/8/34 (H1N1), **nebulized LL-37** (500 µg/mL in 200 µL, once daily day −1 to +7) reduced **lung virus titer ~70–80% at day 3** and improved survival to **~60% vs 0%** in scrambled-peptide/vehicle controls — protection of similar magnitude to zanamivir [50, animal] (species=mouse/BALB-c; n=5 survival, n=3 titer; route=nebulized/intranasal). *(Per the upstream integrity gate, the qualitative in-vivo influenza claim and the zanamivir comparison are abstract-verified; the exact ~70–80% / ~60% figures sit behind a paywall — read them as the study's reported magnitudes, not independently re-derived numbers.)*
- **Mechanism.** LL-37 inhibits influenza A primarily by **direct action on the virion** (pre-incubation of virus with peptide is required for optimal inhibition) and blocks replication at a **post-entry step before viral RNA/protein synthesis**; antiviral activity is **reduced by binding to serum HDL** — a translational caveat for systemic dosing [50, animal].

### 7.3 Wound healing (animal)

- **Diabetic wound (mechanism).** In diabetic mice with full-thickness dorsal wounds, LL-37 **accelerated wound closure** by activating **TFEB-dependent autophagy** (↑ nuclear TFEB; ↑ ATG5, ATG7, beclin-1), with faster closure kinetics vs vehicle [51, animal] (species=mouse, diabetic; n and exact closure % not extractable from abstract — flagged; route=topical/local).
- **MRSA-infected wound (efficacy + healing).** In 6-month-old BALB/c mice (80 animals, 8 groups, **n=10/group**) with full-thickness dorsal wounds inoculated with MRSA (*S. aureus* ATCC 43300, 5×10⁷ CFU/mL, 200 µL), **combined topical + daily intraperitoneal LL-37 (1 mg/kg each route)** reduced day-7 wound bacterial load from **7.8×10⁷ ± 1.4×10⁷ CFU/g to 6.9×10² ± 1.2×10² CFU/g (~5 log reduction; p<0.001)** and improved histologic healing (re-epithelialization 2.70±0.52, granulation 2.90±0.74, collagen organization 2.73±0.49) and angiogenesis (micro-vessel density 271.8±54.9 vessels/mm²; VEGF+ cells 493.5±68.2 cells/mm²), outperforming teicoplanin [52, animal] (species=mouse/BALB-c; n=10/group; route=topical + i.p.).

### 7.4 In-vivo anti-infective (sepsis, pneumonia, systemic infection)

- **Polymicrobial sepsis (CLP) — single-lab flag.** In mice (n=8–15/group) with cecal ligation and puncture, a **single intravenous LL-37 dose immediately after surgery** improved survival dose-dependently — reported survival of **14.3% (1 µg/mouse) and 36.4% (2 µg/mouse)** — while reducing bacterial burden and lowering IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α [53, animal] (species=mouse; n=8–15/group; route=IV). The mechanism (same group) is **inhibition of caspase-1 activation and pyroptosis of peritoneal macrophages** plus cytokine modulation [54, animal]. **⚠ Provenance flag:** refs [53] and [54] are the **same Nagaoka/Juntendo lab** — do NOT count them as independent replication; the CLP survival numbers (14.3%/36.4% are modest even at the benefit dose) rest substantially on one group, and the small per-mouse dose (1–2 µg) is unusual. Treat as promising, not robust.
- **Pseudomonas pneumonia.** In C57Bl/6 wild-type and Camp⁻/⁻ (cathelicidin-knockout) mice (n≥9 per condition at most timepoints), **intranasal human LL-37 (10 µg in 50 µL PBS)** significantly **enhanced clearance of *P. aeruginosa* PAO1** (3×10⁷ CFU intranasal) by 24 h, acting via an **early (~2-fold at 6 h) augmentation of neutrophil recruitment/function** independent of endogenous cathelicidin [55, animal] (species=mouse/C57Bl-6 + Camp⁻/⁻; n≥9; route=intranasal).
- **Systemic *A. baumannii*.** In a mouse model of systemic *A. baumannii* infection, recombinant LL-37 treatment was reported to result in **survival of all treated mice with no detectable blood bacteria**; in vitro the recombinant peptide gave complete kill at 1.5 µg/mL after 40 min. Small/early Tier-2 study; exact n and route not in the accessible abstract — flagged [56, animal] (species=mouse; n/route not stated).

### 7.5 The in-vitro→in-vivo gap (the central caveat)

LL-37's direct antimicrobial potency in clean buffer does not fully translate in vivo: it is partially neutralized by serum/HDL binding (documented for the antiviral arm [50]) and by physiological salt and protein. In-vivo benefit therefore often tracks **immunomodulation** (neutrophil/NET enhancement [55], anti-pyroptosis [54]) as much as direct killing. Cross-group support exists for the *direction* of several domains (in-vitro biofilm/MDR from two independent labs [47][48]; wound-healing from two independent groups [51][52]), but the sepsis-survival numbers are single-lab.

### 7.6 Coverage gaps (Section 7)

- **Sepsis evidence is single-lab** (Nagaoka/Juntendo, [53]+[54]); not independently replicated; survival modest; unusual small dose.
- **Diabetic-wound numbers are abstract-level only** (paywall); mechanism + direction documented, exact n/closure %/timepoints not verifiable.
- **Antiviral in vivo rests on one influenza study** [50]; broader antiviral breadth (RSV, etc.) is largely in vitro and not fully harvested.
- **The in-vitro→in-vivo gap is the section-wide caveat** — serum/HDL/salt attenuation is real.
- **No human RCT or regulatory source** belongs in this preclinical section; human-administration evidence lives in §3.
- **Engineered analogues** (KS-30/KR-20/KR-12, OP-145, SAAP-148) are only partially covered — a deliberate scope boundary.

## 8. The Pathogenic Side (first-class)

This section is carried **first-class** and given **equal weight** with the host-defense side, because LL-37's role as a *driver* of human disease is as well-established as its protective role — and is exactly what the gray-market "infection/immune support" framing omits. Each disease direction is marked explicitly.

### 8.1 Psoriasis — the best-established disease role (innate + adaptive)

**Innate.** In psoriatic skin, pDC **TLR9** is activated by LL-37 complexed with endogenous self-DNA released from dying cells; LL-37 binds and condenses otherwise-inert self-DNA into aggregated structures retained in early endosomes, converting it into a potent trigger of type-I interferon (IFN-α) — a **foundational mechanism of psoriasis initiation.** This pDC self-DNA–LL-37–TLR9–IFN-α axis is the originating "break-self-tolerance" event, and LL-37 is overexpressed in lesional psoriatic skin [22 / 57, mechanism_review/in_vitro]. LL-37 also binds **self-RNA**, protecting it and delivering it to endosomes: self-RNA–LL-37 complexes activate pDCs via **TLR7** (IFN-α) and additionally activate classical myeloid DCs via **TLR8** (TNF-α, IL-6, mDC maturation) [58, in_vitro]. NET-associated RNA (naRNA) complexed with LL-37 triggers **TLR8** (and murine TLR13)–mediated cytokine and further NET release by neutrophils, a **self-amplifying inflammatory cycle**; RNA is abundant in NETs and in psoriatic but not healthy skin (in vitro + in vivo mouse component, n not specified for the in-vivo arm) [59, in_vitro/animal]. (Direction: **PATHOGENIC.**)

**Adaptive.** LL-37 is a **bona fide T-cell autoantigen** in psoriasis: roughly **two-thirds** of patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis harbor CD4+ and/or CD8+ T cells specific for LL-37. LL-37-specific T cells produce IFN-γ (the CD4+ subset also Th17 cytokines), infiltrate lesional skin, can be tracked in blood by tetramer staining, and their **circulating frequency correlates significantly with disease activity** [60, cohort/in_vitro]. This is the single fact that makes exogenous LL-37 mechanistically concerning for anyone with or predisposed to psoriasis (§9).

### 8.2 Rosacea — abnormal cathelicidin processing is causal

Rosacea patients express abnormally high cathelicidin levels in facial skin, and — distinctively — the **proteolytically processed forms** of the peptide differ from those in normal skin, arising from a post-translational processing defect driven by increased epidermal serine protease (stratum corneum tryptic enzyme / **kallikrein-5**) activity cleaving hCAP-18 [61, in_vitro/animal]. These abnormal forms are **directly pathogenic**: injection of rosacea-type cathelicidin peptides — or of the protease — into mouse skin reproduces rosacea-like inflammation, establishing **causality**, not mere association [61, animal — mouse; experimental n not specified]. (Direction: **PATHOGENIC.**)

### 8.3 Lupus / SLE and other autoimmunity

In SLE, activated neutrophils release self-DNA–antimicrobial-peptide complexes as NETs; these LL-37 (and HNP)–self-DNA complexes are found in SLE patient sera and efficiently trigger pDC activation via **TLR9**, driving IFN-α — translating the psoriasis innate mechanism into a *systemic* autoimmune disease [62, cohort/in_vitro]. LL-37 is also an **autoantigen at the adaptive level** in SLE: native and citrullinated LL-37–specific T cells are present and provide help to B cells for autoantibody production (including anti-LL-37 and anti-dsDNA responses); ~45% of SLE patients have LL-37-reactive T cells correlating with anti-LL-37 antibodies and disease activity. Citrullination shapes the autoreactive response [63, cohort/in_vitro]. (Direction: **PATHOGENIC.**)

### 8.4 Atherosclerosis / cardiovascular

LL-37 is present in human atherosclerotic plaques: a **~6-fold increase** in hCAP-18/LL-37 transcript was found in human lesions vs normal arteries, expressed mainly by plaque macrophages and some endothelial cells [64, in_vitro/cohort]. Functionally it acts as an immune modulator in the vessel wall, inducing endothelial **ICAM-1** and **MCP-1 (CCL2)** and amplifying leukocyte recruitment [64, in_vitro]. It is mechanistically **pro-atherogenic via lipid handling**: it enhances macrophage LDL uptake through LDLR, SR-B1, and CD36, increasing cytosolic cholesterol. **Apoe-deficient mice transgenically expressing LL-37** (LL37tg/tg *Apoe*⁻/⁻) developed **larger atheroma** than control *Apoe*⁻/⁻ mice (en face: n=12 vs n=13; aortic sinus: n=11 vs n=13), without a difference in serum cholesterol; and in humans, plasma LL-37/hCAP-18 positively correlated with OxPL-apoB (an atherosclerosis predictor) in individuals with cardiovascular disease (n=20, P<0.01) [65, animal/cohort] (mouse n=11–13/group + human n=20). (Direction: **PATHOGENIC.**)

### 8.5 Cancer — genuinely context-dependent (BOTH directions)

LL-37's role in cancer is **bidirectional and tissue-specific**, governed largely by which membrane receptors a given tumor expresses (FPRL-1/FPR2, EGFR, ErbB2, IGF-1R vs apoptosis-linked GPCR/p53 pathways) — the same peptide can be a growth-promoting "tumor-derived growth factor" in some tissues and a pro-apoptotic tumor suppressor in others [66, mechanism_review].

- **PRO-TUMOR.** LL-37/hCAP-18 overexpression promotes **ovarian** (proliferation, invasion, angiogenesis, immune-cell recruitment; FPRL-1/TLR9), **lung** (a growth factor; EGFR phosphorylation, MEK/ERK), and **breast** (ErbB2/heregulin mitogenic signaling) cancers; pro-tumor effects are also reported in **prostate** (IGF-1R; Erk1/2, Akt) and **melanoma** [66, mechanism_review]. (The ovarian FPRL1/MSC and lung TLR4/Wnt mechanisms are detailed with their primary in-vivo data in §2.6.)
- **ANTI-TUMOR.** LL-37 (and its fragment FK-16) **suppresses colon cancer** via caspase-independent apoptosis (GPCR→p53→Bcl-2/Bax/Bak→AIF/EndoG) and autophagic cell death, and **suppresses gastric cancer** (cell-cycle arrest, apoptosis, proteasome inhibition / BMP-pathway activation, anti-angiogenic). Consistent with a tumor-suppressor role, hCAP-18/LL-37 expression is **down-regulated** in gastric adenocarcinoma and in colon-cancer tissue (strong in normal colon mucosa) [66, mechanism_review].

A consolidated cancer review covering both directions confirms the receptor-governed bidirectionality (pro-tumorigenic in ovarian/lung/breast/prostate/pancreatic, melanoma, skin SCC; anti-tumor in colon/gastric/hematologic/oral SCC) [67, mechanism_review]. **For exogenous LL-37 this is a genuine, unresolved safety consideration — especially for skin/melanoma exposure** (§9).

### 8.6 Amyloid — bidirectional and partly open

- LL-37 **INHIBITS** pathological amyloid: it binds **IAPP/amylin** with **nanomolar affinity** and suppresses its amyloid self-assembly and IAPP-related pancreatic β-cell toxicity in vitro — a *protective* role in the amyloid of type-2 diabetes [27 / 68, in_vitro]. A 2024 review synthesizes LL-37 as broadly **inhibitory** toward disease amyloids (Aβ42 fibril formation and associated neuronal toxicity; IAPP), and characterizes LL-37's own self-association as **amphipathic helical oligomers rather than classical amyloid** [69, mechanism_review]. (Direction: **ANTI-AMYLOID / protective.**)
- **The open question — LL-37's OWN self-assembly.** A verified Tier-1 primary (Engelberg & Landau 2020) shows the LL-37 active core, **LL-37(17–29), self-assembles into densely packed helical fibrils** (associating four-helix bundles) whose formation is causally linked to antibacterial activity by structure-guided mutagenesis (core mutation I24A abolishes both fibril formation and killing). Critically, that primary **explicitly reports the assembly *lacked* amyloid continuous cross-sheets and did *not* bind Thioflavin T** — in contrast to the cross-α amyloid fibrils of the bacterial PSMα3 peptide it otherwise resembles [26 / 70, in_vitro]. So the strongest verified primary characterizes LL-37 self-assembly as **functional helical fibrils, NOT bona fide cross-α amyloid** — concordant with the review framing [69]. The genuinely open question is narrow: whether *any* LL-37 form crosses into true amyloid, and whether AMP–amyloid cross-interactions are net-protective or net-harmful in vivo, remains unsettled. No verified Tier-1 source here establishes LL-37 *forming* cross-α amyloid.

### 8.7 Coverage gaps (Section 8)

- **Animal n underspecified** for the rosacea injection model [61] and the in-vivo naRNA arm [59]; only the atherosclerosis model gave explicit n (11–13/group). Flagged species=mouse, n=not specified.

## 9. Safety & Contraindications

**Headline:** LL-37's safety profile is *dual-natured* like everything else about it. Human safety data is **thin** (two small VLU RCTs + one n=1 melanoma toxicity case report), so AE *rates* in humans largely cannot be stated — only existence-of-AE and mechanistic/preclinical risk. And sitting alongside that thin dataset is a **mechanistic contraindication unique among peptides in this library:** LL-37 is the autoantigen and the type-I-IFN trigger of a common autoimmune skin disease.

### 9.1 Human trial safety (existence-of-safety, not AE rates)

- In the first-in-man Phase I/IIa VLU trial (n=34, 0.5/1.6/3.2 mg/mL vs placebo, topical twice-weekly), the authors reported **no safety concerns** regarding local or systemic adverse events and described treatment as "safe and well tolerated" — an **existence-of-safety** statement, not a quantified AE rate [28, rct]. The **highest dose (3.2 mg/mL) showed no benefit over placebo** while the two lower doses did — efficacy lost at the top dose, consistent with a **narrow therapeutic window** and concentration-dependent cellular toxicity at higher local exposure [28, rct].
- In the Phase IIb trial (149 randomized / 148 treated, 15 sites), **12 non-fatal serious adverse events occurred in 11 patients (7.4%)** — erysipelas (3), cellulitis, cardiac failure, MI, femur fracture, lower-limb fracture, increased creatinine, cerebrovascular accident, acute kidney injury — and **none were assessed as related to study drug** [29, rct]. *This 7.4% is a background SAE rate in an elderly chronic-wound population, NOT a drug-attributable AE rate.* Local reactions were **predominantly mild-to-moderate**; **severe reactions affected ≤5 patients (≤3.4%) per visit**, infection rates were low (2–6%), and no consistent treatment-group differences emerged in labs, vitals, or exams [29, rct].
- In the intratumoral melanoma program, **dermatologic toxicity is a documented adverse event:** a clinicopathologic case report (Dolkar 2018, n=1) detailed cutaneous toxicity from intratumoral LL-37, establishing that **local dermatologic adverse reactions occur** — existence, not a population rate [33, anecdote_aggregate/case-report].

### 9.2 Preclinical cytotoxicity / hemolysis (concentration-dependent membrane toxicity)

The same membrane-permeabilizing mechanism that gives antimicrobial activity damages host cells at sufficient concentration. Native LL-37 **induces hemolysis and is toxic to human leukocytes and T-lymphocytes** via hydrophobic interactions with eukaryotic membranes [71, in_vitro/mechanism_review]. Cytotoxicity is **concentration-dependent**: in human isolated platelets, **1–50 µM showed no toxicity, but 100 µM was significantly toxic** [72, in_vitro]. LL-37 is reported cytotoxic to many human cell types at **~1–10 µM**, and **very high concentrations (~300 µM) are detected in psoriatic lesional skin** — a benchmark for the pathologic-inflammatory range [73, mechanism_review].

### 9.3 The dual-nature mechanistic risks of EXOGENOUS administration (major for LL-37)

- **Autoimmunity / psoriasis-autoantigen risk (a mechanistic contraindication).** LL-37 is a **bona fide T-cell autoantigen in psoriasis** (two-thirds of moderate-to-severe plaque patients; IFN-γ + Th17; circulating frequency correlates with disease activity). **Exogenous LL-37 plausibly supplies the very autoantigen driving psoriatic autoimmunity** [74, mechanism_review/cohort].
- **Pro-inflammatory / type-I-IFN mechanism.** LL-37 complexes with self-DNA/RNA, enters pDCs, bypasses TLR9 self/non-self safeguards, and triggers IFN-α and a self-perpetuating cascade (keratinocyte IL-1/IL-36γ/chemokines, neutrophil recruitment, NET amplification) — the central driver of psoriatic innate inflammation and implicated in SLE and RA (elevated synovial LL-37) [75, mechanism_review]. LL-37 also enables keratinocytes to respond to TLR9 ligands (CpG/genomic DNA), greatly increasing type-I IFN at 0.1–10 µM (within the psoriatic physiologic range) — a keratinocyte-intrinsic amplification route [76, in_vitro].
- **Context-dependent pro-tumor risk.** LL-37 has dual, tissue-specific cancer effects (pro-tumor in ovarian/lung/breast/prostate/pancreatic, **melanoma**, skin SCC; anti-tumor in colon/gastric/hematologic/oral SCC), governed by tumor receptor expression (EGFR, FPR2, TRPV2, P2X7). For exogenous LL-37 this is a genuine, unresolved safety consideration, **especially for skin/melanoma exposure** [77, mechanism_review][67, mechanism_review].
- **Pro-thrombotic potential.** LL-37 (≤50 µM) **activates human platelets** (aggregation, fibrinogen binding, granule secretion, Ca²⁺ mobilization) via FPR2/ALX and **augments thrombus formation**, shortening bleeding time in mice; LL-37 is elevated in sepsis and psoriasis, linking it to thrombotic risk in inflammatory states [72, in_vitro+animal] (human platelets in vitro; mouse thrombosis in vivo).

### 9.4 Contraindications / monitoring / stopping (mechanistic — no approved label exists)

**Contraindications / caution (mechanistic inference, not label-derived):**
- **Psoriasis or psoriatic arthritis (and known LL-37 autoreactivity)** — LL-37 is the psoriasis T-cell autoantigen and innate-inflammation trigger; exogenous administration is **mechanistically contraindicated / flare-risk** [74, mechanism_review/cohort][75, mechanism_review].
- **Other type-I-IFN / autoimmune conditions (SLE, RA)** — LL-37/self-nucleic-acid complexes and NET amplification implicated; caution [75, mechanism_review].
- **Active or history of malignancy — particularly melanoma / skin SCC / breast / lung / ovarian / prostate / pancreatic** — context-dependent pro-tumor signal [77, mechanism_review][67, mechanism_review].
- **Pro-thrombotic states / antiplatelet considerations** — LL-37 activates platelets and augments thrombosis [72, in_vitro+animal].
- **High local concentration** — avoid; cytotoxic/hemolytic above ~50–100 µM and efficacy lost at the top tested topical dose (3.2 mg/mL) [28, rct][72, in_vitro][71, in_vitro].

**Objective monitoring (named assays; risk-floor inference, not a validated LL-37 protocol):**
- **hs-CRP** and inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α; IFN-α surrogate where available) — objective inflammation markers given LL-37's pro-inflammatory/IFN cascade ([[biomarkers/hs-crp]]) [75, mechanism_review]. These are general objective inflammation markers, **NOT validated surrogates of LL-37 activity**; no LL-37-specific validated biomarker of effect exists.
- **CBC / platelet-function awareness** and bleeding-time / thrombotic-symptom vigilance — given the platelet-activation/thrombosis signal [72, in_vitro+animal].
- **Skin examination** for psoriasiform/inflammatory eruptions at and beyond injection/application sites [33, case-report][74, mechanism_review/cohort].
- **Renal markers (creatinine)** — tracked in the Phase IIb safety labs [29, rct].

**Stopping rules (inferred from observed reversibility/toxicity):**
- Discontinue on new/worsening psoriasiform or inflammatory dermatologic reaction (the Dolkar case documents therapy-tied cutaneous toxicity) [33, case-report].
- Discontinue at concentrations producing local irritation; severe local reactions occurred in ≤3.4% per visit at trial doses [29, rct].

### 9.5 Coverage gaps (Section 9)

- **No quantified drug-attributable AE rate exists for LL-37** — the 7.4% Phase IIb figure is a background SAE rate in an elderly comorbid population, not an LL-37 AE rate.
- **Melanoma trial AE tables are not fully published** — the toxicity signal rests on an n=1 case report + a review; AE *rates* for the oncology program cannot be stated.
- **Hemolysis/cytotoxicity numbers are largely review-cited** — the strongest primary figure is the platelet 100 µM threshold; "~300 µM in psoriatic lesions" and "1–10 µM cytotoxic" come from reviews.
- **No approved label → contraindications/monitoring are mechanistic inferences**, not regulator-issued.

## 10. Pharmacokinetics, Dose, Route & Formulation

> **Critical framing:** there is **no published human PK or human dose-finding study** for LL-37, and the "90–120 min half-life" widely quoted is **vendor-only and rejected** (§1.6). Everything below that resembles a "dose" is **practitioner convention**, tagged `practitioner_protocol`, used for route/dose/cycle context ONLY — never as efficacy evidence.

### 10.1 What is actually known about PK and delivery

- **Rapid proteolytic degradation / short half-life** is the documented reality (trypsin, *P. aeruginosa* elastase; salt sensitivity), motivating stabilizing modifications [14, in_vitro][2, mechanism_review]. **No measured human PK half-life exists**; the "90–120 min" figure is untraceable to any primary/review and is not cited as a number.
- **Topical** is the only human-administered route with trial data — all four human trials used topical (VLU/DFU creams) or intratumoral (melanoma) routes, not systemic injection (§3).
- **In-vivo preclinical routes that worked** include nebulized/intranasal (influenza [50]), intranasal (pneumonia [55]), topical + i.p. (MRSA wound [52]), and IV (sepsis [53]) — but those are animal models, and direct antimicrobial potency is attenuated in vivo by serum/HDL/salt (§7.5).

### 10.2 Practitioner dosing conventions (tagged practitioner_protocol — NOT efficacy)

Aggregated across community peptide-dosing guides (these are convention, not evidence; several lack a verifiable author/venue/date and are recorded as convention-aggregate, not attributable protocols):
- **Subcutaneous injection, commonly 100–400 mcg/day** (some guides ~125 mcg/day; others 200–400 mcg/day), often cycled **~4 weeks on / 2 weeks off**, or **200 mcg/day for 5–10 days** during/after acute infection [46, vendor_label][78, practitioner_protocol]. Reconstitute lyophilized powder in bacteriostatic water; refrigerate after reconstitution.
- **Gut/biofilm convention:** some guides describe LL-37 ~100 mcg AM (sometimes stacked with BPC-157) for ~3–4-week "gut repair" cycles, taken fasted [78, practitioner_protocol].
- The dosing pages themselves concede "no scientific data supports the safety or effectiveness of such suggestions" [78, practitioner_protocol].
- **William A. Seeds, MD — *Peptide Protocols, Volume One* (self-published, 2020)** — the canonical practitioner reference — is VERIFIED to exist, but a specific LL-37 dose/cycle inside the Seeds text could **not** be verified from retrievable sources; per integrity rule no Seeds LL-37 protocol is fabricated [79, practitioner_protocol].

### 10.3 The autoimmunity caution consumer guides omit

The single most important thing the consumer dosing guides leave out is the **autoimmunity hazard.** LL-37 is itself a documented autoantigen (native and citrullinated forms drive autoreactive Th17/Tfh responses in psoriasis/psoriatic arthritis and SLE; LL-37/self-DNA-RNA complexes license pDC type-I-IFN autoreactivity) — so exogenous LL-37 in patients with, or predisposed to, autoimmune disease is a real theoretical hazard that "immune support" marketing largely ignores [74, mechanism_review/cohort][80, in_vitro/mechanism_review][63, in_vitro][75, mechanism_review].

### 10.4 Coverage gaps (Section 10)

- **No human PK and no human dose-finding** — the entire dosing landscape is convention.
- **The "90–120 min half-life" is vendor-only and rejected** — not a measured value.
- **The Seeds LL-37 dose could not be located** — not fabricated.
- **Practitioner conventions are SC-injection-centric**, whereas the only human *trial* data are topical/intratumoral — a route mismatch worth noting.

## 11. N=1 / Trial-Design Considerations (goal-agnostic)

This section is **goal-agnostic** — it does not assume any operator goal. It outlines what a rigorous self-experiment would have to contend with, given the evidence state above.

1. **The autoimmunity risk dominates the design.** Unlike most peptides, LL-37 carries a *positive* mechanistic hazard: it is the autoantigen and type-I-IFN trigger of psoriasis and is implicated in SLE/RA. Any N=1 must screen for personal/family autoimmune history first; psoriasis or psoriatic-arthritis history is a mechanistic contraindication (§9). This single consideration outranks every efficacy question.
2. **There is no human baseline to anchor on.** No measured human PK, no human dose-response for any route, no validated biomarker of LL-37 effect. The only human-administration data are topical/intratumoral; the practitioner convention is SC injection — a route with *no* human trial support. Any N=1 is exploratory by definition.
3. **The human efficacy signal is weak and unconfirmed.** The strongest result (Grönberg 2014) did not replicate in the larger Phase IIb; the DFU trial moved only granulation. There is no clinical efficacy literature against which to benchmark an N=1.
4. **Outcome measurement is hard.** For an inflammation/infection use case, objective markers — **hs-CRP** ([[biomarkers/hs-crp]]) and inflammation markers (IL-6, TNF-α) — are the only semi-credible readouts, and even those cannot distinguish an LL-37 effect from regression to the mean or placebo in an uncontrolled N=1. They are general inflammation assays, not LL-37-specific surrogates.
5. **Confounds and stacking.** LL-37 is marketed inside multi-peptide stacks; stacking destroys attribution. Isolation is mandatory to learn anything about LL-37 specifically.
6. **Safety floor dominates.** With a thin human safety dataset and a documented dark side, the conservative posture is to treat LL-37 as an experimental compound, keep cycles short, avoid use during active autoimmune disease or in anyone with relevant cancer history, watch for new psoriasiform/inflammatory skin reactions and thrombotic symptoms, and involve a clinician — consistent with `doctor_discussion_required: true`.
7. **Regulatory/sport reality.** LL-37 is not lawfully compoundable and is prohibited in sport under S0 (§4); for any athlete this is disqualifying regardless of the biology.

The honest summary: LL-37 is a mechanistically fascinating molecule to *think* about, but the absent human PK, the weak/unconfirmed efficacy, the route mismatch between convention and trial data, the absence of a validated readout, and — above all — the autoimmunity hazard make a clean, interpretable, *safe* N=1 very difficult.

## 12. Bottom Line — Supported vs Marketed vs the Dark Side

**What is genuinely supported (mostly preclinical / endogenous biology):**
- LL-37 is the human body's only cathelicidin — a vitamin-D-inducible, broad-spectrum host-defense peptide whose membrane-permeabilizing antimicrobial action, LPS neutralization, FPRL1 chemotaxis, and wound/angiogenesis roles are real and well-characterized.
- Its biology is deep, cross-lab, and multi-country — **diverse provenance (single-lab share ≈0.15)** is a genuine bright spot.
- Preclinical efficacy spans antimicrobial/antibiofilm, antiviral, wound-healing, and anti-infective domains — with cross-group support for several directions, but a real in-vitro→in-vivo gap (serum/HDL/salt attenuation) and a single-lab sepsis-survival dataset.

**What is marketed but NOT supported:**
- **Confirmed human efficacy for any indication.** Four small trials; the strongest (Grönberg 2014) **did not confirm** in the larger Phase IIb (Mahlapuu 2021); the DFU trial helped only granulation; oncology is n=4. No Phase III, no approval.
- **A clean "infection / biofilm / gut / immune support" profile.** The gray-market framing omits the dark side entirely.
- **The "90–120 min half-life" figure** — vendor-only, untraceable, rejected. Native LL-37 has a short but *unquantified* (in humans) half-life.
- **SC-injection dosing** — the practitioner convention has no human trial support; the only human trial routes are topical/intratumoral.

**The dark side (equal billing):**
- LL-37 is a **psoriasis T-cell autoantigen** (≈two-thirds of moderate-to-severe patients) and the founding **self-DNA/RNA → pDC → type-I-IFN** autoimmunity trigger implicated in **psoriasis, SLE, rosacea, and atherosclerosis.**
- It has **context-dependent cancer effects** — pro-tumor in ovarian/lung/breast (and prostate/pancreatic/melanoma/skin SCC), anti-tumor in colon/gastric — so exogenous exposure is a real, unresolved oncologic safety question, especially for skin/melanoma.
- It **activates platelets and augments thrombosis**, and is **hemolytic/cytotoxic at higher concentrations** (≥~50–100 µM).
- Giving exogenous LL-37 plausibly supplies the very autoantigen and IFN-α trigger that drives autoimmune disease — a **mechanistic contraindication** that consumer marketing ignores.

**Net:** LL-37 is a **mechanistically rich, genuinely dual-natured, clinically unproven** experimental peptide. Its host-defense résumé is real but its disease-driving résumé is equally real, and its human-administration efficacy is weak and unconfirmed. It is unapproved, not lawfully compoundable, and prohibited in sport. Treat all human-use framing as off-label experimentation with a documented dark side, weigh the autoimmunity and cancer-context hazards explicitly, and discuss with a clinician.
