# LL-37 (Cathelicidin) — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how LL-37 is actually prescribed, dosed, administered, and reconstituted in practice* — the dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions that circulate among peptide clinics, prescribers, and gray-market vendor channels. It is **not** an efficacy document and **must not** be read as one. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (3), and `vendor_label` (4) sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) purity/reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy claim, and none appears here for that purpose. The efficacy state (four small human administration trials — the strongest, Grönberg 2014, *failed to confirm* on the primary endpoint of the larger Phase IIb HEAL trial; DFU granulation-only; n=4 melanoma; no Phase III; no approval) lives in the LL-37 research report and is unaffected by anything below.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** As of mid-2026 LL-37/cathelicidin is **not an FDA-approved drug for any indication** and is **not on the FDA 503A authorized-bulks list**. It sat in 503A **interim Category 2** ("do not compound") and was **removed from interim Category 2 (~April 16–23, 2026)** as **one of twelve peptides removed in a parallel FDA 503A bulks action** — reflected on FDA's own Category 2 page and corroborated by legal advisories, NOT enumerated in FR Doc 2026-07361 [1, regulatory][16, regulatory]. **Removal from Category 2 is NOT approval and does NOT create a lawful compounding channel**; LL-37 remains outside enforcement discretion (which covers only Category 1) [1, regulatory][2, regulatory]. Unlike BPC-157/KPV/TB-500/MOTS-c (on the July 23–24, 2026 PCAC agenda set by FR Doc 2026-07361, which lists only those seven agenda peptides), **LL-37 is NOT on the July-2026 PCAC agenda — it is deferred to a separate PCAC convening before the end of February 2027**, batched with GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, Dihexa, and PEG-MGF [1, regulatory][2, regulatory]. So the "prescribing practice" described here is a real but **legally unsettled gray-market / telehealth / vendor-research practice, not an approved standard of care.** SC injectable LL-37 in particular is gray-market: no approved injectable LL-37 product exists.

## Dose/route conventions by channel (SC / topical)

LL-37 is used across **two practice channels**, and they are sharply different in unit convention and in evidence backing. The honest split: **topical** is the channel with *some* human-trial backing (the wound-healing program used topical mg/mL formulations); **SC injectable** — the dominant peptide-clinic channel, marketed for chronic infection/biofilm/gut/Lyme/immune — has **no injectable human dose-finding study at all**, so every SC microgram figure is an *anecdotal/community convention extrapolated from preclinical and topical work*, not an established dose [4, practitioner_protocol][5, practitioner_protocol].

**Subcutaneous (SC injectable — gray-market, the main peptide-clinic channel).** SC is the route clinics use for the *systemic* "chronic infection / biofilm / gut / Lyme / immune-support" framings. The recurring SC conventions (all microgram-scale; note the spread reflects the absence of any validated dose):
- **100–125 mcg/day** is the modal "standard" convention. Dick Brashier, MD (peptides.org dosage chart, 2024-01-18) gives **"125 mcg/daily, s.c."** [6, practitioner_protocol]; allaboutpeptides gives **"125 mcg daily subcutaneous injection,"** Weeks 1–4 active / Weeks 5–6 washout, in 4-week blocks [7, practitioner_protocol].
- **200 mcg SC 3×/week** — clinic case-example convention (Revolution Health & Wellness, used in a chronic-eczema case) [5, practitioner_protocol].
- **200–400 mcg daily** — Peptide Dojo dosing guide [9, practitioner_protocol].
- **Frequency band:** clinics quote anywhere from **daily, or 2–3×/week, or 3–5×/week** depending on the framing (infection control vs wound vs immune priming) [4, practitioner_protocol][5, practitioner_protocol][8, practitioner_protocol].
- **Cycle band:** **2–4 weeks on, then ≥2 weeks off** is the most-cited cycle; aggregators caution against continuous use ("avoid immune fatigue / overstimulation") [4, practitioner_protocol]. Some short courses (e.g., 5–10 days during acute illness) and some longer 4–12-week blocks are also quoted [4, practitioner_protocol][7, practitioner_protocol].
- **Upper band:** up to **500 mcg/day** appears in "advanced" aggregator protocols, explicitly flagged by those same pages as not for first-time use [4, practitioner_protocol].

**Topical (gel/cream/solution — the channel with trial backing).** Topical is used for localized wound / skin-infection framings. The convention traces directly to the human VLU trial formulations (read as *administration format*, not efficacy):
- **0.5 and 1.6 mg/mL** solution/gel, **applied twice weekly**, courses of **4–13 weeks** [6, practitioner_protocol][7, practitioner_protocol]. (These are the per-formulation concentrations used in the Grönberg/Mahlapuu VLU trials and the Miranda DFU cream — quoted here only as the topical *format/concentration convention*, with **no efficacy claim attached**; the trial outcomes live in the research report and were largely negative/mixed.)

> **There is no validated human dose-finding study behind any SC number here.** No published human Phase 2/3 dose-ranging trial of *injectable* LL-37 exists. Every SC figure is a *convention* extrapolated from preclinical protocols and clinic/community anecdote. The pages quoting these figures frequently self-disclaim ("no scientific data to support the safety or effectiveness of such suggestions" — Brashier/peptides.org) [6, practitioner_protocol].

## Safety caution (autoimmunity / psoriasis autoantigen — exogenous-administration flare risk)

> **⚠ MANDATORY SAFETY FLAG — the autoimmunity caution the consumer dosing guides omit.** LL-37 is not a neutral "immune-support" peptide. It is a **documented human autoantigen and a driver of inflammatory/autoimmune disease**, and exogenous administration carries a **real, mechanistically grounded autoimmunity/flare risk** that the SC dosing guides above almost universally fail to mention (Peptide Dojo, for example, frames LL-37 only as "supports immune function" with **no** inflammatory-disease warning [9]). Specifically:
>
> - **LL-37 is a bona fide T-cell autoantigen in psoriasis.** Roughly **two-thirds** of patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis harbor CD4+/CD8+ T cells specific for LL-37, and **circulating LL-37-specific T-cell frequency correlates with disease activity** [14, mechanism_review]. Giving exogenous LL-37 to a psoriasis patient (or someone genetically predisposed) is mechanistically adding fuel to the exact autoantigen their disease is built on.
> - **LL-37 is the founding "break-self-tolerance" trigger.** LL-37 complexes with self-DNA/self-RNA and converts otherwise-inert nucleic acids into potent **type-I-interferon (IFN-α)** triggers in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (TLR9/TLR7/TLR8), initiating a self-amplifying inflammatory loop implicated in **psoriasis, lupus/SLE, rosacea, and atherosclerosis** [15, mechanism_review].
> - **Practical caution:** the gray-market "infection / biofilm / gut / Lyme / immune-support" SC framing **omits this dual nature entirely.** Anyone with — or predisposed to — psoriasis, lupus, rosacea, or other type-I-IFN-driven autoimmune disease should regard exogenous LL-37 as **mechanistically contraindicated**, and the autoimmunity/flare risk should be disclosed to any clinician before use. This is a *positive evidence of potential harm*, not merely an absence of safety data.
>
> Additional documented liabilities (research-report sourced, carried here for the prescribing-practice reader): LL-37 induces **platelet activation / thrombus formation** in vitro/in vivo and is **hemolytic/cytotoxic** at higher concentrations; injection-site reactions and transient fatigue are the routinely-reported clinic-level effects [10, practitioner_protocol]. (No quantified drug-attributable AE rate exists for injectable LL-37 — the human safety dataset is the topical VLU trials plus an n=1 melanoma toxicity case report; see research report §9.)

## Reconstitution / formulation notes (purity/dilution arithmetic only)

Injectable LL-37 is sold/dispensed as a **lyophilized powder** (commonly **2 mg, 5 mg, or 10 mg "research-use-only" vials**, "≥98–99% purity (HPLC)") reconstituted with **bacteriostatic water** and stored refrigerated (2–8 °C) after reconstitution, lyophilized at −20 °C [13, vendor_label]. The arithmetic that recurs (admissible as reconstitution math only):

| Vial | + Bac. water | Concentration | Dose | Volume | On U-100 |
|------|--------------|---------------|------|--------|----------|
| 5 mg | 3 mL | 1,667 mcg/mL | 125 mcg | ~0.075 mL | ~7.5 U [7, practitioner_protocol] |
| 5 mg | 2 mL | 2,500 mcg/mL | 125 mcg | 0.05 mL | 5 U [13, vendor_label] |
| 5 mg | 2 mL | 2,500 mcg/mL | 200 mcg | 0.08 mL | 8 U [9, practitioner_protocol] |

Convention note: a **lower concentration → larger draw volume → better precision** for microgram-level dosing on a U-100 insulin syringe. **Identity/purity caveat:** LL-37 is a **long (37-residue) synthetic peptide**; long peptides carry elevated risk of truncation/deletion sequences, deamidation, and TFA counter-ion content that inflates apparent mass — "99% purity" vendor claims are not independently verifiable, and these vials are explicitly **"research use only / not for human or veterinary use"** with no regulatory sterility/purity guarantee. Topicals are compounded to a **concentration (e.g., 0.5–1.6 mg/mL)** rather than a per-dose figure. Research-reagent suppliers specify reconstitution in sterile H₂O at **≥100 µg/mL**, 4 °C for 2–7 days / below −18 °C long-term, with a 0.1% HSA/BSA carrier for long storage [13, vendor_label]. All of this is route/strength bookkeeping only and carries **no efficacy or safety implication.**
