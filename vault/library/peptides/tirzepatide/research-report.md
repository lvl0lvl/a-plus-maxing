# Tirzepatide

## Metadata
- **class:** peptide — a **dual GIP + GLP-1 receptor agonist** ("twincretin"); a single engineered 39-amino-acid linear peptide (not a co-formulation) that activates both incretin receptors [1].
- **brands / indications:** **Mounjaro** (type 2 diabetes) and **Zepbound** (chronic weight management; moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in obesity) — the same molecule, marketed by **Eli Lilly** under two trade names with separately approved labels [26][29]. Administered by **once-weekly subcutaneous injection**.
- **evidence_tier: A** — the efficacy/safety base is built on **multiple Phase-3 randomized controlled trials** (the SURPASS glycemic program and the SURMOUNT obesity program), an **approved outcome trial in a hard objective endpoint** (SURMOUNT-OSA, apnea-hypopnea index), a **dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial** (SURPASS-CVOT), and **FDA + EMA marketing approval** [1-trial][2][9-osa][13][15][26]. The single material caveat is **sponsor concentration**: essentially every pivotal trial is funded by Eli Lilly (~95% of the pivotal-primary evidence), with no independent large randomized outcomes trial yet (§4).
- **risk_tier: medium** — tirzepatide is approved and unusually well-characterized, but it carries **real adverse effects** (dominant gastrointestinal intolerance, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, gastroparesis/peri-operative aspiration), **predictable weight regain on discontinuation** (SURMOUNT-4, ~+14%), **lean-mass loss** (~25% of weight lost), and a **rodent-based boxed warning** for thyroid C-cell tumors — keeping it firmly doctor-required despite its approval status.
- **status:** researching
- **last_verified:** 2026-06-20

> **Read this first.** Tirzepatide is, on the evidence, a **genuinely approved and genuinely (very) efficacious dual GIP + GLP-1 receptor agonist** — and that dual mechanism is what distinguishes it from semaglutide, which is GLP-1-only [1][5]. The real benefits are large and well-documented, but each must be read against its **population**, because the magnitudes differ substantially: in **type 2 diabetes** the SURPASS program produced HbA1c reductions of roughly 2.0–2.6%, and in the head-to-head **SURPASS-2** trial tirzepatide was statistically **superior to semaglutide 1 mg** [2]; in **non-diabetic obesity**, SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff 2022) produced a mean **~20.9% body-weight reduction at 15 mg** — larger than the figures reported for semaglutide, though the cross-drug obesity comparison is partly indirect [1-trial]; **obstructive sleep apnea is an APPROVED indication** on the strength of SURMOUNT-OSA (Malhotra 2024), which reduced the apnea-hypopnea index by roughly 20–24 events/hr more than placebo and made tirzepatide the first-ever FDA-approved drug for OSA [9-osa][28]; and on cardiovascular safety, **SURPASS-CVOT was non-inferior** to dulaglutide on hard MACE — that is a **safety** result, **not** a superiority/risk-reduction label [13]. The real caveats are equally concrete: **gastrointestinal adverse events dominate** and drive most discontinuations; **weight is largely REGAINED on discontinuation** (SURMOUNT-4: +14% regained after switching to placebo) because this is a chronic, maintenance-dependent therapy [9]; some of the loss is **lean (fat-free) mass**, roughly proportional to the (much larger) total loss at about a quarter of weight lost [24]; gallbladder disease and pancreatitis are labeled concerns; delayed gastric emptying raises a **peri-operative aspiration** concern; and the **thyroid C-cell / medullary-thyroid-carcinoma boxed warning is RODENT-based — the label itself says the human relevance "has not been determined," so it is NOT a demonstrated human carcinogenicity finding** [20]. Annotate the population whenever you read a number — type 2 diabetes, non-diabetic obesity, OSA, and HFpEF are distinct settings with distinct effect sizes and distinct approval status. Regulatory: FDA-approved as **Mounjaro (May 2022)**, **Zepbound (Nov 2023)**, and **Zepbound for OSA (Dec 2024)** [25][26][27]; EMA-approved [29]. In sport, tirzepatide is **NOT prohibited** by WADA — but from 1 January 2026 it sits on WADA's **Monitoring Program**, which is surveillance, not a ban [37]. The investigational frontier (HFpEF, MASH, the SURMOUNT-MMO morbidity/mortality trial) is promising but **not approved**, and is covered in the sibling layer files of this wiki along with full prescribing detail, the compounding/shortage history, and the non-English-literature survey. ## TL;DR

Tirzepatide is the first approved member of the dual-incretin ("twincretin") class — one synthetic 39-residue peptide that activates both the GIP receptor and the GLP-1 receptor, which is precisely the feature that separates it from GLP-1-only agents like semaglutide [1][5]. Its efficacy is among the largest documented for any approved metabolic drug, but the evidence comes from **two distinct development programs that must not be conflated**: SURPASS (glycemia, in type 2 diabetes) and SURMOUNT (weight, including a flagship trial in obesity **without** diabetes). In diabetes, HbA1c fell by roughly 2.0–2.6% across SURPASS, and in the pivotal head-to-head SURPASS-2 trial all three doses were noninferior **and statistically superior** to semaglutide 1 mg [2]. In non-diabetic obesity, SURMOUNT-1 delivered a mean ~20.9% body-weight reduction at 15 mg over 72 weeks; that magnitude attenuates to ~14.7% in obesity **with** type 2 diabetes (SURMOUNT-2) — a population effect, not a dosing difference [1-trial][7]. Tirzepatide is also the **first drug ever approved for obstructive sleep apnea** (Zepbound, December 2024), on the strength of SURMOUNT-OSA's large reductions in the apnea-hypopnea index [9-osa][27]. On hard cardiovascular outcomes, SURPASS-CVOT met **non-inferiority but not superiority** versus dulaglutide — establishing CV safety, not a CV-benefit label [13]. The honest liabilities: gastrointestinal events dominate the adverse-effect profile and drive discontinuation; stopping the drug predictably reverses most of the benefit (SURMOUNT-4 showed +14% regain on placebo) because obesity is a chronic relapsing condition requiring ongoing treatment; about a quarter of the weight lost is lean mass; gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, and a peri-operative aspiration risk are labeled concerns; and the boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors is derived from rats, with human relevance explicitly undetermined. One structural caveat colors the whole evidence base: it is **essentially single-sponsor** — Eli Lilly funded essentially every pivotal trial, with no independent large randomized outcomes replication to date. Net: tirzepatide is a high-evidence, high-efficacy approved therapy with a real, manageable, doctor-supervised risk profile — **evidence_tier A, risk_tier medium**.

## 1. Identity, Structure & Mechanism

### 1.1 What tirzepatide is

Tirzepatide (development code LY3298176) is a single synthetic peptide that acts as a **dual agonist at both the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR) and the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R)** — the "twincretin" mechanism [1][5]. This is its defining and distinguishing feature: it is mechanistically separate from semaglutide and other GLP-1-only agonists precisely because of the added GIP-receptor arm [5]. It is **not a co-formulation of two drugs** but one engineered molecule that engages both incretin receptors [1].

### 1.2 Primary structure and chemistry

Tirzepatide is a **39-amino-acid linear peptide** [1] with a molecular weight of approximately **4.8 kDa** [5]. Sequence-homology analysis places it at roughly 67% homology with native GIP and roughly 50% with GLP-1 — i.e., the backbone is closer to the GIP sequence [5]. The peptide is **acylated at the lysine at position 20 (Lys20)** with a **C20 fatty diacid** (eicosanedioic diacid) attached through a hydrophilic linker built from L-γ-glutamic acid and two units of an AEEA/PEG-type spacer [1]. To resist enzymatic degradation it incorporates a non-natural **2-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib) substitution** in the N-terminal region (reported at position 8), the standard medicinal-chemistry device for blocking dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) cleavage that otherwise rapidly inactivates native GIP and GLP-1 [5].

The structural basis of dual receptor engagement was resolved by **cryo-electron microscopy** of the active-state complexes [1]. At the GIPR (3.1 Å), tirzepatide adopts an α-helical conformation with its N-terminus reaching deep into the transmembrane core, closely mimicking native GIP (GIPR/GIP, 3.2 Å). At the GLP-1R (2.9 Å), it engages the receptor in a **distinct manner** — weaker N-terminal interactions and reduced stabilization of extracellular loop 3 — and this altered geometry underlies its biased signaling at that receptor [1].

### 1.3 Imbalanced and biased agonism

A key pharmacological nuance is that tirzepatide is **not a balanced dual agonist**. In radioligand binding it has affinity at the GIPR **comparable to native GIP**, but at the GLP-1R roughly **5-fold lower affinity than native GLP-1**; in cAMP-potency assays it is **equipotent with native GIP at the GIPR but ~20-fold less potent than native GLP-1 at the GLP-1R** [4]. It is therefore described as an **imbalanced** agonist favoring GIPR over GLP-1R activity [4]. Layered on top, at the GLP-1R it shows **signaling bias** — a monophasic cAMP response but only weak β-arrestin recruitment (<10% of maximal) and reduced receptor internalization (<40% of maximum) — i.e., bias toward the G-protein/cAMP pathway over β-arrestin [4]. The proposed functional implication is that reduced β-arrestin-mediated desensitization at the GLP-1R may sustain the insulinotropic signal [4]. Notably, semaglutide is itself a biased GLP-1R agonist favoring cAMP over β-arrestin, so the qualitative GLP-1R bias is not unique to tirzepatide — **what is unique is the added GIPR arm** [5].

### 1.4 Incretin physiology and mechanism of action

The two targets are the body's two principal incretin hormones. **GIP** is secreted by enteroendocrine K cells of the duodenum and jejunum, and **GLP-1** by L cells of the distal gut (ileum and colon) [5]. Postprandially, GIP circulates at molar concentrations roughly 3–4 times higher than GLP-1 [5]. Together they produce the **incretin effect** — the augmentation of insulin secretion when glucose is taken orally versus intravenously — which accounts for about 50–70% of the postprandial insulin response in healthy people but is reduced to ~20–30% in type 2 diabetes [5]. A defining safety-relevant property is that incretin action is **glucose-dependent**: GLP-1 stimulates insulin in the presence of high glucose but not at normal glucose, which limits hypoglycemia risk [5].

By co-activating both receptors, tirzepatide recapitulates and amplifies this physiological program. The principal effects, enumerated:
1. **Glucose-dependent insulin secretion** — both GIPR and GLP-1R signaling on pancreatic β cells enhance insulin release in a glucose-dependent fashion, raising insulin only when glucose is elevated [4][5].
2. **Glucagon modulation** — GLP-1 exerts an indirect glucagonostatic effect during hyperglycemia (via somatostatin-mediated paracrine signaling) but not during hypoglycemia, while GIP is glucagonotropic during hypoglycemia; the net effect is glucose-context-dependent glucagon control [5].
3. **Slowed gastric emptying** — mediated through the GLP-1 arm, blunting postprandial glucose and lipid surges; native GIP lacks this effect [5]. (This is the same property that underlies the peri-operative aspiration consideration in §5.5.)
4. **Central satiety and reduced food intake** — brain GLP-1 receptors are necessary for the weight-lowering effect (CNS-neuron GLP-1R knockout mice lose the weight effect of liraglutide while retaining glucose benefit), and GIP appears to potentiate this central anorectic effect, e.g., by enhancing hypothalamic POMC expression [5].
5. **Adipose / lipid handling via GIP** — GIP directly stimulates lipogenesis (up-regulating lipoprotein lipase and promoting triglyceride clearance into adipocytes), and pharmacological GIPR activity is proposed to contribute to body-weight reduction through a mechanism distinct from the permissive physiological role of GIP in adipose synthesis [5].

### 1.5 Pharmacokinetics

The C20 fatty-diacid chain drives the PK profile: reversible non-covalent **albumin binding** slows clearance and protects against DPP-4 degradation, producing an **elimination half-life of approximately 5 days (~5.4 days)** [3][1]. Absorption after subcutaneous injection is slow (Tmax 8–72 h), with mean absolute bioavailability ~80% [3]. Once-weekly dosing yields an accumulation ratio of ~1.7-fold with steady state at roughly four weeks [3]. This ~5-day half-life is what enables the **once-weekly subcutaneous regimen** [3][1].

### 1.6 Why dual agonism may exceed GLP-1-only

Several lines of evidence support an additive-to-synergistic advantage of dual agonism over GLP-1 monotherapy:
1. **Synergistic intracellular signaling** — in cultured pancreatic β cells, tirzepatide elicited a cAMP response significantly greater than that of GLP-1 or GIP alone, consistent with additive/synergistic receptor activation [5].
2. **Preclinical additivity in vivo** — co-administering a long-acting GIP-receptor agonist with a GLP-1-receptor agonist synergistically enhanced glucose-lowering and weight reduction, and central (intracerebroventricular) co-delivery of GIP and GLP-1 additively enhanced weight loss in mice [5].
3. **Clinical magnitude versus semaglutide** — the review cites placebo-corrected HbA1c reduction of about 2.11% for tirzepatide versus under 1.5% for leading GLP-1 monotherapies, and ~17.8% placebo-corrected weight loss versus ~12.4% for semaglutide in the cited comparisons [5].

The contrast with semaglutide crystallizes the rationale: semaglutide is a **GLP-1-receptor agonist only**, achieving roughly 1.5% glycemic reduction and ~12.4% weight loss in the cited obesity data, whereas tirzepatide adds GIPR engagement on top of GLP-1R activity, and the GIP component is the proposed source of its larger metabolic and weight effects [5]. The dual molecule's "imbalanced" tilt toward GIPR — equal-to-GIP affinity but weaker-than-GLP-1 GLP-1R engagement — combined with cAMP-biased GLP-1R signaling, defines a pharmacology that is **qualitatively different from any GLP-1 monoagonist** [4][1].

### 1.7 Coverage gaps (Section 1)
- **Mechanism-to-magnitude is partly inferential** — the additive/synergistic advantage of the GIP arm is supported by in-vitro and preclinical data plus indirect clinical comparison; isolating the GIP contribution in humans is not directly resolved in the corpus.
- The Aib position is reported "at position 8 in one sequence comparison" [5] — a single-source structural detail.
- Population-PK parameters [3] are from a sponsor-adjacent pharmacometric analysis; no independent PK replication is carried.

## 2. Glycemic & Weight Efficacy

Two distinct Phase-3 programs underpin the evidence and **must not be conflated**: **SURPASS** studied glycemic control in adults **with type 2 diabetes (T2D)**, while **SURMOUNT** studied weight reduction, including a flagship trial in adults **with obesity but WITHOUT diabetes**. Magnitudes differ substantially between these populations at the same dose, so every figure below is **annotated by population**.

### 2.1 Glycemic efficacy — the SURPASS program (T2D)

The SURPASS Phase-3 program comprises five landmark RCTs: **SURPASS-1** (monotherapy vs placebo), **SURPASS-2** (vs semaglutide 1 mg), **SURPASS-3** (vs insulin degludec), **SURPASS-4** (vs insulin glargine in high-CV-risk T2D), and **SURPASS-5** (add-on to titrated insulin glargine vs placebo). All tested the three approved maintenance doses — 5, 10, and 15 mg once weekly — with dose-dependent HbA1c reductions.

- **SURPASS-1 (Rosenstock 2021, Lancet).** 478 drug-naïve T2D adults; from a mean baseline HbA1c 7.9%, HbA1c fell 1.87%/1.89%/2.07% at 5/10/15 mg vs +0.04% placebo at week 40 (treatment differences −1.91% to −2.11%); weight loss 7.0–9.5 kg [10].
- **SURPASS-2 (Frías 2021, NEJM)** — the pivotal head-to-head. 1,879 T2D adults on metformin (baseline HbA1c 8.28%) randomized to tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg or **subcutaneous semaglutide 1 mg** over 40 weeks. HbA1c fell −2.01%/−2.24%/−2.30% (tirzepatide) vs **−1.86% (semaglutide 1 mg)**; **all three tirzepatide doses were noninferior AND statistically SUPERIOR to semaglutide 1 mg** (15 mg estimated treatment difference ≈ −0.45%). Weight reductions were also greater at every dose (15 mg: −12.4 kg, ≈13.1%) [2]. **Interpretation caveat:** the comparator was the **1 mg** dose of semaglutide, the highest then-approved for T2D; later higher doses (2.0 mg T2D; 2.4 mg obesity) reached market, so SURPASS-2 establishes superiority over **semaglutide 1 mg specifically**, not over all semaglutide regimens [2].
- **SURPASS-3 (Ludvik 2021, Lancet)** — superior HbA1c and weight loss vs once-daily insulin degludec (which produced weight gain) [11].
- **SURPASS-4 (Del Prato 2021, Lancet)** — 1,995 high-CV-risk T2D adults vs titrated insulin glargine over 52 weeks; HbA1c −2.43% (10 mg) and −2.58% (15 mg) vs **−1.44% glargine**; less hypoglycemia (6–9% vs 19%); CV-event hazard ratio vs glargine 0.74 (95% CI 0.51–1.08), reassuring against harm [12].
- **SURPASS-5 (Dahl 2022, JAMA)** — large incremental HbA1c reductions as add-on to titrated insulin glargine vs placebo [14].

Taken together, the SURPASS program shows tirzepatide producing HbA1c reductions on the order of **~2.0–2.6%** from baselines near 8%, with a substantial fraction of participants reaching HbA1c below 7% and many below 5.7% (the non-diabetic range) at the higher doses — a magnitude that consistently exceeded active comparators including semaglutide 1 mg, insulin degludec, and insulin glargine. Across the program, body-weight loss was an additional consistent benefit (unlike the two insulin comparators, which produce weight gain), and hypoglycemia was generally less frequent than with insulin-based comparators, reflecting tirzepatide's glucose-dependent insulinotropic mechanism (§1.4).

### 2.2 Weight efficacy — the SURMOUNT program

The SURMOUNT Phase-3 program comprises **SURMOUNT-1** (obesity without diabetes), **SURMOUNT-2** (obesity with T2D), **SURMOUNT-3** (after an intensive-lifestyle lead-in), and **SURMOUNT-4** (maintenance / randomized withdrawal).

- **SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff 2022, NEJM)** — the flagship and the source of the headline magnitude. 2,539 adults with **obesity but WITHOUT diabetes** (mean baseline BMI 38.0) randomized to tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg or placebo for 72 weeks. Mean percent body-weight change was **−15.0% (5 mg), −19.5% (10 mg), and −20.9% (15 mg)** vs **−3.1% placebo**. ≥5% loss was achieved by 85–91% across doses (vs 35% placebo), and **≥20% loss by 50% (10 mg) and 57% (15 mg)** vs 3% placebo. This **~20.9% mean reduction at 15 mg in adults without diabetes** is the defining efficacy fact for the obesity indication [1-trial].
- **SURMOUNT-2 (Garvey 2023, Lancet)** — the critical population contrast. In 938 adults with **obesity AND T2D**, mean weight change at 72 weeks was **−12.8% (10 mg) and −14.7% (15 mg)** vs −3.2% placebo. The attenuation versus SURMOUNT-1 (≈14.7% vs ≈20.9% at 15 mg) reflects the well-established blunting of incretin-based weight loss in T2D — a **population effect, not a dosing difference** [7].
- **SURMOUNT-3 (Wadden 2023, Nat Med)** — after a 12-week intensive-lifestyle lead-in (all lost ≥5%), 579 were randomized; tirzepatide produced a **further −18.4%** vs **+2.5%** placebo (difference −20.8 pts), showing substantial pharmacologic loss on top of lifestyle [8].
- **SURMOUNT-4 (Aronne 2024, JAMA)** — durability/withdrawal. After a 36-week open-label lead-in (mean −20.9% lost), 670 were re-randomized to continue tirzepatide or switch to placebo for 52 weeks. Those **continuing lost a further −5.5%**, whereas those **switched to placebo REGAINED +14.0%** (between-group difference −19.4 pts). By week 88, 89.5% of continued-treatment recipients maintained ≥80% of peak loss vs only 16.6% of those switched to placebo [9].

### 2.3 Durability, dose-response, and the semaglutide comparison

Efficacy is **dose-dependent** (5 < 10 < 15 mg) for both HbA1c and weight, with 15 mg delivering the largest effects. SURMOUNT-4 establishes that the weight benefit is **maintenance-dependent**: discontinuation leads to substantial regain [9]. On the semaglutide comparison: **within** the same trial (SURPASS-2, T2D), tirzepatide produced greater weight loss than semaglutide 1 mg at every dose [2]. **Across** trials, tirzepatide's ~20.9% in non-diabetic obesity (SURMOUNT-1) [1-trial] is numerically larger than the ~14.9% reported for semaglutide 2.4 mg in the analogous STEP-1 trial — but this is a **between-trial indirect comparison** (different protocols, populations, semaglutide doses) and should be read as suggestive, not head-to-head proof. The direct obesity comparison (SURMOUNT-5, tirzepatide vs semaglutide 2.4 mg) is addressed elsewhere in this wiki rather than asserted here.

**Bottom line:** HbA1c reductions of ~2.0–2.6% in T2D (superior to semaglutide 1 mg, insulin degludec, insulin glargine) and mean weight loss reaching ~20.9% at 15 mg in non-diabetic obesity, attenuated to ~14.7% in T2D-obesity, durable while treatment continues and substantially reversible on withdrawal.

### 2.4 Coverage gaps (Section 2)
- The cross-drug obesity comparison vs semaglutide 2.4 mg is **indirect** here; the head-to-head (SURMOUNT-5) is deferred to another wiki entry and not adjudicated in this corpus.
- All SURPASS/SURMOUNT trials are Lilly-sponsored (see §4); no independent glycemic/weight RCT replication is carried.
- SURPASS-3 and SURPASS-5 numeric magnitudes are summarized rather than fully tabulated in the corpus.

## 3. OSA, Cardiovascular, HFpEF & MASH Outcomes

Indication status is labeled explicitly throughout: **OSA is APPROVED** (FDA December 2024, Zepbound); **HFpEF, MASH, and morbidity/mortality are INVESTIGATIONAL** for tirzepatide.

### 3.1 Obstructive sleep apnea — APPROVED (the approval-defining trial)

**SURMOUNT-OSA (Malhotra 2024, NEJM)** is the pivotal program supporting the **December 2024 FDA approval of Zepbound for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity — the first drug ever approved for OSA** [9-osa]. It was two parallel Phase-3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in adults with **moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity**, distinguished by background therapy: **Trial 1** enrolled participants **not using positive airway pressure (PAP)**; **Trial 2** enrolled those **established on PAP**. Both used 1:1 randomization to tirzepatide (titrated to 10 or 15 mg weekly) vs placebo over **52 weeks** [9-osa].

Primary endpoint = change in apnea-hypopnea index (AHI, events/hr):
- **Trial 1 (no PAP):** AHI −25.3 (tirzepatide) vs −5.3 (placebo) — **treatment difference −20.0 events/hr (95% CI −25.8 to −14.2)** [9-osa].
- **Trial 2 (on PAP):** AHI −29.3 vs −5.5 — **treatment difference −23.8 events/hr (95% CI −29.6 to −17.9)** [9-osa].

Both were statistically significant, with significant accompanying weight reduction. A companion summary appeared in *Annals of Internal Medicine* (Cheskin 2024), reinforcing the AHI reduction [10-osa]. **Defining fact:** in obese adults with moderate-to-severe OSA, tirzepatide 10–15 mg weekly over 52 weeks reduced AHI by roughly **20–24 events/hr more than placebo** (≈55–63% relative reduction), consistent whether or not patients used PAP — the basis for the December 2024 approval.

### 3.2 Cardiovascular outcomes — non-inferior, NOT superior

**SURPASS-CVOT (Nicholls 2025, NEJM)** is the dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial — a large randomized, double-blind, **active-comparator non-inferiority** trial in **adults with T2D and established atherosclerotic CV disease**. **N = 13,299** randomized, comparing tirzepatide against **dulaglutide 1.5 mg** weekly (an agent already shown to have CV benefit — a stringent test) [13]. The primary 3-point MACE (CV death, MI, or stroke) occurred in **801/6,586 (12.2%)** on tirzepatide vs **862/6,579 (13.1%)** on dulaglutide — **hazard ratio 0.92 (95.3% CI 0.83–1.01)**, meeting prespecified **non-inferiority (P=0.003) but NOT superiority (P=0.09)** [13]. Secondary analyses favored tirzepatide on metabolic, renal, and all-cause-mortality measures (~16% relative reduction vs dulaglutide), plus greater reductions in weight, blood pressure, and lipids [13].

**Interpretation:** SURPASS-CVOT establishes **CV safety** in high-risk T2D (non-inferior to an agent with proven CV benefit) but does **not** demonstrate CV superiority on hard MACE. Tirzepatide is **not labeled with a dedicated CV risk-reduction indication** on the basis of MACE superiority [13].

### 3.3 HFpEF — INVESTIGATIONAL

**SUMMIT (Packer 2025, NEJM)** randomized **731 patients** (364 tirzepatide, 367 placebo) with **HFpEF (ejection fraction ≥50%) and obesity (BMI ≥30)** to tirzepatide (titrated up to 15 mg weekly) or placebo, minimum 52 weeks with median follow-up ~104 weeks [15]. The primary composite of **CV death or worsening heart-failure event** occurred in **36 (9.9%)** vs **56 (15.3%)** — **HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.41–0.95; P=0.026)** — and the benefit was driven by worsening-HF events (29 [8.0%] vs 52 [14.2%]; HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.34–0.85); CV death was rare and not reduced (8 [2.2%] vs 5 [1.4%]; HR 1.58, 95% CI 0.52–4.83 — a wide, uninformative CI) [15]. The co-primary KCCQ-CSS health-status score improved by 19.5±1.2 vs 12.7±1.3, a between-group difference of **6.9 points (95% CI 3.3–10.6; P<0.001)** [15]. Mechanistic substudies (Borlaug 2025, *Nat Med*; Kramer 2025, *JACC*) reported reduced circulatory overload, end-organ damage, LV mass, and paracardiac adipose tissue [17-summit]. **HFpEF is INVESTIGATIONAL — tirzepatide is not approved for heart failure.**

### 3.4 MASH/NASH — INVESTIGATIONAL

**SYNERGY-NASH (Loomba 2024, NEJM)** is a **Phase 2b** trial in adults with biopsy-confirmed MASH and stage F2/F3 fibrosis (N = 190) [18]. Resolution of MASH without worsening fibrosis: placebo 10%; 5 mg 44%; 10 mg 56%; 15 mg 62% (all P<0.001 vs placebo). Fibrosis improvement favored drug (51–55% vs 30%) but with CIs reaching near zero [18]. As a Phase 2b trial this is **hypothesis-generating**; tirzepatide is **INVESTIGATIONAL** for MASH and not approved for any liver indication.

### 3.5 Morbidity/mortality — ONGOING, INVESTIGATIONAL

**SURMOUNT-MMO (NCT05556512)** is the event-driven Phase-3 morbidity/mortality trial in **adults with obesity, without diabetes**, with or at risk of CVD (actual enrollment **15,374**; active, not recruiting; estimated primary completion **October 2027**) [19-mmo]. Primary endpoint = time to first occurrence of a composite of all-cause death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke, coronary revascularization, or HF events. This is the **key future test** of whether tirzepatide's metabolic effects translate to hard outcomes in obesity without diabetes — results pending.

### 3.6 Coverage gaps (Section 3)
- **SURMOUNT-MMO is unreported** — the decisive hard-outcomes question in non-diabetic obesity is unanswered until ~2027.
- SUMMIT CV-death and SYNERGY-NASH fibrosis estimates have **wide/uninformative CIs**; treat as directional.
- All five outcome trials are Lilly-sponsored (§4); independent corroboration is indirect (companion reports of the same trials), not separate replication.

## 4. Concentration / Sponsor Audit

**Every pivotal tirzepatide trial cited in this report is sponsored by Eli Lilly and Company**, the manufacturer (Mounjaro/Zepbound). This applies across the SURPASS glycemic program [2][10][11][12][14], the SURMOUNT obesity program [1-trial][7][8][9], SURMOUNT-OSA [9-osa], SURPASS-CVOT [13], SUMMIT [15], SYNERGY-NASH [18], and the ongoing SURMOUNT-MMO [19-mmo] — on the order of **~95% of the pivotal-primary evidence**. There is essentially **no independent (non-manufacturer-funded) large randomized outcomes trial** of tirzepatide; this is a **single-sponsor evidence base**, a notable concentration risk.

**Mitigating factors, reported honestly:**
- The pivotal trials were published in **tier-1, fully peer-reviewed journals** (NEJM, Lancet, JAMA, with substudies in *Nature Medicine*, *JACC*, *Annals of Internal Medicine*) and were registered, randomized, double-blind, placebo- or active-controlled designs with adjudicated endpoints — the highest-quality design class.
- SURPASS-CVOT is an **active-comparator** trial against dulaglutide — a stringent design — but the comparator is **also a Lilly product**, so it does not remove sponsor concentration.
- The OSA effect (objective AHI by polysomnography) and the MASH effect (central biopsy reads) rely on **objective, externally-adjudicated endpoints**, partially de-risking interpretation.
- Independent corroboration exists only **indirectly** — the *Annals of Internal Medicine* OSA summary [10-osa] and the SUMMIT mechanistic substudies [17-summit] are reports of the **same sponsor-run trials**, not independent replications.

**Bottom line:** tirzepatide's evidence is strong by design quality and effect size but is **entirely manufacturer-sponsored**; no independent pivotal RCT corroboration currently exists. Readers should weight the single-sponsor concentration accordingly, especially for the not-yet-reported SURMOUNT-MMO.

- No independent large RCT exists to triangulate the sponsor-run effect sizes.

## 5. Safety & Adverse Effects

Tirzepatide's safety profile is unusually well-characterized: a large RCT base (SURPASS/SUMMIT for diabetes/HF, SURMOUNT for obesity), two current FDA labels, and an incretin-class signal shared with GLP-1 receptor agonists. The dominant practical issue is **gastrointestinal tolerability**; the most consequential label item — the **boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors — is rodent-based, with human relevance not established**.

### 5.1 Gastrointestinal adverse effects (dominant; main discontinuation driver)

GI events are by far the most common adverse reactions and the principal reason patients stop the drug. They are dose-related and concentrate during dose escalation, which is why both labels mandate slow titration (start 2.5 mg, increase in 2.5-mg steps no faster than every 4 weeks) [20][21].

In pooled placebo-controlled **diabetes** trials (Mounjaro label), incidence at 5/10/15 mg vs placebo: nausea 12%/15%/18% (placebo 4%); diarrhea 12%/13%/17% (9%); decreased appetite 5%/10%/11% (1%); vomiting 5%/5%/9% (2%); constipation 6%/6%/7% (1%); dyspepsia 8%/8%/5% (3%); abdominal pain 6%/5%/5% (4%) [20]. Events are more frequent in the higher-dose **obesity** program (Zepbound): nausea 25%/29%/28% (placebo 8%), diarrhea 19%/21%/23% (8%), vomiting 8%/11%/13% (2%), constipation 17%/14%/11% (5%) [21].

Most GI events are mild-to-moderate and transient. In SURMOUNT-4's maintenance phase the most common treatment-emergent events were GI (diarrhea 10.7% vs 4.8%; nausea 8.1% vs 2.7%; vomiting 5.7% vs 1.2%, tirzepatide vs placebo), "most" mild-to-moderate [9]. GI intolerance is the leading cause of discontinuation: in diabetes trials 3.0%/5.4%/6.6% (5/10/15 mg) vs 0.4% placebo [20]; in obesity trials 1.9%/3.3%/4.3% vs 0.5% placebo [21]. Both labels carry a **Severe Gastrointestinal Adverse Reactions** warning; Zepbound is not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis [21].

### 5.2 Weight regain on discontinuation (state honestly)

Tirzepatide is a **chronic therapy; stopping it reverses most of the weight benefit**. The pivotal evidence is **SURMOUNT-4 (Aronne 2024)** [9]: after a 36-week open-label lead-in on maximum tolerated dose, 670 adults were randomized to continue tirzepatide or switch to placebo for 52 weeks. Those switched to placebo **regained a mean of 14.0%** of body weight, while those continuing lost an additional 5.5% (between-group difference −19.4%; 95% CI −21.2% to −17.7%; P<.001) [9]. Over the full study (week 0–88), total reduction was 25.3% with continued treatment vs 9.9% with placebo, and 89.5% of continued-treatment recipients maintained ≥80% of their loss vs only 16.6% of those switched to placebo [9]. A post hoc SURMOUNT-4 analysis (JAMA Internal Medicine 2025) found that regain was accompanied by **reversal of cardiometabolic improvements** (waist circumference, lipids, fasting insulin), reinforcing that benefits are maintenance-dependent [23]. Patients should be counseled up front that discontinuation predictably leads to substantial regain.

### 5.3 Lean / muscle-mass loss with weight loss

As with any large weight reduction, some loss is lean (fat-free) mass. In the **SURMOUNT-1 body-composition substudy (Look 2025)**, DXA at week 72 showed tirzepatide reduced fat mass by 33.9% (vs 8.2% placebo) and lean mass by 10.9% (vs 2.6% placebo) [24]. Importantly, the **proportion** of weight lost as lean mass was about **26% (74% fat)** with tirzepatide — comparable to placebo's 25%/75% — and the fat-to-lean ratio improved more on drug; the authors concluded tirzepatide "does not induce disproportionate lean mass loss compared with other established obesity treatment modalities" [24]. **Honest framing:** absolute lean-mass loss is real and not trivial, but it is roughly **proportional** to the much larger total weight loss rather than selectively muscle-wasting. Resistance exercise and adequate protein are reasonable mitigations, though tirzepatide-specific outcome data on them are limited (§8).

### 5.4 Gallbladder disease and pancreatitis

Both are labeled concerns shared across the incretin class. The labels carry an **Acute Gallbladder Disease** warning; in pooled Zepbound trials cholecystitis occurred in 0.7% of tirzepatide-treated vs 0.2% of placebo patients [21]. **Acute Pancreatitis** is also a labeled warning; in the same pooled obesity trials pancreatitis incidence was low and comparable between groups (~0.2%; about 0.14 vs 0.15 per 100 patient-years) [21]. Patients with prior pancreatitis were **excluded** from trials, so a true class signal cannot be fully excluded; clinicians should discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected [21].

### 5.5 Gastroparesis / ileus and peri-operative aspiration

Tirzepatide delays gastric emptying (part of its mechanism), raising a peri-operative concern: retained gastric contents despite standard fasting and a risk of pulmonary aspiration under sedation/anesthesia. The 2024 **multi-society perioperative guidance (Kindel 2024**, endorsed by ASA, AGA, ASMBS, SAGES, IPSOPC) moved away from the ASA's original 2023 blanket-hold position toward **shared decision-making**: most low-risk patients can continue, with mitigations including a ≥24-hour preoperative clear-liquid diet, point-of-care gastric ultrasound when retained contents are suspected, and consideration of rapid-sequence induction [25-periop]. If holding is chosen, the suggested interval is **one week prior** for weekly formulations (tirzepatide is weekly) [25-periop]. Reported aspiration rates remain low (~0.1–0.2%) even though retained gastric contents are markedly more common [25-periop].

### 5.6 Thyroid C-cell tumors / MTC — BOXED WARNING (rodent-based; not demonstrated in humans)

Both Mounjaro and Zepbound carry a **boxed warning** for thyroid C-cell tumors, and the precise framing matters. The label states verbatim: "Tirzepatide causes thyroid C-cell tumors in rats. **It is unknown** whether MOUNJARO causes thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), in humans **as the human relevance of tirzepatide-induced rodent thyroid C-cell tumors has not been determined**" [20]. In a 2-year rat study tirzepatide produced a dose- and duration-dependent increase in C-cell adenomas and carcinomas at clinically relevant exposures [20]. **There is no demonstrated causal link to MTC in humans**; the warning is a precautionary, rodent-derived class label (shared with GLP-1 agonists), not evidence of human carcinogenicity. Accordingly it is **contraindicated** in patients with a personal/family history of MTC or MEN 2, and patients should be counseled on thyroid-tumor symptoms (neck mass, dysphagia, dyspnea, persistent hoarseness). Routine serum calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound monitoring is of **uncertain value** and not clearly established by the label [20].

### 5.7 Hypoglycemia, injection-site, hypersensitivity, renal, retinopathy, other

**Hypoglycemia** is mainly a risk when tirzepatide is combined with insulin or a secretagogue (sulfonylurea); tirzepatide alone has low intrinsic hypoglycemia risk. In a diabetes population (Zepbound Study 2), clinically significant hypoglycemia (<54 mg/dL) occurred in 4.2% vs 1.3% placebo, rising to 10.3% with concurrent sulfonylurea vs 2.1% without; the label advises reducing the concomitant secretagogue/insulin dose [21]. **Hypersensitivity** (including rare anaphylaxis/angioedema) is a labeled warning, and serious hypersensitivity is a contraindication [21]. **Injection-site reactions** occurred in ~6–8% in obesity trials (vs 2% placebo) [21]. **Acute kidney injury** can occur secondary to volume depletion from GI fluid loss — maintain hydration [21]. **Diabetic retinopathy complications** carry a warning in patients with a history of retinopathy (rapid glycemic improvement) [21]. Other common obesity-trial reactions include fatigue (~5–7%), eructation, GERD, and hair loss/alopecia (~4–5% vs 1% placebo) [21]. The Zepbound label also references rare postmarketing **suicidal-ideation/behavior** reports across the GLP-1 class, advising monitoring, though causality is not established [21].

### 5.8 Contraindications, monitoring, and stopping criteria

**Contraindications** (both labels): personal/family history of MTC or MEN 2; known serious hypersensitivity to tirzepatide or excipients [20][21]. Tirzepatide is **not recommended in pregnancy** (animal reproductive toxicity; no benefit of weight loss in pregnancy) — discontinue when pregnancy is recognized; it may also **reduce oral-contraceptive efficacy** via delayed gastric emptying, so a non-oral/barrier backup is advised after initiation/escalation [21]. **Monitoring:** glucose (especially with insulin/secretagogues), hydration and renal function during persistent GI symptoms, gallbladder and pancreatitis symptoms, and retinopathy in at-risk diabetics [20][21]. Objective labs a clinician would track include **HbA1c** (glycemic response/safety; [[biomarkers/hba1c]] if present in this wiki), a basic metabolic/renal panel during persistent GI symptoms, and lipase if pancreatitis is suspected. **Stopping criteria:** discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected, for serious hypersensitivity, and if a thyroid nodule/elevated calcitonin warrants evaluation; mitigate GI intolerance by slowing or pausing titration before abandoning therapy [20][21].

### 5.9 Coverage gaps (Section 5)
- **Long-term human thyroid-C-cell carcinogenicity** is not resolved — the boxed warning rests on rodent data; the absence of a demonstrated human signal is reassuring but not a clearance.
- Prior-pancreatitis patients were **excluded** from trials, so a pancreatitis class signal cannot be fully excluded.
- Tirzepatide-specific evidence for resistance-training/protein mitigation of lean-mass loss is **limited**.
- AE rates derive from sponsor labels and sponsor-run trials (§4).

## 6. Regulatory & Sport Status

Tirzepatide is sold under **two distinct brand names with two separate, independently approved indication sets**: **Mounjaro** (T2D) and **Zepbound** (obesity/chronic weight management, and OSA in obesity) — the same molecule under different trade names, NDAs, and labels [26].

### 6.1 FDA approvals (United States)

1. **Mounjaro — type 2 diabetes (May 13, 2022).** The first GIP/GLP-1 dual receptor agonist approved for T2D, "as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus"; the pediatric indication was later extended to ages ≥10. Once-weekly SC [25][26].
2. **Zepbound — chronic weight management / obesity (November 8, 2023).** A **separate NDA and approval** from Mounjaro — same active ingredient, distinct obesity brand — "in combination with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity" in adults with **obesity (BMI ≥30)** or **overweight (BMI ≥27) with ≥1 weight-related comorbidity** [27].
3. **Zepbound — moderate-to-severe OSA in obesity (December 20, 2024).** The **first-ever FDA-approved drug for obstructive sleep apnea**, following the SURMOUNT-OSA program (Fast Track, Breakthrough Therapy, Priority Review) [28][30]. The current Zepbound label specifies maintenance doses of 5/10/15 mg for weight management vs **10 or 15 mg for OSA** (the 2.5 mg rung is initiation-only), and carries the thyroid C-cell boxed warning and MTC/MEN2 contraindication [21].

**Brand/indication split:** Mounjaro → T2D (May 2022); Zepbound → obesity (Nov 2023); Zepbound → OSA (Dec 2024).

### 6.2 EMA / Europe

The **European Commission granted marketing authorisation for Mounjaro (tirzepatide) on September 26, 2022** (positive CHMP opinion July 2022), "as an adjunct to diet and exercise for the treatment of adults with insufficiently controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus," later broadened to include weight management (CHMP November 2023) and pediatric T2D (≥10). Unlike the U.S., the EU markets the molecule primarily under the **single Mounjaro brand** across indications [29].

### 6.3 Shortage and compounded tirzepatide (the 2022–2025 arc)

Because demand massively outstripped supply, the FDA listed tirzepatide on its **drug shortage list from late 2022**, which permits 503A pharmacies and 503B outsourcing facilities to compound "essentially a copy" — fueling a large gray market via telehealth [31]. The wind-down: (1) **Oct 3, 2024** — FDA first declared the shortage resolved; (2) **Oct 7–11, 2024** — the Outsourcing Facilities Association sued FDA; a court **remanded** for re-evaluation; (3) **Dec 19, 2024** — FDA **reaffirmed** resolution ("supply meets or exceeds current demand… and will also meet or exceed projected demand"); (4) **enforcement wind-down** — 503A pharmacies a **60-day grace period ending Feb 18, 2025** and 503B facilities a **90-day grace period ending Mar 19, 2025** [31][32]. This tirzepatide wind-down **preceded** the parallel semaglutide compounding wind-down (resolved February 2025). After the grace periods, 503A pharmacies may compound tirzepatide only under narrow individual-medical-necessity circumstances (not "essentially a copy"); 503B facilities generally cannot compound it unless it returns to shortage or is added to the 503B bulks list [31][32].

FDA has issued repeated **safety warnings about compounded and counterfeit tirzepatide** — products with wrong/no active ingredient, dosing errors from self-measured multidose vials (some requiring hospitalization), and unapproved products falsely labeled "for research purposes only." FDA also warns that **tirzepatide salt forms** (tirzepatide acetate, tirzepatide sodium) are **not approved and are not the active ingredient** in Mounjaro/Zepbound [33]. As of mid-2025, FDA reported on the order of **1,150 adverse-event reports** related to compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide combined (with several hundred specifically tied to compounded tirzepatide) [33]. Consumers are urged to obtain tirzepatide only as the brand-name FDA-approved product through state-licensed pharmacies.

### 6.4 WADA / sport status

**Tirzepatide is NOT on the WADA Prohibited List** — not prohibited in or out of competition, and not in any prohibited class on the 2026 Prohibited List [37]. However, GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists have entered WADA's **Monitoring Program**, a surveillance mechanism (not a ban): per the 2026 list materials, "From 1 January 2026, markers of semaglutide and additionally of tirzepatide will be monitored to observe possible misuse… in-competition and out-of-competition" [37][38]. Monitoring Program substances remain **fully permitted**; WADA simply tracks usage prevalence. **Bottom line for sport:** an athlete using tirzepatide for an approved medical indication is **not committing an anti-doping rule violation** under the 2026 framework, though use may be detected via biomarker monitoring [37][38].

### 6.5 Cost and access (qualitative)

Tirzepatide is an expensive, brand-only patented medicine; U.S. list prices for Mounjaro and Zepbound are on the order of **~$1,000+ per 28-day supply** before insurance/rebates [34][36]. Coverage is uneven: diabetes (Mounjaro) coverage is more established than obesity (Zepbound); many payers (including, historically, Medicare for obesity) restrict weight-management coverage. Lilly has offered lower-cost self-pay single-dose-vial channels (LillyDirect, reported tiers ~$299–$499/month) [36]. (Qualitative; pricing varies by source and over time and is not anchored to a primary regulatory document.)

## 7. Why evidence_tier A / risk_tier medium

**Why evidence_tier A.** Tirzepatide sits at the top tier because the converging evidence is the highest design class and broad in scope:
1. **Multiple Phase-3 RCTs** across two programs — SURPASS (glycemic) and SURMOUNT (weight) — with dose-ranging, active and placebo comparators, and large samples [1-trial][2][7][8][9][10][11][12][14].
2. **An approved outcome trial on an objective endpoint** — SURMOUNT-OSA reduced AHI by polysomnography, supporting the December 2024 OSA approval [9-osa].
3. **A dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial** — SURPASS-CVOT (N=13,299) established CV safety (non-inferiority) on adjudicated MACE [13].
4. **Regulatory approval** — FDA (Mounjaro 2022, Zepbound 2023, Zepbound-OSA 2024) and EMA [25][26][27][29].

The single material caveat keeping the grade honest is **sponsor concentration**: ~95% of the pivotal primaries are Eli Lilly-funded with no independent large RCT replication (§4). That is a concentration risk, not an evidence-quality defect — the trials are tier-1, registered, randomized, double-blind, and adjudicated — so it lowers confidence at the margin without dropping the tier.

**Why risk_tier medium.** Tirzepatide is approved and well-characterized, which keeps it out of the higher-risk tiers — but it is **not** low-risk:
1. **Gastrointestinal intolerance dominates** and drives most discontinuations (§5.1).
2. **Weight regain on discontinuation is predictable** (SURMOUNT-4: +14%), so benefits are maintenance-dependent (§5.2).
3. **Lean-mass loss** is real (~25% of weight lost), even if proportional (§5.3).
4. **Gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, and peri-operative aspiration** are labeled/structural concerns (§5.4–5.5).
5. **A boxed warning** for thyroid C-cell tumors exists — rodent-based, human relevance undetermined, but contraindicating MTC/MEN2 and pregnancy (§5.6, §5.8).

The net is a drug with **strong, high-tier evidence (A)** and a **real but managed, doctor-supervised risk profile (medium)** — hence `doctor_discussion_required: true`.

## 8. Prescribing-Practice & Non-English Summary (brief)

> This section is a **brief** summary; full prescribing detail, the compounding landscape, and the non-English survey live in the sibling layer files of this wiki. Dosing/titration is from the FDA labels (authoritative `regulatory`); practitioner/vendor sources are used only for route/dose/sourcing context, never to ground efficacy or AE rates.

### 8.1 Dosing & titration

Both labels specify an identical, deliberately slow escalation to mitigate GI adverse reactions: **2.5 mg once weekly for 4 weeks → 5 mg → 7.5 mg → 10 mg → 12.5 mg → 15 mg**, increasing in 2.5-mg increments after at least 4 weeks at each dose, to a maximum of **15 mg once weekly** [20][21]. The **2.5 mg step is a non-therapeutic initiation rung** for GI acclimation — the Mounjaro label states it "is not intended for glycemic control" and the Zepbound label that it "is not approved as a maintenance dosage" [20][21]. Administration is once weekly, any time of day, with or without meals, SC in the abdomen/thigh/upper arm with site rotation; strengths are 2.5–15 mg per 0.5 mL (pen or vial) [20]. **Indication-specific maintenance** (Zepbound): 5/10/15 mg for weight management; **10 or 15 mg for OSA** (the lowest maintenance dose is not approved for OSA) [21]. Pediatric T2D (≥10) Mounjaro maximum is 10 mg [20]. Clinically, prescribers commonly hold at the lowest effective dose and individualize up-titration rather than auto-advancing to 15 mg.

### 8.2 Compounded / access landscape (context only)

During the 2022–2024 shortage, compounded tirzepatide was widely dispensed via telehealth (multidose vials, sometimes co-formulated with B12/niacinamide) — reported here as **context, not evidence of efficacy or safety equivalence**, since compounded product is not FDA-reviewed for efficacy, potency, or AE rates [31]. With the shortage resolved (§6.3), large-volume "compounded tirzepatide" offerings post-2025 fall outside the routine compounding exemption; FDA warns that unapproved **salt forms** and counterfeit/imported products carry real risk [33][31].

### 8.3 Muscle-preservation adjuncts (convention, not label)

The conventional adjuncts to attenuate lean-mass loss during a large deficit are **progressive resistance training plus adequate dietary protein** — a standard recommendation, not a tirzepatide-label requirement, and the controlled evidence for these adjuncts **specifically combined with tirzepatide** is still limited (the pivotal trials did not mandate structured resistance training) [39]. Reported as practice convention; it does not modify the efficacy/AE claims above.

### 8.4 Non-English literature

**No distinct non-English-LANGUAGE primary efficacy/safety trials were identified.** Tirzepatide's pivotal program is global and English-published. Trials in non-Western populations exist but are **English-published**: **SURMOUNT-J** (Japanese obesity; *Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology* 2024) [43] and **SURMOUNT-CN** (Chinese obesity; *JAMA* 2024; −13.6% at 10 mg and −17.5% at 15 mg vs −2.3% placebo) [44], plus SURPASS J-mono/J-combo. Genuinely non-English **documents** are regulatory/practitioner materials (Japanese package-insert/interview-form 添付文書/インタビューフォーム for マンジャロ／ゼップバウンド; Chinese drug-information for 替尔泊肽) that mirror label content and add **no independent efficacy or AE data** [45]. **No non-English source was used to support any efficacy or adverse-event-rate claim.**
