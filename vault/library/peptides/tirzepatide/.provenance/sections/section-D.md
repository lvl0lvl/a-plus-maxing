## Section D — Safety & adverse effects

Tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist marketed as Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes; U.S. approval 2022) and Zepbound (chronic weight management). Its safety profile is unusually well-characterized for a recent agent: it rests on a large randomized-controlled-trial base (SUMMIT/SURPASS for diabetes, SURMOUNT for obesity), two current FDA labels, and an incretin-class adverse-effect signal shared with GLP-1 receptor agonists. The dominant practical issue is gastrointestinal tolerability; the most consequential label item — a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors — is, as detailed below, based on rodent data whose human relevance has not been established. This section quantifies adverse effects from trials and the labels and states the known liabilities (weight regain on discontinuation, lean-mass loss) honestly.

### Gastrointestinal adverse effects (dominant; main discontinuation driver)

GI events are by far the most common adverse reactions and the principal reason patients stop the drug. They are dose-related and concentrate during dose escalation, which is why both labels mandate slow titration (starting 2.5 mg, increasing in 2.5-mg steps no faster than every 4 weeks) [regulatory].

In the pooled placebo-controlled **diabetes** trials (Mounjaro label), incidence at the 5/10/15 mg doses vs placebo was: nausea 12%/15%/18% (placebo 4%); diarrhea 12%/13%/17% (placebo 9%); decreased appetite 5%/10%/11% (placebo 1%); vomiting 5%/5%/9% (placebo 2%); constipation 6%/6%/7% (placebo 1%); dyspepsia 8%/8%/5% (placebo 3%); abdominal pain 6%/5%/5% (placebo 4%) [regulatory]. GI events are more frequent and more intense in the higher-dose **obesity** program: in the pooled Zepbound weight-management trials, nausea ran 25%/29%/28% across 5/10/15 mg (placebo 8%), diarrhea 19%/21%/23% (placebo 8%), vomiting 8%/11%/13% (placebo 2%), and constipation 17%/14%/11% (placebo 5%) [regulatory].

Most GI events are mild to moderate and transient. In SURMOUNT-4's randomized maintenance phase the most common treatment-emergent events were GI — diarrhea 10.7% vs 4.8%, nausea 8.1% vs 2.7%, vomiting 5.7% vs 1.2% (tirzepatide vs placebo) — and "most gastrointestinal events were mild to moderate in severity" [rct]. Nonetheless GI intolerance is the leading cause of discontinuation. Discontinuation due to GI adverse reactions rose with dose: in diabetes trials 3.0%/5.4%/6.6% for 5/10/15 mg vs 0.4% placebo [regulatory]; in obesity trials 1.9%/3.3%/4.3% vs 0.5% placebo, with all-cause adverse-reaction discontinuation of 4.8%/6.3%/6.7% vs 3.4% placebo [regulatory]. The Mounjaro and Zepbound labels carry a Severe Gastrointestinal Adverse Reactions warning; Zepbound is not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis [regulatory].

### Weight regain on discontinuation (state honestly)

Tirzepatide is a chronic therapy; stopping it reverses most of the weight benefit. The pivotal evidence is **SURMOUNT-4** (Aronne et al., JAMA 2024; PMID 38078870) [rct]. After a 36-week open-label lead-in on maximum tolerated dose (10 or 15 mg), 670 adults with obesity were randomized to continue tirzepatide or switch to placebo for 52 weeks. During that double-blind period, participants switched to placebo **regained a mean of 14.0% of body weight**, whereas those continuing tirzepatide lost an additional 5.5% — a between-group difference of −19.4% (95% CI −21.2% to −17.7%; P < .001). Over the full study (week 0 to 88) total weight reduction was 25.3% with continued tirzepatide vs 9.9% with placebo, and 89.5% of continued-treatment recipients maintained at least 80% of their weight loss vs only 16.6% of those switched to placebo [rct]. A post hoc SURMOUNT-4 analysis (JAMA Internal Medicine, 2025) found that weight regain after withdrawal was accompanied by a corresponding reversal of cardiometabolic improvements (waist circumference, lipids, fasting insulin), reinforcing that the benefits are maintenance-dependent rather than durable after stopping [rct]. Patients should be counseled up front that discontinuation predictably leads to substantial regain.

### Lean / muscle-mass loss with weight loss

As with any large weight reduction, some of the loss is lean (fat-free) mass. In the SURMOUNT-1 body-composition substudy (Look et al., Diabetes Obes Metab 2025; PMID 39996356), DXA at week 72 showed tirzepatide reduced fat mass by 33.9% (vs 8.2% placebo) and lean mass by 10.9% (vs 2.6% placebo) [rct]. Importantly, the *proportion* of weight lost as lean mass was about 26% (74% fat) with tirzepatide vs 25%/75% with placebo, and the fat-to-lean ratio improved more on drug; the authors concluded tirzepatide "does not induce disproportionate lean mass loss compared with other established obesity treatment modalities" [rct]. The honest framing: absolute lean-mass loss is real and not trivial, but it is roughly proportional to the (much larger) total weight loss rather than selectively muscle-wasting. Resistance exercise and adequate protein are reasonable mitigations, though tirzepatide-specific outcome data on that are limited.

### Gallbladder disease and pancreatitis

Both are labeled concerns shared across the incretin class. The labels carry an **Acute Gallbladder Disease** warning; in pooled Zepbound weight-reduction trials cholecystitis occurred in 0.7% of tirzepatide-treated vs 0.2% of placebo patients [regulatory]. **Acute Pancreatitis** is also a labeled warning: in those same pooled obesity trials pancreatitis incidence was low and comparable between groups (~0.2%; about 0.14 vs 0.15 per 100 patient-years) [regulatory]. Patients with prior pancreatitis were excluded from trials, so a true class signal cannot be fully excluded; clinicians should discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected and evaluate persistent severe abdominal pain [regulatory].

### Gastroparesis / ileus and peri-operative aspiration risk

Tirzepatide delays gastric emptying (part of its mechanism), which raises a peri-operative concern: retained gastric contents despite standard fasting and a risk of pulmonary aspiration under sedation/anesthesia. The 2024 multi-society perioperative guidance (Kindel et al.; PMID 39370500, endorsed by ASA, AGA, ASMBS, SAGES, IPSOPC) moved away from the ASA's original 2023 blanket-hold position toward shared decision-making: most low-risk patients can continue therapy, with mitigations including a ≥24-hour preoperative clear-liquid diet, point-of-care gastric ultrasound when retained contents are suspected, and consideration of rapid-sequence induction [regulatory/mechanism_review]. If holding is chosen, the suggested interval is the day of surgery for daily formulations and one week prior for weekly formulations (tirzepatide is weekly) [regulatory]. Reported aspiration rates remain low (on the order of 0.1–0.2%) even though retained gastric contents are markedly more common [regulatory/mechanism_review].

### Thyroid C-cell tumors / MTC — BOXED WARNING (rodent-based; not demonstrated in humans)

Both Mounjaro and Zepbound carry a **boxed warning** for thyroid C-cell tumors. The precise framing matters and must not be overstated. The label states verbatim: "Tirzepatide causes thyroid C-cell tumors in rats. **It is unknown** whether MOUNJARO causes thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), in humans **as the human relevance of tirzepatide-induced rodent thyroid C-cell tumors has not been determined**" [regulatory]. In a 2-year rat study tirzepatide produced a dose- and duration-dependent increase in C-cell adenomas and carcinomas at clinically relevant exposures [regulatory]. There is **no demonstrated causal link to MTC in humans**; the warning is a precautionary, rodent-derived class label (shared with GLP-1 agonists), not evidence of human carcinogenicity. Accordingly, it is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or MEN 2, and patients should be counseled on thyroid-tumor symptoms (neck mass, dysphagia, dyspnea, persistent hoarseness). Routine serum calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound monitoring is of uncertain value and not clearly established by the label [regulatory].

### Hypoglycemia, injection-site, hypersensitivity, renal, retinopathy

**Hypoglycemia** is mainly a risk when tirzepatide is combined with insulin or an insulin secretagogue (sulfonylurea); tirzepatide alone has low intrinsic hypoglycemia risk. In a diabetes population (Zepbound Study 2) clinically significant hypoglycemia (<54 mg/dL) occurred in 4.2% on tirzepatide vs 1.3% placebo, rising to 10.3% with concurrent sulfonylurea vs 2.1% without [regulatory]. The label advises reducing the dose of the concomitant secretagogue/insulin [regulatory]. **Hypersensitivity reactions** (including rare anaphylaxis/angioedema) are a labeled warning and serious hypersensitivity is a contraindication. **Injection-site reactions** occurred in roughly 6–8% in obesity trials (vs 2% placebo) [regulatory]. **Acute kidney injury due to volume depletion** can occur secondary to GI fluid loss (dehydration from vomiting/diarrhea) — maintain hydration [regulatory]. **Diabetic retinopathy complications** carry a warning in patients with a history of retinopathy, reflecting effects associated with rapid glycemic improvement [regulatory]. Other common reactions in obesity trials include fatigue (~5–7%), eructation, GERD, and hair loss/alopecia (~4–5% vs 1% placebo) [regulatory]. The Zepbound label also references rare postmarketing suicidal-ideation/behavior reports across the GLP-1 class, advising monitoring, though a causal association is not established [regulatory].

### Contraindications, monitoring, and stopping criteria

**Contraindications** (both labels): personal or family history of MTC or MEN 2; known serious hypersensitivity to tirzepatide or excipients [regulatory]. Tirzepatide is **not recommended in pregnancy** (animal reproductive toxicity; weight loss offers no benefit in pregnancy) — discontinue when pregnancy is recognized; it may also reduce the efficacy of oral contraceptives via delayed gastric emptying, so a non-oral or barrier backup method is advised after initiation/escalation [regulatory]. **Monitoring:** glucose (especially with insulin/secretagogues), hydration and renal function during persistent GI symptoms, gallbladder and pancreatitis symptoms, and retinopathy in at-risk diabetics [regulatory]. **Stopping criteria:** discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected, for serious hypersensitivity, and if a thyroid nodule/elevated calcitonin warrants evaluation; mitigate GI intolerance by slowing or pausing titration before abandoning therapy [regulatory].
