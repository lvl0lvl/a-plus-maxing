## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### Identity and molecular class

Tirzepatide (development code LY3298176; marketed by Eli Lilly as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for chronic weight management) is a single synthetic peptide that acts as a **dual agonist at both the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR) and the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R)** — the so-called "twincretin" mechanism [1] [mechanism_review]. This is its defining and distinguishing feature: it is mechanistically separate from semaglutide and other GLP-1-only agonists precisely because of the added GIP-receptor component [5] [mechanism_review]. It is not a co-formulation of two drugs but one engineered molecule that engages both incretin receptors [1] [mechanism_review].

### Primary structure and chemistry

Tirzepatide is a **39-amino-acid linear peptide** [1] [mechanism_review] [2] [mechanism_review]. Sequence-homology analysis places it at roughly 67% homology with native GIP and roughly 50% homology with GLP-1, i.e., the backbone is closer to the GIP sequence than to GLP-1 [5] [mechanism_review]. Its molecular weight is approximately **4.8 kDa** [5] [mechanism_review].

The peptide carries a fatty-acid modification that is central to both its stability and its long duration of action. It is **acylated at the lysine residue at position 20 (Lys20)** [1] [mechanism_review] [2] [mechanism_review]. The acyl group is a **C20 fatty diacid** (eicosanedioic diacid) attached through a hydrophilic linker built from L-γ-glutamic acid and two units of 8-amino-3,6-dioxaoctanoic acid (an AEEA/PEG-type spacer) [1] [mechanism_review] [2] [mechanism_review]. The lysine bearing this chain sits near the middle of the molecule [1] [mechanism_review]. To resist enzymatic degradation, the peptide incorporates a non-natural **2-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib) substitution** in the N-terminal region (reported at position 8 in one sequence comparison) [5] [mechanism_review]; the same Aib motif is the standard medicinal-chemistry device used in incretin mimetics to block dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) cleavage that otherwise rapidly inactivates native GIP and GLP-1 [5] [mechanism_review].

The structural basis of dual receptor engagement was resolved by cryo-electron microscopy of the active-state complexes [2] [mechanism_review]. At the GIPR (GIPR/tirzepatide complex, 3.1 Å), tirzepatide adopts an α-helical conformation with its N-terminus reaching deep into the transmembrane core in a pose closely mimicking native GIP (GIPR/GIP, 3.2 Å), with Tyr1 buried in the transmembrane bundle and hydrogen-bonding to receptor residues [2] [mechanism_review]. At the GLP-1R (GLP-1R/tirzepatide, 2.9 Å), tirzepatide engages the receptor in a **distinct manner** — with weaker N-terminal interactions and reduced stabilization of extracellular loop 3 (ECL3) — and this altered binding geometry underlies its biased signaling at that receptor [2] [mechanism_review].

### Imbalanced and biased agonism

A key pharmacological nuance is that tirzepatide is **not a balanced dual agonist**. In radioligand competition binding it shows affinity at the GIPR **comparable to native GIP**, but at the GLP-1R it has roughly **5-fold lower affinity than native GLP-1**; in cAMP-accumulation potency assays it is **equipotent with native GIP at the GIPR but ~20-fold less potent than native GLP-1 at the GLP-1R** [4] [in_vitro]. The drug is therefore described as an **imbalanced** agonist favoring GIPR activity over GLP-1R activity [4] [in_vitro].

Layered on top of this imbalance is **signaling bias** at the GLP-1R. At that receptor tirzepatide drives a monophasic cAMP response but recruits β-arrestin only weakly (low efficacy, <10% of maximal effect) and internalizes the GLP-1R less effectively than native GLP-1 (<40% of maximum) [4] [in_vitro]. It thus shows bias toward the cAMP (G-protein) pathway over β-arrestin recruitment, whereas at the GIPR its behavior parallels native GIP across pathways [4] [in_vitro]. The functional implication proposed by the authors is that reduced β-arrestin-mediated desensitization at the GLP-1R may sustain the insulinotropic signal [4] [in_vitro]. Notably, semaglutide is itself reported to be a biased GLP-1R agonist favoring cAMP over β-arrestin, so the qualitative bias at the GLP-1R is not unique to tirzepatide — what is unique is the added GIPR arm [5] [mechanism_review].

### Incretin physiology underlying the mechanism

The two targets are the body's two principal incretin hormones. **GIP** is secreted by enteroendocrine K cells of the duodenum and jejunum; **GLP-1** is secreted by L cells of the distal gut (ileum and colon) [5] [mechanism_review]. Postprandially, GIP circulates at molar concentrations roughly 3-4 times higher than GLP-1 [5] [mechanism_review]. Together these hormones produce the **incretin effect** — the augmentation of insulin secretion when glucose is taken orally versus intravenously — which accounts for about **50-70% of the postprandial insulin response in healthy individuals** but is **reduced to roughly 20-30% in type 2 diabetes** [5] [mechanism_review]. A defining safety-relevant property of incretin action is that it is **glucose-dependent**: GLP-1 stimulates insulin in the presence of high glucose (≈450 mg/dL) but not at normal glucose (≈99 mg/dL), which limits hypoglycemia risk [5] [mechanism_review].

### Mechanism of action of tirzepatide

By co-activating both incretin receptors, tirzepatide recapitulates and amplifies the physiological incretin program. The principal effects, enumerated:

1. **Glucose-dependent insulin secretion** — both GIPR and GLP-1R signaling on pancreatic β cells enhance insulin release in a glucose-dependent fashion, raising insulin only when glucose is elevated [4] [in_vitro] [5] [mechanism_review].
2. **Glucagon modulation** — GLP-1 exerts an indirect glucagonostatic effect during hyperglycemia (via somatostatin-mediated paracrine signaling) but not during hypoglycemia, while GIP is glucagonotropic during hypoglycemia; the net effect is glucose-context-dependent glucagon control [5] [mechanism_review].
3. **Slowed gastric emptying** — mediated through the GLP-1 arm, blunting postprandial glucose and lipid surges; native GIP lacks this effect [5] [mechanism_review].
4. **Central satiety and reduced food intake** — brain GLP-1 receptors are necessary for the weight-lowering effect (CNS-neuron GLP-1R knockout mice lose the weight effect of liraglutide while retaining glucose benefit), and GIP appears to potentiate this central anorectic effect, e.g., by enhancing hypothalamic POMC expression [5] [mechanism_review].
5. **Adipose / lipid handling via GIP** — GIP directly stimulates lipogenesis (up-regulating lipoprotein lipase and promoting triglyceride clearance into adipocytes), and pharmacological GIPR activity is proposed to contribute to body-weight reduction through a mechanism distinct from the permissive physiological role of GIP in adipose synthesis [5] [mechanism_review].

### Pharmacokinetics

The C20 fatty-diacid chain drives the pharmacokinetic profile. Reversible non-covalent **albumin binding** through that side chain slows clearance and protects the peptide from DPP-4 degradation, producing a long elimination half-life [1] [mechanism_review] [5] [mechanism_review]. Population pharmacokinetic analysis reports an **elimination half-life of approximately 5 days (≈5.4 days)** [3] [cohort]. Absorption after subcutaneous injection is slow, with time to peak concentration (Tmax) ranging from **8 to 72 hours** [3] [cohort], and mean absolute bioavailability of about **80%** [3] [cohort]. With once-weekly dosing the accumulation ratio is about **1.7-fold**, and steady state is reached after roughly four weeks [3] [cohort]. Population estimates include a central volume of distribution of ~2.47 L/70 kg and apparent steady-state clearance of ~0.061 L/h [3] [cohort]. This ~5-day half-life is what enables the **once-weekly subcutaneous regimen** [3] [cohort] [1] [mechanism_review].

### Why the dual mechanism may produce larger effects than GLP-1-only agents

Several lines of evidence support an additive-to-synergistic advantage of dual agonism over GLP-1 monotherapy:

1. **Synergistic intracellular signaling** — in cultured pancreatic β cells, tirzepatide elicited a cAMP response significantly greater than that of GLP-1 or GIP alone, consistent with additive/synergistic receptor activation [5] [in_vitro].
2. **Preclinical additivity in vivo** — co-administering a long-acting GIP-receptor agonist with a GLP-1-receptor agonist synergistically enhanced glucose-lowering and weight reduction, and central (intracerebroventricular) co-delivery of GIP and GLP-1 additively enhanced weight loss in mice [5] [animal (mouse, n not stated)].
3. **Clinical magnitude versus semaglutide** — the review cites placebo-corrected HbA1c reduction of about 2.11% for tirzepatide versus under 1.5% for leading GLP-1 monotherapies, and ~17.8% placebo-corrected weight loss versus ~12.4% for semaglutide in the cited comparisons [5] [mechanism_review].

The contrast with **semaglutide** crystallizes the rationale: semaglutide is a **GLP-1-receptor agonist only**, achieving roughly 1.5% glycemic reduction and ~12.4% weight loss in the cited obesity data, whereas tirzepatide adds GIPR engagement on top of GLP-1R activity, and the GIP component is the proposed source of its larger metabolic and weight effects [5] [mechanism_review]. The dual molecule's "imbalanced" tilt toward GIPR — equal-to-GIP affinity but weaker-than-GLP-1 GLP-1R engagement — combined with cAMP-biased GLP-1R signaling, defines a pharmacology that is qualitatively different from any GLP-1 monoagonist [4] [in_vitro] [2] [mechanism_review].
