## Section C — OSA/CV/HFpEF/MASH + concentration audit

Scope note: This section covers tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist) across obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), cardiovascular (CV) outcomes, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), and an ongoing morbidity/mortality endpoint trial. Indication status is labeled explicitly throughout: OSA is an **APPROVED** indication (FDA December 2024, Zepbound); HFpEF, MASH, and morbidity/mortality are **INVESTIGATIONAL** for tirzepatide as of this writing.

### OSA — APPROVED indication (this is the approval-defining trial)

**SURMOUNT-OSA** is the pivotal program supporting the December 2024 FDA approval of tirzepatide (Zepbound) for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity — the first drug ever approved for OSA. The trial was reported by Malhotra et al. in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2024 (PMID 38912654, verified to author + year + journal). `[rct]`

Design and population: SURMOUNT-OSA was actually two parallel phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in adults with **moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity**, distinguished by background therapy. **Trial 1** enrolled participants **not using positive airway pressure (PAP)** at baseline; **Trial 2** enrolled participants **established on PAP** at baseline. Both used 1:1 randomization to tirzepatide (titrated to maximum tolerated dose, 10 mg or 15 mg once weekly) versus placebo over **52 weeks**. `[rct]` [1]

Magnitude (primary endpoint = change in apnea-hypopnea index, AHI, events/hr):
- **Trial 1 (no PAP):** AHI fell −25.3 events/hr (95% CI −29.3 to −21.2) with tirzepatide vs −5.3 (95% CI −9.4 to −1.1) with placebo — a **treatment difference of −20.0 events/hr (95% CI −25.8 to −14.2)**. `[rct]` [1]
- **Trial 2 (on PAP):** AHI fell −29.3 events/hr (95% CI −33.2 to −25.4) vs −5.5 (95% CI −9.9 to −1.2) — a **treatment difference of −23.8 events/hr (95% CI −29.6 to −17.9)**. `[rct]` [1]

Both treatment differences were statistically significant, and tirzepatide also produced significant body-weight reduction versus placebo in both cohorts. A companion summary of the same trial appeared in *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2024 (Cheskin LJ, PMID 39348697), reinforcing that in adults with moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity, tirzepatide reduced the apnea-hypopnea index. `[rct]` [2]

DEFINING FACT (OSA): In obese adults with moderate-to-severe OSA, tirzepatide 10–15 mg weekly over 52 weeks reduced AHI by roughly **20–24 events/hr more than placebo** (≈55–63% relative reduction from baseline), with consistent benefit whether or not patients were on PAP — the basis for the December 2024 Zepbound OSA approval.

### Cardiovascular outcomes

**SURPASS-CVOT** is the dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial, reported by Nicholls et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2025 Dec 18 (PMID 41406444, verified). `[rct]` [3]

Design and population: A large randomized, double-blind, active-comparator non-inferiority trial in **adults with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD)**. **N = 13,299 randomized** (final analysis ~6,586 tirzepatide vs ~6,579 dulaglutide), comparing tirzepatide against **dulaglutide 1.5 mg** weekly (an active comparator already shown to have CV benefit, making this a stringent test). `[rct]` [3]

Result (primary 3-point MACE = CV death, MI, or stroke): The primary composite occurred in **801/6,586 (12.2%)** on tirzepatide vs **862/6,579 (13.1%)** on dulaglutide — **hazard ratio 0.92 (95.3% CI 0.83–1.01)**, meeting prespecified **non-inferiority (P=0.003)** but **not superiority (P=0.09)**. `[rct]` [3] Secondary analyses reported by the investigators favored tirzepatide on metabolic and renal measures and on all-cause mortality (an approximately 16% relative reduction vs dulaglutide), alongside greater reductions in weight, blood pressure, and lipids. `[rct]` [3]

Interpretation: SURPASS-CVOT establishes **CV safety** for tirzepatide in high-risk T2D (non-inferior to an agent with proven CV benefit) but does **not** demonstrate CV superiority over dulaglutide on hard MACE. Tirzepatide is approved for T2D and obesity but is **not labeled as having a dedicated CV risk-reduction indication** on the basis of MACE superiority. `[regulatory]`

### HFpEF — INVESTIGATIONAL

**SUMMIT** is the HFpEF trial, reported by Packer et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2025 Jan 30 (PMID 39555826, verified). `[rct]` [4]

Design and population: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in **patients with HFpEF (ejection fraction ≥50%) and obesity (BMI ≥30)**. **N = 731** (364 tirzepatide, 367 placebo), tirzepatide titrated up to **15 mg** weekly, minimum 52 weeks with median follow-up ~104 weeks. `[rct]` [4]

Results: The primary composite of **CV death or worsening heart-failure event** occurred in **36 (9.9%)** on tirzepatide vs **56 (15.3%)** on placebo — **HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.41–0.95; P=0.026)**. The benefit was driven by worsening HF events (29 [8.0%] vs 52 [14.2%]; HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.34–0.85); CV death was rare and not reduced (8 [2.2%] vs 5 [1.4%]; HR 1.58, 95% CI 0.52–4.83 — wide CI, uninformative). The co-primary KCCQ-CSS health-status score improved by 19.5±1.2 vs 12.7±1.3 (between-group difference **6.9 points; 95% CI 3.3–10.6; P<0.001**). `[rct]` [4]

Mechanistic corroboration within the SUMMIT program (same sponsor): Borlaug et al. (*Nature Medicine* 2025, PMID 39551891) reported reductions in circulatory overload and end-organ damage, and Kramer et al. (*JACC* 2025, PMID 39566869) reported reduced LV mass and paracardiac adipose tissue — both substudies of the same trial population. `[mechanism_review]` [5] HFpEF is **INVESTIGATIONAL**; tirzepatide is not approved for heart failure.

### MASH/NASH — INVESTIGATIONAL

**SYNERGY-NASH** is the phase 2b MASH trial, reported by Loomba et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2024 Jul 25 (PMID 38856224, verified). `[rct]` [6]

Design and population: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2b trial in adults with **biopsy-confirmed MASH and stage F2 or F3 fibrosis**. **N = 190** randomized (157 with evaluable week-52 biopsy), tirzepatide 5, 10, or 15 mg weekly vs placebo over **52 weeks**. `[rct]` [6]

Results (resolution of MASH without worsening of fibrosis): **placebo 10%; 5 mg 44% (+34 pts, 95% CI 17–50); 10 mg 56% (+46 pts, 95% CI 29–62); 15 mg 62% (+53 pts, 95% CI 37–69)**, all P<0.001 vs placebo. Fibrosis improvement (≥1 stage without MASH worsening): placebo 30% vs 51–55% across tirzepatide doses (point estimates favoring drug, but CIs reaching near zero). `[rct]` [6] As a **phase 2b** trial, this is hypothesis-generating; tirzepatide is **INVESTIGATIONAL** for MASH and not approved for any liver indication.

### Ongoing — INVESTIGATIONAL

**SURMOUNT-MMO** (NCT05556512) is the event-driven phase 3 morbidity/mortality trial in **adults with obesity, without diabetes**, with or at risk of CVD. Per the ClinicalTrials.gov record: **actual enrollment 15,374**, status **active, not recruiting**, estimated primary completion **October 2027**. Primary endpoint = time to first occurrence of a composite of **all-cause death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke, coronary revascularization, or heart failure events**. Results are pending; this is **INVESTIGATIONAL** and the key future test of whether tirzepatide's metabolic effects translate to hard outcomes in obesity without diabetes. `[regulatory]` [7]

### Concentration / sponsor audit

**Every pivotal trial cited in this section is sponsored by Eli Lilly and Company**, the manufacturer of tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound). This applies to SURMOUNT-OSA [1], SURPASS-CVOT [3], SUMMIT [4], SYNERGY-NASH [6], and the ongoing SURMOUNT-MMO [7]. There is essentially **no independent (non-manufacturer-funded) large randomized outcomes trial** of tirzepatide; this is a single-sponsor evidence base, a notable concentration risk.

Mitigating factors, reported honestly:
- All five trials were published in **tier-1, fully peer-reviewed journals** (NEJM ×4, with substudies in *Nature Medicine*, *JACC*, *Circulation*, *Annals of Internal Medicine*), and were registered, randomized, double-blind, placebo- or active-controlled designs with adjudicated endpoints — the highest-quality design class.
- SURPASS-CVOT is an **active-comparator** trial against dulaglutide (also a Lilly product), which is a stringent design but does not remove sponsor concentration; the comparator and the test drug share a manufacturer.
- The OSA effect (objective AHI by polysomnography) and the MASH effect (central biopsy reads) rely on **objective, externally-adjudicated endpoints** rather than sponsor-reported soft measures, partially de-risking interpretation.
- Independent corroboration exists only indirectly: the *Annals of Internal Medicine* summary [2] and the SUMMIT mechanistic substudies [5] are reports of the **same sponsor-run trials**, not independent replications.

Bottom line: tirzepatide's outcomes evidence is strong by design quality and effect size but is **entirely manufacturer-sponsored**. No independent pivotal RCT corroboration currently exists; readers should weight the single-sponsor concentration accordingly, especially for the not-yet-reported SURMOUNT-MMO.
