## Section B — Glycemic + weight efficacy

Tirzepatide is a once-weekly dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist whose efficacy on both glycemia and body weight is among the largest documented for any approved pharmacotherapy in its class. Two distinct development programs underpin the evidence base, and they must not be conflated: the **SURPASS** program studied glycemic control in adults **with type 2 diabetes (T2D)**, while the **SURMOUNT** program studied weight reduction, including a flagship trial in adults **with obesity but WITHOUT diabetes**. Magnitudes differ substantially between these populations at the same dose, so every figure below is annotated by population. `[type: regulatory/rct]`

### Glycemic efficacy — the SURPASS program (T2D)

The SURPASS phase-3 program comprises five landmark randomized controlled trials, enumerated here: **SURPASS-1** (monotherapy vs placebo), **SURPASS-2** (vs semaglutide 1 mg), **SURPASS-3** (vs insulin degludec), **SURPASS-4** (vs insulin glargine in high cardiovascular-risk T2D), and **SURPASS-5** (add-on to titrated insulin glargine vs placebo). All tested the three approved maintenance doses — 5 mg, 10 mg, and 15 mg once weekly — and across the program HbA1c reductions were dose-dependent. `[type: rct]`

**SURPASS-1 (Rosenstock et al., 2021, Lancet)** randomized 478 drug-naïve T2D adults to tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg or placebo as monotherapy. From a mean baseline HbA1c of 7.9%, HbA1c fell by 1.87%, 1.89%, and 2.07% at the 5, 10, and 15 mg doses respectively at week 40, versus a +0.04% change on placebo (treatment differences −1.91% to −2.11%). Dose-dependent body-weight loss ranged from 7.0 to 9.5 kg. `[type: rct]` [3]

**SURPASS-2 (Frías et al., 2021, NEJM)** is the pivotal head-to-head trial. It randomized 1,879 T2D adults on background metformin (mean baseline HbA1c 8.28%, mean weight 93.7 kg) to tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg or **subcutaneous semaglutide 1 mg** once weekly, open-label, over 40 weeks. HbA1c reductions were −2.01%, −2.24%, and −2.30% for tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg versus **−1.86% for semaglutide 1 mg**. **All three tirzepatide doses were noninferior and statistically SUPERIOR to semaglutide 1 mg for HbA1c reduction** (the 15 mg estimated treatment difference vs semaglutide was approximately −0.45%). Body-weight reductions were also greater with tirzepatide at every dose, with estimated treatment differences versus semaglutide of −1.9 kg, −3.6 kg, and −5.5 kg for 5/10/15 mg; the 15 mg dose reduced weight by 12.4 kg (≈13.1%). `[type: rct]` [2]

It is important to interpret this comparison carefully: SURPASS-2 compared tirzepatide against the **1 mg** dose of semaglutide, which was the highest semaglutide dose approved for T2D at the time of trial design. Subsequently higher semaglutide doses (2.0 mg for T2D; 2.4 mg for obesity) reached market, so SURPASS-2 establishes superiority over semaglutide 1 mg specifically, not over all semaglutide regimens. `[type: rct]` [2]

**SURPASS-3 (Ludvik et al., 2021, Lancet)** compared tirzepatide against once-daily insulin degludec as add-on to metformin ± SGLT2 inhibitor, demonstrating superior HbA1c control and weight loss with tirzepatide (degludec, as an insulin, produced weight gain). **SURPASS-4 (Del Prato et al., 2021, Lancet)** studied 1,995 T2D adults with elevated cardiovascular risk against titrated insulin glargine over 52 weeks: HbA1c fell −2.43% (10 mg) and −2.58% (15 mg) with tirzepatide versus **−1.44% with glargine** — roughly a full additional percentage point — while hypoglycemia was less frequent with tirzepatide (6–9% vs 19% with glargine), and the cardiovascular event hazard ratio versus glargine was 0.74 (95% CI 0.51–1.08), reassuring against harm. **SURPASS-5 (Dahl et al., 2022, JAMA)** added tirzepatide to titrated insulin glargine versus placebo, again showing large incremental HbA1c reductions. `[type: rct]` [4][5][6]

Taken together, the SURPASS program shows tirzepatide producing HbA1c reductions on the order of ~2.0–2.6% from baselines near 8%, with a substantial fraction of participants reaching HbA1c below 7% and many below 5.7% (the non-diabetic range) at higher doses — a magnitude that consistently exceeded active comparators including semaglutide 1 mg, insulin degludec, and insulin glargine. `[type: rct]`

### Weight efficacy — the SURMOUNT program

The SURMOUNT phase-3 program comprises the landmark obesity trials, enumerated here: **SURMOUNT-1** (obesity without diabetes), **SURMOUNT-2** (obesity with T2D), **SURMOUNT-3** (tirzepatide after an intensive-lifestyle lead-in), and **SURMOUNT-4** (maintenance / randomized withdrawal). `[type: rct]`

**SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff et al., 2022, NEJM)** is the flagship weight-loss trial and the source of the headline magnitude. It enrolled 2,539 adults with **obesity but WITHOUT diabetes** (BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a weight-related complication; mean baseline BMI 38.0) randomized to tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg or placebo for 72 weeks alongside lifestyle counseling. Mean percent body-weight change was **−15.0% (5 mg), −19.5% (10 mg), and −20.9% (15 mg)** versus **−3.1% on placebo**. The proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss was 85–91% across doses (vs 35% placebo), and **≥20% weight loss was achieved by 50% (10 mg) and 57% (15 mg)** of participants, versus 3% on placebo. The most common adverse events were gastrointestinal and mostly mild-to-moderate. This **~20.9% mean weight reduction at 15 mg in adults without diabetes** is the defining efficacy fact for the obesity indication. `[type: rct]` [1]

**SURMOUNT-2 (Garvey et al., 2023, Lancet)** is the critical population contrast: in 938 adults with **obesity AND T2D**, mean weight change at 72 weeks was **−12.8% (10 mg) and −14.7% (15 mg)** versus −3.2% placebo (treatment differences −9.6 and −11.6 percentage points). The clear attenuation versus SURMOUNT-1 (≈14.7% vs ≈20.9% at 15 mg) reflects the well-established phenomenon that incretin-based weight loss is blunted in people with T2D relative to people without diabetes — a population effect, not a dosing difference. `[type: rct]` [7]

**SURMOUNT-3 (Wadden et al., 2023, Nature Medicine)** tested whether tirzepatide adds to lifestyle intervention: after a 12-week intensive lifestyle lead-in in which all participants lost ≥5%, 579 were randomized to tirzepatide or placebo. From randomization to week 72, tirzepatide produced a **further −18.4%** weight change versus **+2.5%** on placebo (treatment difference −20.8 percentage points), demonstrating substantial pharmacologic weight loss on top of, and far exceeding, lifestyle alone. `[type: rct]` [8]

**SURMOUNT-4 (Aronne et al., 2024, JAMA)** addresses durability and withdrawal. In this randomized-withdrawal design, 670 participants who had lost a mean of **20.9% during a 36-week open-label tirzepatide lead-in** were re-randomized to continue tirzepatide or switch to placebo for an additional 52 weeks. Those **continuing tirzepatide lost a further −5.5%**, whereas those **switched to placebo REGAINED +14.0%** (between-group difference −19.4 percentage points). By week 88, 89.5% of continued-tirzepatide participants maintained ≥80% of their peak weight loss versus only 16.6% of those switched to placebo. `[type: rct]` [9]

### Durability, dose-response, and cross-trial weight comparison

Across both programs, efficacy is **dose-dependent** (5 < 10 < 15 mg) for both HbA1c and weight, with the 15 mg dose delivering the largest effects. SURMOUNT-4 establishes that the weight benefit is **maintenance-dependent**: discontinuation leads to substantial regain, consistent with obesity as a chronic relapsing condition requiring ongoing treatment rather than a finite course. `[type: rct]`

On the cross-trial weight comparison to semaglutide: within the same trial (SURPASS-2, T2D), tirzepatide produced greater weight loss than semaglutide 1 mg at every dose [2]. Across separate trials, tirzepatide's ~20.9% mean weight loss in non-diabetic obesity (SURMOUNT-1) [1] is numerically larger than the ~14.9% reported for semaglutide 2.4 mg in the analogous STEP-1 trial; however, this is a **between-trial indirect comparison** (different protocols, populations, and semaglutide doses) and should be read as suggestive rather than as head-to-head proof. The head-to-head obesity trial (SURMOUNT-5, tirzepatide vs semaglutide 2.4 mg) is the appropriate source for a direct obesity comparison and is addressed elsewhere in this wiki rather than asserted here. `[type: rct]`

**Bottom line:** Tirzepatide is genuinely and substantially efficacious — HbA1c reductions of ~2.0–2.6% in T2D (superior to semaglutide 1 mg, insulin degludec, and insulin glargine) and mean weight loss reaching ~20.9% at 15 mg in non-diabetic obesity, attenuated to ~14.7% in T2D-obesity, durable while treatment continues and substantially reversible upon withdrawal. `[type: rct]`
