# MOTS-c — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for MOTS-c (Mitochondrial Open Reading Frame of the 12S rRNA Type-C). Non-English sources are admissible under the same tier rules as English; language is noted on each entry. Every citation below was located via a retrievable identifier (PMID / DOI / stable journal URL). No citation is fabricated.

Key admissibility distinction applied throughout: a study **authored by researchers at a Chinese or Japanese institution but published in English** (e.g., a PubMed-indexed English article in Scientific Reports or ACS Chemical Neuroscience, or an English-language conference abstract in a Japanese-published journal) is NOT counted as a "genuinely non-English source" — it is already reachable by the English layer. Only articles whose **primary publication language is Russian, Chinese, or Japanese** are counted here as genuine non-English primaries. The MOTS-c literature landscape differs sharply from TB-500 in this respect: every identified English-published study from Chinese institutions (seven papers, 2021–2024; see boundary section below) and Japanese institutions (five items, 2016–2025; see boundary section below) is excluded from the non-English count, and the genuine non-English literature is absent across all three languages. This is a genuine finding, not a gap in the search effort.

## Originator-country note

MOTS-c was discovered at the **University of Southern California** (Lee, Cohen, et al., 2015) and the originator-country literature is therefore **English** — covered by the main English research layer. No distinct non-English originator-country literature exists. This parallels the TB-500 finding for its US-origin literature.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

It adds nothing beyond the English corpus, because there is no admissible non-English primary. The finding is:

1. **Russian:** Zero admissible primaries. No Russian research group has published a MOTS-c study in Russian-language press. The one CyberLeninka article mentioning MOTS-c is a passing citation in a review on extracellular mitochondria — not a primary MOTS-c study.

2. **Chinese:** Zero admissible primaries. Chinese-institution MOTS-c research is extensive but exclusively published in English-language international journals. The Chinese-language press carries no MOTS-c primary that an exhaustive search of CNKI, CQVIP, Wanfang, and PKU-CCJ portals could locate.

3. **Japanese:** Zero admissible primaries. Japanese-group MOTS-c research (Fuku/Zempo/Kumagai SNP work, Kumagai muscle-atrophy and CK2 work) is entirely published in English. Two candidate items in Japanese journals — a conference poster abstract in 順天堂醫事雑誌 (2018) and a single-page conference abstract in 体力科学 (2025) — are English-language texts, not Japanese-language primaries. PubMed with Japanese language filter returned 0 results. J-STAGE and CiNii returned no genuine Japanese-language MOTS-c article.

The non-English layer therefore neither adds new evidence nor contradicts the English corpus. The English corpus — dominated by the USC lab (discovery, mechanism), Chengdu Sport University group (cardiac/exercise), Xuzhou Medical University group (pain), and the Fuku/Zempo/Kumagai group (SNP genetics, muscle biology) — is the entirety of the peer-reviewed MOTS-c evidence base as of mid-2026.
