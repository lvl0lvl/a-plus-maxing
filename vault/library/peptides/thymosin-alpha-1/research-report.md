# Thymosin Alpha-1 (Thymalfasin; Zadaxin)

## Metadata

- **class:** peptide (endogenous thymic peptide; approved pharmaceutical outside the US)
- **evidence_tier:** B — Multiple RCTs across HBV, CHC, vaccine adjuvancy, and sepsis. Marketing approval in >35 countries (China, Italy, India, and others) for chronic hepatitis B and C. The HBV evidence is the strongest: multiple registrational RCTs with ~40% virological response rates versus ~9% control. Vaccine-adjuvant signal is replicated across independent US academic and Italian groups. ## Summary

Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1; INN: thymalfasin; brand: Zadaxin, SciClone Pharmaceuticals) is a 28-amino-acid N-terminally acetylated peptide derived from the natural cleavage of prothymosin-α, the 113-amino-acid nuclear precursor. It was isolated from bovine calf thymus in 1977 by Allan Goldstein and colleagues at George Washington University, who established its sequence, named the thymosin peptide family, and proposed a role in T-lymphocyte regulation and differentiation [1, in_vitro]. The mature peptide (Ac-Ser-Asp-Ala-Ala-Val-Asp-Thr-Ser-Ser-Glu-Ile-Thr-Thr-Lys-Asp-Leu-Lys-Glu-Lys-Lys-Glu-Val-Glu-Glu-Glu-Ala-Glu-Asn) carries a molecular weight of approximately 3108 Da; N-terminal acetylation is required for full biological activity. Thymalfasin is the INN for the synthetic form, manufactured identically to the endogenous peptide sequence.

**What Tα1 is, precisely stated:** a bidirectional immunomodulator with documented TLR2 and TLR9 agonist activity on dendritic cells, driving Th1 polarization and CTL priming while simultaneously engaging an IDO/Treg tolerogenic arm that prevents immune overactivation. The net immunological effect in any given disease context reflects the prevailing immune tone rather than a fixed directional program — this bidirectionality is mechanistically real and context-dependent, and it underlies both Tα1's therapeutic versatility and its exceptionally clean safety record.

**The genuinely exceptional finding — approved pharmaceutical status:** Thymalfasin holds marketing approval in more than 35 countries — China, Italy, India, Philippines, Singapore, Russia, Argentina, Peru, and others across Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East — for chronic hepatitis B, chronic hepatitis C, and as an immunomodulatory adjuvant. This is not analogous to the unapproved, single-lab-studied experimental peptides. Thymalfasin carries an International Nonproprietary Name, has undergone formal Phase 3 registrational review in multiple jurisdictions, has a pharmacovigilance record spanning more than 25 years of post-market use, and has been studied in over 4,400 patients across controlled trials. This places it in a fundamentally different regulatory and evidence category than BPC-157, TB-500, Epithalon, or the other compounds in this library.

**The HBV evidence is the strongest clinical signal:** Chien et al. (1998) demonstrated ~40% complete virological response (HBV DNA clearance + HBeAg clearance) with a 26-week Tα1 regimen versus ~9% in controls in a three-arm RCT (n≈98, Taiwan; p=0.004) [2, rct]. A phase III US multicenter double-blind RCT (Mutchnick et al., 1999; n=97) showed numerically higher but statistically non-significant response (25% vs. 13%), an important cautionary data point [3, rct]. A meta-analysis of eight RCTs combining Tα1 with lamivudine found HBeAg seroconversion of 45.1% vs. 15.2% [4, meta_analysis]. Multiple independent groups — Andreone at Bologna, Mutchnick at Wayne State, Lim at National University Hospital Singapore, and Chan's CUHK meta-analysis — contributed CHB evidence outside the SciClone registrational track.

**The vaccine-adjuvant signal is real and independently replicated:** Gravenstein et al. (1989), working at US academic centers without commercial sponsorship, conducted the cleanest positive vaccine-adjuvant RCT: 90 elderly men (mean age 77.3 years) randomized double-blind to Tα1 plus influenza vaccine versus vaccine plus placebo showed significantly greater antibody titers in the Tα1 arm (p=0.023) [5, rct] — notably, the Gravenstein trial measured antibody-titer surrogates, not clinical-protection endpoints. Italian academic groups independently replicated the signal in hemodialysis patients (Carraro et al. 2012) [6, open_label]. These data predate SciClone's commercial entry and carry the cleanest independence status in the Tα1 literature.

**The most load-bearing honest point — sepsis deflation:** The early ETASS RCT (2013; n=361; Wu et al., Critical Care) showed a non-significant 28-day primary endpoint (26.0% vs 35.0%; RR 0.74; p=0.062). The in-hospital mortality figure (28.7% vs. 39.4%; p=0.032) is a secondary endpoint from the full text, not the headline result [7, rct]. This signal was treated, for a decade, as preliminary evidence warranting a definitive trial. That trial — TESTS (2025; BMJ; n=1,089 mITT; 22 Chinese centers; double-blind, placebo-controlled, Phase 3) — was definitively null on all outcomes: 28-day all-cause mortality HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.77–1.27; p=0.93) [8, rct]. A pre-specified age-stratified subgroup showed a directional signal in patients ≥60 years (see §2.4 for the full analysis and caveats — it is hypothesis-generating only, with CIs crossing 1.0). **Current evidence does not support Tα1 for unselected sepsis.** The TESTS trial was co-funded by SciClone Pharmaceuticals, making this a disconfirmatory sponsor-funded trial — an intellectually honest data point that cuts against commercial interest.

**COVID-19 evidence is observational only and contradicted:** Liu et al. (2020) reported 30-day mortality of 11.1% vs. 30.0% in a retrospective Wuhan cohort — with n=9 treated patients [9, cohort]. Contradictory observational data exists; no adequately powered RCT has been published.

**Cancer adjunct evidence is moderate-for-NSCLC, exploratory elsewhere:** A meta-analysis of 10 small Chinese RCTs in NSCLC found OR 1.86 for response rate; melanoma phase II RCTs showed 50% objective response; HCC evidence is retrospective. No practice-changing phase III cancer trial exists.

**Concentration: BELOW the 70% flag — genuinely independent evidence:** At least 8 distinct independent academic research groups from the USA, Italy, Hong Kong, Singapore, mainland China, and France have contributed primary evidence across different indications and decades. SciClone Pharmaceuticals directly co-funded approximately 1 of ~12 distinct primary trial clusters (the TESTS sepsis trial). This is a level of independent breadth that distinguishes Tα1 sharply from single-investigator series (Khavinson peptides, BPC-157 Cohen program). Estimated maximum SciClone concentration: 15–20% of the primary evidence base.

**Regulatory — the US situation is nuanced and consequential:** Tα1 has NO FDA marketing approval for any indication. Orphan drug designations exist for melanoma and hepatocellular carcinoma (these confer exclusivity windows if approved but do not constitute approval) [10, mechanism_review]. The FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted against adding Tα1 to the 503A Bulks List in December 2024, effectively rendering it off-limits for US 503A compounding pharmacies [11, regulatory]. This distinguishes the US access landscape sharply from the 35+ countries where it is a routinely prescribed pharmaceutical.

**WADA:** Thymosin beta-4 (TB-500) is explicitly prohibited under WADA 2026 S2.3. Thymosin alpha-1 is NOT explicitly named. The "thymosin" name shared by Tα1 and Tβ4 is a source of consumer confusion; these are structurally and functionally distinct peptides with different sequences, mechanisms, and evidence bases. S0 and the broad S2 catch-all introduce theoretical ambiguity; verification with the relevant national anti-doping organization is required before any athletic use.

**Anti-aging and longevity use:** The mechanistic rationale for Tα1 in immunosenescence is coherent — thymic involution reduces endogenous Tα1 production, and Tα1 IL-7 stimulation, dendritic-cell priming, and Treg–Th1 balance restoration are precisely the deficits of the immunosenescent state. The vaccine-adjuvant evidence in elderly populations is the most direct human support. However, there is no dedicated longevity or lifespan RCT, and the anti-aging/immune-restoration use is explicitly an extension beyond approved indications. Grey-market US "Tα1" carries purity concerns (6–15% below claimed levels per vendor-ecosystem surveys; no independent analytical-lab corroboration of this range exists) and the Tα1-vs-Tβ4 identity confusion is documented in the vendor landscape.

## 1. Identity and Molecular Mechanism

### 1.1 Identity

Thymosin alpha-1 is the first and best-characterized active peptide from thymosin fraction 5 (TF5), a partially purified thymic extract developed in the 1970s. The 1977 Goldstein et al. paper in *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* established the 28-amino-acid sequence and named the thymosin family convention, proposing a role in the regulation and differentiation of T lymphocytes [1, in_vitro]. The molecular weight of the acetylated mature peptide is approximately 3108 Da, identical to the Zadaxin clinical standard. The N-terminal acetyl group on the first residue (serine) is required for full biological activity; unacetylated analogs show sharply reduced immunomodulatory potency.

**Precursor and processing.** Tα1 is a cleavage product of prothymosin-α (ProTα), a 113-amino-acid nuclear protein that functions independently as a chromatin-associated factor. The lysosomal asparaginyl endopeptidase legumain (mammalian AEP) cleaves ProTα at Asn28-Gly29 and Asn35-Gly36, generating Tα1 and the related fragment thymosin alpha-11; this has been demonstrated in mammalian tissue extracts, confirming Tα1 is a genuine endogenous peptide rather than an extraction artifact [12, in_vitro]. Endogenous Tα1 levels decline with age in parallel with thymic involution, which is the biological substrate for its proposed immunosenescence relevance.

### 1.2 Molecular Mechanism of Immunomodulation

Tα1 exerts pleiotropic effects across innate and adaptive immune compartments. Its development as a therapeutic preceded full target deconvolution — Garaci et al. (2024) describe it as a paradigmatic "phenotypic drug" whose receptor-level biology was clarified relatively late [13, mechanism_review]. The mechanistic picture maps onto three partially overlapping nodes: (1) TLR-dependent innate sensing and dendritic-cell programming, (2) T-cell maturation and Th1/Th2 re-balancing, and (3) tolerogenic/regulatory immune tuning via IDO.

**TLR2 and TLR9 agonism.** The strongest mechanistic evidence positions Tα1 as a TLR2 and TLR9 agonist on myeloid and plasmacytoid dendritic cells. Romani et al. (2006), working in murine bone marrow-derived DCs and human monocyte-derived DCs, showed that Tα1 induces IDO expression via a TLR9-dependent mechanism requiring type I interferon receptor co-signaling; TLR9-deficient DCs did not respond [14, animal]. Bozza et al. (2007) confirmed the TLR9/MyD88/IRF7 axis in vivo using murine cytomegalovirus infection in BALB/c and C57BL/6 backgrounds (n=8–12 per group): Tα1 protected susceptible mice from lethal infection by activating plasmacytoid DCs through TLR9 → MyD88 → IRF7, driving IFN-α and IFN-γ production; protection was abrogated in TLR9-KO and MyD88-KO mice [15, animal]. Tao et al. (2023) summarize TLR2 engagement activating the MyD88/NF-κB/p38 MAPK pathway toward pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, while TLR9 additionally drives the MyD88/IRF7 branch toward type I interferon induction [16, mechanism_review].

**Dendritic-cell maturation and Th1 polarization.** Tα1-stimulated DCs upregulate MHC class II (HLA-DR), CD80, CD86, and IL-12 p70. The downstream consequence is polarization of naive T cells toward Th1 and CD8+ cytotoxic T-lymphocyte (CTL) phenotypes. In chronic HCV patients, Tα1 treatment increased IL-2 and IFN-γ while decreasing Th2 cytokines IL-4 and IL-10 [17, mechanism_review]. NK cell cytotoxicity is enhanced in immunodeficiency contexts, though this mechanism is less fully characterized at the molecular level.

**Tolerogenic arm: IDO, regulatory T cells, and bidirectional immunomodulation.** A defining feature of Tα1 is its capacity to simultaneously support effector immunity and immune tolerance. Romani et al. (2006) showed that Tα1-primed DCs that induced IDO also generated regulatory T cells (Tregs) and IL-10 production while maintaining Th1-polarizing capacity in parallel — an immune balance phenotype rather than pure immune activation [14, animal]. In the context of pre-existing inflammatory or autoimmune conditions, Tα1's induction of the IDO/Treg arm may explain its anti-inflammatory effects; in immunodeficient or infection states, the TLR9/Th1/CTL arm predominates. The Romani et al. (2012) *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* review synthesizes this as Tα1 being able to "enhance T cell, dendritic cell, and antibody responses, modulate cytokine and chemokine production, and block steroid-induced apoptosis of thymocytes" depending on immunological context [17, mechanism_review]. The state of knowledge as of 2024 acknowledges this bidirectionality as mechanistically real but context-dependent; the peptide's net immunological effect reflects the prevailing immune tone rather than a fixed directional program [13, mechanism_review].

**Antioxidant effects (in vitro).** Tα1 reduces intracellular reactive oxygen species and enhances catalase, superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase activity in A549 human lung adenocarcinoma cells at 3–12 µg/mL [18, in_vitro]. Garaci et al. (2024) list antioxidant activity among Tα1's characterized biological effects [13, mechanism_review]. Whether this reflects direct Nrf2 pathway activation or indirect downstream MAPK signaling is not established, and no clinical trial has isolated antioxidant activity as a primary endpoint.

**T-cell maturation.** Tα1 promotes differentiation of CD4+/CD8+ T cells from precursors and rescues T-cell function in immunosuppressed states, including radiation, chemotherapy, tumor burden, and aging. In a Lewis lung carcinoma mouse model (C57BL/6), Naylor et al. (2007) demonstrated that Tα1 combined with IRX-2 cytokine immunotherapy produced synergistic survival improvements, consistent with Tα1 potentiating cytokine-driven T-cell restoration rather than acting through an independent pathway [19, animal].

## 2. Clinical Evidence: Per-Indication Tiers

### 2.1 Chronic Hepatitis B — Registrational Evidence (Strongest Tier)

The clearest RCT evidence for Tα1 is in chronic hepatitis B (CHB). Chien et al. (1998) published a three-arm RCT in *Hepatology* (n≈98 HBeAg-positive CHB patients, Taiwan) comparing 26-week Tα1 (SC injection), 52-week Tα1, and observation [2, rct]. At 18-month follow-up, complete virological response rates were 40.6% (26-week arm), 26.5% (52-week arm), and 9.4% (control); the 26-week arm was significantly superior (p=0.004). This trial established the standard 6-month dosing regimen and formed part of the registration package. No SciClone funding was disclosed; the sponsor was the Taiwanese government research system.

Mutchnick et al. (1999), a phase III multicenter double-blind placebo-controlled US trial (n=97, Wayne State University), administered Tα1 1.6 mg SC twice weekly for 6 months [3, rct]. Numerically higher response rates (25% vs. 13% sustained HBV DNA loss) favored Tα1 but did not reach statistical significance (p<0.11). This is the critical cautionary datum: not all CHB trials hit their primary endpoint, and population differences (this trial enrolled anti-HBe, HBV-DNA-positive patients) may partly explain the discrepancy with the Chien cohort.

A meta-analysis of eight RCTs (n=583 patients), all predominantly Chinese trials, comparing lamivudine + Tα1 versus lamivudine alone reported HBeAg seroconversion of 45.1% vs. 15.2% in favor of the combination [4, meta_analysis].

Independent academic contributions to the CHB evidence include: Andreone et al. at the University of Bologna (parallel Italian RCT comparing Tα1 versus IFN-α in anti-HBe HBV-DNA-positive patients) [20, rct]; Lim et al. at National University Hospital Singapore (98 HBeAg-positive patients, randomized placebo-controlled trial of Tα1 plus lymphoblastoid IFN; *Antiviral Therapy*, 2006) [21, rct]; and Chan et al. at the Chinese University of Hong Kong, whose five-trial meta-analysis (353 patients; *Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics*, 2001) found significant delayed virological response at 12 months (OR 2.67; 95% CI 1.25–5.68) [22, meta_analysis]. None of these trials disclosed SciClone funding, establishing a genuine multi-national, multi-group, largely independent CHB evidence base.

**Evidence tier for CHB: Moderate-strong.** Multiple RCTs in registrational dossiers; primary endpoint failure in the US phase III trial is the main caveat; approved in >35 countries, not FDA.

**Modern treatment context:** The CHB evidence base is largely pre-modern-nucleos(t)ide-analogue era. The current first-line standard of care — entecavir and tenofovir disoproxil/alafenamide — achieves durable viral suppression in >95% of patients with excellent tolerability, shifting the clinical goalposts substantially. Analogous to the DAA revolution that rendered Tα1's HCV indication obsolete (§2.2), the nucleos(t)ide analogue era raises the bar for any Tα1-based CHB regimen: the clinically relevant question is no longer whether Tα1 beats observation or lamivudine (the trial comparators) but whether it adds value over or alongside entecavir/tenofovir, which has not been directly tested. The approval history and registrational evidence remain genuine; the clinical treatment context has evolved since the trials were run.

### 2.2 Chronic Hepatitis C — Combination with Interferon (Weak-Moderate)

Sherman et al. (1998) conducted a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial (*Hepatology*; n=109 CHC patients; USA) comparing interferon-alpha + Tα1 (1.6 mg SC twice weekly) versus interferon + placebo [23, rct]. End-of-treatment biochemical response was 37.1% vs. 16.2% (p=0.04); sustained biochemical response was 14.2% vs. 8.1% (not statistically significant). A pooled analysis of available combination trials found significant end-treatment biochemical response (44.7% vs. 22%; p=0.01) but non-significant sustained response (22.4% vs. 11.1%; p=0.10) [24, meta_analysis].

Andreone et al. at Bologna conducted a separate double-blind placebo-controlled pilot trial in chronic hepatitis C (*Liver*, 1996; PMID 8873009; n=19) [25, rct]. Italian group, independent of SciClone's registrational track. **This was a null result:** Tα1 did not clear HCV-RNA; only one patient achieved transient ALT normalization and subsequently relapsed. It should not be read as supportive HCV efficacy evidence.

**Contextual note:** The DAA (direct-acting antiviral) revolution has made interferon-based HCV regimens clinically obsolete. Tα1's HCV indication reflects a pre-DAA approval era; it is no longer clinically relevant in DAA-accessible markets.

**Evidence tier for CHC: Weak-to-moderate.** End-of-treatment signal; sustained response not robustly demonstrated; clinical setting is now obsolete.

### 2.3 Vaccine Adjuvant — Elderly and Dialysis Populations (Moderate)

Gravenstein et al. (1989) conducted the founding vaccine-adjuvant RCT in *Journal of the American Geriatrics Society* (n=90 enrolled, n=85 analyzed; mean age 77.3 years, long-term care setting; randomized double-blind; 5 subjects excluded due to unacceptable sera) [5, rct]. Tα1 (900 µg/m² SC twice weekly × 8 doses) alongside trivalent influenza vaccination produced significantly greater antibody titers (p=0.023 in the 85-subject analyzed cohort), driven by the oldest subjects (≥77 years; p=0.039). **Surrogate caveat:** the Gravenstein trial measured antibody-titer surrogates, not clinical-protection endpoints (influenza incidence or confirmed illness). Antibody titers are the accepted immunogenicity surrogate for influenza vaccine trials, but titer augmentation does not guarantee a proportional reduction in clinical disease — the clinical-endpoint translation is addressed only in the subsequent Phase 2 influenza-incidence data (§3.2). This trial was conducted by US academic groups (Ershler, Gravenstein at the Medical College of Wisconsin and Brown University) before Zadaxin reached market, with no commercial sponsorship — the cleanest independence status in the Tα1 literature.

Ershler, Gravenstein, and Geloo (2007), summarizing multiple completed Tα1 influenza-vaccine trials in *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*, reported that in a subsequent Phase 2 study, biweekly Tα1 alongside influenza vaccination reduced confirmed influenza incidence from 19% (vaccine alone) to 6% (vaccine + Tα1; p=0.002) — a clinically meaningful reduction [26, mechanism_review]. The article explicitly calls for larger RCTs.

Carraro et al. (2012), an Italian academic group (*Vaccine*; n=94 hemodialysis patients), demonstrated that Tα1 co-administration at 3.2 mg and 6.4 mg with adjuvanted pandemic H1N1 influenza vaccine achieved CHMP seroconversion and seroprotection criteria that the vaccine-alone arm failed in this dialysis population (p<0.01); both Tα1 doses were well tolerated with no hematological or biochemical adverse effects [6, open_label]. The signal extends to immunocompromised non-elderly populations and was conducted by Italian academic groups independent of the US Ershler/Gravenstein collaboration.

Panatto et al. (2011) provided a narrative review of vaccine-adjuvant trials in elderly and immunocompromised cohorts, concluding that Tα1 enhances T-cell, dendritic-cell, and antibody responses and improves seroprotection [27, mechanism_review].

An ongoing Phase 1 safety trial (NCT06821100; sponsor: Methodist Hospital Research Institute) is enrolling adults ≥65 years to receive thymalfasin as adjuvant to COVID-19 booster vaccination, representing the contemporary extension of the vaccine-adjuvant hypothesis to mRNA-platform vaccines [28, regulatory]; no efficacy results are yet available. Note: this is a Phase 1 trial (safety primary), not an efficacy RCT.

**Evidence tier for vaccine adjuvant: Moderate.** Biologically well-grounded; consistent signal across independent US academic and Italian groups; positive elderly trial predates SciClone market entry; modest aggregate scale; no large modern phase III trial.

### 2.4 Sepsis — Two Landmark RCTs and the Definitive Deflation

**ETASS (2013; Wu et al., *Critical Care*):** n=361 (Tα1 n=181; control n=180); multicenter, single-blind RCT in six tertiary hospitals in China; patients with severe sepsis [7, rct]. The **primary endpoint — 28-day all-cause mortality** — showed 26.0% (Tα1) vs. 35.0% (control); RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.54–1.02), p=0.062 by unadjusted analysis; a pre-specified log-rank survival analysis reached p=0.049. **The primary endpoint did not reach p<0.05.** In-hospital mortality (a **pre-specified secondary endpoint** in the full-text ETASS analysis) showed 28.7% vs. 39.4%, RR 0.73, p=0.032 — this is a secondary-analysis figure from the full text, not the trial's headline result. However, no significant differences appeared in ICU mortality, ventilation-free days, ICU-free days, or duration of mechanical ventilation. Funding: Guangdong Natural Science Foundation and Guangdong Medical Scientific Research Foundation — academic/government; no commercial sponsor; no competing interests declared.

ETASS also documented that Tα1 significantly improved monocyte HLA-DR expression on Days 3 and 7 — a direct readout of immune reconstitution, and a mechanistic corroboration of the immunosenescence hypothesis (monocyte HLA-DR is the same marker depressed in the immunoparalyzed and immunosenescent state).

**TESTS (2025; Wu et al., *BMJ*):** n=1,089 mITT (Tα1 n=542; placebo n=547); 22-centre, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial in China (September 2016–December 2020) [8, rct]. This is the definitive, adequately powered trial. Primary outcome: 28-day all-cause mortality 23.4% (Tα1) vs. 24.1% (placebo), HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.77–1.27), p=0.93. Null result. No significant difference in any secondary or safety endpoint. A pre-specified age-stratified subgroup interaction was significant (p=0.01): patients ≥60 years showed a directional benefit (HR 0.81; 95% CI 0.61–1.09) while those <60 showed a directional signal in the opposite direction (HR 1.67). This interaction is hypothesis-generating — not confirmatory, given the CI crossing 1.0 in the older subgroup — but consistent with the immunosenescence framing that Tα1 may reconstitute immune function in those whose immunity is already depleted, while potentially dysregulating responses that remain intact. Sponsor: SciClone Pharmaceuticals (listed alongside Sun Yat-sen University and Guangdong Clinical Research Center); multiple senior authors disclosed consultancy fees from SciClone. The sponsor-funded nature of a null trial that goes against commercial interest is itself an important data point.

**Sepsis meta-analyses.** Liu et al. (2016), a systematic review in *BMC Infectious Diseases* pooling 19 RCTs (1,354 patients, all China), reported significant 28-day mortality reduction (RR 0.59; 95% CI 0.45–0.77; p<0.001) [29, meta_analysis]. A 2025 *Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology* meta-analysis (Gu et al.; 11 RCTs; 1,927 patients; all China) found pooled OR 0.73 (95% CI 0.59–0.90; p=0.003) [30, meta_analysis]. However, the 2025 meta-analysis authors explicitly noted that high-quality and multicenter subgroup analyses showed no mortality benefit and that trial sequential analysis indicated insufficient sample size even after pooling — before TESTS. After TESTS (n=1,089 alone), the positive meta-analytic signal is largely neutralized by the weight of the definitive Phase 3 result.

**Honest verdict on sepsis:** ETASS (2013) showed a suggestive in-hospital mortality signal in a sub-analysis with marginal primary-endpoint significance. The large, double-blind, adequately powered TESTS Phase 3 trial (2025) was negative on all outcomes. Current evidence does NOT support Tα1 for unselected sepsis.

### 2.5 COVID-19 — Observational Data Only (Weak)

Liu et al. (2020; *Clinical Infectious Diseases*; n=76 severe COVID-19 patients from two Wuhan hospitals; retrospective cohort) reported 30-day mortality of 11.1% in Tα1-treated patients (n=9) vs. 30.0% in untreated comparators (n=10), p=0.044 [9, cohort]. This is a very small retrospective study with serious confounding risk: the treated group comprised n=9 patients, selection was non-random, and the comparison group was contemporaneous but not randomized. The finding is hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

Subsequent observational studies produced contradictory results: one analysis of 771 COVID-19 patients found no mortality association in critically ill patients; another propensity-matched retrospective study (Wang et al.) found immunomodulatory therapies including Tα1 associated with higher mortality, likely confounded by severity-of-illness channeling bias. No adequately powered RCT of Tα1 for COVID-19 has been published.

**The largest COVID-19 observational study — and its directional-harm result — must be stated explicitly.** Liu et al. (2021; *Frontiers in Immunology* 12:673693; PMID 34408744), a multicenter retrospective cohort spanning five tertiary hospitals in Hubei, China (December 2019–March 2020; n=2,282; Tα1 recipients n=306; controls n=1,976; funded by China's National Natural Science Foundation; no COI declared), is the largest Tα1 COVID-19 cohort in the published literature [31, cohort]. **It found that Tα1 was associated with WORSE outcomes, not better:** adjusted OR 1.5 (95% CI 1.1–2.1, p=0.028) for non-recovery; non-recovery rate 65/306 (21.2%) in Tα1 recipients vs. 290/1,976 (14.7%) controls; in-hospital mortality approximately 20.2% (Tα1) vs. approximately 13.7% (control); ARDS rates approximately 33.9% (Tα1) vs. approximately 25.2% (control). The authors concluded that Tα1 "was associated with an increased non-recovery rate, especially in those with greater disease severity." This directional-harm finding almost certainly reflects severity-of-illness channeling bias (sicker patients were preferentially treated with Tα1), but after adjustment the harm association persisted. The honest characterization of the COVID-19 evidence is therefore not merely "mixed" — the single largest cohort showed an adjusted harm signal. This makes the pre-existing small-positive (Liu 2020, n=9 treated) and small-null findings even weaker as an evidence base, and reinforces that **Tα1 cannot be recommended for COVID-19/acute respiratory illness on existing data.**

**Evidence tier for COVID-19: Weak (observational only), with the largest study showing an adjusted harm association.** The n=9-treated Liu 2020 positive signal is massively outweighed by the n=306-treated Liu 2021 harm finding. Cannot be interpreted as efficacy evidence; the directional-harm signal in the largest cohort is load-bearing for any plan considering Tα1 for respiratory use.

### 2.6 Cancer Adjunct — Phase II RCTs and Small Meta-Analyses (Moderate for NSCLC; Exploratory Elsewhere)

The cancer evidence was synthesized by Costantini et al. (2019; *Frontiers in Oncology* 9:873; DOI 10.3389/fonc.2019.00873; ERC-funded) [32, mechanism_review]. **COI note:** Enrico Garaci (who co-founded the commercial thymosin program and has long-term ties to the Tα1 IP lineage) and Allan Goldstein (the original discoverer; co-founder of Alpha 1 Biomedicals) are listed as co-authors of this review, despite no formal COI being declared. The synthesis conclusions on cancer evidence should be read with this relationship in mind — the review is a starting point, not a disinterested third-party appraisal:

**NSCLC:** A meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (n=724 advanced/stage III–IV NSCLC patients) comparing Tα1 plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone found OR 1.86 for overall response rate and OR 3.06 for tumor control rate. A phase II sequential chemoimmunotherapy study (cisplatin + etoposide + Tα1 + IFN-α2a; n=56) achieved 43% objective response and 12.3-month median survival. A separate analysis reported 14.3% higher 5-year survival in stage IIIA NSCLC with Tα1 co-administration (HR=0.72; p=0.032). All trials were small-to-medium, predominantly Chinese; independent replication outside China is lacking.

An early Italian open-label trial (Salvati, Rasi, Portalone, Antilli, Garaci; *Anticancer Research*, 1996) combined Tα1 with low-dose IFN-α after ifosfamide in NSCLC, representing the European academic entry into Tα1 oncology [33, open_label].

**Melanoma:** A phase II multicenter dose-ranging RCT used dacarbazine ± IFN-alpha ± Tα1 and reported 50% objective response in the combination arm. Sequential Tα1-then-ipilimumab in a compassionate-use program showed 38.4 months median OS versus 8 months historical ipilimumab-alone — but this is uncontrolled retrospective data, not an RCT.

**HCC:** Retrospective evaluations of post-resection adjuvant Tα1 and combination TACE + Tα1 studies. No adequately powered RCT for HCC as primary endpoint.

**Evidence tier for cancer: Moderate for NSCLC (meta-analysis of multiple RCTs, consistent directional signal); exploratory/phase II for melanoma; retrospective for HCC.** No practice-changing phase III cancer trial in any cancer type. Population is largely Chinese; generalizability is uncertain.

### 2.7 Per-Indication Evidence Summary

| Indication | Best evidence design | Evidence tier | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic hepatitis B | Multiple RCTs + registration package | Moderate-strong | Genuine regulatory signal; not all trials hit primary endpoint; approved >35 countries, not FDA |

## 3. Immunosenescence, Immune Restoration, and the Aging Context

### 3.1 Mechanistic Rationale for Immunosenescence Applications

Aging is accompanied by thymic involution — the progressive, age-dependent atrophy of the thymus that begins in early adulthood and reduces naïve T-cell output by roughly 90% by the seventh decade. The consequences are a narrowed T-cell receptor repertoire, impaired responses to novel antigens (including vaccines), accumulating exhausted and senescent effector-memory cells, and a low-grade chronic inflammatory tone termed "inflammaging." Because Tα1 is a native thymic peptide, serum levels decline in parallel with thymic involution, and this age-correlated fall has been proposed as both a biomarker of immune age and a pharmacological target.

A 2025 mechanism-focused review in *International Journal of Molecular Sciences* (Simonova et al.) synthesized the mechanistic rationale for Tα1 in aging [34, mechanism_review]. The authors document that Tα1 stimulates IL-7 secretion (a key thymocyte-survival cytokine), activates protein kinase C to prevent thymocyte apoptosis, upregulates HLA-DR and co-stimulatory markers (CD40, CD80, CD86) on dendritic cells, and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines TNFα, IL-1β, and IL-6 while elevating IL-10. These are precisely the deficits of the immunosenescent state, providing a coherent mechanistic rationale — though this is a narrative synthesis, not a clinical intervention meta-analysis.

### 3.2 Human RCT Evidence in Elderly and Immunocompromised Populations

The most concrete human evidence for Tα1's immunosenescence relevance is the Gravenstein (1989) elderly influenza vaccine RCT (n=90 enrolled, n=85 analyzed; p=0.023 in the analyzed cohort) [5, rct] and the Ershler (2007) summary of multiple completed vaccine-adjuvant trials [26, mechanism_review], both described in §2.3 above. The key additional datum from Ershler: a Phase 2 study reduced confirmed influenza incidence from 19% (vaccine alone) to 6% (vaccine + Tα1; p=0.002) in elderly populations — a clinical endpoint, not just an immunogenicity surrogate. The Carraro (2012) hemodialysis pilot trial extends the signal to an immunocompromised non-elderly population via an Italian academic group independent of the Ershler/Gravenstein collaboration [6, open_label].

### 3.3 Immune Reconstitution in Severe Illness: The Sepsis-Immunosenescence Parallel

The sepsis-induced immunoparalysis state — collapsed monocyte HLA-DR, lymphopenia, exhausted CD8+ T cells — is mechanistically analogous to accelerated immunosenescence. ETASS demonstrated that Tα1 significantly improved monocyte HLA-DR in septic patients on Days 3 and 7 [7, rct], providing direct human mechanistic evidence that Tα1 can reconstitute an immunosenescent-like phenotype. The TESTS Phase 3 trial (§2.4) included a pre-specified age-stratified interaction that was directionally consistent with this immunosenescence hypothesis — though with CIs crossing 1.0 and the overall trial decisively null, the finding is hypothesis-generating only [8, rct] and should not be interpreted as evidence of efficacy in any subgroup.

### 3.4 Limits of the Aging-Immune Evidence

The positive vaccine-adjuvant trials in elderly populations are real, replicated across independent groups, and published in credible journals. They are, however, modest in scale: the two key elderly trials total a few hundred subjects, not thousands. The clinical significance of augmented antibody titers versus protection against disease endpoints was addressed only in the Phase 2 influenza-incidence study. No completed large-scale RCT of Tα1 as a vaccine adjuvant in the modern high-dose or mRNA-vaccine era exists. The anti-aging/longevity use — extending Tα1's immunomodulatory logic to slow immunosenescence and improve healthspan — is a mechanistically plausible extrapolation from the vaccine-adjuvant evidence, but it has not been directly tested in a lifespan or healthspan-endpoint RCT.

## 4. Global Approval Landscape and Evidence Independence

### 4.1 Global Approval Status

Thymalfasin (Zadaxin) received its first international approvals beginning 1999 and currently holds marketing approval in more than 35 countries [10, mechanism_review] [35, regulatory]. Approved jurisdictions include China (the dominant market, with approval for chronic hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and use as an immunomodulatory adjuvant in cancer and critical illness), Italy (national approval covering hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and melanoma), the Philippines, Singapore, India, Argentina, Peru, Russia, and additional markets across Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East. Approved indications vary by jurisdiction; the core approvals are for chronic hepatitis B and C.

**FDA (United States):** Tα1 has NO FDA marketing approval for any indication. Orphan Drug Designations were obtained for malignant melanoma and hepatocellular carcinoma [10, mechanism_review]; these confer exclusivity windows if approved but do not constitute marketing approval. SciClone elected not to pursue a full FDA NDA for standard hepatitis indications, judging the US hepatitis B market insufficient relative to the Phase 3 investment required.

**EU:** No EMA centralized marketing approval exists. Italy's national procedure is the primary European regulatory foothold.

The regulatory contrast with unapproved grey-market research peptides is substantive and real. Thymalfasin has undergone formal Phase 3 registrational review in multiple jurisdictions, carries an INN, has an established pharmacovigilance record spanning decades of post-market use — markers of regulatory maturity entirely absent from compounds like BPC-157, TB-500, Epithalon, or Khavinson series peptides.

### 4.2 Evidence Independence: An Honest Assessment

Tα1's literature is structurally different from single-group or single-sponsor series. Multiple independent threads converge:

**US academic thread (1980s–2000s):** Ershler and Gravenstein at the Medical College of Wisconsin and Brown University conducted vaccine-adjuvant RCTs without commercial sponsorship before SciClone's market entry. Mutchnick at Wayne State University ran the Phase III CHB trial with government/academic funding. These constitute the most conflict-free primary evidence.

**Italian clinical thread:** Garaci, Romani, and Italian collaborators at the University of Rome Tor Vergata and IRCCS San Raffaele ran parallel programs on Tα1 in infectious disease, cancer immunology, and aspergillosis — funded through Italian research foundations and ERC grants, not SciClone [32, mechanism_review]. Note: the Garaci group's independence from SciClone is genuine on funding grounds, but Garaci has long-standing ties to the Tα1 program as a principal investigator across decades; Garaci and Goldstein (the discoverer) are co-authors on the Costantini 2019 cancer synthesis review without a formal COI declaration (see §2.6). This is a program-affiliation relationship distinct from commercial sponsor funding, but worth noting when weighting synthesis reviews originating from this group. Andreone at Bologna ran independent hepatitis trials [20, rct] [25, rct]. Carraro ran the hemodialysis vaccine trial independently [6, open_label].

**Asian academic threads:** The CUHK group (Chan, Tang, Tam, Sung) ran an independent meta-analysis [22, meta_analysis]. Lim at NUH Singapore ran an independent HBV trial [21, rct]. Wu and Guan at Sun Yat-sen University ran ETASS with academic government funding (SciClone did not sponsor ETASS) [7, rct]. Multiple additional Chinese academic centers contributed to the sepsis and hepatitis meta-analyses.

**SciClone-sponsored work:** The TESTS Phase 3 sepsis trial co-funded by SciClone is the primary instance of commercial sponsor concentration in the Tα1 literature [8, rct]. Registrational hepatitis B/C approvals in China, Singapore, and other markets drew on both independent academic trials and SciClone-organized registration packages.

**Caveats on independence:** The Italian and Chinese threads overlap with jurisdictions where thymalfasin is approved and commercially available, which means investigator independence and industry relationships require scrutiny on a trial-by-trial basis. The US academic thread is the cleanest. For the sepsis literature, all primary RCTs are China-sourced, which limits generalizability and raises publication ecosystem concerns (publication bias was detected in the 2025 meta-analysis [30, meta_analysis]). The Garaci/Romani Italian group has long-standing ties to the Tα1 program, though external funding sources are documented.

**Concentration verdict: BELOW the 70% flag.** SciClone directly co-sponsored approximately 1 of ~12 distinct primary trial clusters. Estimated maximum sponsor concentration is 15–20% of the primary evidence base. This is a genuine multi-group, multi-country, multi-indication evidence base — one of the more independently replicated in the immunomodulatory peptide space. The sharp contrast with single-investigator series (Khavinson, BPC-157 Cohen program) is real.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics, Dosing, and Route

### 5.1 Route of Administration

Thymosin alpha-1 is administered exclusively by **subcutaneous (SC) injection** [35, regulatory]. The lyophilized powder (Zadaxin) is reconstituted with 1 mL sterile water for injection immediately before use, yielding a 1.6 mg/mL solution. Intravenous and oral routes are not established for clinical use. SC administration provides near-complete bioavailability with rapid absorption.

### 5.2 Pharmacokinetics

The PK profile of Tα1 was characterized in a randomized three-way crossover study (n=9 healthy volunteers) comparing three marketed formulations at 900 µg/m² SC (Rost et al., 1999; *International Journal of Clinical Pharmacology and Therapeutics*) [36, rct]:

| Parameter | Observed values |
|---|---|
| Tmax | 1–2 hours post-SC injection |
| Cmax | 30–80 µg/L (formulation-dependent) |
| AUC(0–∞) | 95–267 µg·h/L |
| t½ | <3 hours (Zadaxin label cites ~2 hours) |
| Volume of distribution | ~30–40 L |
| Urinary excretion | 31–60% of administered dose (Zadaxin prescribing information) |
| Accumulation | None observed on repeat dosing |

Serum concentrations return to baseline within 24 hours. Dose-proportional kinetics were confirmed across single doses of 0.8–6.4 mg and multi-dose regimens of 1.6–16 mg over 5–7 days (Zadaxin prescribing information) [35, regulatory]. Tα1 is not metabolized by hepatic CYP450 enzymes; renal clearance accounts for the majority of elimination. No clinically significant drug-drug interactions have been identified. Tα1 is the active form and is not a prodrug.

### 5.3 Approved and Trial-Derived Dosing

**All doses in this section are label-derived or from peer-reviewed trial protocols — not grey-market sources. These are citable.**

**Chronic Hepatitis B and C (Approved Label):**
- Dose: 1.6 mg (equivalent to 900 µg/m²) SC **twice weekly**
- Duration: 6 to 12 months
- Special populations: patients <40 kg receive 40 µg/kg SC twice weekly

This foundational approved regimen was the reference dose for the hepatitis RCTs.

**Sepsis (ETASS Trial Protocol):**
- Dose: 1.6 mg SC twice daily × 5 consecutive days, then once daily × 2 days
- Initiation: within 4 hours of enrollment
- No Tα1-related SAEs were recorded

**Vaccine Adjuvant (Trial-Derived):**
- 900 µg/m² SC twice weekly (Gravenstein elderly RCT) [5, rct]
- 3.2 mg or 6.4 mg SC (Carraro hemodialysis trial) [6, open_label]
- Biweekly injections beginning at time of vaccination (general adjuvant protocol per Ershler review) [26, mechanism_review]

**Grey-market / longevity-community conventions:** Communities discussing Tα1 for "immune optimization" cite doses of 0.5–1.6 mg SC twice weekly on various schedules. No vendor, protocol document, or anecdotal aggregate grounds these as evidence-based. These conventions are noted for context only; no number from grey-market sources grounds any claim in this report.

## 6. Safety Profile

### 6.1 Overview

Tα1 carries a genuinely exceptional clinical safety record. This is an approved pharmaceutical with more than 25 years of post-market use in more than 35 countries, studied in over 4,400 patients across controlled trials. Key markers:

- Zero drug-related severe adverse events (SAEs) in the ETASS RCT [7, rct]
- Zero Tα1-related SAEs across 19 pooled sepsis RCTs in the Liu et al. (2016) meta-analysis [29, meta_analysis]
- No treatment discontinuations due to Tα1 intolerance in any trial arm reviewed
- The TESTS Phase 3 trial (n=1,089) confirmed no significant difference in safety endpoints between Tα1 and placebo [8, rct]

**Critical scope of this safety record:** The exceptional safety profile above — zero drug-related SAEs, >25-year post-market record — applies to **GMP-manufactured Zadaxin/thymalfasin** (the approved pharmaceutical product with characterized potency, sterility, and purity). It does NOT extend to grey-market "Tα1" research chemicals of unverified identity and purity. Grey-market product carries documented purity shortfalls (~6–15% below label) and a real identity-confusion risk: some grey-market "Tα1" products are actually Thymosin Beta-4 (Tβ4/TB-500), a completely different peptide that is WADA-prohibited. The safety record of the approved GMP product cannot be assumed to transfer to uncharacterized preparations.

This safety record contrasts sharply with experimental peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c) that lack approved-drug safety databases.

### 6.2 Documented Adverse Events

**Common (mild, infrequent):**
- Injection-site reactions: erythema, mild discomfort, local tenderness — the dominant AE class
- Transient mild injection-site atrophy (rare, with repeated dosing)

**Less common:**
- Polyarthralgia with hand edema (rare; Zadaxin label)
- Rash (rare; Zadaxin label)

**Combination therapy (Tα1 + interferon):** AEs in combination studies (fever, fatigue, myalgia, nausea, vomiting, neutropenia) are attributed predominantly to interferon, not Tα1 monotherapy. When assessed as monotherapy, Tα1 AEs are indistinguishable from local injection-site effects.

**Systemic immunological AEs:** No documented signal for cytokine storm, severe autoimmune disease induction, or immunogenicity-related systemic reactions has emerged from the controlled trial record. The bidirectional immunomodulatory mechanism — Th1 upregulation in immunodeficiency; Treg promotion in hyperinflammation — is hypothesized to underlie its tolerability even in inflammatory conditions.

### 6.3 Theoretical Concerns and Honest Limitations

**Autoimmune induction:** Tα1 has a theoretical capacity to exacerbate pre-existing autoimmune disease via T-cell activation. No clinical trial has documented this. The Zadaxin label notes caution in immunosuppressed contexts but does not list active autoimmune disease as an absolute contraindication. Clinical guidance documents flag it as a monitoring point [37, practitioner_protocol].

**Long-term carcinogenicity data:** Formal carcinogenicity studies were not completed as part of the approval program; this gap is acknowledged in the label [35, regulatory]. No signal has emerged in >25 years of post-market use, but the formal study gap exists.

**Immunogenicity (compounded products):** The FDA's December 2024 PCAC evaluation raised concerns about peptide impurities and immunogenicity risk in non-approved compounded formulations — distinct from the approved Zadaxin product [11, regulatory]. This concern applies specifically to compounded Tα1, not to Zadaxin.

**Trial geography:** The bulk of sepsis and oncology RCTs were conducted in China. Generalizability to Western ICU populations and non-Asian genetic backgrounds has not been formally tested.

## 7. Contraindications and Monitoring

### 7.1 Absolute Contraindications

1. **Hypersensitivity** to thymalfasin or any excipient
2. **Organ transplant recipients under deliberate immunosuppression** — Tα1 may worsen acute or chronic graft-versus-host disease and cause engraftment failure in HSCT recipients [35, regulatory]

### 7.2 Precautions and Relative Contraindications

| Context | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Active autoimmune disease | Use with caution; immune stimulation may exacerbate; monitoring recommended [37, practitioner_protocol] |
| Pregnancy (Category C) | Use only if clearly needed; no adequate human data [35, regulatory] |
| Nursing mothers | Unknown excretion in breast milk; exercise caution [35, regulatory] |
| Pediatric use (<18 years) | Safety and efficacy not established [35, regulatory] |
| Co-administered immunosuppressants | Effect may be attenuated or conflicting; avoid unless risk/benefit clearly favors [35, regulatory] |

### 7.3 Monitoring Parameters

- Injection sites: inspect for erythema, induration, atrophy at each visit
- Liver function: standard monitoring in hepatitis indications per co-managing protocol
- Immune labs (if indicated): CD4/CD8 counts and NK cell activity were monitored in sepsis/HIV studies; not mandated by label for standard hepatitis indications; **monocyte HLA-DR** (the mechanistically-relevant ETASS biomarker for immune reconstitution) is not a routine clinical assay but is the most direct validated Tα1 pharmacodynamic endpoint from controlled trials
- hs-CRP: useful as a **general systemic-inflammation safety screen**, not as a Tα1-specific pharmacodynamic marker; the mechanistically-relevant biomarker in ETASS was monocyte HLA-DR, not hs-CRP; a rising hs-CRP warrants clinical reassessment of any inflammatory process, not a Tα1-specific readout
- Drug interactions: no established PK interactions; no CYP450 involvement; do not mix Tα1 with other drugs in the same syringe

### 7.4 Stopping Criteria

No lab-threshold-based stopping rule is established in the clinical literature. Based on the trial record:
- Discontinue immediately on hypersensitivity reaction
- Reassess if signs of graft rejection or GVHD emerge in transplant-adjacent use

## 8. Regulatory Status

### 8.1 International Approval (>35 Countries)

Zadaxin is a fully approved prescription product regulated under the pharmaceutical approval frameworks of China, Italy, India, Philippines, Singapore, Argentina, Peru, Russia, and others across Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East [35, regulatory]. In approved markets, obtaining Zadaxin is a standard Rx process with regulated purity, potency, and sterility. Chinese generic thymalfasin (post-patent expiry) follows the same regulatory track as a legitimate approved pharmaceutical tier.

### 8.2 United States — NOT FDA-Approved; 503A Off-Limits

**Tα1 has no FDA marketing approval for any indication in the United States.** Orphan Drug Designations for malignant melanoma and hepatocellular carcinoma do not constitute marketing approval [10, mechanism_review]. SciClone elected not to pursue a full NDA for standard hepatitis indications.

**503A compounding status (December 2024–present):** The FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted against adding Tα1 to the 503A Bulks List in December 2024, recommending against permitting 503A compounding [11, regulatory]. The FDA's own position was that thymosin alpha-1 free base and acetate should not be included. PCAC votes are advisory; final notice-and-comment rulemaking had not been issued as of mid-2026, but the operative regulatory position is off-limits for 503A pharmacies. Practitioners seeking to use Tα1 in the US must source the approved Zadaxin product through international channels or obtain it as a research-use item; neither route constitutes ordinary US clinical prescribing.

**503B outsourcing facilities:** The compounding landscape is evolving; some analyses suggest 503B outsourcing facilities may retain pathways under different standards, but this should be verified against current FDA guidance before reliance.

### 8.3 WADA Status — Nuanced; Requires Verification

**Thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4; TB-500) is explicitly prohibited** under WADA 2026 Prohibited List S2.3 (Growth Factors and Growth Factor Modulators) [38, regulatory]. Tα1 and Tβ4 are **structurally and functionally distinct peptides** — they share the "thymosin" name but differ in sequence, receptor targets, molecular weight, and primary pharmacology. The "thymosin" label shared by both compounds is a documented source of consumer confusion that some vendors exploit.

**Thymosin alpha-1 is NOT explicitly named in the WADA 2026 Prohibited List** [38, regulatory]. It does not appear in S2.3 alongside Tβ4 and its derivatives.

Two catch-all provisions require verification before any athletic use:
1. **S0 (Non-Approved Substances):** WADA S0 prohibits substances with no current governmental regulatory health authority approval. Because Tα1 IS approved in >35 countries, S0 does not straightforwardly apply — but this is not a settled interpretation.
2. **S2 broad catch-all language** covering substances affecting muscle, tendon, or ligament protein synthesis, vascularisation, energy utilization, regenerative capacity, or fibre-type switching. Tα1's primary mechanism is T-cell maturation/immunomodulation, not direct muscle/tendon growth factor activity — the S2 catch-all is unlikely to apply, but sport-specific rulings vary.

**Operational guidance:** Verify with the relevant national anti-doping organization or WADA prohibited-list substance check tool before using Tα1 in any athletic context. Do not assume it is cleared solely because Tβ4 and Tα1 are different peptides; the WADA list is not exhaustive and national organizations may interpret catch-alls differently.

## 9. Concentration, COI, Commercialization, and Sourcing

### 9.1 Research Concentration Audit

The Tα1 literature represents one of the broadest independent evidence bases among peptide therapeutics in this class. At least 8 distinct independent academic research groups have contributed primary evidence, with no single group or sponsor accounting for more than 15–20% of the primary evidence base:

1. **Goldstein lab / George Washington University (USA):** Original discovery group. Allan L. Goldstein isolated Tα1 from thymic extract in 1977, establishing the sequence and founding the thymosin family [1, in_vitro]. The discovery work was conducted within the NCI/NIH academic funding sphere, without commercial sponsorship.

2. **Garaci / Tor Vergata Rome group (Italy):** Enrico Garaci's Department of Experimental Medicine and Surgery at the University of Rome Tor Vergata produced extensive preclinical and phase-II oncology work (melanoma, NSCLC, HCC), funded through Italian research foundations and ERC grants, independent of SciClone's registrational program [32, mechanism_review] [33, open_label].

3. **Andreone / University of Bologna group (Italy):** Conducted independent hepatitis B and C RCTs in the 1990s, published in *Hepatology* and *Liver*, without SciClone COI disclosure [20, rct] [25, rct].

4. **Ershler, Gravenstein / US academic aging group:** Medical College of Wisconsin and Brown University conducted vaccine-adjuvant RCTs in elderly populations before SciClone's market entry, with no commercial sponsorship [5, rct] [26, mechanism_review].

5. **Chan / Chinese University of Hong Kong group:** H.L. Chan, J.L. Tang, W. Tam, and J.J. Sung conducted a five-trial HBV meta-analysis (353 patients) independent of SciClone's clinical program [22, meta_analysis].

6. **Mutchnick / Wayne State University group (USA):** Ran the Phase III US multicenter CHB RCT (97 patients; double-blind, placebo-controlled) with government/academic funding, no SciClone COI disclosed [3, rct].

7. **Lim / National University Hospital Singapore group:** Conducted a randomized placebo-controlled HBV trial (98 HBeAg-positive patients) with Australian and Southeast Asian co-investigators, no SciClone funding per disclosed records [21, rct].

8. **Wu / Sun Yat-sen University Critical Care group (China):** Led ETASS (n=361; funded by Guangdong Natural Science Foundation — no commercial sponsor declared) [7, rct] and TESTS (n=1,089; co-funded by SciClone Pharmaceuticals) [8, rct]. The SciClone co-funding of TESTS is the primary instance of commercial sponsor concentration in the Tα1 sepsis literature; ETASS is correctly separated as non-SciClone-funded.

Additional independent contributors include multiple COVID-19 cohort groups (Liu/Ruijin, others; China National Natural Science Foundation funding, no COI declared) [31, cohort] and Italian academic groups (Carraro, dialysis vaccine trial) [6, open_label].

**Concentration verdict: BELOW the 70% flag. Genuine breadth.** SciClone's direct sponsorship touches approximately 1 of ~12 distinct primary trial clusters. This contrasts sharply with single-group series (Khavinson peptides, Cohen BPC-157 program). The literature is genuinely diverse across geography (USA, Italy, Hong Kong, Singapore, mainland China, France), decade (1975–2025), and indication (HBV, HCV, sepsis, cancer, COVID-19, vaccine adjuvancy).

### 9.2 COI and Commercialization Landscape

**Allan Goldstein (academic founder):** Goldstein co-founded Alpha 1 Biomedicals, the original commercialization vehicle, to develop thymosin peptides therapeutically — a standard academic-to-commercial translation path. In 1990, Alpha 1 licensed North American rights to SciClone; in 1994 this became an exclusive worldwide license. In December 1997, Alpha 1 sold its royalty rights to SciClone for approximately $1.93M (cash + SciClone stock), completing the IP transfer [39, anecdote_aggregate].

**SciClone Pharmaceuticals:** A US-listed specialty pharma company (NASDAQ: SCLN) that in-licensed Tα1, branded it as Zadaxin, and built its commercial franchise predominantly in China. By 2004, Zadaxin revenues were ~$22.8M (91% China); by 2011, ~$100M (97% China). In October 2017, a consortium led by GL Capital Management, Bank of China Group Investment, CDH Investments, Ascendent Capital Partners, and Boying Investments acquired SciClone for approximately $11.18/share (~$605M total; wire-reported figure, not independently confirmed against primary SEC filing) [39, anecdote_aggregate]. SciClone continues operating as a private company with the China commercialization business intact.

**COI in specific trials:** The TESTS Phase 3 (PMID 39814420) disclosed that multiple senior authors received grants or consultancy fees from SciClone Pharmaceuticals — the primary instance of SciClone COI in the primary trial literature [8, rct]. ETASS (PMID 23327199) declared no competing interests and listed academic Chinese government funding only [7, rct]. The hepatitis meta-analyses by Chan [22, meta_analysis] and the Andreone, Lim, and Mutchnick trials disclose no SciClone COI.

**Generics landscape:** Following patent expiration, thymalfasin generics entered the Chinese market. Multiple Chinese manufacturers now produce generic thymalfasin injections under Chinese GMP standards — a legitimate approved pharmaceutical tier, distinct from the grey-market research chemical category.

### 9.3 Sourcing Realism: Dual Reality

**Tier A — Legitimate approved pharmaceutical (Zadaxin/thymalfasin):**
In the 35+ approved markets, Zadaxin is a prescription product with regulated purity, potency, and sterility. Obtaining it is a standard Rx process. Chinese generic thymalfasin follows the same regulatory track. The compound's identity (synthetic 28-mer, N-acetylated, identical to the endogenous peptide) and biological activity are precisely characterized. This tier has a >25-year post-market safety record.

**Tier B — Grey-market "thymosin alpha-1" research chemicals (US and non-approved markets):**
In the United States, Tα1 is not FDA-approved and cannot legally be compounded under 503A frameworks following the December 2024 PCAC vote. This creates conditions for grey-market procurement. Grey-market research-grade Tα1 from peptide vendors carries two documented problems: (a) **purity shortfalls** — vendor-ecosystem reports and grey-market peptide surveys cite actual purity 6–15% below claimed levels, with truncated sequences common due to cost-optimized synthesis errors; **no independent analytical-laboratory studies with verified chain-of-custody have been published confirming this range**, so the figure reflects the vendor ecosystem's own disclosure rather than externally corroborated assay data; and (b) **identity confusion** — some vendors mislabel products as thymosin alpha-1 that are in fact Thymosin Beta-4 (TB-500/TB4Frag), a completely unrelated peptide.

**The Tα1-vs-Tβ4 (TB-500) distinction is critical:** These are not related forms of the same compound. Tα1 is a 28-amino-acid immune modulator with TLR2/TLR9 agonism and an approved-drug safety record. Tβ4 is a fragment of 43-amino-acid thymosin beta-4, which regulates actin polymerization and promotes tissue repair, and is explicitly prohibited under WADA 2026 S2.3. Research on one does not transfer to the other. The shared "thymosin" label is a nomenclatural coincidence, not a biological relationship.

## 10. Bottom Line

Thymosin alpha-1 is the most thoroughly evidenced immunomodulatory peptide in this library — and the only one with a genuine approved-drug status, an INN, and a multi-decade pharmacovigilance record. A clear-eyed honest accounting requires holding several truths simultaneously:

**What is genuinely established:**
1. Tα1 is a bidirectional immunomodulator with well-characterized TLR2/TLR9/IDO/Treg mechanisms, demonstrably different from the poorly characterized experimental peptides.
2. The HBV vaccine-adjuvant evidence is real, robustly replicated, and came from independent groups that predated SciClone's commercial involvement.
3. The safety record of the approved Zadaxin formulation — zero drug-related SAEs across controlled trials, >25 years post-market — is exceptional in this peptide class and cannot be matched by any unapproved compound.
4. The evidence is genuinely multi-group, multi-country, and multi-indication; the concentration verdict is below the 70% flag.

**What honest accounting requires stating plainly:**
1. The sepsis mortality signal was extinguished by the large, definitive TESTS Phase 3 trial (HR 0.99). Any framing that treats ETASS as confirmatory evidence for sepsis is now inconsistent with the best available data.
2. COVID-19 evidence is observational, small (n=9 treated in the lead study), and contradicted. It cannot be cited as efficacy evidence.
3. The cancer evidence (NSCLC meta-analysis, melanoma phase II) is exploratory, predominantly Chinese, and not practice-changing. No phase III cancer trial has been completed.
4. The anti-aging/longevity use is an extension beyond approved indications with no longevity-endpoint RCT. The mechanistic rationale is coherent; the clinical evidence is a warranted extrapolation from vaccine-adjuvant immunosenescence data, not a direct test.
5. In the United States, Tα1 is effectively inaccessible through legal compounding channels as of 2026. Grey-market product carries purity and identity risks, including the documented Tα1/Tβ4 confusion.

**What a thoughtful clinician or researcher would take from this entry:** Thymosin alpha-1 is not a hyped experimental compound with a single-lab preclinical dataset — it is a pharmacologically characterized, approved-outside-the-US, multi-decade-monitored pharmaceutical. The immunosenescence rationale and vaccine-adjuvant evidence are the best-grounded uses in a healthy-aging context. The sepsis data should now be read as net-null following TESTS. US access requires navigating regulatory complexity that makes the grey-market route the default but not the sanctioned one.

**What this entry does not resolve:** Several questions remain genuinely open for future research. First, the TESTS age-stratified subgroup interaction (detailed in §2.4; hypothesis-generating only — single null-trial subgroup, CI crosses 1.0) suggests that a dedicated elderly-sepsis or immune-depleted-patient trial might yield a different result than the unselected TESTS population; this remains an untested hypothesis, not a signal of efficacy. Second, whether Tα1 as a vaccine adjuvant to mRNA-platform COVID-19 and influenza boosters in adults over 60 provides measurable protection against infection endpoints (not just immunogenicity surrogates) is an outstanding evidence gap that the ongoing NCT06821100 trial will partially address. Third, the mechanism by which Tα1 can simultaneously drive Th1 polarization and IDO/Treg tolerance remains undercharacterized in terms of the precise cellular conditions that determine which arm predominates — resolving this would sharpen the prediction of which disease populations are most likely to benefit. Fourth, the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic behavior of the approved Zadaxin formulation versus grey-market compounded preparations (which the December 2024 PCAC specifically flagged as having impurity and immunogenicity risks) has never been directly compared in a controlled study. Fifth, no pharmacogenomic data exist on whether specific HLA alleles or TLR2/TLR9 polymorphisms modulate Tα1 response — a gap that may partly explain the variable response rates across the HBV trial literature.
