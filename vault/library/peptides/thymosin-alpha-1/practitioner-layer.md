# Thymosin Alpha-1 (Thymalfasin / Zadaxin) — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how thymosin alpha-1 is actually prescribed, accessed, dosed, and monitored in practice* — the approved ex-US prescribing pathway for Zadaxin (thymalfasin), the US regulatory dead-end (no FDA approval + PCAC voted against 503A compounding in December 2024), and the grey-market US reality that operates in the gap. Source-tagging rule: `regulatory` (Tier 1) and `rct` (Tier 2) ground safety and monitoring parameters. `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4), and `vendor_label` (Tier 4) are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions. **None** of these lower tiers grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** Thymalfasin (Tα1) is a **genuine approved pharmaceutical in more than 35 countries** — China, Italy, India, Philippines, Singapore, Argentina, Peru, Russia, and others — for chronic hepatitis B, chronic hepatitis C, and immunomodulatory cancer adjuvancy. This is a real, multi-decade, pharmaceutical-grade prescribing pathway with an INN (thymalfasin), post-market pharmacovigilance spanning more than 25 years, and GMP-manufactured product (Zadaxin, SciClone Pharmaceuticals). **In the United States, no such pathway exists.** Tα1 has no FDA marketing approval, and the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) voted 4–17–0 against adding it to the 503A Bulks List in December 2024, effectively placing it off-limits for US 503A compounding pharmacies. The US grey-market reality — where "thymosin alpha-1" is sold as a research chemical or obtained through grey-market channels for immune support and longevity use — is documented below with appropriate source tags and one critical identity hazard: **many buyers receive or believe they are receiving thymosin alpha-1 when the product is actually thymosin beta-4 / TB-500 — a completely different peptide with different mechanisms, evidence, WADA status, and regulatory class.** These two are not interchangeable.

## Approved Prescribing: Zadaxin / Thymalfasin (Ex-US)

### Approved Indications

The Zadaxin prescribing information (as available through RxList and the international PI) lists the following approved indications [1, regulatory]:

- **Chronic Hepatitis B (CHB):** Monotherapy or combination with interferon-alpha in patients ≥18 years with compensated liver disease and HBV replication (serum HBV DNA positive, HBeAg positive)
- **Chronic Hepatitis C (CHC):** Combination therapy with interferon-alpha in patients with CHC
- **Cancer immunomodulation / chemotherapy adjuvancy:** Immunomodulatory adjuvant during chemotherapy cycles for multiple cancer types — non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), melanoma, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), breast cancer, NHL, colorectal, head and neck, leukemias, pancreatic, and renal cell carcinoma; the specific approval scope varies by jurisdiction

**What this approval is, precisely:** A standard pharmaceutical Rx process in approved markets. Zadaxin is a lyophilized, sterile, GMP-manufactured product with characterized potency and sterility. Chinese generic thymalfasin (post-patent expiry) follows the same Chinese NMPA pharmaceutical approval track. This is not analogous to grey-market experimental peptides.

**What this approval is not:** FDA approval for any indication. EMA marketing authorization for any indication. Authorization to use Tα1 for anti-aging, longevity, immune optimization, or Lyme co-treatment — these are extensions beyond all approved indications.

**Modern context caveat:** The CHB and CHC approval evidence base predates current-standard nucleos(t)ide analogues (entecavir, tenofovir) and direct-acting antivirals, which achieve >95% viral suppression with excellent tolerability. Tα1-based regimens have not been directly compared to these modern standards. Approved indications remain real; their clinical positioning in 2026 is as adjuncts or alternatives in selected populations, not first-line therapy. [1, regulatory]

Not an approved label dose; framing only for clinician context. [2, rct]

### Contraindications (Approved Label)

1. **Hypersensitivity** to thymalfasin or any excipient of the formulation [1, regulatory]
2. **Organ transplant recipients under deliberate immunosuppression** — Tα1 immune enhancement may worsen acute or chronic graft rejection and cause engraftment failure in hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) recipients; cases of fatal immune hemolytic anemia in HSCT context have been identified [1, regulatory; 3, anecdote_aggregate]

### Warnings and Precautions (Approved Label)

| Context | Guidance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Transient ALT elevation ("flare") | Common in HBV patients; reflects immune activation against virus-infected hepatocytes; continue unless liver failure signs develop | [1, regulatory] |
| Active autoimmune disease | Label recommends caution; immune stimulation may exacerbate active autoimmune processes; specialist management required | [1, regulatory] |
| Co-administered immunosuppressants | Tα1 may counteract intentional immunosuppression; conflicting pharmacological effects | [1, regulatory] |
| Pregnancy (Category C) | Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks; no adequate human data | [1, regulatory] |
| Nursing mothers | Unknown excretion in breast milk; exercise caution | [1, regulatory] |
| Pediatric use (<18 years) | Safety and efficacy not established | [1, regulatory] |
| Long-term carcinogenicity | Formal studies not completed; no signal has emerged in >25 years of post-market use; gap exists formally | [1, regulatory] |

**ALT flare disclosure for CHB practitioners:** A transient rise in ALT during the first weeks of Tα1 therapy is expected in CHB patients as immune-mediated lysis of infected hepatocytes resumes. The PI directs practitioners to continue therapy unless signs of hepatic decompensation emerge (jaundice, encephalopathy, coagulopathy, ascites). This is a label-grounded monitoring point requiring clinical judgment in CHB management context. [1, regulatory]

### Adverse Events (Approved Label Safety Record)

**Dominant AE class: injection-site reactions.** Erythema, mild discomfort, local tenderness, and transient induration at injection sites are the primary patient-reported AEs; these are mild and self-limiting. [1, regulatory]

| AE | Frequency | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Injection site erythema/discomfort | Common (dominant AE class) | [1, regulatory] |
| Transient mild injection site atrophy | Rare, with repeated dosing | [1, regulatory] |
| Polyarthralgia with hand edema | Rare | [1, regulatory] |
| Rash | Rare | [1, regulatory] |
| Hypersensitivity/allergic reaction | Very rare; discontinue if serious | [1, regulatory] |

**Safety record from controlled trials:** Zero drug-related SAEs in ETASS (n=361) [4, rct]; zero Tα1-related SAEs across 19 pooled sepsis RCTs in Liu et al. (2016) meta-analysis [5, meta_analysis]; no treatment discontinuations due to Tα1 intolerance in the TESTS Phase 3 trial (n=1,089) [6, rct]. **Scope of this safety record:** These findings apply to **GMP-manufactured Zadaxin/thymalfasin** — the approved pharmaceutical product. They do NOT automatically extend to grey-market "Tα1" research chemicals of unverified purity and identity. Grey-market product carries documented purity shortfalls and identity confusion with the WADA-prohibited Tβ4/TB-500. The exceptional approved-product safety record cannot be assumed to transfer to uncharacterized preparations. This is the most relevant safety signal for practitioner assessment: the approved formulation has an exceptional >25-year post-market record, distinct from grey-market compounded product.

**Honest comparison context:** AE rates in Tα1 + interferon combination trials (fever, fatigue, myalgia, nausea, neutropenia) reflect interferon toxicity, not Tα1 monotherapy toxicity. Tα1 monotherapy AEs are essentially local ISR only.

## US Regulatory Status: No Pathway

### Summary Statement

**In the United States, there is currently no legitimate prescribing pathway for thymosin alpha-1, whether by FDA approval or 503A compounding.** This is a clear, binary regulatory finding, not a gray-area status. Both routes are blocked.

### Route 1: FDA Marketing Approval — Does Not Exist

Tα1 has no FDA marketing approval for any indication. Two FDA Orphan Drug Designations exist (malignant melanoma and hepatocellular carcinoma) — these confer exclusivity windows *if* a future approval occurs; they are not marketing approval. SciClone elected not to pursue a full NDA for standard hepatitis indications in the US market. As of mid-2026, no IND-based therapeutic use or NDA filing for Tα1 is actively on the FDA approval pathway. [7, regulatory]

### Route 2: 503A Compounding — PCAC Voted Against (December 2024)

**The December 4, 2024 PCAC meeting voted 4–17–0 against adding thymosin alpha-1 (both free base and acetate forms) to the 503A Bulks List.** [7, regulatory]

**FDA's stated position:** Thymosin alpha-1 free base and acetate should not be included on the 503A Bulks List. [7, regulatory]

**FDA-cited safety concerns (December 2024 PCAC materials):** [7, regulatory; 8, regulatory]
- Immunogenicity risk from peptide-related impurities, particularly for subcutaneous administration — peptide aggregates and truncated-sequence impurities in non-GMP compounded preparations may trigger antibody responses
- Inadequate pharmaceutical-quality characterization of bulk active pharmaceutical ingredient
- Limited or absent human safety data specifically for proposed administration routes in compounding context

**Practical effect of the vote:** The operative regulatory position as of mid-2026 is that 503A compounding pharmacies may not compound thymosin alpha-1 for patient use. Final notice-and-comment rulemaking may further formalize this prohibition; the December 2024 PCAC vote is the current operative signal.

**Timeline context:** Tα1 was placed in FDA's Category 2 interim 503A bulks list (blocked compounding) in September 2023. Nominators withdrew nominations in September 2024, which removed it from the Category 2 list procedurally — but this removal did not authorize compounding; it moved the substance to PCAC review. PCAC reviewed and voted against inclusion in December 2024. FDA subsequently evaluated on its own initiative. As of mid-2026, a July 23–24, 2026 PCAC meeting reviewed other peptides (BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTs-C, Emideltide, Semax, Epitalon) but **Tα1 was not on the July 2026 docket** — its negative December 2024 vote was not scheduled for re-review. [9, anecdote_aggregate]

**503B outsourcing facilities:** 503B facilities may compound only drugs on the Category 1 outsourcing bulk substances list or FDA-approved drugs in shortage. Tα1 is not on the 503B bulks list and has no FDA approval. The 503B pathway is not available for Tα1. [8, regulatory]

### What Practitioners in the US Do in Practice

Without a legal compounding pathway, US practitioners interested in Tα1 for patients are operating in one of two paradigms, neither of which constitutes routine licensed prescribing of an approved product:

1. **Sourcing Zadaxin from approved markets:** Obtaining the approved pharmaceutical product from China, Italy, or another approved market for a specific patient. This is legally complex (import regulations, patient-specific import pathways) and not a standardized clinical channel in the US.
2. **Research-chemical grey market:** Using or directing patients toward grey-market "thymosin alpha-1" research powders — effectively the same US grey-market pathway that applies to all unapproved peptides, without the legal foundation that 503A compounding would otherwise provide. This pathway carries all the sourcing and identity risks described below.

**The practical effect:** A US-licensed clinician discussing "Tα1 protocols" for immune support is working outside any approved or compounding-authorized framework, in the same legal and sourcing territory as unapproved experimental peptides — regardless of Tα1's approved-drug status in other countries. The approved-drug status does not transfer to the US context.

## Off-Label / Grey-Market Reality ("Thymosin Alpha-1" for Immune Support / Longevity)

### CRITICAL IDENTITY HAZARD: Tα1 vs. Tβ4 (TB-500)

**This is the most important practitioner safety point for the US grey-market context.** Thymosin alpha-1 and thymosin beta-4 / TB-500 share the "thymosin" name and both originate from thymus biology research. They are **structurally and functionally distinct peptides with no meaningful pharmacological relationship:**

| Property | Thymosin Alpha-1 (Tα1) | Thymosin Beta-4 / TB-500 |
|---|---|---|
| Amino acid sequence | 28-mer (N-terminally acetylated) | Thymosin β4 = 43-mer; TB-500 = 7-mer fragment (Ac-LKKTETQ) |
| Protein family | Prothymosin-α cleavage product | β-thymosin family; regulates actin polymerization |
| Primary mechanism | TLR2/TLR9 → dendritic cells → T-cell maturation/Th1 polarization | G-actin sequestration → cell migration, angiogenesis, tissue repair |
| Evidence base | Approved pharmaceutical (>35 countries); decades of clinical RCTs; HBV registration dossiers | Strong animal data; limited human trials; no pharmaceutical approval |
| WADA 2026 status | NOT explicitly named; likely permissible (verify with NADO) | **PROHIBITED — S2.3 (Growth Factors, Related Substances, Mimetics)** |
| US regulatory status | No FDA approval; 503A compounding off-limits | No FDA approval; not on 503A Bulks List |

**The confusion is documented in the vendor landscape:** Some vendors mislabel Tβ4 / TB-500 as Tα1, or sell them under interchangeable framing. An athlete who believes they purchased Tα1 (apparently WADA-permissible) but received TB-500 (WADA-prohibited) faces a doping violation. A patient expecting immunomodulatory benefit from Tα1 but receiving a tissue-repair actin-regulator will not get the expected effect. **Identity confusion is real, documented, and consequential.** [12, vendor_label; 13, anecdote_aggregate]

**Operational rule for practitioners:** Before discussing any thymosin-family product with a patient, confirm explicitly whether the product is thymosin alpha-1 (28-mer immunomodulator, Zadaxin INN: thymalfasin) or thymosin beta-4 / TB-500 (actin regulator, WADA-prohibited). Request a vendor Certificate of Analysis (CoA) naming the specific peptide sequence and independent lab verification if possible. Do not accept "thymosin" as an adequate product description.

### The US Grey-Market Procurement Reality

US practitioners or patients seeking Tα1 for immune support or longevity without traveling to an approved-market country are sourcing from research-chemical vendors operating under "for research use only" disclaimers. This is the structural reality following the December 2024 PCAC vote; it is not a niche phenomenon.

**Known quality concerns with research-grade US Tα1:** [14, anecdote_aggregate]
- Purity shortfalls: vendor-ecosystem reports cite actual purity 6–15% below claimed levels with truncated sequences common; these figures are not independently corroborated by published analytical laboratory studies with verified chain-of-custody — they reflect the vendor ecosystem's own disclosures
- Identity: Tα1/Tβ4 confusion is documented (see above)
- Sterility: lyophilized research powders are not manufactured under pharmaceutical GMP; sterility assurance is not equivalent to Zadaxin

**The FDA's immunogenicity concern for compounded product is precisely about this:** peptide impurities and aggregates in non-GMP preparations may trigger antibody responses via subcutaneous administration. The December 2024 PCAC materials specifically flagged this for Tα1 compounded product — the same concern applies to research-chemical product by inference. [7, regulatory; 8, regulatory]

## Practitioner Safety Points: What Tα1's Approved Record Actually Shows

Tα1 has a genuine clinical safety advantage over unapproved experimental peptides — this advantage is real and should be accurately characterized, not overstated.

### What Is Established: The Exceptional Safety Record

- Zero drug-related SAEs in ETASS RCT (n=361) [4, rct]
- Zero Tα1-related SAEs across 19 pooled sepsis RCTs (n=~1,354) in Liu et al. (2016) meta-analysis [5, meta_analysis]
- No treatment discontinuations due to Tα1 intolerance in TESTS Phase 3 (n=1,089) [6, rct]
- No documented signal for cytokine storm, autoimmune induction, or severe immunogenicity-related systemic reactions in the controlled trial record
- >25 years post-market pharmacovigilance in >35 countries without a class-wide SAE signal
- This record reflects the **approved Zadaxin GMP product** — it does not automatically transfer to grey-market research-chemical Tα1

### Specific Cautions (Label-Grounded)

**Autoimmune disease:** Tα1 is a bidirectional immunomodulator. Its Th1-polarizing arm could theoretically exacerbate active autoimmune conditions through T-cell activation and Th1-mediated inflammatory amplification. The Zadaxin label recommends caution in active autoimmune disease. No clinical trial has documented autoimmune exacerbation with Tα1 in monitored patients; the concern is mechanistic and precautionary, not epidemiologically quantified. [1, regulatory] Specialist management is required before use in any patient with active autoimmune disease.

**Organ transplant recipients — ABSOLUTE CAUTION:** This is the strongest specific risk signal. Tα1's immune enhancement directly opposes the intentional immunosuppression required to prevent graft rejection. HSCT recipients are at specific risk of graft-versus-host disease and engraftment failure. Fatal immune hemolytic anemia in the HSCT context has been identified. The Zadaxin label explicitly warns against use in deliberately immunosuppressed patients unless the risk-benefit assessment clearly favors use. [1, regulatory] In practice, this is an effective absolute contraindication in organ transplant recipients.

**Immunogenicity of compounded/grey-market product (December 2024 PCAC finding):** The FDA's concern at PCAC was specifically about peptide impurities and aggregates in non-GMP compounded preparations creating immunogenicity risk — a risk distinct from the approved Zadaxin product. Practitioners using research-chemical or compounded-outside-503A Tα1 should understand that the exceptional approved-product safety record does not extend to uncharacterized preparations with potential impurity profiles. [7, regulatory; 8, regulatory]

**TSH abnormalities (CHC combination therapy):** Combination of Tα1 with interferon in hepatitis C patients has produced TSH abnormalities; this is primarily attributed to interferon's thyroid effects but should be monitored in any CHC treatment context. [1, regulatory]

**Carcinogenicity data gap:** Formal long-term carcinogenicity studies were not completed as part of the Zadaxin approval program. No signal has emerged in >25 years of post-market use; the formal gap exists and is labeled. [1, regulatory]

### WADA Status: Not Prohibited — But Verify

**Thymosin alpha-1 is NOT explicitly named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List.** It does not appear in S2.3 alongside thymosin beta-4 and its derivatives. [20, regulatory]

**The catch-all provisions require verification before any athletic use:**
- **S0 (Non-Approved Substances):** Because Tα1 IS approved in >35 countries, S0 does not straightforwardly apply; interpretation varies
- **S2 broad catch-all:** Tα1's primary mechanism (T-cell maturation/immunomodulation) is distinct from muscle/tendon growth factor activity; S2 catch-all is unlikely to apply, but is not a settled ruling
- **National anti-doping organizations** may interpret catch-alls differently from WADA central

**Operational rule:** Verify with the relevant national anti-doping organization or WADA prohibited-list substance check tool before any athletic use. The Tβ4/TB-500 confusion risk is an additional reason to verify product identity independently.

## Monitoring Framework (Off-Label / Ex-US-Approved Context)

If a clinician outside a Zadaxin-approved prescribing context is managing a patient using grey-market or ex-US-approved Tα1, the following monitoring framework is grounded in: (a) the Zadaxin label's stated precautions and AE profile [1, regulatory]; (b) the TESTS/ETASS/clinical trial monitoring protocols. This is a rational extrapolation, not a validated clinical guideline for the off-label longevity or immune-support use.

**Baseline (before starting):**

| Domain | Tests | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Hypersensitivity / allergy history | Allergy history; prior peptide reactions | Label contraindication: serious hypersensitivity to thymalfasin or excipients |
| Transplant / immunosuppression status | Medication review; graft history | Absolute caution: label warns against use in deliberately immunosuppressed patients |
| Autoimmune disease | Medical history; active disease assessment | Label recommends caution; Th1-polarizing mechanism |
| Liver function (if hepatitis context or elevated risk) | ALT, AST, total bilirubin | ALT flare expected in HBV patients; decompensation exclusion |
| Thyroid (if co-administering interferon) | TSH | TSH abnormalities reported in Tα1 + interferon CHC combination |
| Immune baseline (optional, if clinical indication) | CBC with differential, lymphocyte subsets (CD4/CD8/NK) | Establishes baseline for immune response monitoring in immunodeficiency contexts |
| hs-CRP (optional, general inflammatory screen) | hs-CRP at baseline | General systemic-inflammation safety screen — **not** a Tα1-specific pharmacodynamic marker; the mechanistically-relevant ETASS marker was monocyte HLA-DR, not hs-CRP; rising hs-CRP warrants reassessment of any inflammatory process, not Tα1-specific readout |
| Product identity verification | Request vendor CoA; confirm Tα1 (not Tβ4/TB-500) | Tα1/Tβ4 confusion is documented; identity must be confirmed before any use |

**Follow-up:**

| Timing | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Week 2–4 | ISR assessment; injection site rotation review; early tolerability |
| Week 6–8 | Liver function if in hepatitis context or if elevated ALT at baseline |
| Month 3 | Response assessment; immune labs if relevant at baseline; thyroid if interferon co-treatment |
| End of cycle | Evaluate continuation vs. treatment holiday; re-assess for any autoimmune signs |

**Stopping criteria (label-grounded, extended to off-label use):**

- Serious hypersensitivity: discontinue immediately; emergency intervention
- Signs of transplant rejection, GVHD, or engraftment failure: discontinue immediately + escalate
- Hepatic decompensation (jaundice, encephalopathy, coagulopathy, ascites) in hepatitis context: discontinue
- Significant worsening of autoimmune disease: discontinue + specialist review

**Honest limit:** The trial populations are hepatitis patients and sepsis patients, not healthy adults pursuing longevity or immune optimization. Monitoring parameters above are rational extrapolations; applicability to a healthy-adult off-label user has not been formally tested. The exceptional safety record of approved Zadaxin in sick-patient populations does not validate absence of unknown risks in a healthy-aging context over multi-year administration.
