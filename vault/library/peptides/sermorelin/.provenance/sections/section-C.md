## Section C — Adult/off-label evidence + concentration audit

**Demarcation up front.** The popular *adult* uses of sermorelin (GRF(1-29)) — anti-aging, body recomposition, sleep, recovery, "GH optimization" / athletic enhancement — are **OFF-LABEL**. The only approval sermorelin (Geref) ever held in the US was pediatric: a diagnostic test for GH reserve and treatment of idiopathic GH deficiency in children; that approved/evidenced lineage is covered in Section B. The US-approved product was discontinued by EMD Serono in 2008 and the FDA approval was formally withdrawn effective ~2009 (the agency stating the withdrawals were *not* for safety/efficacy reasons); adult sermorelin today is supplied only through compounding pharmacies — there is no FDA-approved adult sermorelin product [1] `regulatory`. **Every adult finding below is INVESTIGATIONAL**, and most of it derives from a small cluster of old (1992–2006), small (n = 9–89) trials in **aging adults** (typically 55–76 y with low baseline IGF-1) — *not* healthy young athletes. Throughout, note that "raises GH / IGF-1" is a **biomarker**, not a demonstrated clinical benefit.

### Aging-adult GHRH(1-29) / sermorelin trials (INVESTIGATIONAL)

**Corpas et al. 1992 — biomarker reversal, no body-composition endpoint.** Healthy old men (n = 10, mean 68 y) vs young men (n = 9), GHRH(1-29) 0.5 mg and 1 mg subcutaneously twice daily for 14 days. Dose-related increases in 24-h GH and IGF-1; at the high dose the old-vs-young GH/IGF-1 gap was abolished. The authors framed this as biomarker normalization and *hypothesized* (did not test) downstream body-composition benefit [2] `rct` (crossover, aging adults). This is a 2-week pharmacodynamic study, not an efficacy trial.

**Vittone et al. 1997 — single nightly dose; biomarkers up, body composition flat.** Eleven healthy non-obese men, 64–76 y, low baseline IGF-1; GHRH(1-29) 2 mg nightly SC for 6 weeks. Nocturnal GH release and peak amplitude rose (P < .02), but **IGF-1, IGFBP-3, and GHBP did not change**, and there was **no change in weight, BMI, waist-to-hip ratio, or DEXA muscle/fat**. Two of six strength measures and one endurance measure improved. The authors explicitly concluded single nightly dosing is **less effective than multiple daily doses** [3] `rct` (aging adults). *Caution: vendor blogs frequently attribute "improved body composition / walking speed" to "Vittone 1997"; the actual paper found no DEXA body-composition change — those functional claims come from a different, non-sermorelin study (see flag below).*

**Khorram, Laughlin & Yen 1997 (JCEM) — the most-cited adult body-composition signal.** Single-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, 19 age-advanced adults (10 women, 9 men, 55–71 y), [Nle27]GHRH(1-29)-NH2 10 µg/kg nightly SC for 16 weeks (4-week placebo lead-in). IGF-1 rose within 2 weeks (P < .05); nocturnal GH was sustained in both sexes; skin thickness increased in both sexes (P < .05). However, the anabolic endpoints were **sex-restricted**: lean body mass, insulin sensitivity, and libido improved in **men only** (P < .05–.01), not women [4] `rct` (aging adults). Note this used the **[Nle27] norleucine-substituted** analog, a stabilized GHRH(1-29) variant — closely related to but not identical to clinical sermorelin acetate. n = 19 is small and the design is single-blind.

**Vitiello, Moe, Merriam et al. 2006 (Neurobiol Aging) — aging-adult cognition with GHRH(1-29).** Randomized placebo-controlled, 89 healthy older adults (mean 68 y), 6 months of daily GHRH(1-29)/sermorelin acetate (the UW Geref program). Significant gains in fluid-intelligence domains — WAIS-R performance IQ (P < .01), picture arrangement (P < .01), psychomotor speed ("finding A's", P < .01), working memory; no change in crystallized intelligence [5] `rct` (aging adults). This is the **legitimate sermorelin cognition RCT** — and it is in *aging* adults, not a general anti-aging claim.

**Sleep.** Older-adult GHRH(1-29) work (e.g., Güldner 1997, n = 13; Perras 1999) reports modest increases in slow-wave / NREM sleep and fewer nocturnal awakenings `mechanism_review` (per review aggregation [6]) — small, polysomnography-marker studies, not clinical insomnia trials.

### Critical mis-credit flags (do NOT attribute to sermorelin)

- **Baker et al. 2012 (Arch Neurol)** — the well-known 152-adult cognition + body-composition RCT (executive function ↑; body fat −7.4%, lean mass +3.7%) used **tesamorelin (stabilized GHRH analog, Egrifta)**, *not* sermorelin [7] `rct` (aging adults). **Do not credit to sermorelin.**
- **Friedman et al. 2013** brain-GABA cognition study — also **tesamorelin** `rct`.
- The widely circulated "shortened time to walk 30 m / climb four flights of stairs + reduced abdominal visceral fat" results (Veldhuis/Taaffe-type postmenopausal & older-men work) were obtained with **recombinant human GHRH-1,44-amide**, the full-length 44-residue peptide — **not GHRH(1-29)/sermorelin** [6] `mechanism_review`. The vendor-blog conflation of these into sermorelin's résumé is unsupported.

### What is ABSENT

There are **no large, modern, double-blind RCTs of sermorelin in healthy adults** for anti-aging, muscle hypertrophy, fat loss, or athletic performance. The adult evidence is entirely (a) old (1992–2006), (b) small (n = 9–89), (c) in **aging adults with low baseline IGF-1**, and (d) often single-blind or pharmacodynamic. Specifically, the following adult domains have **ZERO robust sermorelin data**: **healthy-adult athletic/strength performance** (the only strength signal is 2/6 measures in 11 elderly men, with no DEXA change); **healthy-adult (non-aging) anti-aging / longevity** (no trial enrolled healthy young/middle-aged adults); **fat loss in healthy adults** (the fat-loss data are tesamorelin or GHRH-1,44, not sermorelin). Demonstrated effects are confined to **biomarker change (GH/IGF-1)** plus, in aging adults only, modest skin-thickness, sex-restricted lean-mass, and cognition signals.

### Concentration audit

Enumerated distinct adult-efficacy *primary* trials that genuinely used **GHRH(1-29) / sermorelin** (excluding tesamorelin and GHRH-1,44 mis-credits, and excluding review-only sleep aggregations without an opened primary):

1. **Corpas et al. 1992** — JCEM — NIA / Johns Hopkins–NIH (Blackman/Harman lineage), aging-adult GHRH(1-29) PD.
2. **Vittone et al. 1997** — Metabolism — Johns Hopkins / NIH (Blackman, Busby-Whitehead), GHRH(1-29) nightly.
3. **Khorram, Laughlin & Yen 1997** — JCEM — **UC San Diego (Yen lab)**, [Nle27]GHRH(1-29).
4. **Vitiello, Moe, Merriam et al. 2006** — Neurobiol Aging — **University of Washington / VA Puget Sound (Merriam–Schwartz "Geref" program)**, GHRH(1-29) cognition.

Lab/lineage attribution:
- **UW / VA Puget Sound (Merriam–Schwartz–Vitiello "Geref aging" program):** 1 of 4 enumerated (Vitiello 2006). The same group authored most of the *aging-GHRH* literature broadly, but only one of the four sermorelin-specific adult primaries here.
- **Johns Hopkins / NIH (Blackman lineage):** 2 of 4 (Corpas 1992, Vittone 1997).
- **UC San Diego (Yen lab):** 1 of 4 (Khorram 1997).

**Single-lineage share = 2/4 = 50%** (Johns Hopkins/NIH lineage is the largest). This is **below the ≥70% flag threshold** — the four sermorelin-specific adult primaries are spread across three independent groups, so the adult sermorelin evidence base is *not* single-lab-captured. **However**, two qualifications: (i) the *total* corpus is only four small old trials, so independence does not equal robustness; and (ii) if one (incorrectly) folds in the tesamorelin/GHRH-1,44 cognition and body-composition studies, the **UW/Merriam group's share rises sharply and would approach/exceed the 70% flag** — which is precisely why the mis-credit demarcation matters. **No ≥70% single-lineage flag is raised for the correctly-scoped sermorelin set.**
