## Section B — Approval history + pediatric-GHD/diagnostic efficacy

> **Scope note:** This section covers the *regulatory* history of sermorelin (GRF(1-29), brand **Geref**, sponsor **Serono / EMD Serono**) and the *evidenced* efficacy in its FDA-approved populations: **(a) growth-failure children with idiopathic GH deficiency (pediatric GHD)** and **(b) diagnostic GHRH-stimulation testing of pituitary GH secretory capacity.** Every efficacy figure below is annotated with its trial population. Pediatric-GHD growth-velocity efficacy is **not** evidence for adult anti-aging use (that is Section C). One enumerated trial below (Kirk 1994) was conducted in **idiopathic short stature, NOT GHD** and is flagged accordingly.

### Regulatory history (Geref / Serono)

Sermorelin is the acetate salt of an amidated synthetic 29-amino-acid peptide (GRF(1-29)NH2) corresponding to the biologically active amino-terminal segment of the 44-residue endogenous growth-hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH/GRF); it raises plasma GH by stimulating the anterior pituitary somatotrophs to release endogenous GH. `[mechanism_review]` [1][5]

Two distinct Geref products were FDA-approved, both held by Serono (later EMD Serono): `[regulatory]` [1][3]

1. **Geref (Geref Diagnostic), sermorelin acetate 0.05 mg base/ampule — NDA 19-863, initially approved December 28, 1990.** Indicated **for evaluating the ability of the somatotroph of the pituitary gland to secrete growth hormone** — i.e., a single-IV-injection GHRH-stimulation diagnostic agent (1 µg/kg). `[regulatory]` [1][3]
2. **Geref, sermorelin acetate 0.5 mg and 1.0 mg base/vial — NDA 20-443, initially approved September 26, 1997.** Indicated **for the treatment of idiopathic growth hormone deficiency in children with growth failure** (pediatric GHD growth therapy). `[regulatory]` [1][3]

So the approved label scope was, accurately: **a diagnostic GHRH-stimulation product (1990)** and a **pediatric idiopathic-GHD growth-treatment product (1997)** — both from Serono. There was no approved adult anti-aging or adult body-composition indication for Geref. `[regulatory]` [1][3]

#### Withdrawal (~2008) — commercial, NOT safety/efficacy

Both products were discontinued by EMD Serono in 2008. The diagnostic 0.05 mg/amp product was reported discontinued in a letter dated **July 11, 2008**; the pediatric-treatment 0.5/1.0 mg/vial product was reported discontinued in a letter dated **December 2, 2008**, with a request to withdraw NDA 20-443. FDA subsequently withdrew approval of the applications. `[regulatory]` [1]

Crucially, the FDA formally determined — published in the **Federal Register, March 4, 2013** — that **Geref (all three presentations: 0.5 mg/vial, 1.0 mg/vial, and 0.05 mg/amp) was NOT withdrawn from sale for reasons of safety or effectiveness.** `[regulatory]` [1] This is the load-bearing integrity fact for this compound: the disappearance of Geref from the US market was a **commercial/business decision**, not a regulatory safety action. "Withdrawn for commercial reasons" ≠ "banned" and ≠ "found unsafe or ineffective." `[regulatory]` [1] Contributing market context (sponsor-side commercial rationale, reported in secondary regulatory-summary sources rather than the primary FDA determination): by the late 2000s pediatric GHD was overwhelmingly treated with daily recombinant somatropin (which, as the trials below show, produced larger height-velocity gains), eroding the commercial case for a GHRH product. `[regulatory]` [3]

### Diagnostic efficacy — GHRH-stimulation testing (pediatric & mixed populations)

The diagnostic rationale is that a single IV dose of sermorelin (1 µg/kg) provokes a GH surge from a structurally intact pituitary; a subnormal response flags impaired GH secretory capacity.

- **Ranke et al., 1986** tested GRF(1-29)NH2 at **1 µg/kg IV** in **131 children and adolescents (45 with idiopathic GHD)** plus young-adult volunteers, measuring maximal GH over 120 min alongside basal somatomedin-C (IGF-1). A **maximal GH >10 ng/mL was defined as normal**, and the authors concluded that GRF testing combined with basal somatomedin-C measurement offers a safe, convenient way to diagnose GHD in clinically suspected cases (with supplementary testing sometimes needed). `[cohort]` Population: **children/adolescents with suspected/idiopathic GHD.** [2]
- The review literature concurs that **IV sermorelin 1 µg/kg is a rapid and relatively specific provocative test** for GHD in children, producing **fewer false-positive (falsely subnormal) GH responses than other provocative tests.** `[mechanism_review]` [4][5]

**Important diagnostic limitation (annotated):** A *normal* GH response to IV sermorelin **cannot exclude GHD of hypothalamic origin**, because sermorelin acts at the pituitary downstream of the hypothalamic defect; a subnormal response to a separate provocative test is still needed to confirm such cases. `[mechanism_review]` [4][5] This is why the diagnostic product assessed *pituitary somatotroph capacity* specifically, rather than serving as a standalone GHD rule-out. `[regulatory]` [1]

### Treatment efficacy — pediatric idiopathic GHD (growth-velocity trials)

The pivotal evidence supporting the 1997 pediatric indication is the **Geref International Study Group** multicenter trial. Enumerated pediatric/short-stature trials follow.

**Pivotal trial — Thorner et al. (Geref International Study Group), 1996.** `[open_label]`
- *Population:* **110 previously untreated prepubertal children with GH deficiency** (86 evaluable for efficacy). **(Pediatric GHD.)**
- *Intervention:* GHRH-(1-29) **30 µg/kg SC once daily at bedtime, up to 12 months**, multicenter open-label.
- *Outcome:* Mean height velocity rose from **4.1 ± 0.9 cm/yr at baseline to 8.0 ± 1.5 cm/yr at 6 months and 7.2 ± 1.3 cm/yr at 12 months.** **74% of children had a "good response" at 6 months.** Bone-age/height-age ratio at 12 months (1.04 ± 0.58) was not significantly different from unity, i.e., no disproportionate bone-age advancement.
- *Safety/tolerability:* Well tolerated; no adverse biochemical/hormonal changes, no change in fasting glucose, no excessive IGF-1 generation. [6]

**Supporting evidence — Prakash & Goa review, 1999** (BioDrugs), the principal published review of Geref's clinical dossier. `[mechanism_review]`
- For **treatment** it states that **once-daily SC sermorelin 30 µg/kg at bedtime is effective in some prepubertal children with idiopathic GHD**, with **significant height-velocity increases sustained over 12 months** and data in a few children suggesting maintenance through **36 months. (Pediatric idiopathic GHD.)**
- It explicitly notes that these height-velocity increases were **less than those achieved with once-daily SC somatropin 30 µg/kg** — i.e., GHRH therapy was efficacious but inferior to direct recombinant GH replacement, consistent with sermorelin's mechanism (it requires a responsive pituitary). [5]
- The same review notes the **effect on final adult height was undetermined** at publication — long-term/adult-height outcome data were incomplete. [5]

**Non-GHD comparator trial — Kirk et al., 1994 (FLAGGED: NOT GHD).** `[rct]`/`[open_label]`
- *Population:* **18 prepubertal children with IDIOPATHIC SHORT STATURE — NOT GH-deficient** (height <3rd centile but peak GH >20 mU/L on provocation; 17 evaluable). This trial is included for completeness/contrast and **must not be read as pediatric-GHD efficacy.**
- *Intervention:* GHRH(1-29)NH2 **20 µg/kg SC twice daily for 12 months.**
- *Outcome:* Mean height velocity rose from **4.8 ± 0.9 cm/yr pre-treatment to 7.2 ± 1.6 cm/yr at 12 months (P = 0.001)**; predicted final height improved by a mean of 3.4 ± 2.6 cm; slow-growing children responded more. Glucose, insulin, and IGF-1 rose during therapy, and after stopping, growth velocity returned toward pre-treatment values within 12 months. [7]

**Enumeration of treatment/short-stature trials cited (N = 2 primary, plus 1 diagnostic + 1 review):**
1. Thorner et al. / Geref International Study Group 1996 — **pediatric GHD**, 30 µg/kg SC daily, 12 mo (PIVOTAL). [6]
2. Kirk et al. 1994 — **idiopathic short stature (NOT GHD)**, 20 µg/kg SC BID, 12 mo (contrast only). [7]
3. Ranke et al. 1986 — diagnostic GRF(1-29)NH2 1 µg/kg IV in 131 children incl. 45 idiopathic GHD. [2]
4. Prakash & Goa 1999 — review summarizing the Geref pediatric-GHD treatment + diagnostic dossier. [5]

### Population-integrity summary

- Geref's evidenced efficacy is in **(a) prepubertal/pediatric idiopathic GHD growth failure** (Thorner/Geref International 1996; reviewed by Prakash & Goa 1999) and **(b) diagnostic pituitary GH-secretory-capacity testing** (Ranke 1986; reviewed Prakash & Goa 1999). `[open_label]`/`[cohort]`/`[mechanism_review]` [2][5][6]
- The single idiopathic-short-stature trial (Kirk 1994) is **not** GHD evidence and is labeled as such. [7]
- **No source in this section supports adult anti-aging, body-composition, or longevity efficacy** — none of the approved indications or cited trials addressed adults for therapeutic benefit (the only adult use was diagnostic GH-axis testing). Adult claims belong to Section C. `[regulatory]` [1][3]
