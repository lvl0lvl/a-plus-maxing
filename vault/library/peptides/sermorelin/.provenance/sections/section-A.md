## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### 1. Identity and nomenclature

Sermorelin is the acetate salt of an amidated synthetic 29-amino-acid peptide designated **GRF(1-29)NH2** (equivalently GHRH(1-29)NH2). It reproduces exactly the first 29 residues, counting from the N-terminus, of endogenous human growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), with a C-terminal amide [regulatory] [1]. The defining structural fact is enumerated below.

Sermorelin is the **single shortest synthetic GHRH fragment that retains the full biological activity of the parent hormone**: Prakash and Goa describe it as "a 29 amino acid analogue of human growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH)... the shortest synthetic peptide with full biological activity of GHRH" [mechanism_review] [2]. Because it is the minimal fully active fragment, sermorelin functions as the **prototype of the GRF(1-29) class** of GHRH-receptor agonists; later analogues in this class (see §6) are stabilized derivatives built on the same 29-residue scaffold.

Sermorelin must not be conflated with two other named GHRH analogues that are structurally distinct:

1. **Tesamorelin** is a synthetic analogue of the **full-length 44-residue** GHRH(1-44) carrying a trans-3-hexenoyl (hexenoyl, C6) acyl group anchored on the N-terminal tyrosine; that N-terminal cap confers serum stability versus native GHRH [rct] [3]. Tesamorelin is therefore a (1-44)-based, N-acylated molecule — not a (1-29) fragment.
2. **CJC-1295 (modified GRF(1-29), "Mod GRF 1-29")** is, by contrast, a member of the same GRF(1-29) family as sermorelin — a stabilized sermorelin analogue carrying amino-acid substitutions (and, in the DAC variant, an albumin-binding maleimide) on the 29-residue backbone (see §6).

### 2. Relationship to full-length GHRH(1-44)

Endogenous human GHRH circulates principally as a 44-residue amidated peptide, GHRH(1-44)NH2, secreted by the hypothalamus into the hypophyseal portal system. Structure–activity work established that biological potency resides almost entirely in the N-terminal portion of the molecule: the first ~29 residues are sufficient for high-affinity receptor engagement and full GH-releasing activity, while the C-terminal residues (30–44) are largely dispensable for intrinsic activity. Sermorelin is the pharmaceutical embodiment of that finding — it is GHRH(1-44) truncated to its minimal active core and C-terminally amidated [mechanism_review] [2]. The N-terminal tyrosine (Tyr1) and the adjacent residues are critical: they are both essential for receptor activation and the site of the body's primary inactivation step (see §4).

### 3. Mechanism of action — receptor and signal transduction

Sermorelin acts on the somatotroph the same way native GHRH does, by engaging the **growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor (GHRH-R)**. The GHRH-R is a **class B (secretin-family) G-protein-coupled receptor** that is "physiologically mainly expressed on the growth-stimulating somatotropic cells in the pituitary gland"; the human receptor is a 423-amino-acid protein encoded on chromosome 7p14-15 [mechanism_review] [4]. A second independent review concurs that "the GHRHR is a member of the class II B GPCR family, which couples predominantly to the Gs-adenylate cyclase-cAMP signaling pathway" [mechanism_review] [5].

The downstream cascade proceeds as a canonical Gs/cAMP/PKA pathway. On agonist binding, "a stimulatory G protein... activates adenylyl cyclase to produce cAMP, leading to activation of protein kinase A (PKA)" [mechanism_review] [5]. Halmos et al. describe the same sequence and its terminal exocytotic step: "the activation of adenylate cyclase increases cAMP concentration, resulting in an increase in Ca2+ ions, and consequent GH release into the systemic circulation," with PKA activation also "determining cell proliferation, and GH and GHRH-R synthesis." Mechanistically, the rise in cAMP leads to "the opening of a sodium channel... a voltage-dependent Ca2+ channel will open, allowing calcium influx, which directly causes the release of GH stored in secretory granules" [mechanism_review] [4]. Thus sermorelin drives **both** acute release of pre-formed GH from secretory granules (via cAMP→Ca2+→exocytosis) **and** sustained GH gene transcription/synthesis (via PKA) [mechanism_review] [4].

The clinical-pharmacology endpoint is straightforward: "Sermorelin acetate for injection increases plasma growth hormone (GH) concentration by stimulating the pituitary gland to release GH" [regulatory] [1]. Released GH then drives the second tier of the somatotropic axis — hepatic production of insulin-like growth factor I: GH "stimulates the production of insulin-like growth factor I (IGF-I) in the liver," which mediates many of GH's peripheral anabolic and growth effects [mechanism_review] [4].

A mechanistically important corollary distinguishes sermorelin from exogenous recombinant GH. Because sermorelin acts one step upstream — at the pituitary rather than by directly supplying hormone — it **augments the organism's own physiologic, pulsatile GH secretion** rather than imposing a non-physiologic flat exposure; it depends on, and works through, an intact pituitary somatotroph population and the normal feedback architecture of the GH/IGF-1 axis [regulatory] [1]; [mechanism_review] [2]. This pituitary-dependence is also why sermorelin historically functioned as a **diagnostic** probe of pituitary GH reserve (an IV 1 µg/kg challenge test) in addition to its therapeutic use [mechanism_review] [2]; [regulatory] [6].

### 4. Pharmacokinetics

Sermorelin is administered **parenterally (subcutaneous or intravenous)**; it is not orally bioavailable as a peptide [regulatory] [1]. Its defining PK feature is a **very short circulating half-life**. The Geref (sermorelin acetate) labeling states that "the half-life of Sermorelin is short, 11-12 minutes after either intravenous or subcutaneous administration," and that after a 2 mg subcutaneous dose "peak concentrations of sermorelin were reached in 5-20 minutes" [regulatory] [1]. This ~11–20-minute window is the figure that should anchor any claim about sermorelin's duration; the broader "~10–20 min" range commonly quoted is consistent with this label value [regulatory] [1].

The short half-life is a direct consequence of rapid enzymatic degradation. The principal inactivation route for the GHRH/GRF(1-29) scaffold is cleavage by **dipeptidyl peptidase-IV (DPP-IV)** at the N-terminus: "Dipeptidylpeptidase IV inactivates the active form of GHRH in blood to its more stable inactive metabolite GHRH(3-44)-NH2" [mechanism_review] [5]. The analogous Tyr1-Ala2 cleavage applies to the (1-29) fragment, and the resulting truncated metabolites have essentially no residual GH-releasing activity. General serum peptidase activity contributes additional clearance. This degradation biology is precisely what the stabilized GRF(1-29) analogues (§6) and tesamorelin (via N-terminal acylation) [rct] [3] were engineered to overcome.

A frequently noted functional point is that the GH-releasing *effect* outlasts the parent peptide's measurable presence, because a brief pituitary stimulus triggers a self-limited endogenous GH pulse; this preserved pulsatility, together with rapid clearance, is the pharmacodynamic rationale for sermorelin's physiologic profile [regulatory] [1]; [mechanism_review] [2]. (Quantitative duration-of-effect figures from non-Tier sources are not asserted here.)

### 5. Regulatory identity

Synthetic GHRH(1-29), as sermorelin acetate, was a marketed FDA-approved drug: it was approved as a **diagnostic** agent for adult GH deficiency (1 µg/kg single IV; Geref Diagnostic) and as a **therapy** for short stature in pediatric GH deficiency (30 µg/kg/day SC; Geref) before being withdrawn from sale for commercial (non-safety, non-efficacy) reasons, a status formally documented by the FDA [regulatory] [6]. Current human use of sermorelin is therefore predominantly via pharmacy compounding rather than an approved finished product [regulatory] [6].

### 6. The GRF(1-29) family and GHRP-synergy rationale

**The GRF(1-29) family.** Sermorelin's clinical limitation — its minutes-long half-life — motivated a family of stabilized analogues built on the same 29-residue backbone. The best known is **CJC-1295 without DAC ("Modified GRF 1-29" / Mod GRF 1-29)**, a sermorelin analogue carrying point substitutions (classically at the DPP-IV cleavage site and other peptidase-sensitive positions) that markedly slow enzymatic degradation while preserving GHRH-R agonism. The **CJC-1295 with DAC** variant adds a Drug Affinity Complex (an albumin-binding moiety) that extends the half-life into the multi-day range by tethering the peptide to serum albumin. All of these remain GHRH-R agonists of the GRF(1-29) lineage and share sermorelin's receptor and signaling mechanism (§3); they differ from it essentially in pharmacokinetic stability, not in receptor target. (Stabilization is the same conceptual strategy as tesamorelin's N-terminal hexenoyl cap, applied to the (1-44) scaffold [rct] [3].)

**GHRP-synergy rationale.** In practice GRF(1-29) agonists are commonly co-administered with a **growth hormone-releasing peptide / secretagogue (GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin, or ipamorelin)**. The mechanistic basis is that these GHRPs act on a **different receptor** — the **ghrelin receptor GHS-R1a** — rather than on the GHRH-R [mechanism_review] [7]. Because the two ligand classes engage distinct receptors on (and around) the somatotroph, their effects on GH output are more than additive: GHRH and ghrelin/GHS show "a synergistic relationship... in the release of GH," and GHRH can act as "an allosteric modulator of the ghrelin receptor GHS-R1a," modifying ghrelin-associated intracellular signaling [mechanism_review] [7]. The dual-pathway rationale — a GHRH-R agonist (sermorelin or a GRF(1-29) analogue) raising the cAMP-driven secretory drive while a GHS-R1a agonist amplifies pulse amplitude and adds an inositol-phosphate signaling component — is therefore grounded in two genuinely separate somatotroph receptor systems converging on GH exocytosis [mechanism_review] [7]; [mechanism_review] [4].

In summary, sermorelin is the prototype GRF(1-29)NH2 GHRH agonist: a minimal fully active GHRH fragment that drives Gs/cAMP/PKA signaling at the somatotroph GHRH-R to evoke pulsatile, physiologic GH (and downstream hepatic IGF-1) secretion, limited pharmacokinetically by rapid DPP-IV-mediated degradation, and extended either by family analogues (CJC-1295 / Mod GRF 1-29) or complemented by mechanistically distinct GHS-R1a-acting GHRPs.
