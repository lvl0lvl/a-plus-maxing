## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### Identity and classification

Semaglutide is a **glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist** — specifically an acylated, long-acting analogue of the native incretin hormone GLP-1(7-37) — developed and marketed by Novo Nordisk [regulatory]. The U.S. prescribing information explicitly classifies it as "a GLP-1 analogue with 94% sequence homology to human GLP-1" [1, regulatory]. It is therefore a *peptide* drug, not a small molecule, and acts as a full agonist at the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R), a class B G-protein-coupled receptor [2, mechanism_review].

Semaglutide is supplied in two route-distinct formulation families, both Novo Nordisk products [1, regulatory]:

1. **Once-weekly subcutaneous injection** — branded **Ozempic** (type 2 diabetes mellitus, T2D) and **Wegovy** (chronic weight management). Approved injectable strengths include 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 1 mg and 2 mg, administered once weekly at any time of day, with or without meals [1, regulatory].
2. **Once-daily oral tablet** — branded **Rybelsus**, co-formulated with the absorption enhancer SNAC, in 3 mg, 7 mg and 14 mg strengths [3, regulatory].

The contrast — once-weekly injectable versus once-daily oral — is a defining feature of the molecule and follows directly from its pharmacokinetics and from the formulation chemistry of the oral product (detailed below).

### Primary structure: the engineered modifications

Native human GLP-1(7-37) is rapidly degraded in vivo by the protease dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) and is cleared renally, giving the endogenous hormone a half-life of only ~1–2 minutes [2, mechanism_review]. Semaglutide was engineered from this backbone with **three** deliberate modifications [2, mechanism_review; 1, regulatory]:

1. **Aib8 substitution** — the alanine at position 8 is replaced with α-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib), a non-coded amino acid. Position 8 is the DPP-4 cleavage site; substituting Aib renders the peptide resistant to DPP-4 degradation while retaining high GLP-1R affinity. The label describes this as modification "in position 8 to provide stabilization against degradation by the enzyme dipeptidyl-peptidase 4 (DPP-4)" [1, regulatory; 2, mechanism_review].
2. **Arg34 substitution** — lysine at position 34 is replaced with arginine. This removes a competing lysine so that acylation can be directed site-specifically to the single remaining lysine (Lys26) [2, mechanism_review].
3. **Lipidation at Lys26** — a **C18 fatty di-acid** (octadecanedioic acid) is attached to Lys26 through a hydrophilic linker composed of a γ-glutamic acid (γGlu) spacer and two 8-amino-3,6-dioxaoctanoic acid (OEG) spacers (the γGlu-2×OEG linker). Knudsen and Lau report that "a C18 di-acid together with a γGlu-2xOEG linker resulted in the highest albumin affinity combined with GLP-1R potency" [2, mechanism_review]. The label summarizes the same chemistry as "modification of position 26 lysine with a hydrophilic spacer and a C18 fatty di-acid" [1, regulatory].

The unacylated peptide backbone of semaglutide, when crystallized with the GLP-1R, is structurally essentially identical to native GLP-1(7-37), confirming that the modifications protect the molecule without abolishing its receptor-binding pharmacophore [2, mechanism_review].

### How the modifications confer a ~1-week half-life

The C18 fatty di-acid is the key to semaglutide's long duration of action. The di-acid binds **reversibly and with high affinity to circulating serum albumin**; the label states semaglutide "is extensively bound to plasma albumin (>99%)" [1, regulatory]. This albumin "depot" achieves three pharmacokinetic effects simultaneously [2, mechanism_review; 1, regulatory]:

- **Reduced renal filtration** — albumin-bound peptide is too large to be filtered by the glomerulus, slowing clearance.
- **Protection from enzymatic degradation** — albumin binding plus the Aib8 substitution shields the peptide from DPP-4 and other proteases.
- **A slow-release reservoir** — only the small free fraction is pharmacologically and metabolically available at any instant.

The net result is an elimination half-life in humans of **approximately 1 week (~165 hours)**; the prescribing information states that "with an elimination half-life of approximately 1 week, semaglutide will be present in the circulation for about 5 weeks after the last dose" [1, regulatory]. This is precisely what permits once-weekly subcutaneous dosing.

### Comparison to liraglutide and to GIP/GLP-1 co-agonists

Semaglutide's design is an iterative improvement on **liraglutide** (Victoza/Saxenda), the earlier Novo Nordisk GLP-1 analogue. Liraglutide carries a shorter **C16 mono-acid (palmitate)** acyl chain joined by a γGlu linker only (no OEG spacers), giving weaker, lower-affinity albumin binding and an elimination half-life of roughly 13 hours — hence liraglutide is dosed **once daily**, whereas semaglutide's C18-di-acid/γGlu-2×OEG design lifts the half-life to ~1 week and enables **once-weekly** dosing [2, mechanism_review]. Both molecules share the Aib8-equivalent strategy for DPP-4 resistance and act through the same GLP-1R [2, mechanism_review]. (Dulaglutide is another once-weekly GLP-1 RA but uses a different long-acting strategy — fusion of two GLP-1 analogue peptides to an IgG4 Fc fragment rather than fatty-acid acylation [2, mechanism_review].)

By contrast, **tirzepatide** (Mounjaro/Zepbound, Eli Lilly) is a *different molecule* and a *different pharmacological class*: it is a **dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor co-agonist** — a single peptide that activates both the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) receptor and the GLP-1R — rather than a GLP-1-only agonist like semaglutide [4, mechanism_review]. It should not be conflated with semaglutide despite overlapping indications.

### Mechanism of action

As a GLP-1R agonist, semaglutide reproduces and amplifies the physiological actions of the incretin hormone GLP-1, acting at multiple tissues [1, regulatory; 5, mechanism_review]:

1. **Glucose-dependent insulinotropic effect** — semaglutide "stimulates insulin secretion... in a glucose-dependent manner" from pancreatic β-cells, meaning insulin release is augmented only when blood glucose is elevated. This glucose dependence is the basis for the low intrinsic hypoglycaemia risk of the GLP-1 RA class [1, regulatory].
2. **Glucagon suppression** — it "lowers glucagon secretion... in a glucose-dependent manner" from pancreatic α-cells, reducing hepatic glucose output [1, regulatory].
3. **Slowed gastric emptying** — semaglutide produces "a minor delay in gastric emptying in the early postprandial phase," blunting post-meal glucose excursions and contributing to satiety; this gastric effect is mediated partly via vago-vagal circuitry [1, regulatory; 5, mechanism_review].
4. **Central/hypothalamic satiety and reduced energy intake** — GLP-1 receptors in the brain mediate the appetite-suppressing and weight-lowering effects. Activation occurs at hindbrain circumventricular sites accessible to circulating drug — the **area postrema (AP)** and **nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS)** — and at hypothalamic populations including the **arcuate and dorsomedial nuclei**; these neurons "are clearly activated by GLP-1RAs and appear to mediate at least some of the effects" on eating control, increasing satiation and reducing energy intake [5, mechanism_review].

These four actions together explain semaglutide's dual therapeutic profile: improved glycaemic control in T2D (insulin/glucagon/gastric effects) and clinically meaningful weight loss (central satiety + reduced energy intake).

### The incretin physiology behind the drug

The "incretin effect" is the observation that oral glucose elicits a substantially larger insulin response than an equivalent intravenous glucose load, because nutrient passage through the gut releases the incretin hormones GLP-1 (from intestinal L-cells) and GIP (from K-cells) [2, mechanism_review]. In T2D the incretin effect is blunted, and native GLP-1's ultra-short half-life (DPP-4 degradation within minutes) makes the unmodified hormone unsuitable as a drug [2, mechanism_review]. Semaglutide's engineering — DPP-4 resistance plus albumin-mediated prolongation — converts this fleeting endogenous signal into a sustained pharmacological agonist that can be dosed once weekly [2, mechanism_review].

### Pharmacokinetics summary and the oral formulation problem

- **Subcutaneous semaglutide:** elimination half-life ~1 week (~165 h); >99% albumin-bound; once-weekly dosing; steady state reached in 4–5 weeks [1, regulatory].
- **Oral semaglutide (Rybelsus):** Peptides are normally destroyed by gastric acid/proteases and absorbed negligibly, so oral delivery required a formulation solution. Rybelsus co-formulates each tablet with **salcaprozate sodium (SNAC)** — chemically *sodium N-(8-[2-hydroxybenzoyl]amino)caprylate* — an absorption enhancer that "facilitates the absorption of semaglutide after oral administration," with absorption occurring "predominantly... in the stomach" by creating a localized region of higher pH that protects the peptide from proteolytic degradation and promotes its transcellular passage across the gastric epithelium [3, regulatory]. Even so, the **estimated absolute oral bioavailability is only approximately 0.4% to 1%** across the 3 mg, 7 mg and 14 mg doses [3, regulatory]. This low and variable absorption is why Rybelsus must be taken **once daily on an empty stomach in the morning with no more than 4 ounces (~120 mL) of plain water**, after which the patient must **wait at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking other beverages, or taking other oral medications** [3, regulatory]. Once absorbed, the systemic molecule is the same semaglutide with the same ~1-week half-life [3, regulatory].
