## Section C — CV / renal / other outcomes + concentration audit

Semaglutide (a GLP-1 receptor agonist; Ozempic/Rybelsus for type 2 diabetes [T2D], Wegovy for chronic weight management) is among the few weight/metabolic agents with a portfolio of dedicated, hard-endpoint cardiovascular-outcomes trials (CVOTs) and a renal-outcomes trial. The defining integrity issue in this section is **population**: the pivotal CVOTs span three distinct populations — T2D (SUSTAIN-6), overweight/obesity **without** diabetes (SELECT), and T2D **with** chronic kidney disease (FLOW) — and the magnitude and approved-indication status differ by population. Each claim below is annotated accordingly.

### Cardiovascular outcomes

**SELECT — the landmark non-diabetic finding [1]** `[rct]`. SELECT (Lincoff AM et al., *N Engl J Med* 2023; "Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes"; PMID 37952131, verified) randomized **17,604** adults aged ≥45 with **BMI ≥27 and pre-existing established cardiovascular disease but WITHOUT diabetes** to subcutaneous semaglutide **2.4 mg** weekly vs placebo, with mean follow-up of **39.8 months**. The primary three-point MACE composite (cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke) occurred in **6.5% (569/8,803) of the semaglutide arm vs 8.0% (701/8,801) of placebo**, hazard ratio **0.80 (95% CI 0.72–0.90; P<0.001)** — a roughly 20% relative risk reduction [1]. This is the key, oft-mis-cited result: the benefit was demonstrated in a population **without diabetes**, establishing a cardiovascular indication driven by adiposity/cardiometabolic risk rather than glycemic control. On the basis of SELECT, semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy) carries an **approved** cardiovascular risk-reduction indication for adults with established CVD plus obesity/overweight. Tolerability caveat from the same trial: permanent discontinuation for adverse events was substantially higher on semaglutide (16.6% vs 8.2%), driven by gastrointestinal effects [1].

**SUSTAIN-6 — the original T2D signal [2]** `[rct]`. SUSTAIN-6 (Marso SP et al., *N Engl J Med* 2016; "Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes"; PMID 27633186, verified) randomized **3,297** patients **with type 2 diabetes** (83% with established CVD, CKD, or both) to semaglutide **0.5 mg or 1.0 mg** weekly vs placebo over 104 weeks. The primary MACE composite occurred less often with semaglutide: hazard ratio **0.74 (95% CI 0.58–0.95; P<0.001 for noninferiority)** [2]. SUSTAIN-6 was designed as a pre-approval safety (noninferiority) CVOT and was not powered for superiority, but the point estimate showed a 26% relative reduction. **Integrity note — the retinopathy signal:** SUSTAIN-6 reported a **significant increase in diabetic retinopathy complications** (vitreous hemorrhage, blindness, or need for intravitreal treatment/photocoagulation), hazard ratio **1.76 (95% CI 1.11–2.78; P=0.02)** [2]. This is widely attributed to the magnitude/rapidity of early glycemic lowering in patients with pre-existing retinopathy rather than a direct retinotoxic effect, but it remains a labeled caution and an honestly-reported harm in this section.

Population distinction (do not conflate): SUSTAIN-6 = **T2D**; SELECT = **non-diabetic** overweight/obese with CVD. The mechanism of CV benefit is plausibly shared (weight loss, blood-pressure and inflammatory reduction, possible direct vascular effects), but the two trials are not interchangeable evidence.

### Renal outcomes

**FLOW — kidney outcomes in T2D + CKD [3]** `[rct]`. FLOW (Perkovic V et al., *N Engl J Med* 2024; "Effects of Semaglutide on Chronic Kidney Disease in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes"; PMID 38785209, verified) randomized **3,533** patients **with type 2 diabetes AND chronic kidney disease** (eGFR 50–75 with UACR >300, or eGFR 25–<50 with UACR >100) to semaglutide **1.0 mg** weekly vs placebo; the trial was stopped early for efficacy at a median **3.4 years** follow-up. The primary composite (kidney failure, ≥50% sustained eGFR decline, or death from kidney or cardiovascular causes) occurred in **331 vs 410 first events**, hazard ratio **0.76 (95% CI 0.66–0.88)** — a **24% relative risk reduction** [3]. This population is again distinct: T2D **with** established CKD, not the general T2D or non-diabetic populations above. FLOW underpins semaglutide's role in diabetic kidney disease and complements the dedicated SGLT2-inhibitor and finerenone renal evidence.

### Other / emerging indications

- **MASH/NASH — ESSENCE [4]** `[rct]`. Sanyal AJ et al., *N Engl J Med* 2025 ("Phase 3 Trial of Semaglutide in Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis"; PMID 40305708, verified). In the part-1 histology analysis (**800 patients**; biopsy-confirmed MASH, fibrosis stage F2–F3) semaglutide 2.4 mg produced **steatohepatitis resolution without worsening fibrosis in 62.9% vs 34.3%** (difference 28.7 points; 95% CI 21.1–36.2; P<0.001) and **fibrosis improvement without worsening MASH in 36.8% vs 22.4%** (difference 14.4 points; 95% CI 7.5–21.3; P<0.001) [4]. **Status:** semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy) received **FDA accelerated approval for MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis in August 2025**; this is now an approved indication (accelerated, confirmatory data pending), not purely investigational.
- **Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction — STEP-HFpEF [5]** `[rct]`. Kosiborod MN et al., *N Engl J Med* 2023 ("Semaglutide in Patients with Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction and Obesity"; PMID 37622681, verified). In adults with **obesity-related HFpEF**, semaglutide 2.4 mg improved symptoms/physical limitations (KCCQ clinical summary score), body weight, and exercise function vs placebo [5]. **Status: INVESTIGATIONAL/emerging for HFpEF** — symptom and functional endpoints, not a dedicated HFpEF hard-outcome (hospitalization/mortality) approval; HFpEF is not an approved indication.
- **Alcohol use disorder [6]** `[rct]`. Hendershot CS et al., *JAMA Psychiatry* 2025 ("Once-Weekly Semaglutide in Adults With Alcohol Use Disorder: A Randomized Clinical Trial"; PMID 39937469, verified). A randomized trial reporting reductions in alcohol consumption/craving. **Status: INVESTIGATIONAL/emerging** — early-phase signal, no regulatory indication.
- **Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA).** **Integrity correction:** the pivotal OSA GLP-class trial (SURMOUNT-OSA) studied **tirzepatide, a different drug** — it is NOT semaglutide and is not attributable here. I found **no dedicated semaglutide OSA outcomes trial** in this retrieval session; any OSA benefit for semaglutide is at most an unproven, weight-loss-mediated hypothesis and should be treated as **INVESTIGATIONAL/unestablished**.

Enumeration of emerging/investigational tracks discussed (4): MASH (approved, accelerated), HFpEF (investigational), alcohol use disorder (investigational), OSA (no semaglutide-specific trial found — unestablished).

### Concentration / sponsor audit

The pivotal semaglutide outcomes evidence is **highly concentrated in a single sponsor, Novo Nordisk** — the drug's manufacturer. Of the six landmark trials cited here, the five registrational/clinical-program CVOT/renal/MASH/HFpEF trials (SELECT, SUSTAIN-6, FLOW, ESSENCE, STEP-HFpEF [1–5]) were Novo Nordisk-funded, with sponsor involvement in design, conduct, analysis, and reporting that is standard for an industry drug-development program. This single-sponsor concentration is **expected** for an approved, on-patent drug — the company that owns the molecule funds the trials required for approval — but it is a real source of potential bias (publication, analytic, and endpoint-selection bias) and must be stated plainly: the cardiometabolic semaglutide evidence base is **not independent of the manufacturer**. The notable exception in this section is the **alcohol-use-disorder trial [6]**, which is an investigator/academically-driven study (JAMA Psychiatry) rather than a Novo registrational trial — i.e., the off-label/emerging signals are where non-sponsor groups are active.

Mitigating factors that partially offset sponsor concentration: (a) the trials are **large, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, with hard adjudicated endpoints** (MACE, kidney failure, biopsy histology), which constrains some forms of bias; (b) **independent regulatory review** (FDA/EMA) scrutinized the primary datasets; and (c) **directional consistency across three different populations** (non-diabetic CVD, T2D, T2D+CKD) and across organ systems (heart, kidney, liver) is harder to explain by sponsor bias alone. **Honest limitation of this retrieval:** within this session I was **unable to open an independent real-world/registry cohort** (e.g., target-trial-emulation EHR studies) to a degree sufficient to cite under the integrity rule (cite-only-what-you-open); such corroborating observational evidence is reported to exist in the literature but is **not cited here** because it was not verified in-session. The reader should weight that gap when assessing independence.
