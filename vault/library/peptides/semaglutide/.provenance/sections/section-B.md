## Section B — Glycemic + weight efficacy

Semaglutide is a once-weekly (subcutaneous) or once-daily (oral) GLP-1 receptor agonist whose efficacy has been characterized across two distinct, separately-powered phase-3 programs at different doses: **SUSTAIN / PIONEER for type 2 diabetes (T2D)** glycemic control, and **STEP for chronic weight management** (largely in people **without** diabetes). These are different populations, different licensed doses, and different magnitudes — figures are NOT interchangeable across programs, and population is annotated on every claim below.

### Glycemic efficacy in type 2 diabetes (SUSTAIN, injectable)

The SUSTAIN program established once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide (licensed T2D doses 0.5 mg and 1.0 mg; 2.0 mg added later) as a high-efficacy glycemic agent. Across the program, HbA1c reductions clustered around **~1.5%** at the 1.0 mg dose, accompanied by weight loss — substantially larger than most oral antihyperglycemics [1].

The most informative head-to-head is **SUSTAIN-7** `[rct]`, an open-label phase-3b trial of **1,201 adults with T2D on metformin** (HbA1c 7.0–10.5%), randomized over **40 weeks** to semaglutide vs the GLP-1 RA dulaglutide [1]:
- Semaglutide **0.5 mg**: HbA1c **−1.5%**, weight **−4.6 kg** — vs dulaglutide 0.75 mg: −1.1%, −2.3 kg `[rct]` [1].
- Semaglutide **1.0 mg**: HbA1c **−1.8%**, weight **−6.5 kg** — vs dulaglutide 1.5 mg: −1.4%, −3.0 kg `[rct]` [1].

Semaglutide was superior to dulaglutide on both glycemic control and bodyweight at matched dose tiers — a result that helped position semaglutide as the most potent injectable GLP-1 RA of its class era (population: T2D on metformin) `[rct]` [1].

### Glycemic efficacy in T2D — oral semaglutide (PIONEER, Rybelsus)

The PIONEER program tested **oral** semaglutide (licensed doses 3, 7, 14 mg once daily) in T2D, demonstrating that an orally bioavailable GLP-1 RA could deliver clinically meaningful glycemic control [5].

**PIONEER 1** `[rct]` — Aroda et al., monotherapy in **703 adults with T2D** (baseline HbA1c 8.0%) over **26 weeks**; placebo-subtracted HbA1c reductions were dose-dependent: **3 mg −0.6%, 7 mg −0.9%, 14 mg −1.1%**, with placebo-subtracted weight change of −0.1 / −0.9 / **−2.3 kg** respectively (T2D, monotherapy) `[rct]` [2].

**PIONEER 2** `[rct]` — Rodbard et al., oral semaglutide **14 mg vs empagliflozin 25 mg** in **822 adults with T2D uncontrolled on metformin**; at week 26 HbA1c fell **−1.3%** (semaglutide) vs −0.9% (empagliflozin), with weight loss of **−4.7 kg vs −3.8 kg** at week 52 (T2D on metformin) `[rct]` [3]. Oral semaglutide was superior on HbA1c.

The PIONEER overview confirms dose-dependent HbA1c lowering across the program, with the 14 mg dose providing the greatest glycemic and weight effect and broadly tracking — though not exceeding — the injectable program's magnitudes (T2D) `[regulatory/review]` [5].

**Enumerated landmark glycemic trials referenced:** SUSTAIN-7 (vs dulaglutide) [1]; PIONEER 1 (oral monotherapy) [2]; PIONEER 2 (oral vs empagliflozin) [3]; PIONEER overview [5]. (The broader SUSTAIN 1–6 trials are summarized via the ~1.5% HbA1c clustering noted above; only trials whose primary sources were opened are cited individually.)

### Weight efficacy in obesity (STEP, semaglutide 2.4 mg)

The STEP program tested the **higher 2.4 mg once-weekly** dose (the obesity-indication dose, marketed as Wegovy — distinct from the ≤1.0–2.0 mg T2D doses) for chronic weight management.

**STEP-1** `[rct]` — **the landmark obesity trial** — Wilding JPH et al., *N Engl J Med* **2021**;384(11):989-1002 (**PMID 33567185**), verified to exact author/year/journal. In **1,961 adults with overweight/obesity WITHOUT diabetes** (BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a comorbidity), randomized 2:1 to semaglutide 2.4 mg vs placebo for **68 weeks** alongside lifestyle intervention [4]:
- **Mean weight change −14.9%** (semaglutide) vs −2.4% (placebo); treatment difference **−12.4 percentage points** `[rct]` [4].
- Absolute change **−15.3 kg** vs −2.6 kg `[rct]` [4].
- Achieving **≥5%** loss: **86.4%** vs 31.5%; **≥10%**: **69.1%** vs 12.0%; **≥15%**: **50.5%** vs 4.9% (all P<0.001) `[rct]` [4].

This **~15% mean weight loss in adults without diabetes** is the defining efficacy fact of the obesity program and is accurately represented above (population: non-diabetic obesity) `[rct]` [4].

**STEP-2** `[rct]` — Davies M et al., *Lancet* 2021;397(10278):971-984 (PMID 33667417). Critically, in **1,210 adults with overweight/obesity AND type 2 diabetes**, the **same 2.4 mg dose** produced a **smaller** mean weight loss of **−9.6%** vs −3.4% placebo (difference −6.2 pp; p<0.0001); ≥5% loss in 68.8% vs 28.5% `[rct]` [6]. The lower magnitude vs STEP-1 illustrates why T2D and non-diabetic obesity figures must NOT be generalized across one another (population: T2D obesity) `[rct]` [6].

**STEP-4** `[rct]` — Rubino D et al., *JAMA* 2021 (PMID 33755728), a maintenance/withdrawal design: after a 20-week run-in to 2.4 mg in **803 adults without diabetes**, continuing semaglutide produced **−7.9%** further change (weeks 20→68) vs **+6.9% regain** on switch to placebo (difference −14.8 pp; P<.001) `[rct]` [7]. This demonstrates that the effect is **maintained only with continued treatment** — weight is regained on discontinuation (population: non-diabetic obesity) `[rct]` [7].

**STEP-8** `[rct]` — Rubino DM et al., *JAMA* 2022;327(2):138-150 (PMID 35015037), a head-to-head vs the GLP-1 RA liraglutide 3.0 mg daily in adults with overweight/obesity **without diabetes**; semaglutide 2.4 mg produced significantly greater weight loss than liraglutide (population: non-diabetic obesity) `[rct]` [8].

**Enumerated landmark weight trials referenced:** STEP-1 (non-diabetic obesity) [4]; STEP-2 (T2D obesity) [6]; STEP-4 (maintenance/withdrawal) [7]; STEP-8 (vs liraglutide) [8]. (STEP-3 [intensive behavioral therapy] and STEP-5 [2-year durability] form the rest of the maintenance/durability arm of the program; only opened primary sources are cited individually.)

### Dose-response, durability, and the cross-program picture

- **Dose-response is consistent within each program:** oral semaglutide 3→7→14 mg gives −0.6→−0.9→−1.1% HbA1c [2]; injectable 0.5→1.0 mg gives −1.5→−1.8% HbA1c and −4.6→−6.5 kg [1]; the obesity 2.4 mg dose drives the ~15% weight effect [4].
- **Glycemic magnitude (T2D):** ~1.5–1.8% HbA1c at top licensed injectable dose [1]; up to −1.3% for oral 14 mg vs an active comparator [3].
- **Weight magnitude depends sharply on population:** ~14.9% (non-diabetic obesity, 2.4 mg) [4] vs ~9.6% (T2D obesity, same dose) [6] — the T2D attenuation is real and reproducible.
- **Durability requires continuation:** STEP-4 withdrawal shows regain off-drug [7]; efficacy is sustained, not curative.
