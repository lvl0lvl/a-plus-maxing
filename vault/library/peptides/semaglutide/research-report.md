# Semaglutide (GLP-1 receptor agonist; Ozempic / Wegovy / Rybelsus)

## Metadata
- **Class:** peptide — engineered GLP-1 analog (94% homology to human GLP-1)
- **Evidence tier:** **S** — one of the deepest, highest-quality RCT bases in medicine (STEP, SUSTAIN, SELECT, FLOW, STEP-HFpEF, ESSENCE), FDA-approved across multiple indications.
- **Risk tier:** **moderate** — well-characterized but real: a boxed warning, common GI AEs, and notable serious AEs; mandatory contraindication screening.
- **Goal caveat:** for the operator's actual goal (post-illness deconditioning recovery / rebuilding lean mass) this is a **goal MISMATCH** — see §4.
- **Last verified:** 2026-06-18

## TL;DR

Semaglutide is an engineered, once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist with an enormous, high-quality evidence base:
~15% mean weight loss (STEP 1), a 20% reduction in major cardiovascular events in non-diabetics with CVD+obesity
(SELECT), plus diabetes, kidney (FLOW), heart-failure (STEP-HFpEF), and MASH (ESSENCE) benefits. It is genuinely
FDA-approved and obtainable by prescription. **But it is the wrong tool for THIS operator's stated goal.** The goal
is post-illness deconditioning recovery / rebuilding — and semaglutide is a weight-LOSS drug that (a) **reduces
absolute lean mass** alongside fat (−9.7% lean in the STEP 1 DEXA substudy), (b) **reverses on discontinuation**
(STEP 4 regain → effectively lifelong therapy), and (c) suppresses appetite, which is counterproductive for someone
under-fueled or trying to rebuild. It is a superb drug deployed **only when fat loss is genuinely indicated**, paired
with resistance training + high protein — not a recovery peptide. Safety is well-mapped (boxed thyroid-C-cell warning;
GI AEs; pancreatitis/gallbladder/NAION signals). WADA: monitored, not prohibited (as of the 2026 list). The compounded
gray market that briefly made it cheap is **winding down** (FDA declared the shortage resolved Feb 2025).

## 1. Identity & Mechanism

- **What it is:** a GLP-1 analog, 94% homology to human GLP-1 `[regulatory: FDA Ozempic label §12.1]`, with three
  engineered changes — **Aib8** (removes the DPP-4 cleavage site), **Arg34** (directs acylation), and a **C18
  fatty-diacid on Lys26** (drives >99% albumin binding) — converting a minutes-long native peptide into a once-weekly
  drug `[mechanism_review: Lau & Knudsen, J Med Chem 2015, PMID 26308095]`.
- **Mechanism:** GLP-1 receptor agonism → glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucose-dependent glucagon suppression,
  slowed gastric emptying; centrally (area postrema, hypothalamus, NTS) it reduces appetite/energy intake `[regulatory:
  FDA label §12.1]/[mechanism_review: PMC8820179]`. CV benefit in SELECT appears partly pleiotropic/anti-inflammatory.
- **Half-life ~1 week (~165 h)**, >99% albumin-bound `[regulatory: FDA label §12.3]`.

## 2. Human Clinical Evidence (by indication)

### 2.1 Obesity / weight (the goal-adjacent one)
- **STEP 1 (NEJM 2021, PMID 33567185), n=1,961, 68 wk, 2.4 mg/wk:** **−14.9%** vs −2.4% placebo; 86% reached ≥5% loss. `[rct]`
- **STEP 1 DEXA substudy (J Endocr Soc 2021), n=140:** total fat −19.3%, visceral fat −27.4%, **lean body mass −9.7%
  (absolute)** but lean:total ratio improved ~+3 pp — fat loss predominates; lean loss is the obligatory consequence of
  a large energy deficit, not a direct catabolic drug effect. `[open_label/exploratory — conference abstract tier]`
- **STEP 4 (JAMA 2021, PMID 33755728):** withdrawal at wk 20 → **+6.9% regain** over wk 20–68 vs −7.9% in continuers —
  benefit requires continued use. `[rct]`

### 2.2 Type 2 diabetes
- **SUSTAIN-6 (NEJM 2016, PMID 27633186), n=3,297:** HbA1c −1.1 to −1.4%; weight −3.6 to −4.9 kg; **MACE HR 0.74
  (0.58–0.95)** — but diabetic-retinopathy complications rose (3.0% vs 1.8%). `[rct]`

### 2.3 Cardiovascular (non-diabetic CVD + obesity)
- **SELECT (NEJM 2023, PMID 37952131), n=17,604:** 3-point **MACE HR 0.80 (~20% reduction)**; ~8.5 pp placebo-adjusted
  weight loss — the basis for the 2024 CV-risk-reduction indication. `[rct]`

### 2.4 Emerging (verified)
- **FLOW (NEJM 2024), CKD in T2D, n=3,533:** major kidney events **HR 0.76**; −20% all-cause death. `[rct]`
- **STEP-HFpEF (NEJM 2023), n=529:** KCCQ +7.8, 6-min walk +20.3 m. `[rct]`
- **ESSENCE (NEJM 2025), MASH F2–F3:** steatohepatitis resolution 62.9% vs 34.3%. `[rct]`

### 2.5 Lean-mass building / post-illness recovery (the operator's goal)
- **NO ADMISSIBLE PRIMARY FOUND.** No RCT supports semaglutide for building lean mass or post-illness/deconditioning
  recovery — it is a weight-loss agent that *reduces* absolute lean mass. Uncontrolled cohorts (e.g. handgrip gains in
  sarcopenic-obesity) are confounded by weight loss; one cohort even reports accelerated sarcopenia in older T2D adults.
  (verified absence + goal mismatch.)

## 3. Safety, Contraindications, Monitoring (FDA Wegovy/Ozempic label of record)

- **Boxed warning:** thyroid C-cell tumors / medullary thyroid carcinoma (rodent signal; human relevance undetermined) —
  **contraindicated with personal/family MTC or MEN2** `[regulatory: FDA Wegovy boxed warning + §5.1]`.
- **Common AEs (Wegovy 2.4 mg):** nausea 44% vs 16% placebo, diarrhea 30% vs 16%, vomiting 24% vs 6%, constipation
  24% vs 11% — titration-dependent `[regulatory]`.
- **Serious/notable:** acute pancreatitis (§5.2), gallbladder disease (§5.3), AKI from volume depletion (§5.5),
  hypoglycemia with insulin/SU (§5.4), diabetic-retinopathy complications (§5.8), ileus (post-marketing), **NAION** —
  EMA PRAC (June 2025) concluded a *very rare* (~1/10,000) effect; supporting cohort HR 4.28 `[cohort: PMID 38958939]`.
- **Anesthesia:** delayed gastric emptying → **aspiration risk** under general anesthesia/deep sedation (§5.10) — inform
  the anesthesia team / hold before elective procedures.
- **Contraindications:** MTC/MEN2, serious hypersensitivity; **pregnancy** (discontinue ≥2 months before planned
  pregnancy given the long washout). Caution: prior pancreatitis, gastroparesis.
- **Monitoring:** renal function during severe GI AEs; glucose if on insulin/SU; retinopathy in T2D; pancreatitis/
  gallbladder symptoms; thyroid symptoms.
- **Anti-doping:** **NOT prohibited** — on the WADA **Monitoring Program** (2024–), monitored (not banned) as of the
  2026 list; WADA is considering future prohibition `[regulatory: WADA 2026]`. *(A vendor page claiming an "S4 ban" was
  false and excluded.)*

## 4. The Goal-Mismatch Caveat (FIRST-CLASS)

Unlike Tesamorelin (population transfer) or Tα1 (efficacy story shifting), semaglutide's caveat is a **goal mismatch**:
the evidence is overwhelming and real, but it answers a *different question* than the operator's. The operator's goal is
**post-illness deconditioning recovery / rebuilding**; semaglutide is a **weight-loss / appetite-suppressing** drug that
**reduces absolute lean mass** and whose effect **reverses on stopping**. For a deconditioned or under-fueled person it
is, if anything, counterproductive unless there is genuine excess adiposity to address. The correct framing for the
wiki: a **first-line tool when fat loss is clinically indicated** (and then paired with resistance training + adequate
protein to defend lean mass, with MTC/MEN2 screened out) — **not** a recovery or performance peptide. evidence_tier S
for what it does; **off-target for the stated goal**.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics & Dosing

- **Wegovy (obesity):** SC weekly, titrate 0.25 → 0.5 → 1.0 → 1.7 → **2.4 mg** over ~16 wk (slow titration limits GI AEs).
- **Ozempic (T2D):** SC weekly 0.25 → 0.5 → 1.0 → max **2 mg**. **Rybelsus (oral T2D):** 3 → 7 → **14 mg** daily.
- **PK:** t½ **~1 week (~165 h; range ~139–184)**; SC bioavailability **~89%**; oral bioavailability **~1%** (SNAC
  enhancer; must take fasting with ≤4 oz water, wait ≥30 min); steady state 4–5 wk `[regulatory: FDA labels; mechanism_review PMC9272494]`.
- **Administration:** SC abdomen/thigh/upper-arm, rotate; storage refrigerated, room-temp ≤28 d.

## 6. Regulatory & Sourcing (the compounding saga)

- **FDA approvals:** Ozempic (T2D, **2017**), Rybelsus (oral T2D, **2019**), Wegovy (obesity, **2021**; CV-risk
  reduction, **2024**), Ozempic CKD (**2025**). EMA-approved (Ozempic 2018, Wegovy 2022). Manufacturer: Novo Nordisk. `[regulatory]`
- **Compounding saga (the sourcing-realism story):** semaglutide was on the FDA shortage list → mass compounding →
  **FDA declared the shortage resolved Feb 21 2025** → enforcement-discretion wind-down (503A **Apr 22 2025**, 503B
  **May 22 2025**). FDA's position: compounded **salt forms (semaglutide sodium / acetate) are NOT the approved active
  ingredient** and have no lawful compounding basis `[regulatory: FDA]`. So the cheap gray-market channel is closing.
- **Cost:** branded ~$1,000–1,350/mo list (Novo self-pay ~$349–499; Medicare-negotiated $274 effective 2027). Coverage
  is broader for the T2D indication than for obesity.
- **Non-English:** the pivotal program is global multicenter but **English-published** (NEJM/Lancet) — no distinct
  non-English primary located.
- **Prescribing-practice:** on-label titration is standard; "microdosing" is an off-label practice with **no RCT support**
  (`practitioner_protocol`, dose/route only — never efficacy). See the layers.

## 7. Bottom line for the operator

Semaglutide is a genuinely great, FDA-approved drug with tier-S evidence — but it is **the wrong tool for the stated
recovery/rebuilding goal**. Reserve it for the case where fat loss is clinically warranted (then resistance training +
protein + MTC/MEN2 screen), discussed at the July-2026 visit. Do not source it as a compounded "salt-form" product
(not the approved API, and the channel is closing). For *building* back after illness, this is off-target.
