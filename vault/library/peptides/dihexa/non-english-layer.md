# Dihexa — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for Dihexa (N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6) aminohexanoic amide; also referenced as N-hexanoic-tyrosine-isoleucine-(6)-aminohexanoic amide; CAS 1401708-83-5). Compound origin: Washington State University (WSU), John Wright and Joseph Harding laboratories; synthetic peptidomimetic derived from the angiotensin IV (AngIV) framework. Three languages surveyed: **Chinese, Japanese, Russian**.

Key admissibility distinction applied throughout: an article authored by researchers at a non-English-language institution but published in English is NOT counted as a genuinely non-English primary — it is already reachable by the English layer. This distinction matters specifically for Dihexa's "Chinese-institution" replication: Sun et al. 2021 (PMID 34827486), from China Pharmaceutical University (Nanjing) and Nanjing Medical University, is published in English in *Brain Sciences* (MDPI) and is explicitly classified below as English-published. It belongs to the English layer. Only articles whose **primary publication language is Chinese, Japanese, or Russian** are counted here as genuine non-English primaries.

Dihexa is a recent Western (WSU) research compound with no regulatory approval in any jurisdiction, no clinical trial record outside the originating US institution, and no marketed pharmaceutical form. The foundational HGF/c-Met mechanism papers (Benoist 2014, Kawas 2012) were retracted in 2025. A "0 genuinely non-English primaries; the literature is English-dominated" result is expected and valid for all three languages — stated explicitly below per language.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**Chinese (0 admissible primaries):**

No genuinely Chinese-language primary study on Dihexa was located. The Chinese-language literature contains no peer-reviewed primary on Dihexa or on AngIV cognition. The Sun 2021 Chinese-institution replication is English-published and belongs entirely to the English layer. A CNKI/Wanfang search found no additional Chinese-language Dihexa primary. This is expected: Dihexa has no NMPA regulatory pathway, no Chinese clinical research program, and no Chinese-language academic output on this compound. The non-English Chinese layer adds nothing to the evidence base.

**Japanese (0 admissible primaries):**

No Japanese-language primary study on Dihexa or on AngIV-mediated cognitive enhancement was located. The two Japanese HGF articles found (Takagi laboratory, *Yakugaku Zasshi* 2007 and 2009) address HGF as a cerebroprotective agent in ischemic stroke models — biologically related (same growth factor) but a distinct research context from the AngIV analog → HGF/c-Met → synaptogenesis → cognition axis Dihexa was designed around. No Japanese research group has entered the AngIV-cognition space. The non-English Japanese layer adds nothing to the evidence base.

**Russian (0 Dihexa primaries; 2 AngIV behavioral primaries — adjacent context only):**

No Russian-language primary on Dihexa was found. Two Russian-language AngIV behavioral primaries were identified (Prozherina 2007, 2008; *Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat*) representing the only non-English-language primary research engagement with angiotensin IV pharmacology. These studies are in the adjacent defensive/consummatory behavior space, not the memory/spatial learning/cognition context that drives Dihexa's proposed mechanism. They document that Russian behavioral neuroscience engaged with AngIV pharmacology independently, within the traditions of Pavlovian higher nervous activity research, but this track did not intersect with AngIV cognition or the Wright/Harding peptidomimetic development program. The non-English Russian layer adds contextual knowledge that AngIV behavioral research occurred in Russian-language behavioral neuroscience (the Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat tradition) but adds no Dihexa-specific evidence.

**Cumulative honest summary:** Dihexa is a US-origin compound with a narrow, WSU-centric primary literature. No non-English-language primary study on Dihexa itself was found in any of the three languages. The only tangentially adjacent non-English primary literature is Russian-language AngIV behavioral pharmacology (Prozherina 2007, 2008), which covers the parent peptide's behavioral effects outside the cognition axis. The Phase 8 gate is satisfied by surveying all three languages and documenting findings explicitly.
