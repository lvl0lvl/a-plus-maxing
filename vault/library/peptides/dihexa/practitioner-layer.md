# Dihexa — Practitioner / Grey-Market Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how Dihexa is actually accessed, dosed, and (where applicable) monitored in the grey-market nootropic space* — the dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions that circulate in vendor listings, forum threads, and clinic pages, plus the regulatory and compounding status that governs whether any legitimate clinical or prescribing pathway exists. It is **not** an efficacy document. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (Tier 3), and `vendor_label` / `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4) sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) purity/reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first — NO approved pathway exists.** As of mid-2026, Dihexa is **not FDA-approved for any indication anywhere in the world** and has **no IND, NDA, or regulatory classification under any jurisdiction's therapeutic-use framework**. Dihexa acetate was removed from FDA's Category 2 "do not compound" restricted list on April 22, 2026, as part of a 12-peptide reclassification action — but removal from Category 2 is **NOT approval** and does **NOT authorize compounding**: it means only that FDA's active "significant safety concern" designation was lifted pending further review. Dihexa now awaits **PCAC (Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee) review before February 2027** before it could be recommended for addition to the 503A bulk drug substances list; FDA rulemaking would then follow. In the meantime, no 503A pharmacy is authorized to compound Dihexa, and no 503B outsourcing facility may produce it. **There is no legal prescription pathway for Dihexa in the United States.** Globally: not approved in Canada, the EU, the UK, or Australia. Dihexa is a research chemical with an explicit "not for human use" classification across all legitimate supply channels.
>
> **The foundational mechanism papers are retracted.** The two papers establishing that Dihexa's cognitive effects depend on HGF/c-Met-driven synaptogenesis — Kawas et al. (JPET 2012, PMID 22129598) and Benoist et al. (JPET 2014, PMID 25187433) — were formally retracted in April 2025 following a Washington State University investigation that found figures contained falsified and fabricated data. The BDNF-potency marketing claim ("seven orders of magnitude more potent than BDNF," circulated as "100 million times more potent") traces directly to the now-retracted Benoist 2014 paper. Buyers are sold on discredited science. A third paper (McCoy 2013, PMID 23055539) has carried an Expression of Concern since September 2021. The Dihexa market operates almost entirely on the strength of retracted claims.
>
> **WADA S0 — prohibited at all times.** Dihexa is a non-approved substance prohibited under WADA Section S0 (Non-Approved Substances) in-competition and out-of-competition as of the 2026 Prohibited List, which entered force January 1, 2026. Any athlete subject to anti-doping rules faces disqualification regardless of dose or route.

## Critical hazards (MUST precede any convention documentation)

These five hazards are load-bearing for any practitioner reading this document. They are stated here — before dose conventions — because a reader who exits early must carry them.

### 1. Retracted-mechanism reality

The "100 million times more potent than BDNF" claim that drives virtually all grey-market Dihexa marketing traces directly to Benoist et al. (JPET 2014), formally retracted April 2025 for fabricated/falsified figures [1, animal]. The companion paper establishing the HGF/c-Met mechanism is also retracted [2, animal]. The dosing-anchor paper carries an Expression of Concern [3, animal]. The primary author whose work is retracted (Leen Kawas) was simultaneously CEO of Athira Pharma, the company commercializing the related clinical compound fosgonimeton; Athira settled a $4 million DOJ False Claims Act claim in January 2025 over NIH grants citing the manipulated figures [13, regulatory]. Buyers are being sold on the strength of retracted science. No independent laboratory has replicated the specific HGF/c-Met synaptogenesis mechanism [see research-report.md, source 5 (Sun 2021) and source 8 (Wells 2024)]. This does not establish that the compound has no effect — but it does mean the mechanism and potency claims that constitute the entire marketing rationale are currently unsupported in the non-retracted literature.

### 2. c-Met oncogenic / tumor-promotion theoretical risk

HGF/c-Met (MET) receptor tyrosine kinase is one of the most well-characterized oncogenic pathways in cancer biology. Constitutive or hyperactivated HGF/c-Met signaling drives tumor proliferation, invasion, angiogenesis, and metastasis across hepatocellular carcinoma, non-small-cell lung cancer, gastric cancer, breast cancer, glioblastoma, and multiple other malignancy types. This is not a theoretical oncology hypothesis — it is the reason the FDA approved capmatinib (2020) and tepotinib (2021) as selective c-Met inhibitors for MET-exon-14-skipping NSCLC. Dihexa's proposed therapeutic action is the pharmacological inverse: potentiation of HGF-driven c-Met phosphorylation. **No carcinogenicity study — rodent chronic or otherwise — has ever been conducted for Dihexa in any species.** Absence of carcinogenicity data is the normal state for an academic compound that never reached IND development; it does not mean the risk was assessed and found absent. The clinical imperative: (a) **personal or family history of any cancer, especially c-Met-driven cancers (hepatocellular, NSCLC, gastric, GBM), is a serious precaution and arguably an absolute contraindication**; (b) **concurrent use with capmatinib, tepotinib, or any other c-Met inhibitor is pharmacodynamically contraindicated** — a c-Met agonist and a c-Met inhibitor operate in direct pharmacological opposition, and co-administration is irrational without specific mechanistic evidence of benefit; (c) any individual choosing to use Dihexa in a clinical oversight context should maintain age-appropriate oncologic surveillance as the sensible monitoring posture.

### 3. Zero human safety data

No Phase 1 trial, no pharmacokinetic study, no human toxicology dossier, and no adverse-event registry exist for Dihexa in any human being. ClinicalTrials.gov returns zero results for any Dihexa study [4, regulatory]. Every "side effect" description in vendor copy and aggregator articles is extrapolated from rodent preclinical data or uncontrolled user anecdotes. Human pharmacokinetics (oral bioavailability, Cmax, Tmax, half-life, volume of distribution, metabolic pathways, renal vs. hepatic clearance) are uncharacterized. No validated human dose exists — all figures circulating in the grey-market are animal-to-human allometric extrapolations applied without any human PK anchor.

### 4. Dihexa ≠ fosgonimeton / ATH-1017 (failed clinical compound)

Grey-market Dihexa buyers frequently conflate the research chemical with fosgonimeton (also ATH-1017, NDX-1017), the structurally distinct, subcutaneously administered HGF/MET-activating small molecule developed by Athira Pharma (subsequently rebranded LeonaBio). These are **different compounds with different structures, formulations, and routes of administration.** Fosgonimeton's human data do NOT transfer to Dihexa. Fosgonimeton failed all three of its clinical trials: ACT-AD Phase 2 (NCT04491006, n=77; June 2022, missed primary ERP endpoint), LIFT-AD Phase 2/3 (NCT04488419, n=554; results posted April 2025, missed primary endpoint: Global Statistical Test and all key secondaries including ADAS-Cog11 and ADCS-ADL23), and SHAPE Phase 2 for Parkinson's disease dementia / DLB (December 2023, halted early for futility) [5, regulatory]. Athira/LeonaBio discontinued fosgonimeton development for Alzheimer's, DLB, and PDD as of late 2024 and pivoted to an oral HGF/MET modulator ATH-1105 in Phase 1 [5, regulatory]. The clinical failure of the most advanced HGF/MET program in neurodegeneration is translational context for the class — not proof that Dihexa will fail, but evidence that the HGF/MET therapeutic hypothesis has not yet been clinically confirmed.

### 5. WADA S0 prohibition

Dihexa is a non-approved substance prohibited under WADA Section S0 (Non-Approved Substances) in-competition and out-of-competition as of the 2026 Prohibited List (effective January 1, 2026) [6, regulatory]. Section S0 is a catch-all covering "any pharmacological substance which is not addressed by any of the subsequent sections of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use." Dihexa satisfies both prongs. Athletes subject to anti-doping rules face disqualification regardless of dose, route, or claimed purpose.

## Dose / cycle / route conventions (each tagged, no number grounded)

> **Grounding rule:** all figures below are community conventions or vendor copy, tagged accordingly. No dose number in this section constitutes a validated therapeutic dose. Human dose-finding data do not exist. Do not interpret these figures as safe, effective, or appropriate starting points — they are the conventions that circulate, documented so a practitioner can recognize them, not endorse them.

### Convention A — Oral: low-start titration (most common community pattern)

The most broadly circulating community convention for oral Dihexa use:

| Phase | Duration | Oral daily dose |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| Tolerance assessment | Weeks 1–2 | 5 mg once daily, AM |
| Standard | Weeks 3–4 | 10 mg once daily, AM |
| Extended (experienced) | Weeks 4–6 | 15–20 mg once daily, AM |

- **Frequency:** once daily
- **Timing:** morning, strongly preferred — late-day dosing (after approximately 2 PM) is consistently associated with disrupted sleep onset and vivid dreams in community reports
- **Cycle structure:** 2–6 weeks active, 4–8 weeks off; BHRC convention extends to 12+ weeks with physician oversight [7, anecdote_aggregate]; community aggregators recommend 4–8 weeks maximum [8, anecdote_aggregate]
- **Fat co-administration:** Dihexa's lipophilicity (LogP ~2.8) means oral absorption is consistent with lipid carrier; MCT oil capsules or dosing alongside a fat-containing meal are the common community approach [8, anecdote_aggregate]

Sources: [7, anecdote_aggregate]; [8, anecdote_aggregate]; peptidesinsider.com Dosage guide [12, anecdote_aggregate]. Convention ONLY — grounds NO efficacy claim.

### Convention B — Oral: Biomogging community range

A broader community dosing table, aggregated from multiple forum threads and vendor copy, reflecting the full range of oral doses reported:

| Use context | Daily dose |
|-------------|-----------|
| First trial | 8–12 mg |
| Learning / cognitive push | 10–20 mg |
| Post-mTBI recovery (self-reported) | 8–15 mg |
| Aging-brain protocols | 15–25 mg (some: split AM + early PM) |
| High-end community reports | 25–45 mg |

- **Onset:** no acute effect; self-reported subjective changes emerge in weeks 2–3; claimed peak window weeks 4–6
- **Cycle structure:** 4–8 weeks on, 4–12 weeks off minimum

Source: [8, anecdote_aggregate]. These ranges reflect what the community self-reports, not validated parameters. The upper end (25–45 mg) exceeds what would be naively extrapolated from rat allometric doses and has no safety anchor. Convention ONLY — grounds NO efficacy claim.

### Convention C — Subcutaneous injection (less common)

A minority practice reports subcutaneous injection, typically with lower absolute doses than the oral convention:

- **Dose range:** 1–5 mg once daily (most commonly 2–5 mg) [12, anecdote_aggregate]
- **Cycle:** 2–4 weeks active; 4–8 weeks off
- **Vehicle note:** Dihexa's poor water solubility creates reconstitution challenges for injectable use; DMSO or PEG vehicles are sometimes described but carry their own irritation and toxicity profile; bacteriostatic water alone may produce incomplete dissolution [8, anecdote_aggregate]

Convention ONLY — grounds NO efficacy claim. No human PK data exist for subcutaneous Dihexa.

### Convention D — Intranasal (minority practice)

A small fraction of community reports describe intranasal administration as a lower-dose alternative:

- **Dose range:** 10–25 mcg daily (microdose range — substantially lower than oral conventions) [8, anecdote_aggregate]
- Rationale in community: direct CNS delivery, bypassing first-pass metabolism; entirely speculative for Dihexa specifically

Convention ONLY — grounds NO efficacy claim. No human PK or bioavailability data exist for intranasal Dihexa.

### Convention divergence is substantive and must be disclosed

Oral conventions range from 5 mg to 45+ mg daily across community sources. The subcutaneous convention spans 1–5 mg. The intranasal convention uses doses three orders of magnitude lower than the high-end oral convention. This divergence is not noise — it reflects the complete absence of any human PK anchor. No convention is validated by a human dose-finding trial. Any clinical oversight should treat all figures as provisional, not evidence-based parameters.

## Preclinical dose reference (animal data only — NOT human dosing)

These figures are cited only to establish the originating context from which community human conventions are extrapolated. They constitute `animal` tier evidence, not human dosing guidance.

- **Intracerebroventricular (rat):** 0.1–1 nmol [14, animal]
- **Intraperitoneal (rat):** 0.05–0.50 mg/kg [14, animal]
- **Oral (rat):** 1.25–2.0 mg/kg/day; the McCoy 2013 paper (under Expression of Concern) used 2.0 mg/kg oral to reverse scopolamine-induced spatial memory deficit in aged rats [3, animal]

Naive HED (human equivalent dose) extrapolation from rat oral data using the BSA-correction factor (×6.2 for rat-to-human) yields approximately 14–32 mg for a 70 kg adult — the origin of the community's oral convention range. This extrapolation does not constitute a validated human dose and ignores all PK unknowns.

Reconstitution conventions below are admin bookkeeping drawn from vendor labels and community aggregators. They cannot ground efficacy or safety claims.

**Injectable (if reconstituting lyophilized powder):**

| Vial size | Bac. water added | Concentration | 2 mg dose | 5 mg dose |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| 10 mg | 1.0 mL | 10 mg/mL | 0.20 mL (20 U on U-100) | 0.50 mL (50 U) |

Source: [12, anecdote_aggregate]. Convention table only.

- **Solubility caution:** Dihexa has poor water solubility (LogP ~2.8). Bacteriostatic water alone may produce incomplete dissolution. Some community sources describe warming the bac. water to 37°C before injection into the vial and allowing 10–15 minutes of gentle swirling. Particulate matter before injection is a contamination hazard. Clear solution is required before use [8, anecdote_aggregate].
- **Reconstitution technique (if using):** wipe stoppers with alcohol swab; inject bac. water slowly down the vial wall (do not stream directly onto lyophilized cake); swirl gently — do NOT shake (agitation may degrade peptide bonds); label with date and concentration
- **Storage:** lyophilized powder: −20°C long-term; reconstituted solution: 2–8°C; use within 21–28 days (community convention [8, anecdote_aggregate]; shorter is more conservative)
- **Injectable syringe:** standard U-100 insulin syringe (27–31G, 0.5 mL or 1.0 mL)
- **DMSO vehicles:** some injectable protocols describe DMSO as a solubility aid; DMSO carries its own toxicity profile (local dermatitis, systemic absorption including CNS and hepatic effects, garlic-breath indicator of systemic uptake); this is an additional unstudied risk layer

**Oral capsules (pre-formulated, if purchased):**

- Swallow whole with water; take with a fat-containing meal or in MCT oil capsule for most consistent absorption [7, anecdote_aggregate; 8, anecdote_aggregate]
- Storage: room temperature, protected from light; stated shelf life 12–24 months sealed [12, anecdote_aggregate]

## Practitioner monitoring framework (experimental — no validated clinical protocol exists)

No published clinical guideline, society recommendation, or clinical protocol exists for monitoring Dihexa use in humans. If a clinician were maintaining oversight of a patient who had chosen to use Dihexa — acknowledging the zero-human-data reality and the retracted mechanism — the following framework is the rational minimum, grounded in the c-Met oncogenic risk profile and what the only human clinical program in this mechanistic class (fosgonimeton Phase 1b) tracked for safety.

**The c-Met oncogenic risk is the governing concern for monitoring design.** All other monitoring (cognitive baseline, sleep, neurological symptoms) is secondary to the cancer-biology concern.

**Baseline (before any use — required, not optional):**

| Domain | Tests | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Oncologic screening | Age-appropriate cancer screening per current USPSTF guidelines; CBC with differential | Baseline before any c-Met pathway agonist; excludes active malignancy; establishes CBC reference |
| Hepatic | ALT, AST, GGT, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin | c-Met is overexpressed in hepatocellular carcinoma; hepatic metabolic baseline; analogous to fosgonimeton Phase 1b safety endpoints |
| Renal | Creatinine, BUN, eGFR (CMP) | Clearance baseline; fosgonimeton Phase 1b excluded eGFR <60 |
| CBC | Full CBC with differential | Baseline hematology before any unapproved compound with theoretical pro-proliferative mechanism |
| Cognitive baseline | Validated instrument (MoCA, MMSE, or clinic-preferred cognitive battery) | Without a baseline, no within-subject change can be assessed; any "improvement" claim is uninterpretable |
| Cancer/family history review | Structured intake | Active malignancy, prior c-Met-driven cancer, or first-degree family member with c-Met-associated cancer (hepatocellular, gastric, NSCLC, GBM) = serious precaution; arguably absolute contraindication |

**On-cycle (weeks 4–6 of first cycle):**

- ALT, AST (hepatic safety signal)
- CBC with differential (proliferative signal)
- Cognitive reassessment with baseline-matched instrument
- Sleep diary or structured sleep assessment (vivid dreams and insomnia are the most consistent community-reported adverse effects; late-day dosing exacerbates)
- Physical inspection / self-report for new lumps, unexplained weight loss, GI symptoms, persistent neurological signs (headache with focal signs, new visual symptoms)

**Stopping rules (rational — not validated by any trial):**

- ALT or AST > 3× upper limit of normal at any on-cycle measurement
- New lymphadenopathy, mass, unexplained weight loss, or hemoptysis
- Persistent (>4-week) injection-site induration or abscess (if injectable route)
- Any clinical suspicion of malignancy — suspend and pursue workup before any reconsideration

**Concurrent medication contraindication:**

Any concurrent use of capmatinib, tepotinib, or other c-Met inhibitors is pharmacodynamically contraindicated. A c-Met pathway agonist and a c-Met pathway inhibitor administered together constitute direct pharmacological opposition; no rational therapeutic rationale for co-administration exists, and the theoretical harm (either blunting the oncologic treatment or driving paradoxical signaling) is real. This is not a speculative drug-interaction — it follows directly from the proposed mechanism.
