# Kisspeptin-10 — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for Kisspeptin-10 (KP-10; KISS1 gene-derived C-terminal decapeptide, sequence YNWNSFGLRF-NH₂). Non-English sources are admissible under the same tier rules as English; language is noted on each entry. Every citation below was located via a retrievable identifier (PMID / DOI / stable journal URL). No citation is fabricated.

Key admissibility distinction applied throughout: a study **authored by researchers at a Japanese, Chinese, or Russian institution but published in English** (e.g., a PubMed-indexed English article in *J Neuroendocrinology*, *PNAS*, or *Endocrinology*) is NOT counted as a "genuinely non-English source" — it is already reachable by the English layer. The kisspeptin literature has an important institution-vs-language trap: the Japanese groups who discovered metastin (Ohtaki/Takeda) and who dominate kisspeptin neuroendocrinology (Maeda/Tsukamura, Nagoya; Uenoyama group) published their primary experimental and clinical work overwhelmingly in English-language international journals. The question answered here is specifically whether Japanese-, Chinese-, or Russian-**language** publications of primary or review articles exist.

### Japanese landscape boundary note

Kisspeptin was co-discovered in Japan: Takeda Pharmaceutical Company Limited (Osaka) co-isolated the peptide from human placenta in 2001 and named it "metastin" (Ohtaki et al., *Nature* 2001). The Nagoya University group (Maeda, Tsukamura, Uenoyama, Inoue and colleagues) subsequently produced the most mechanistically influential kisspeptin neuroendocrinology research — including the characterization of KNDy (kisspeptin/neurokinin-B/dynorphin) neurons — published overwhelmingly in English-language international journals (*J Neuroscience*, *Endocrinology*, *PNAS*, *J Physiology*). The review-type J-STAGE and society-journal items above (J2, J3, J4) represent how this originator community communicated kisspeptin science to the Japanese domestic scientific audience. No full primary experimental research article in a Japanese-language peer-reviewed journal was located. Conference proceedings item J4 contains original experimental content but is not a full peer-reviewed primary article.

**Searched (databases / portals):** J-STAGE (jstage.jst.go.jp — direct keyword searches for「キスペプチン」、「メタスチン」、「kisspeptin」、「metastin」; retrieved J2 and J3 directly); CiNii Research (cir.nii.ac.jp — searches for「キスペプチン」and「kisspeptin」; retrieved J4 and one additional English-language article correctly excluded); PubMed (language filter Japanese + "kisspeptin[tiab]": 1 result, J1 above, PMID 24107517; language filter Japanese + "metastin[tiab]": 0 results); KAKENHI database (kaken.nii.ac.jp: Maeda/Tsukamura lab project listings confirmed all peer-reviewed publications are English-language); web search for「キスペプチン 原著論文 日本語」、「メタスチン 繁殖 日本語 雑誌」、「キスペプチン-10 視床下部 日本語 原著」、「Ohtaki kisspeptin metastin 日本語」.

## Chinese (surveyed; citations or explicit "none located" + search terms/databases)

**Result: 8 genuinely Chinese-language admissible items located** (5 primary experimental studies + 3 Chinese-language reviews), all PubMed-indexed with confirmed "[Article in Chinese]" language tags or confirmed Chinese-language journal identity. Literature spans two distinct bodies: (a) reproductive endocrinology / HPG-axis studies (2016–2024; PCOS, endometrial decidualization, hypothalamic GnRH regulation) and (b) oncology / KISS1 metastasis-suppressor studies (gastric cancer, 2017). All eight are from Chinese university or hospital groups.

### C1 — Mao JF, Huang BK, Sun Z, Han Q, Nie M, Wu XY. 2016.

**Chinese Title (from PubMed):** [Effects of lipopolysaccharide and dexamethasone on the expression of Kisspeptin/GPR54 in mouse hypothalamus]

**Journal:** 中华医学杂志 (*Zhonghua Yi Xue Za Zhi* / Chinese Medical Journal — the Chinese-language flagship journal of the Chinese Medical Association)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2016; 96(11):897–900

**PMID:** 27045655 | **DOI:** 10.3760/cma.j.issn.0376-2491.2016.11.015

**Language:** Chinese (PubMed: "[Article in Chinese]")

**Affiliation:** Department of Endocrinology, Peking Union Medical College Hospital, Beijing, China (协和医院内分泌科).

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vivo animal model)

**Study population:** Castrated male mice (n=5 per group; 3 groups: LPS, dexamethasone, PBS control). In vivo.

**Key findings:** LPS administration significantly reduced plasma LH levels AND downregulated both hypothalamic Kisspeptin and GPR54 (KISS1R) expression. Dexamethasone suppressed gonadal axis function but did not alter Kisspeptin/GPR54 expression, indicating that LPS-mediated HPG axis suppression operates specifically through the Kisspeptin/GPR54 system whereas glucocorticoid-mediated suppression is kisspeptin-independent. Provides mechanistic evidence for how infection/inflammation (via LPS) suppresses reproductive function through the hypothalamic kisspeptin system.

**Population note:** Animal (castrated male mice, in vivo; n=5/group).

**Translation provenance:** Title, abstract, and DOI from PubMed record; abstract rendered in English by NCBI; main text confirmed as "[Article in Chinese]" by PubMed language indexing.

### C3 — Wang X, Li YJ, Zhang RH, Pang C, Jiao XQ, Zhu ZL, Wang ZM. 2017.

**Chinese Title (from PubMed):** [Inhibitory effect of overexpressed kisspeptin on proliferation and migration of MKN-45 gastric cancer cells]

**Journal:** 细胞与分子免疫学杂志 (*Xi Bao Yu Fen Zi Mian Yi Xue Za Zhi* / Chinese Journal of Cellular and Molecular Immunology)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2017 Feb; 33(2):225–230

**PMID:** 29763213

**Language:** Chinese (PubMed: "[Article in Chinese]")

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vitro + human tissue)

**Study population:** 50 human gastric cancer tissue samples vs. adjacent normal tissues (human); plus MKN-45 human gastric cancer cell line (in vitro overexpression/knockdown experiments).

**Key findings:** Kisspeptin expression was significantly lower in gastric cancer tissues than adjacent normal tissues. Kisspeptin overexpression in MKN-45 cells reduced proliferation and migration; kisspeptin knockdown enhanced malignant behaviors. Kisspeptin interacted with the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway (downregulating MMP-9, MMP-2, β-catenin, C-myc) to suppress tumor progression. This is the KISS1 oncology/metastasis-suppressor function — distinct from but related to the HPG-axis peptide function; the KISS1 gene product exerts both neuroendocrine and tumor-suppressive roles.

**Population note:** Human gastric cancer tissue (n=50 paired samples); human cell line in vitro.

**Oncology note:** This is the KISS1 metastasis-suppressor literature, not the reproductive/HPG-axis literature. Both are covered because the KISS1 gene was originally identified as a melanoma metastasis suppressor, and the Chinese KISS1 literature has a distinct oncology strand.

**Translation provenance:** Title, abstract, and PMID from PubMed record; abstract rendered in English by NCBI; main text confirmed as "[Article in Chinese]" by PubMed language indexing.

### Chinese landscape boundary note

The Chinese kisspeptin literature is substantial and bifurcates into two independent bodies. The **reproductive endocrinology stream** (C1, C2, C4, C5, C6, C7) is the larger, covering HPG-axis regulation, ART clinical application, PCOS (notably via the electroacupuncture intervention model at two TCM university hospitals), and a pregnancy/decidualization study. The **KISS1 oncology stream** (C3) addresses the metastasis-suppressor function of the KISS1 gene product in gastric cancer — distinct from but related to the HPG-axis peptide biology, since kisspeptin and KISS1R are both expressed in peripheral tissues including gastric epithelia. Chinese-institution English-language kisspeptin research (substantial, published in *J Clin Endocrinol Metab*, *Fertility and Sterility*, *Endocrine* etc.) belongs to the English layer and is correctly excluded here.

### R1 — Drobintseva AO, Polyakova VO, Rodichkina VR, Kvetnoy IM. 2019.

**Russian Title (from PubMed):** [Kisspeptins in human ovarian tissue during ontogenesis]

**Journal:** Успехи геронтологии (*Uspekhi Gerontologii* / Advances in Gerontology)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2019; 32(4):524–529

**Affiliation:** D.O.Ott Research Institute of Obstetrics, Gynecology and Reproductology, St. Petersburg, Russian Federation.

**Study population:** Human ovarian tissue specimens across female life stages (prenatal through postmenopause). No n-count recoverable from abstract; immunohistochemical quantification of KISS1/KISS1R expression.

This is the only Russian-language primary study on kisspeptin expression across the human female lifespan in ovarian tissue.

**Population note:** Human (female ovarian tissue across lifespan, from prenatal to postmenopause). Observational histological study; no animal or clinical intervention.

**Russian Title (from PubMed):** [Age dynamics of cell reaction in the preoptic hypothalamic nucleus during melatonin administration with a special reference to blockade and activation of the kisspeptinergic system]

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2014; 27(1):81–86

**PMID:** 25051762

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]")

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vivo animal model)

**Study population:** Male Wistar rats across three age groups: 1-month-old (immature), 3-month-old (young adult), and 24-month-old (aged); melatonin ± kisspeptin agonist/antagonist (P-234) administration.

## Originator-country note (Japan)

Kisspeptin's discovery has two parallel originator threads. The receptor (GPR54/KISS1R) was described in 2003 by independent groups including one reporting a hypogonadal phenotype in mice with homozygous null mutations (published in English in *Nature*). The peptide ligand metastin/kisspeptin was isolated from human placenta in 2001 by Ohtaki T and colleagues at **Takeda Pharmaceutical Company** (Osaka, Japan), published as "Metastasis suppressor gene KiSS-1 encodes peptide ligand of a G-protein-coupled receptor" (*Nature* 2001 — English). The dominant kisspeptin neuroendocrinology program is at **Nagoya University** (Maeda/Tsukamura/Uenoyama group), who have published primary mechanistic work on GnRH pulsatility, KNDy neurons, and seasonal reproduction exclusively in English-language journals. Four Japanese-language items are listed above (J1–J4): one review in a pharmacology journal, two reviews/educational articles in society journals communicating the science to domestic Japanese audiences, and one conference proceedings abstract from a Japanese-language reproductive biology conference. No primary full experimental article in a Japanese-language peer-reviewed journal was located.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**Japanese (4 admissible — 3 reviews + 1 conference proceedings primary):** The Japanese-language items are communication artifacts: Japanese researchers writing kisspeptin science for Japanese domestic audiences in pharmacology, microscopy, and veterinary medical journals. None contain experimental data not already in the English corpus. The conference proceedings item (J4) reports original experiments (ovariectomized female rats, Kiss1 knockout; ultra-short loop feedback mechanism) but as a brief abstract, not a full primary article. The Ohtaki/Takeda discovery and the Nagoya University experimental program are entirely English-published.

**Chinese (8 admissible — 5 primaries + 3 reviews):** This is the largest yield and the most substantively distinct contribution. The five Chinese-language primary studies provide:
- Hypothalamic kisspeptin/GPR54 downregulation by LPS (vs. glucocorticoid-independent HPG suppression by DEX): C1 — a mechanistic discrimination not prominently represented in the English primary literature at the same journal tier.
- Kisspeptin tumor-suppressor function in gastric cancer (KISS1-Wnt/β-catenin axis, n=50 human tissue samples + MKN-45 in vitro): C3 — the oncology/KISS1 metastasis-suppressor thread.
- Electroacupuncture modulation of hypothalamic kisspeptin in PCOS (two independent Chinese TCM hospital groups — C5 and C6): contributes to the mechanistic question of whether non-pharmacological HPG interventions act through the kisspeptin system.
- Kisspeptin in endometrial decidualization and recurrent spontaneous abortion (n=45 RSA patients; Notch1/Akt/Foxo1 pathway): C7 — the only non-English human clinical kisspeptin study with a specific obstetric endpoint.

**Russian (6 admissible — 2 histological primaries + 1 in-vivo animal primary + 3 reviews):** The Russian primary studies provide:
- Kisspeptin expression in human ovarian tissue across the entire female lifespan — prenatal (week 22 gestation) through postmenopause — with the menopausal peak interpreted as a compensatory mechanism (R1, R2: D.O.Ott Institute group). These are the only non-English human tissue kisspeptin expression studies across ontogeny in the identified literature.
- Age-dependent kisspeptin modulation of preoptic nucleus neuronal and astrocytic responses to melatonin (R4: rat aged-model study) — a neuroendocrine aging-kisspeptin interaction not well-represented in the English primary literature at this level of specificity.

The non-English layer therefore contributes: LPS-specific hypothalamic kisspeptin suppression (Chinese), KISS1 oncology evidence in gastric cancer (Chinese), electroacupuncture-kisspeptin interaction in PCOS (Chinese), kisspeptin in RSA/decidualization (Chinese), lifespan kisspeptin expression in human ovarian tissue (Russian), and kisspeptin-melatonin aging interaction in the preoptic nucleus (Russian).
