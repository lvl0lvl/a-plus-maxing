# Kisspeptin-10 (KP-10)

## Metadata

- **class:** peptide (endogenous neuropeptide; KISS1 gene product; hypothalamic HPG-axis regulator)
- **evidence_tier:** C — Genuine human physiology data in small proof-of-concept studies; KP-10's own direct human evidence is limited to approximately three small trials (n ≤ 10 per study). The substantial HPG-axis clinical literature belongs to the pharmacokinetically distinct isoform KP-54. The human sexual/limbic evidence is five high-quality RCTs published in tier-1 journals, but all from a single research group (Imperial College London) with no independent replication. No Phase 3 trial exists for any kisspeptin form.
- **risk_tier:** experimental — No FDA-approved product, no 503A compounding pathway (PCAC voted against, October 2024), and WADA-explicit prohibition under S2.2.1 for males at all times. Grey-market preparations carry unverifiable purity and sequence-fidelity risks. The bolus-stimulates / continuous-desensitizes pharmacology is documented in human subjects and constitutes a genuine risk for naive dosing. (No investigational-no-approved-product enum exists; "experimental" is the valid tier for this profile.)
- **status:** researching
- **last_verified:** 2026-06-21

## Summary

Kisspeptin-10 (KP-10; sequence YNWNSFGLRF-NH₂; molecular weight ~1302.5 Da) is the 10-amino-acid C-terminal decapeptide of the KISS1 gene product, produced by proteolytic processing of the 145-amino-acid prepropeptide [1, mechanism_review]. It is the minimal bioactive fragment of the kisspeptin family, retaining full agonist activity at KISS1R (GPR54) — a Gq/11-coupled seven-transmembrane receptor expressed on hypothalamic GnRH neurons — and sharing approximately equipotent receptor-binding affinity with the longer isoforms KP-13, KP-14, and KP-54 in radioligand displacement assays (IC₅₀ ~1.0 nM at human KISS1R in CHO cells) [5, animal]. KISS1R binding triggers a canonical Gαq/11 → PLC-β → IP₃ → intracellular Ca²⁺ → membrane-depolarization cascade that drives pulsatile GnRH secretion, positioning kisspeptin as the master upstream regulator of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis [6, mechanism_review].

**What the research genuinely shows.** KP-10 has solid mechanistic grounding as an HPG-axis activator: genetic loss-of-function mutations in KISS1R cause idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (IHH) in humans [3, cohort], and IV bolus KP-10 dose-dependently raises LH (peak LH ~12.4 IU/L at 1 µg/kg in healthy men) [9, open_label]. A sequenced program of fMRI and HSDD trials, all from Imperial College London, shows that IV kisspeptin modulates limbic/sexual brain circuits, reduces neural correlates of sexual aversion, and increases penile tumescence by 56% over placebo in men with confirmed HSDD [21, rct], [24, rct], [25, rct]. The IVF literature establishes that kisspeptin can substitute for hCG as an oocyte-maturation trigger in high-OHSS-risk women with an excellent safety profile [18, open_label].

**The load-bearing honest caveats — read before applying any claim.** Four structural constraints on this evidence base are not quibbles; they are the organizing logic of this entire entry:

**Caveat 1 — KP-10's own direct human evidence is thin.** Only approximately three small studies have administered KP-10 specifically to humans: George et al. 2011 (n=6/4 healthy men, IV bolus/infusion) [9, open_label], Jayasena et al. 2011 (n=12 healthy men and women, IV/SC bolus) [28, open_label], and George et al. 2013 (n=5 hypogonadal diabetic men + 7 controls) [17, open_label]. The vast majority of the HPG-stimulation, hypothalamic-amenorrhea, and IVF evidence is for **KP-54** — a distinct isoform with a circulating half-life of approximately 28 minutes in humans (27.6 min, Dhillo 2005 [11, rct]) versus KP-10's ~4 minutes [7, open_label]. KP-10 and KP-54 bind the same receptor and show broadly similar per-receptor-occupancy potency in head-to-head pharmacological comparison [12, open_label], but KP-10's dramatically shorter half-life means that equivalent mass doses produce far shorter-duration and lower-peak plasma exposures. Any claim that "kisspeptin stimulates LH / restores cycles / triggers ovulation" that draws on a KP-54 study is extrapolated, not directly established, for KP-10.

**Caveat 2 — The human sexual/limbic evidence is single-center and unreplicated.** All five human RCTs demonstrating that kisspeptin modulates sexual brain processing and HSDD symptoms were conducted at Imperial College London by the Comninos/Dhillo group [21, rct], [22, rct], [23, rct], [24, rct], [25, rct]. No independent group has run a confirmatory kisspeptin fMRI or HSDD efficacy study. The trials are methodologically sound, published in tier-1 journals (JCI, JCI Insight, JAMA Network Open), and represent the most rigorous human evidence that a peripheral peptide can modulate central sexual-desire circuitry — but they cannot yet be considered independently replicated. Additionally, the fMRI and HSDD trials used **KP-54** (or did not specify the isoform, with KP-54 being the standard IV formulation at Imperial), not KP-10. Consumer claims that "KP-10 improves libido" are extrapolating from KP-54 HSDD data produced by a single group.

**Caveat 3 — The bolus-stimulates / continuous-desensitizes pharmacology is a real clinical risk.** High-dose or continuous kisspeptin exposure causes KISS1R phosphorylation, β-arrestin recruitment, and receptor internalization, resulting in paradoxical HPG-axis suppression rather than stimulation [6, mechanism_review]. In women with hypothalamic amenorrhea, twice-daily subcutaneous KP-54 produced robust LH responses on Day 1 (peak 24.0 ± 3.5 IU/L) but near-complete tachyphylaxis by Day 14 (2.5 ± 2.2 IU/L) [15, open_label]. In healthy men, the 3 µg/kg IV bolus produced a blunted LH response versus the 1 µg/kg bolus — an early signal of dose-dependent receptor saturation [9, open_label]. This means naive daily or high-frequency grey-market dosing can paradoxically suppress testosterone rather than raising it, converting a stimulatory agent into an HPG suppressant within approximately two weeks of continuous exposure.

**Caveat 4 — WADA explicitly prohibits kisspeptin; FDA has no approved product and closed the compounding pathway.** Kisspeptin was first explicitly named in the 2024 WADA Prohibited List update (effective January 1, 2024) under S2.2.1 (Testosterone-Stimulating Peptides in Males) and is carried into the current 2026 Prohibited List [32, regulatory]. The prohibition is in-competition and out-of-competition for males. (Note: the S2.2.1 sub-section number is from secondary sources — the primary WADA PDF was access-blocked at research time; corroborated via USADA's 2024 key-changes document and multiple secondary regulatory summaries reproducing the S2.2.1 language verbatim.) The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted against adding kisspeptin-10 to the 503A Bulk Drug Substances List in October 2024, citing insufficient physicochemical characterization, parenteral immunogenicity risk, inadequate effectiveness evidence, and available approved alternatives [33, regulatory]. No kisspeptin form holds marketing approval anywhere in the world.

**Class context: PT-141 is the approved HSDD peptide, not KP-10.** The FDA-approved peptide for female HSDD is bremelanotide/Vyleesi (PT-141), a melanocortin MC4R/MC3R agonist with a completely distinct mechanism — dopaminergic/oxytocinergic arousal via the nucleus accumbens/VTA pathway, not GnRH/limbic command [27, regulatory]. Bremelanotide's approval rests on two 24-week phase 3 RCTs in 1,247 women. Kisspeptin's regulatory status and evidence base are both substantially less mature. These compounds' evidence bases must not be merged.

**Concentration note.** The broad kisspeptin human-translational literature clears the 70% single-group concentration flag: at least four verified independent groups have conducted original human-dosing studies (Imperial College London, University of Edinburgh/MRC, Harvard/MGH, Mayo Clinic). Estimated Imperial share of the full human-translational literature is approximately 45–55%. However, the human sexual-brain and HSDD sub-literature — which is the primary basis for consumer "libido" claims — is effectively 100% Imperial-concentrated with no independent replication. The two bodies of literature have meaningfully different concentration profiles, and any entry presenting HSDD evidence must distinguish them.

**MVT-602 pipeline note.** The only KISS1R agonist in active clinical development is MVT-602 (TAK-448; Myovant Sciences/Sumitomo Pharma America), a synthetic analog with a half-life of approximately 108 minutes IV. Phase 2a data in healthy premenopausal women have been published; no Phase 3 trial has been registered as of mid-2026 [33, regulatory]. KP-10 and MVT-602 are distinct compounds; clinical trial results for MVT-602 are not attributable to KP-10.

## 1. Identity and Molecular Mechanism

### 1.1 The KISS1 Gene Product and Peptide Family

Kisspeptin-10 is the 10-amino-acid C-terminal fragment of the KISS1 gene product — a 145-amino-acid prepropeptide (in humans; rodents produce a 52-residue longest processed form) cleaved by endoproteases to yield a family of C-terminally amidated peptides that share the pharmacophore RF-NH₂ motif [1, mechanism_review]. The four biologically recognized isoforms in humans are KP-54 (the longest processed form, originally isolated from human placenta in 2001 and named "metastin" for its tumour-metastasis suppressor activity) [2, in_vitro], KP-14 and KP-13 (shorter processed fragments detected in human plasma and isolated alongside KP-54) [4, in_vitro], and KP-10, the minimal bioactive C-terminal decapeptide. The gene itself was first described in 1996 as a melanoma metastasis suppressor (introduction of normal chromosome 6 into metastatic melanoma cells suppressed metastasis by ~95% without affecting primary tumour growth), yielding the name KISS1 — a combination of an interim sequence designation and the Hershey, Pennsylvania laboratory of origin [3, cohort].

The KP-10 sequence is Tyr-Asn-Trp-Asn-Ser-Phe-Gly-Leu-Arg-Phe-NH₂ (YNWNSFGLRF-NH₂), C-terminally amidated; molecular formula C₆₃H₈₃N₁₇O₁₄; molecular weight approximately 1302.5 Da [5, animal]. The decapeptide is highly conserved across mammalian species (one amino acid difference between human and rodent sequences) and retains full receptor-binding potency relative to the longer isoforms: competitive radioligand binding in CHO cells expressing human KISS1R yields an IC₅₀ of approximately 1.0 nM for KP-10, comparable to KP-54 and in some assays approximately 8-fold more potent, identifying the C-terminal decapeptide as the pharmacophore of the entire family [5, animal], [6, mechanism_review].

The name "metastin" coined by Ohtaki et al. at Takeda Chemical Industries (2001) [2, in_vitro] and "kisspeptin" coined independently by Kotani et al. (2001) [4, in_vitro] are both in current use as synonyms; KISS1R is the accepted receptor nomenclature (GPR54 is retained as a synonym in older literature). The history is not merely taxonomic: these two 2001 papers, published simultaneously by fully independent groups (Takeda/Tsukuba and an EU/Belgian consortium), represented the first identification of the endogenous ligand for orphan receptor GPR54. The foundational human genetics paper — Seminara et al. 2003 in the New England Journal of Medicine — established causal necessity of KISS1R for puberty by identifying homozygous loss-of-function mutations (L148S; compound R331X/X399R) in consanguineous pedigrees with IHH, confirmed by GPR54-null mouse knockout validation [3, cohort]. This genetic evidence places kisspeptin in the rare category of peptides whose physiological necessity is established at the human population level, not merely inferred from pharmacological studies.

### 1.2 KISS1R Signal Transduction: Gαq/11 to GnRH

KP-10, KP-13, KP-14, and KP-54 are all full agonists at KISS1R (gene locus Xq21.3), a seven-transmembrane Gq/11-coupled receptor [1, 6, mechanism_review]. Upon KP-10 binding, the canonical intracellular cascade proceeds through Gαq/11 activation, phospholipase C-β (PLC-β) cleavage of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP₂), and generation of inositol-1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP₃) and diacylglycerol (DAG). IP₃ mobilizes intracellular Ca²⁺ from the endoplasmic reticulum; DAG activates protein kinase C (PKC); the combined signaling drives membrane depolarization of the GnRH neuron, triggering action potential firing and GnRH peptide secretion into the hypophysial portal circulation [6, mechanism_review].

This mechanism is established from multiple converging lines: rodent in vitro electrophysiology, calcium-imaging in hypothalamic slice preparations, cell-line characterization in CHO cells expressing human KISS1R [5, animal], and the critical human pharmacological validation that GnRH receptor antagonists completely block the gonadotropin response to intravenous KP-10 [6, mechanism_review] — confirming the GnRH relay as obligate in humans rather than representing a direct KP-10 effect on pituitary gonadotrophs.

### 1.3 KP-10 as Hypothalamic Pulse Generator

Kisspeptin neurons of the arcuate nucleus (ARC) and anteroventral periventricular nucleus (AVPV) project to GnRH neuronal perikarya and nerve terminals in the median eminence [8, mechanism_review]. The ARC population co-expresses neurokinin B and dynorphin — the "KNDy neurons" (Kisspeptin-Neurokinin B-Dynorphin) — and optogenetic and chemogenetic studies in transgenic calcium-reporter mice have established tight temporal correlation between synchronized KNDy neuronal activity and pulsatile LH secretion, identifying these neurons as the mammalian GnRH pulse generator pacemaker [8, mechanism_review]. KP-10 administered exogenously mimics this endogenous pulsatile signal: IV bolus KP-10 at 1 µg/kg in healthy men produced peak serum LH of 12.4 ± 1.7 IU/L at 30 minutes versus baseline 4.1 ± 0.4 IU/L, and continuous IV infusion at 1.5 µg/kg/h increased LH pulse frequency from 0.7 ± 0.1 to 1.0 ± 0.2 pulses/hour in the same study (n=6 IV bolus phase / n=4 infusion phases, within-subject crossover) [9, open_label].

Gain-of-function KISS1R mutations (e.g., R385P) produce the mirror phenotype: central precocious puberty [6, mechanism_review]. KISS1 itself was subsequently shown causal: a homozygous inactivating KISS1 mutation (p.Pro74Ser) was identified in a consanguineous Turkish kindred with normosmic IHH [6, mechanism_review], establishing both ligand and receptor as non-redundant gatekeepers of pubertal onset and demonstrating that the kisspeptin → GnRH → HPG-axis cascade is not merely modulatory but structurally required for the reproductive axis to function.

### 1.4 Central vs. Peripheral Kisspeptin Biology

KISS1R is expressed in both central and peripheral tissues. Central expression is highest in the hypothalamic ARC and AVPV, where it drives reproductive axis pulsatility. Peripheral expression occurs in pituitary, gonads, placenta (the original discovery tissue for KP-54/metastin), liver, kidney, vascular smooth muscle, and myocardium [1, mechanism_review]. Peripheral kisspeptin has been investigated in placental biology (markedly elevated circulating KP-54 during human pregnancy reflects placental secretion), insulin secretion, and cardiovascular tone, but these roles remain mechanistically less defined than the central HPG function. A dual pathway has been proposed for kisspeptin's effects on sexual behavior: HPG-axis priming (downstream gonadal steroid production) combined with direct limbic circuit engagement through nitric oxide signaling in the ventromedial hypothalamus, a pathway demonstrated in rodent models that does not require downstream hormonal mediation [26, mechanism_review]. This dual-pathway model is the mechanistic basis for the human fMRI findings discussed in Section 3.

### 1.5 Pharmacokinetics of KP-10 and the KP-10 vs. KP-54 Half-Life Gap

**KP-10 plasma half-life: ~4 minutes.** In mice, measured plasma disappearance of equimolar doses gives KP-10 a circulating half-life of approximately 4 minutes versus KP-54's approximately 32 minutes [10, animal]. In humans, IV bolus KP-10 yields a measured half-life of 3.8 ± 0.3 minutes in men and 4.1 ± 0.4 minutes in follicular-phase women [28, open_label]. The short half-life reflects KP-10's susceptibility to N-terminal tyrosine cleavage and aminopeptidase degradation; KP-54's 44-residue N-terminal extension protects the C-terminal pharmacophore, yielding a human KP-54 half-life of approximately 28 minutes (27.6 min measured in Dhillo 2005 [11, rct]); mouse KP-54 half-life is approximately 32 minutes [10, animal].

This pharmacokinetic gap has profound in vivo consequences. After equimolar subcutaneous dosing in mice, KP-54 reaches peak plasma levels approximately 50-fold higher than KP-10 [10, animal]. KP-10 appears to act primarily at GnRH nerve terminals in the median eminence, which are accessible outside the blood-brain barrier, while KP-54 additionally activates GnRH neuron perikarya situated behind the BBB — as evidenced by differential c-FOS immunostaining patterns [10, animal]. In a direct human head-to-head pharmacological comparison (Jayasena et al. 2015; n=10 healthy men, three escalating IV doses, single-blinded crossover), both isoforms showed "broadly similar potencies" for LH stimulation despite KP-54 having ~37-fold higher plasma immunoreactivity at equivalent molar doses — confirming per-receptor-occupancy equivalence [12, open_label]. GnRH was approximately 3-fold more potent than KP-10 and approximately 2-fold more potent than KP-54 at matched molar doses in the same study.

The practical implication is that any KP-54 dosing study cannot be numerically translated to KP-10 dosing without accounting for the half-life differential. A dose of KP-54 producing a 30-minute LH surge requires a substantially higher molar dose of KP-10 to match, and the KP-10 dose must be administered more frequently (or as continuous infusion) to achieve equivalent duration of receptor engagement.

### 1.6 The Bolus-Stimulates / Continuous-Desensitizes Pharmacodynamic Dichotomy

The most clinically critical pharmacodynamic property of KP-10 — and the one most systematically ignored in grey-market promotion — is the dose- and frequency-dependent receptor desensitization that converts a stimulatory agent into an HPG suppressant.

High or continuous kisspeptin exposure causes KISS1R phosphorylation, β-arrestin-1/2 recruitment, and clathrin-mediated receptor internalization (internalization mechanism established in vitro; β-arrestin evidence from cell lines; confirmed functionally in rodent and primate in vivo and in human clinical studies) [6, mechanism_review]. In rodent and primate models, continuous kisspeptin infusion initially stimulates LH and then abolishes the LH response to a subsequent bolus; responsiveness is restored within approximately 21 hours of stopping infusion [6, mechanism_review]. In women with hypothalamic amenorrhea (n=5), IV KP-54 infusion at the highest dose (1.0 nmol/kg/h) showed peak LH at approximately 5 hours then a ~50% progressive decline despite continued infusion, placing the desensitization at the KISS1R/kisspeptin-neuron level (a GnRH challenge confirmed preserved pituitary responsiveness) [7, open_label]. In healthy men, the 3 µg/kg IV bolus produced a blunted LH response compared with the 1 µg/kg bolus — an in-human signal of dose-dependent receptor saturation [9, open_label]. And in women with hypothalamic amenorrhea treated with twice-daily subcutaneous KP-54 for 14 days, peak LH on Day 1 was 24.0 ± 3.5 IU/L and had fallen to 2.5 ± 2.2 IU/L by Day 14 — near-complete tachyphylaxis within two weeks [15, open_label].

Intermittent pulsatile administration, by contrast, maintains receptor responsiveness. Twice-weekly (rather than twice-daily) subcutaneous KP-54 for 8 weeks in women with hypothalamic amenorrhea produced only partial rather than complete desensitization, and reproductive hormone release was maintained across the 8-week period [16, open_label]. The clinical trials that produced positive results — whether for HPG stimulation, HSDD, or IVF triggering — all used either single bolus administration or carefully spaced pulsatile dosing. The therapeutic window is narrow, context-dependent, and dependent on dosing interval discipline. Naïve continuous or frequent daily administration by self-injecting consumers risks producing HPG suppression rather than stimulation — the pharmacological opposite of the intended effect.

## 2. Reproductive and HPG-Axis Clinical Evidence

### 2.1 Framing: KP-10 vs. KP-54 in the Human Clinical Literature

Before reviewing individual studies, the isoform attribution discipline established in Section 1.5 must be applied systematically. The table at the end of this section lists every cited study with its KP-form annotation. Where KP-54 is the study agent, the evidence for KP-10 is extrapolated from receptor-level equivalence data, not directly established.

### 2.2 HPG-Axis Stimulation in Healthy Men: Proof of Concept

The first-in-human kisspeptin study — Dhillo et al. 2005, a double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover RCT in 6 healthy adult men — administered intravenous KP-54 and demonstrated dose-dependent elevation of LH (mean 90-min LH from 4.2 to 10.8 IU/L; p<0.001), FSH (3.2 to 3.9 IU/L; p<0.001), and testosterone (21.7 to 24.9 nmol/L at 180 minutes; p<0.001) versus saline, with an estimated plasma half-life of 27.6 minutes [11, rct]. This is proof-of-concept physiology in 6 men; KP-54 is the agent.

The dedicated KP-10 human dose-response study — George et al. 2011, a within-subject crossover in 6 healthy adult men (IV bolus phase) and 4 (infusion phases) — established that IV bolus KP-10 dose-dependently raises LH (peak at 1 µg/kg: 4.1 → 12.4 IU/L), that continuous KP-10 infusion at 1.5 µg/kg·h increases LH pulse frequency (0.7 → 1.0 pulses/h), and that higher-dose infusion (4 µg/kg·h for 22.5 hours) raises testosterone from 16.6 to 24.0 nmol/L without tachyphylaxis over that duration [9, open_label]. This is the primary KP-10-specific human evidence for HPG engagement. Very small n; open-label design; healthy men only. The finding that 3 µg/kg IV blunted LH relative to 1 µg/kg is the in-human desensitization signal documented in Section 1.6.

The human head-to-head isoform comparison — Jayasena et al. 2015, a single-blinded crossover in 10 healthy men — tested IV infusions of KP-10, KP-54, and GnRH at three escalating molar doses and found broadly similar LH stimulatory potencies for both kisspeptin isoforms despite KP-54's 37-fold higher plasma immunoreactivity, establishing per-receptor-occupancy equivalence and quantifying the GnRH benchmark [12, open_label]. This is the key pharmacological comparison that supports using KP-54 data as a structural analog reference for KP-10 receptor biology — while the plasma-exposure gap renders them non-interchangeable as dosing references.

### 2.3 HPG-Axis Stimulation in Healthy Women: Cycle-Phase Dependence

Dhillo et al. 2007 — the first human study of kisspeptin in women (dose-escalation n=3–4 per dose; cycle-phase comparison n=8 healthy women with regular cycles) — administered IV KP-54 and demonstrated dose-dependent LH increases (0.2–6.4 nmol/kg range) with a critical cycle-phase gating effect: mean LH AUC increase was greatest in the preovulatory (late follicular) phase (~20.64 IU/L) and near-absent in the early follicular phase (0.12 IU/L), demonstrating that estrogen priming is required for full LH responsiveness to kisspeptin [13, open_label]. This cycle-phase gating is pharmacologically important for any therapeutic timing consideration. Small n; open-label; healthy women; KP-54 agent.

Jayasena et al. 2013 — a prospective, single-blinded, crossover trial in 10 healthy women (5 per arm) — administered twice-daily subcutaneous KP-54 for 7 days during the mid-follicular phase (days 7–14), finding shortened mean cycle length (28.6 → 26.8 days), advanced luteal-phase onset, and increased dominant follicle diameter on day 15 without abolishing menstrual cyclicity [14, open_label]. This established that intermittent pulsatile-ish dosing in healthy women does not suppress the axis over a 7-day window, providing early evidence for the dosing-interval principle that less frequent administration preserves receptor responsiveness [16, open_label].

Sexual dimorphism in KP-10 response was documented by Jayasena et al. 2011 in the key KP-10-specific pharmacology study (IV bolus 0.3–10 nmol/kg in men and women; SC bolus 2–32 nmol/kg in women; measured half-life 3.8 ± 0.3 min in men, 4.1 ± 0.4 min in follicular-phase women): gonadotropin response to equivalent molar doses was substantially greater in women during the preovulatory phase than in men or early-follicular-phase women, consistent with the estrogen-priming gate [28, open_label].

### 2.4 Hypothalamic Amenorrhea and Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism

The hypothalamic amenorrhea (HA) literature is the most mechanistically coherent application of kisspeptin pharmacology: women with HA have intact pituitary and gonadal function but deficient hypothalamic GnRH pulsatility, the precise node where kisspeptin acts. However, this evidence is entirely KP-54, and the tachyphylaxis findings within it carry direct implications for any therapeutic use of KP-10.

Jayasena et al. 2009 enrolled 10 women with hypothalamic amenorrhea (n=5 received kisspeptin-54; n=5 received saline) in an acute + chronic subcutaneous dosing study [15, open_label]. In the 5 kisspeptin-treated participants, acute twice-daily subcutaneous KP-54 (6.4 nmol/kg) stimulated LH (Day 1 peak: 24.0 ± 3.5 IU/L) and FSH (9.1 ± 2.5 IU/L), but chronic administration at the same frequency caused near-complete tachyphylaxis by Day 14 (LH 2.5 ± 2.2 IU/L; FSH 0.5 ± 0.5 IU/L) with preserved GnRH responsiveness — confirming KISS1R-level desensitization and ruling out twice-daily dosing as a sustainable therapeutic schedule. This is the canonical human tachyphylaxis demonstration.

Jayasena et al. 2010 applied a lower-frequency dosing strategy — twice-weekly rather than twice-daily subcutaneous KP-54 for 8 weeks in women with HA — and produced only partial desensitization with maintained reproductive hormone release across the 8-week period [16, open_label]. This established the dosing-interval principle that governs all subsequent therapeutic thinking in the HA application.

Jayasena et al. 2014, a single-blinded placebo-controlled dose-escalation trial in 5 women with functional HA, administered continuous IV KP-54 infusion at 0.01–1.0 nmol/kg/h for 8–10 hours [7, open_label]. LH pulse number increased ~3-fold (1.6 → 5.0 pulses/8 h; p<0.01) and LH peak secretory mass ~6-fold (3.92 → 23.44 IU/L; p<0.05) versus vehicle at optimal doses. At the highest dose (1.0 nmol/kg/h), LH secretion peaked at 5 hours and then declined ~50%, demonstrating dose-dependent desensitization during extended high-dose infusion. This is the foundational proof-of-concept that pharmacological kisspeptin can restore LH pulsatility in HA, and simultaneously the human demonstration of continuous-infusion desensitization.

No kisspeptin form has reached Phase 3 evaluation for hypothalamic amenorrhea or IHH.

### 2.5 Functional Hypogonadism with Type 2 Diabetes: KP-10 Specifically

George et al. 2013 — a proof-of-concept study in 5 men with type 2 diabetes and mild biochemical hypogonadism (mean testosterone 8.5 nmol/L) compared with 7 age-matched healthy controls — administered IV bolus and infusion of **KP-10** specifically [17, open_label]. KP-10 increased LH pulse frequency (0.6 → 0.9 pulses/h) and testosterone (8.5 → 11.4 nmol/L) in hypogonadal diabetic men. This is one of only three KP-10-specific human studies and the only one conducted in a pathological clinical population. Very small n; proof-of-concept design only; Clinical Endocrinology (Wiley); does not constitute evidence for a treatment indication. The finding is conceptually interesting as it demonstrates KP-10 can engage the HPG axis in men with functional/central hypogonadism, not only healthy volunteers.

### 2.6 IVF Oocyte-Maturation Trigger: KP-54 Evidence, KP-10 Not Studied

**Critical note before reading this subsection:** Both IVF trials described below used KP-54, not KP-10. Kisspeptin's use as an IVF trigger is not established for KP-10; any extrapolation assumes receptor-level equivalence (supported by Jayasena 2015 pharmacology) but would require substantially different molar dosing to compensate for KP-10's 4-minute half-life versus KP-54's ~28-minute half-life in humans.

The mechanistic rationale for kisspeptin as an IVF trigger is compelling: conventional hCG triggers oocyte maturation by directly activating the ovarian LH receptor for >7 days, causing sustained ovarian stimulation that drives ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) in susceptible women. Kisspeptin acts upstream at the hypothalamus to generate an endogenous self-limiting LH surge (~45 IU/L peak at ~5 hours, returning to baseline within 12–14 hours), avoiding the sustained ovarian stimulation that precipitates OHSS.

Abbara et al. 2015 — a Phase 2, multi-dose, open-label, randomized but unblinded dose-finding trial at Hammersmith Hospital (n=60 women aged 18–34 at high OHSS risk; antral follicle count >23 or AMH ≥40 pmol/L) — tested single subcutaneous KP-54 injections at 3.2, 6.4, 9.6, or 12.8 nmol/kg as oocyte-maturation trigger [18, open_label]. Oocyte maturation rate was 95% of women; live birth rate per transfer was 45% overall (62% at 9.6 nmol/kg); critically, zero women developed moderate, severe, or critical OHSS (only mild early OHSS in 3 women, mild late in 1). This is the pivotal dose-finding study. Open-label, no direct hCG comparator arm; JCEM Tier 1.

Abbara et al. 2017 — a Phase 2 randomized, double-blind (patients, embryologists, and IVF clinicians blinded) trial in 62 women at high OHSS risk — compared single versus double (second injection at +10 hours) KP-54 injections [19, rct]. The primary endpoint — proportion achieving oocyte yield ≥60% — was significantly better with double dosing (45% → 71%; p=0.042); live birth rate per cycle improved from 19.4% (single) to 39% (double; 6/31 vs. 12/31) — a pre-specified secondary outcome. No increase in OHSS frequency. This is a genuine Phase 2 RCT in a tier-1 journal (Human Reproduction). Combined, these two IVF trials represent the most mature evidence base for therapeutic kisspeptin use, but it is KP-54 evidence at a single center (Hammersmith), not yet a multicenter Phase 3 program.

### 2.7 Kisspeptin as a Diagnostic Probe for Pubertal Integrity

Chan et al. 2020 — a prospective longitudinal cohort study at an academic medical center (n=16 children, 3 girls and 13 boys, with delayed or stalled puberty) — used kisspeptin (KP-54) as a single-administration functional probe: all 8 children with LH response ≥0.8 mIU/mL subsequently progressed through puberty; all 8 with LH response ≤0.4 mIU/mL reached age 18 without pubertal development (p=0.0002) [20, cohort]. Performance exceeded GnRH-stimulated LH, inhibin B, and genetic testing. This is proof-of-concept for kisspeptin as a functional probe of GnRH-neuron integrity, not a treatment. Small n=16; single academic center; longitudinal cohort; JCEM Tier 1.

## 3. Sexual and Limbic Effects: Evidence, Class Context, and the Single-Center Ceiling

### 3.1 Evidence-Tier Ceiling for Sexual/Limbic Claims

The human evidence linking kisspeptin to sexual and limbic effects has five structural constraints that cap the maximum defensible tier for sexual claims at *promising-but-single-center-unreplicated*:

1. **Small n.** Every human study has 29–37 participants per arm.
2. **Single center.** All five RCTs were conducted at Imperial College London by the Comninos/Dhillo group. No other research site has run a kisspeptin sexual-brain study.
3. **Proof-of-concept design.** Studies are short, single-session crossover fMRI trials and brief HSDD crossover RCTs — not powered for long-term clinical efficacy endpoints.
4. **No independent replication.** Zero independently led groups have confirmed the central finding: that peripherally administered kisspeptin enhances human sexual brain processing.
5. **Surrogate and mechanistic endpoints.** Primary outcomes are brain-activation fMRI maps, penile tumescence (objective), and psychometric questionnaires — not long-term clinical outcomes (sustained desire, relationship satisfaction, quality of life at ≥6 months).

The studies are methodologically sound RCTs published in tier-1 journals. The ceiling reflects the state of the field, not flaws in individual papers.

Additionally: these fMRI and HSDD studies used IV kisspeptin administered in hospital settings, not subcutaneous self-injection of grey-market KP-10. The isoform used is IV kisspeptin (consistent with the KP-54 preparation used as the standard clinical research formulation at Imperial); these are not KP-10 studies. The mechanistic rationale for expecting KP-10-specific replication of these findings exists (receptor equivalence), but the evidence was not obtained with KP-10.

### 3.2 The Imperial fMRI Program: Kisspeptin and the Limbic/Sexual Brain

**Healthy heterosexual men (Comninos et al. 2017).** The foundational human sexual-brain study enrolled 29 healthy heterosexual men (mean age 25.0 years) in a randomized, double-blinded, 2-way crossover, placebo-controlled design [21, rct]. Intravenous kisspeptin enhanced activation of the anterior and posterior cingulate cortex and left amygdala in response to sexual images, and of the thalamus and globus pallidus in response to couple-bonding images. Crucially, amygdala enhancement during bonding images correlated with improved positive mood, and enhanced limbic responses correlated with reduced sexual aversion on psychometric scales — the first evidence that peripheral kisspeptin reshapes limbic-sexual processing in humans. Published in the Journal of Clinical Investigation (Tier 1). PMID 28112678.

**Resting-state network connectivity (Comninos et al. 2018).** A follow-on analysis in the same n=29 cohort showed that kisspeptin modulated default-mode network (DMN) and salience network resting-state connectivity, strengthening amygdala–cingulate and hippocampus–cingulate functional coupling [22, rct]. The magnitude of DMN modulation was largest in men with lower baseline reward-drive traits (r=−0.489, p=0.008), and both network shifts predicted reduced sexual aversion. The finding mechanistically links a single peripheral kisspeptin exposure to sustained changes in limbic network architecture at rest. Published in JCI Insight (Tier 1). PMID 30333302.

**Olfactory and visual attraction in healthy men (Yang et al. 2020).** A further randomized, double-blind, 2-way crossover, placebo-controlled fMRI trial enrolled 33 healthy heterosexual men (mean age 24.5 years) [23, rct]. Kisspeptin significantly amplified amygdala and thalamic activity during exposure to an established feminine scent, and enhanced medial prefrontal cortex and superior frontal gyrus responses during facial-attractiveness evaluation. Effects were largest in men reporting lower baseline sexual quality of life — suggesting kisspeptin may preferentially engage the system in individuals with sub-optimal baseline desire rather than producing uniform arousal enhancement. Published in JCI Insight (2020). DOI 10.1172/jci.insight.133633.

### 3.3 HSDD Trials: Moving from Healthy Volunteers to Clinical Populations

**Men with HSDD (Mills et al. 2023).** A double-blind, 2-way crossover, placebo-controlled RCT enrolled 32 men (37 randomized) with physician-confirmed hypoactive sexual desire disorder [24, rct]. Kisspeptin significantly modulated brain activity in the sexual-processing network versus placebo on whole-brain fMRI analysis, enhancing activation in the left middle frontal gyrus and anterior cingulate cortex. Penile tumescence measured objectively was 56% greater with kisspeptin than placebo at the 8-minute mark of a sexual video task (p=.02). Participants also reported increased "happiness about sex" (p=.02). All authors were affiliated with Imperial College London and Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust. Published in JAMA Network Open (2023). PMID 36735255.

**Women with HSDD (Thurston et al. 2022).** A parallel double-masked, placebo-controlled, 2-way crossover RCT enrolled 32 premenopausal women (40 randomized; mean age 29.2 years) with HSDD [25, rct]. IV kisspeptin deactivated the left inferior and middle frontal gyri during erotic stimuli — regions hypothesized to be hyperactivated in HSDD — and activated the right postcentral and supramarginal gyrus. Enhanced hippocampal activity correlated with baseline sexual distress (r=0.469, p=.007); posterior cingulate activation correlated with reduced sexual aversion (r=0.476, p=.005). Self-reported sexiness increased (p=.04). No adverse effects reported among completers. All lead authors are affiliated with Imperial College London. Published in JAMA Network Open (2022). PMID 36287566. **Tolerability note:** 8 of 40 randomized women did not complete the crossover (~20% dropout), with the IV-administration burden in a repeated clinical-visit crossover design cited as the primary driver — a real-world barrier for a self-administration indication. **Endpoint limitation note (applicable to both HSDD trials):** neither trial used a validated composite sexual-function scale (FSFI, IIEF, or DSDSS) as its primary endpoint; primary endpoints were brain-activation fMRI maps, penile tumescence (men), and individual psychometric items. The absence of a pre-specified validated composite endpoint is a recognized evidence limitation for regulatory and clinical-significance purposes.

**Frontal deactivation: mechanistic interpretation is model-dependent.** The left inferior and middle frontal gyrus deactivation during erotic stimuli observed in the women's HSDD trial [25, rct] is discussed by the authors under a dual-control model (inhibition-of-inhibition: reduced sexual inhibition in women whose HSDD may be inhibition-dominant). An important alternative interpretation is that frontal deactivation during erotic stimuli is also observed in healthy volunteers without HSDD (a pattern documented in broader fMRI sexual-arousal literature), making the deactivation alone insufficient to establish a clinical HSDD-rescue mechanism. The clinical significance and directionality of the frontal deactivation pattern are model-dependent and cannot be resolved from a single-center proof-of-concept study.

**What the combined HSDD evidence shows — and does not show.** Across n=32 men and n=32 women with confirmed HSDD in two double-blind crossover RCTs, IV kisspeptin consistently modulated limbic and prefrontal sexual-processing regions versus placebo, reduced neural correlates of sexual aversion, produced objective pro-erectile improvement in men, and increased self-reported desire metrics. These are genuine pharmacodynamic effects in a clinical population, published in tier-1 journals. Neither trial was powered to establish long-term clinical efficacy, used a validated composite sexual-function score as its primary endpoint, or conducted outside an IV hospital setting; both author groups explicitly call for multicenter phase 2/3 trials with oral or subcutaneous formulations. Kisspeptin has no regulatory approval for HSDD or any sexual-health indication.

### 3.4 Mechanistic Position in the Sexual-Peptide Class

Three peptide classes have been investigated for sexual and desire effects in humans with distinct mechanisms and completely non-interchangeable evidence bases.

**Kisspeptin (KISS1R agonist; HPG axis + limbic command).** KP-10 acts upstream of the HPG axis at hypothalamic GnRH neurons, driving pulsatile GnRH release and, indirectly, downstream LH/FSH and gonadal steroid production. The sexual and limbic effects appear partly GnRH-independent in rodent models: central kisspeptin directly stimulates amygdala circuits and lordosis behavior through nitric oxide signaling in the ventromedial hypothalamus without requiring downstream hormonal mediation [26, mechanism_review]. The human fMRI data are consistent with a dual pathway — HPG priming plus direct limbic activation. This makes kisspeptin functionally the most upstream of the three approaches, operating at the hypothalamic command level of desire rather than at the peripheral arousal level.

**Bremelanotide / PT-141 (melanocortin MC4R/MC3R agonist; Vyleesi; FDA-approved for premenopausal women with HSDD).** Bremelanotide is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide that does not act on KISS1R or GnRH neurons. MC4R and MC3R activation in the hypothalamic paraventricular nucleus and medial preoptic area drives dopaminergic arousal through the nucleus accumbens/ventral tegmental area and enhances oxytocin secretion — a distinct central pathway from kisspeptin's GnRH-limbic axis. Its pivotal evidence base comprises two 24-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials in 1,247 premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD, which supported FDA approval in June 2019 as Vyleesi (1.75 mg SC self-injection on demand) [27, regulatory]. Bremelanotide is the **only** peptide in this compound class with FDA approval for HSDD. Common adverse effects include nausea (40%), flushing (20%), and injection-site reactions (13%). KP-10's approval status, trial scale, and administration route are all substantially less mature.

**Melanotan-II (MT-II; non-selective melanocortin agonist; not approved anywhere).** MT-II is a non-selective agonist at MC1R, MC3R, and MC4R, producing sexual arousal through the same MC4R pathway as bremelanotide but with substantially greater off-target pigmentation (MC1R) and cardiovascular risk. It is not approved in any jurisdiction; it circulates as an unregulated research chemical. The evidentiary quality is substantially below both kisspeptin (peer-reviewed fMRI RCTs) and bremelanotide (FDA-reviewed Phase 3 program). Attribution discipline: MT-II's sexual effects come from small, often uncontrolled human reports and animal data — none of which is attributable to kisspeptin or bremelanotide.

**Mechanistic summary.** KP-10 operates at the hypothalamic command level (HPG axis plus direct limbic activation), acting upstream of desire itself. Melanocortins operate at a mid-level arousal node (dopaminergic/oxytocinergic). PDE5 inhibitors operate at the peripheral vascular level. The three systems are complementary rather than redundant, and their evidence bases must not be merged.

### 3.5 Independent-Replication Assessment

**The single-center concentration is real and material.** Every published human fMRI study linking kisspeptin to sexual or limbic brain processing, and both HSDD trials, originated from a single research group: Comninos and Dhillo at the Section of Endocrinology and Investigative Medicine, Imperial College London [21, 22, 23, 24, 25, rct]. The 2017 JCI, 2018 JCI Insight, 2020 JCI Insight, 2022 JAMA Network Open, and 2023 JAMA Network Open papers share lead authors with institutional affiliations limited to Imperial College London, Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust, Invicro/Hammersmith (an imaging CRO on the same campus), and Statsconsultancy Ltd. No independently led group has replicated the central finding that peripherally administered kisspeptin enhances human sexual brain processing.

**What independent groups have contributed.** The Seminara/Crowley group at Harvard/MGH established the foundational KISS1R IHH genetics (2003) [3, cohort] and have published on kisspeptin's role in GnRH pulse generation. The Edinburgh/MRC group (Anderson, George) contributed the KP-10-specific bolus/infusion pharmacology studies [9, open_label]. The Ohtaki group at Takeda contributed the in vitro receptor characterization [2, in_vitro]. None of these independent groups have run a human sexual-brain fMRI or HSDD efficacy study.

**Verdict.** The sexual/limbic evidence for kisspeptin in humans is promising but has a single-center provenance that constitutes a meaningful limitation. The studies are not fraudulent or p-hacked — they are methodologically sound RCTs in tier-1 journals — but they cannot be considered independently replicated. This is a standard early-phase trajectory for an emerging neuropeptide. Future credibility requires multicenter phase 2/3 trials and non-Imperial confirmation of the limbic fMRI signature.

## 4. Safety, Pharmacokinetics, Regulatory Status, and Monitoring

### 4.1 Pharmacokinetic Summary

KP-10 has an exceptionally short plasma half-life of approximately 3.8–4.1 minutes following IV bolus administration, measured in healthy men and women [28, open_label]. This rapid clearance is intrinsic to the unprotected decapeptide structure. In contrast, KP-54 has a human half-life of approximately 28 minutes (27.6 min, Dhillo 2005 [11, rct]; ~32 min in mice [10, animal]); the synthetic analog MVT-602 has a half-life of approximately 108 minutes IV. Biologically meaningful KP-10 plasma levels persist for only 20–30 minutes after a bolus, requiring repeated pulsatile administration or continuous infusion for sustained HPG-axis engagement — and as documented in Section 1.6, continuous infusion at high doses triggers desensitization rather than sustained stimulation.

KP-10 appears to act primarily at GnRH nerve terminals in the median eminence (outside the blood-brain barrier), while KP-54 additionally activates GnRH neuron perikarya behind the BBB, explaining why equimolar subcutaneous doses of KP-54 produce dramatically different in vivo LH profiles than KP-10 despite near-identical in vitro binding [10, animal]. This BBB-access differential is relevant to any central/limbic effect claim: the fMRI and HSDD trials used IV kisspeptin, not subcutaneous KP-10, and the route, isoform, and systemic exposure context all differ from typical grey-market self-injection practice.

### 4.2 Dosing in Published Human Trials

All dosing figures derive from published academic trials. Grey-market dose numbers (circulating SC injection amounts such as "100–200 µg") are not grounded in any published clinical trial and are not cited here.

**IV bolus in healthy men (George et al. 2011 [9, open_label]):** Dose-ranging 0.01–3.0 µg/kg. Peak LH stimulation at 1 µg/kg; blunted response at 3 µg/kg (dose-dependent receptor saturation signal). The 1 µg/kg dose is the best-characterized effective bolus dose for KP-10 in healthy adult men.

**IV bolus and SC bolus (Jayasena et al. 2011 [28, open_label]):** IV bolus 0.3–10 nmol/kg in men and women; SC bolus 2–32 nmol/kg in women. IV bolus reliably raised LH and FSH in men at all tested doses and in women during the preovulatory (but not early follicular) phase. SC bolus produced qualitatively similar but attenuated peak LH due to slower absorption.

**Continuous IV infusion in healthy men (George et al. 2011 [9, open_label]):** 1.5 µg/kg·h raised LH pulse frequency (0.7 → 1.0 pulses/h; p=0.01); 4 µg/kg·h for 22.5 h raised testosterone (16.6 → 24.0 nmol/L; p<0.001) without desensitization over that duration in healthy men.

**Continuous IV infusion in women with HA (Jayasena et al. 2014, KP-54 [7, open_label]):** 0.01–1.0 nmol/kg/h for 8–10 hours; desensitization at 1.0 nmol/kg/h after ~5 hours.

**Subcutaneous chronic (Jayasena et al. 2009, KP-54 [15, open_label]):** 6.4 nmol/kg twice daily for 14 days; complete tachyphylaxis by Day 14. Twice-weekly for 8 weeks [16, open_label]: partial desensitization, axis maintained.

**IVF trigger (Abbara et al. 2015, KP-54 [18, open_label]):** Single subcutaneous 9.6 nmol/kg produced the best efficacy (62% live birth rate per transfer); range tested was 3.2–12.8 nmol/kg.

**Subcutaneous infusion in healthy women (Narayanaswamy et al. 2016, KP-54 [29, open_label]):** 0.1–1.0 nmol/kg/h for 8 h; significant LH and FSH rise without tachyphylaxis; response correlated with baseline estradiol.

### 4.3 Safety Profile in Academic Trials

Across published human trials in healthy volunteers and clinical populations:

**No serious adverse events** have been attributed to KP-10 or KP-54 at study doses. Blood pressure, heart rate, and standard laboratory parameters remained stable across all reporting studies [9, 28, open_label].

**IVF safety (KP-54; n=60; Abbara et al. 2015 [18, open_label]):** Only 3/60 women (5%) developed mild early OHSS; none developed moderate, severe, or critical OHSS. No participant required medical intervention. The self-limiting endogenous LH surge mechanism (peak ~45 IU/L at ~5 h, returning to baseline within 12–14 h) is the proposed mechanism for avoiding sustained ovarian stimulation.

**HSDD trials (n=32 men + 32 women [24, 25, rct]):** No adverse effects reported in either crossover RCT.

**Immunogenicity:** No immunogenic reactions were reported in academic trials. The FDA PCAC briefing (October 2024) noted theoretical immunogenicity risk via parenteral routes — peptide structure, aggregation potential, and impurity profile in compounded preparations — as a manufacturing-quality concern rather than an effect observed in academic trials [33, regulatory].

**Honest limits.** Academic trials are small (typically n=4–62), conducted in controlled research settings, of short duration (hours to weeks), and use pharmaceutical-grade research-formulation peptide under clinical monitoring. There are no long-term or chronic-dosing safety data in humans. Endocrine effects beyond the study window are not characterized. Extrapolation to self-administration of uncharacterized grey-market preparations is not supported by trial evidence.

**Contraindications (not formally established; no approved product):** Hormone-sensitive conditions — hormone-receptor-positive malignancies, sex-steroid-dependent disorders, precocious puberty risk — are theoretical cautions given KP-10's capacity to raise LH, FSH, and gonadal steroids. Pregnancy warrants caution by mechanism (risk of premature LH surge, luteal disruption). Paradoxical HPG suppression with naive high-frequency dosing is the most operationally relevant risk for grey-market use.

### 4.4 Regulatory Status

**FDA:** Kisspeptin-10 is not FDA-approved for any indication. No approved drug product containing any kisspeptin isoform exists in the US, EU, or Japan as of mid-2026.

**503A/503B compounding:** The FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) evaluated kisspeptin-10 for inclusion on the 503A Bulk Drug Substances List at its October 29, 2024 meeting and recommended **against inclusion**, citing: insufficient physicochemical characterization in public literature; significant parenteral immunogenicity risk; inadequate effectiveness evidence for secondary hypogonadism; and existence of FDA-approved alternatives [33, regulatory]. As of mid-2026, kisspeptin-10 remains in Category 2 (bulk drug substances that may present significant safety risks, not eligible for 503A compounding). Compounding pharmacies may not lawfully prepare kisspeptin-10 under current US rules.

**WADA status:** Kisspeptin and its agonist analogues were **first explicitly named** in the **2024 WADA Prohibited List update** (effective January 1, 2024) under **S2.2.1 — Testosterone-Stimulating Peptides in Males**, alongside chorionic gonadotrophin, luteinizing hormone, and GnRH and its agonist analogues (buserelin, deslorelin, goserelin, histrelin, leuprorelin, nafarelin, triptorelin), and this prohibition is **carried into the current 2026 Prohibited List** [32, regulatory]. The prohibition is in-competition and **out-of-competition** (S2 is an "at all times" category), and applies specifically to males. (The S2.2.1 sub-section number is from secondary sources — the primary WADA PDF was access-blocked at research time; corroborated via USADA's 2024-list key-changes document and secondary regulatory summaries reproducing the S2.2.1 language verbatim; disclosed.) The mechanism is direct: KP-10 activates KISS1R → GnRH release → pituitary LH/FSH secretion → testicular testosterone synthesis, constituting a testosterone-pathway stimulator at the hypothalamic level. Analytical LC-HRMS detection methods for kisspeptin and analogues in serum and urine have been developed for doping control, confirming assay feasibility for enforcement [30, mechanism_review].

### 4.5 MVT-602 Pipeline and Commercialization

No approved pharmaceutical product containing KP-10 or any kisspeptin isoform exists as of mid-2026. The only KISS1R agonist in active clinical development is **MVT-602** (also known as TAK-448; Myovant Sciences/Sumitomo Pharma America), a synthetic oligopeptide KISS1R agonist with a half-life of approximately 108 minutes IV — dramatically longer than native KP-10 (~4 min) or KP-54 (~28 min in humans [27.6 min, Dhillo 2005]) — and a markedly more prolonged LH surge profile (peak LH AUC 169 vs. 38.5 IU·h/L for KP-54) [31, rct].

Phase 2a trials in 75 healthy premenopausal women with and without ovarian stimulation were completed and published in Fertility and Sterility (January 2024) [31, rct]. Authors include Myovant Sciences employees (Ufer, Wu, Lee, Arjona Ferreira, Migoya) alongside Dhillo and Abbara from Imperial College London, who disclose consulting fee relationships with Myovant Sciences — a real but circumscribed COI (primarily in the IVF/analog development work where Myovant funded the MVT-602 trials, not in the foundational HPG stimulation or HSDD mechanistic studies; disclosures are present and publicly accessible). No Phase 3 trial has been registered or announced. Myovant was acquired by Sumitomo Pharma America (December 2023); continuation of the MVT-602 program has not been publicly confirmed as of mid-2026.

MVT-602 is a distinct compound from KP-10; clinical trial results for MVT-602 are not attributable to KP-10. Kisspeptin-10 itself has no identified development-stage sponsor.

### 4.6 Monitoring Parameters (Research Context Framework)

For any supervised research or clinical protocol context:

| Parameter | Rationale | Suggested timing |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Serum LH, FSH | Direct pharmacodynamic targets; confirm axis response or detect desensitization signal | Baseline + 30–60 min post-dose; at regular intervals during any multi-day protocol |
| Total and free testosterone (males) | Downstream endpoint; elevation confirms HPG engagement; suppression signals tachyphylaxis | Baseline; 24–48 h post-dose for bolus; end of infusion period |
| Estradiol (females) | Confirms ovarian response; required for IVF-trigger or HA-restoration context | Baseline; pre-trigger + 12–36 h post |
| Vitals (BP, HR) | Monitored in all academic trials; stable to date | Each administration visit |
| Injection-site assessment | Theoretical aggregation/immunogenicity concern at SC routes | Each SC administration |

**Stopping criteria framework:** Unexpected LH/FSH suppression below baseline on repeat dosing (tachyphylaxis signal requiring dose interval reassessment); any systemic allergic or anaphylactoid response; unanticipated HPG stimulation in hormone-sensitive conditions.

## 5. Concentration, COI, and Sourcing Realism

### 5.1 Field Concentration Profile

The foundational kisspeptin discovery came from two fully independent groups publishing simultaneously in 2001–2003: Ohtaki and colleagues at Takeda Chemical Industries (Tsukuba, Japan) [2, in_vitro] and Seminara, Crowley, and colleagues at Massachusetts General Hospital/Harvard [3, cohort] — neither group connected to each other or to Imperial College London.

The human-translational literature (administering kisspeptin peptides to human subjects and measuring hormonal or CNS endpoints, 2005–2024) maps onto a small set of verified groups conducting original human-subject dosing studies:

1. **Imperial College London** (Dhillo, Jayasena, Abbara, Comninos, Mills, Thurston) — HPG stimulation, IVF oocyte maturation, HSDD/sexual-brain fMRI; dominant group by publication volume.
2. **University of Edinburgh / MRC Centre for Reproductive Health** (Anderson, Skorupskaite, George) — KP-10 bolus-and-infusion dose-response in men and hypogonadism.
3. **Mayo Clinic** (Veldhuis) — pulse-waveform analysis collaborations with Edinburgh [9, open_label].
4. **Harvard/MGH reproductive endocrine unit** (Seminara, Crowley) — post-discovery administration studies in IHH patients.

For the **broad field** (all four groups), Imperial's estimated share of all unique human-dosing trial entries published between 2005 and 2024 is approximately 45–55%. This clears the 70% single-group concentration flag for the field as a whole. Four verified independent human-dosing groups is a genuine multi-group literature.

For the **human sexual-brain and HSDD sub-literature specifically**, the concentration is qualitatively different: all five human fMRI and HSDD trials originate from Imperial [21, 22, 23, 24, 25, rct]. Effective concentration is approximately 100% for the sub-literature that underpins consumer libido claims. The two bodies of literature have meaningfully different concentration profiles, and any presentation of HSDD evidence must state this distinction.

### 5.2 Sourcing Realism for Grey-Market KP-10

Consumer-marketed "kisspeptin-10" sold as a research peptide carries several verification problems:

**Identity and purity are unverifiable.** Research-chemical suppliers offer KP-10 as lyophilized powder with no independent quality oversight. Without verified mass spectrometry or HPLC documentation from a regulated facility, purity and sequence fidelity cannot be confirmed by the end user. The C-terminal amidation (–NH₂) required for receptor binding is frequently absent in low-quality syntheses, yielding a pharmacologically inactive preparation.

**KP-10 vs. KP-54 confusion.** Consumer sources routinely conflate KP-10 and KP-54, which have meaningfully different circulating half-lives (~4 min vs. ~28 min), blood-brain barrier access profiles, and in vivo LH kinetics [10, animal]. The fMRI and HSDD studies administered IV kisspeptin (formulated as KP-54 in the Imperial program); a consumer injecting KP-10 and expecting identical CNS/libido effects is applying evidence from a different molecule delivered by a different route.

**Desensitization risk of naïve dosing.** Continuous or frequent high-dose KISS1R stimulation causes β-arrestin-mediated receptor internalization and HPG suppression rather than stimulation. Daily or frequent KP-10 use without clinical titration risks near-complete tachyphylaxis within approximately 14 days — the opposite of the intended testosterone-boosting effect, documented in human subjects [15, open_label].

**No validated subcutaneous formulation for humans.** All human RCTs with positive outcomes used IV infusion under clinical supervision. Subcutaneous bioavailability data in humans are sparse (the Jayasena 2011 SC bolus study is the main reference [28, open_label]), and no controlled human trial has validated a SC self-injection dosing protocol for any kisspeptin isoform in the HSDD or testosterone-optimization indication.

**The 503A denial closes the only licit US pharmacy pathway.** Any grey-market KP-10 product reaching US consumers is operating entirely outside regulatory oversight, without the manufacturing quality controls that are the baseline assumption of the academic trial evidence.
