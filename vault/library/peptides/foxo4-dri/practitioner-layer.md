# FOXO4-DRI — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how FOXO4-DRI is actually accessed and dosed in practice* — the dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions that circulate in grey-market dosing aggregators and biohacker communities, plus the regulatory and compounding status that governs whether any legitimate clinical prescribing pathway exists. It is **not** an efficacy document. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (Tier 3), and `vendor_label` / `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4) sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) purity/identity/reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** As of mid-2026, FOXO4-DRI is **not an FDA-approved drug for any indication**. It carries **no IND (Investigational New Drug) application on the public record**, no completed human clinical trial at any phase, and no USP/NF monograph. FOXO4-DRI does **not appear on the FDA 503A bulk drug substances list — neither Category 1 nor Category 2**. It has never been nominated to the 503A Category 2 interim list and is not scheduled for any PCAC evaluation. There is accordingly **no legitimate 503A compounding pathway** for FOXO4-DRI: a 503A pharmacy cannot lawfully compound it because it holds no Category 1 status, no approved drug status, and no USP monograph. Any 503A pharmacy dispensing FOXO4-DRI to patients would be doing so outside any settled regulatory authorization. The "prescribing practice" described in this document is therefore a real but entirely unregulated and legally unauthorized grey-market/research-chemical practice — not an approved standard of care and not a lawfully compounded therapy. [1, regulatory]
>
> **No legitimate prescribing pathway exists.** Unlike peptides such as semaglutide or sermorelin — which have FDA-approved formulations and hence a defined prescribing pathway — FOXO4-DRI has no such pathway. No pharmaceutical company has filed an IND for FOXO4-DRI for any therapeutic indication in any jurisdiction with a public registry. No clinical-grade FOXO4-DRI supply chain exists. Cleara Biotech's next-generation analogs (CL04177, CL04183) are in IND-enabling preclinical development as of mid-2026, but no human trial is registered anywhere and no Phase I has begun [2, regulatory]. Human exposure to FOXO4-DRI is confined entirely to grey-market self-administration using research-chemical product sourced from laboratory supply vendors. The practitioner-practice layer this document describes is that grey-market reality — not a clinical standard of care.
>
> **CRITICAL identity problem, read before any other section.** FOXO4-DRI is a ~48-amino-acid D-retro-inverso peptidomimetic. The D-chirality is essential to both its proteolytic stability and its senolytic mechanism — an L-amino acid substitution at any position yields a molecule with the same molecular weight but profoundly different pharmacology. Standard reversed-phase HPLC (the basis of consumer-grade certificates of analysis) **cannot verify D-amino acid chirality**; it confirms purity by size and hydrophobicity, not stereochemical identity. Only chiral-specific HPLC or two-dimensional NMR can confirm D-configuration, and neither is offered by grey-market suppliers. Purchased material of uncertain chirality is, in practice, a compound of uncertain identity and uncertain activity. This is not a theoretical concern — it is the central sourcing problem for this peptide and the reason any self-experimentation dose carries a layer of risk that is absent for simpler L-amino acid peptides [3, vendor_label].

**Why no legitimate 503A compounding pathway exists.** FOXO4-DRI is structurally ineligible for 503A compounding on multiple independent grounds: (a) it is not on the FDA's Category 1 bulk substance list (nominated and under evaluation), (b) it is not on the Category 2 list (approved for compounding as part of a defined drug product), (c) it has no USP/NF monograph, (d) it has no approved drug product from which a compounder could make a "minor deviation" under any interpretation, and (e) it has never been nominated to the PCAC for evaluation. A compounder would need a Category 1 or Category 2 designation — or an active approved drug product — to lawfully compound it for a specific patient under 503A. None of those bases exist. [1, regulatory]

**Structural complexity as an additional compounding barrier.** Beyond regulatory status, FOXO4-DRI presents a practical synthesis challenge that places it in a different category from shorter peptides like BPC-157 (15 aa, L-amino acid) or TB-500 (43 aa, L-amino acid). As a ~48-amino-acid all-D-amino acid retro-inverted peptide, FOXO4-DRI requires approximately 46–48 sequential coupling steps in solid-phase peptide synthesis, each with D-amino acid building blocks that are substantially more expensive than their L-enantiomers. Any coupling failure produces truncated or deletion peptides with near-identical molecular weight. Verification of completed D-chirality is analytically demanding (chiral HPLC or 2D NMR). No 503A compounding pharmacy has the analytical infrastructure to verify this peptide's identity beyond standard HPLC purity. Even if the regulatory barriers were resolved, the synthesis and verification demands would make legitimate compounding of correctly characterized FOXO4-DRI materially harder than for any currently compounded peptide [3, vendor_label].

## Safety as the dominant practitioner concern

The dominant practitioner-safety consideration for FOXO4-DRI is not a dosing titration concern — it is a mechanism-to-toxicity concern that cannot be resolved by adjusting a dose.

**On-target p53 activation in non-senescent tissues.** FOXO4-DRI directly engages p53 — the master regulator of apoptosis, cell-cycle arrest, DNA damage response, wound healing, and immune function — and activates the p53 mitochondrial apoptosis pathway. The compound's selectivity rests entirely on FOXO4 being differentially elevated in senescent cells, such that only senescent cells have a nuclear FOXO4–p53 complex for FOXO4-DRI to disrupt. If that expression filter is imperfect in any tissue — if any non-senescent cell has sufficient FOXO4 expression for FOXO4-DRI to displace it from p53 — the compound delivers p53-mediated apoptosis to a non-senescent cell. The published scientific literature explicitly identifies this as the central clinical-translation risk: "FOXO4-DRI targets the tumor suppressor p53, a crucial protein, which may lead to various side effects in clinical trials" [8, mechanism_review]. This is not a theoretical footnote from external critics — it is the canonical framing by researchers within the field itself.

**The cardiotoxicity concern.** FOXO4 protein is expressed in normal human heart and testis tissue in addition to senescent cells. The Zhang 2020 Leydig-cell study (the only published independent replication in aged male mice) explicitly flagged that "special attention must be paid to muscle damage, especially cardiotoxicity," acknowledging an inability to fully evaluate this risk in their mouse system [9, animal]. No published study has assessed cardiac tissue histopathology, troponin I/T, CK-MB, or other cardiac biomarkers at time points appropriate for detecting delayed myocardial injury following FOXO4-DRI. The concern is identified in the literature and then left unaddressed — an unresolved gap in an organ system that does not regenerate well.

**The Born/Adnot 2023 adverse finding.** Born, Lipskaia, Adnot, and colleagues at INSERM U955 / Hôpital Henri Mondor published data in *Circulation* (PMID 36515093) demonstrating that eliminating senescent pulmonary endothelial cells — using several senolytic strategies including FOXO4-DRI — **paradoxically worsened pulmonary hemodynamics** in mouse models of pulmonary hypertension [10, animal]. Senescent cells in the pulmonary vascular niche were exerting a protective or compensatory function. This is the only primary FOXO4-DRI study to report pathological worsening of disease, published in a high-impact cardiovascular journal, and it concretely demonstrates that the framing "senescent cells are harmful, remove them" does not hold across all tissue contexts. For individuals with pulmonary hypertension or significant cardiovascular disease, this finding represents a specific disease-context risk beyond the general on-target concern.

**Senescence-has-protective-roles caveat.** Cellular senescence is not uniformly harmful. Senescent cells suppress tumorigenesis by permanently arresting damaged cells, contribute to wound healing by recruiting immune cells, participate in embryonic tissue remodeling, and support liver regeneration [11, mechanism_review]. Indiscriminate systemic senolysis risks removing beneficial senescent-cell populations alongside pathological ones. This is not a speculative concern — the Born/Adnot 2023 *Circulation* data cited above demonstrate it empirically for one specific tissue context. Any grey-market self-experiment with FOXO4-DRI is a systemic senolytic intervention with no information about which senescent-cell populations exist or what functions they are performing in that individual at that time.

**Practical implication for any clinical or grey-market use.** For the practitioner-layer purposes:

1. Active or recent malignancy is the clearest absolute contraindication — FOXO4-DRI activates p53-centric apoptosis; in cells with upregulated p53 due to oncogenic stress, off-target killing is plausible
2. Significant cardiovascular disease or pulmonary hypertension is a high-caution contraindication in light of the Born/Adnot 2023 finding
3. Active infection, recent surgery, or wound healing in progress are high-caution situations because p53-mediated apoptosis of immune effector cells and proliferating repair cells could impair recovery
4. Pregnancy is an absolute contraindication — FOXO4 is expressed in placenta; embryotoxic risk is entirely uncharacterized
5. Athletes subject to the World Anti-Doping Code are prohibited in- and out-of-competition under WADA S0 (Non-Approved Substances) — FOXO4-DRI holds no regulatory approval in any jurisdiction [12, regulatory]

These five items are not clinical guidelines — no clinical guidelines exist. They are a mechanism-derived risk floor. A practitioner who ignored them would be without any published safety rationale for doing so.

## Grey-market dose/cycle/route conventions

There is **no human dose-finding trial for FOXO4-DRI** and **no validated therapeutic dose in any species for subcutaneous administration**. All published animal efficacy data used intraperitoneal (IP) or intravenous (IV) injection at 5 mg/kg in mice. Subcutaneous administration has not been evaluated in any published study. Every figure below is a *convention* extrapolated by grey-market biohacker communities from animal protocols with no validated human equivalent, no PK anchor, and no route-bioavailability data for the SC route. These figures carry no evidentiary weight; they are documented because they exist.

**Convention 1 — Aggregator titration protocol (250–500 mcg SC daily, 16-week cycle):**

The main grey-market convention circulating via dosing-aggregator sites as of mid-2026:

| Weeks | Daily dose (SC) | Rationale (per source) |
|-------|-----------------|------------------------|
| 1–4 | 250 mcg SC | "Low-dose induction to assess tolerance" |
| 5–8 | 375 mcg SC | "Mid-cycle escalation" |
| 9–12 | 500 mcg SC | "Full protocol dose" |
| 13–16 | 500 mcg SC | "Maintenance" |

- **Frequency:** Daily subcutaneous
- **Route:** Subcutaneous, abdomen or thigh
- **Reconstitution reference:** 10 mg vial + 3.0 mL bacteriostatic water → 3.33 mg/mL concentration; 7.5 U on U-100 syringe = 250 mcg; 11.25 U = 375 mcg; 15 U = 500 mcg
- **Explicit source disclaimer:** "Community SubQ use was never tested for bioavailability, safety, or effect in humans."
- **Mouse protocol reference cited by source:** 5 mg/kg IP, three doses over five days

Source: **Peptide Dosing Protocols** (peptidedosingprotocols.com), FOXO4-DRI Protocol Guide, retrieved June 2026 [7, anecdote_aggregate]. Author: Garret Grant, Founder & Lead Researcher. Explicit "no human clinical trial exists." Used here for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution convention ONLY; **grounds no efficacy claim.**

**Critical dosing-math note (not in the aggregator source):** The mouse efficacy dose was 5 mg/kg IP over three doses in five days. A simple allometric conversion to a 70 kg human — with no adjustment for route bioavailability — produces approximately 350 mg per dose, or approximately 1,050 mg per three-dose cycle. The grey-market convention of 250–500 mcg/day is approximately 700–1,400-fold below a naive allometric equivalent of the published mouse dose. Whether this reflects a biologically meaningful dose in humans — or a pharmacologically negligible one — is unknown because no human PK data exist. There is no validated human dose. The grey-market figures are not validated downward adjustments; they are community-generated numbers with no mechanistic or pharmacokinetic basis.

- **Frequency:** Every other day, three total doses per cycle
- **Cycle interval:** Repeat cycle every 3–6 months

Source: [7, anecdote_aggregate] for reconstitution targets; [3, vendor_label] for storage conditions.

- **Reconstitution technique:** Wipe stopper with alcohol swab; inject bacteriostatic water slowly down the vial wall; swirl gently (do NOT shake — agitation stresses peptide bonds); inspect for clarity; label with preparation date
- **Storage:** Lyophilized powder at −20°C; reconstituted at 2–8°C, use within 5 days (conservative window preferred; some sources state up to 14 days); protect from light
- **Route:** Subcutaneous; abdomen, thigh, or upper arm; rotate injection sites
- **Syringe:** Standard U-100 insulin syringe (27–31G, 0.5 mL or 1.0 mL)
- **Net peptide content caveat:** The NovoPro Labs CoA cited in Section 1 reports 69.92% net peptide content at 98.03% HPLC purity [6, vendor_label]. If this ratio is representative of the grey-market supply (which cannot be confirmed), a nominally "10 mg" vial contains approximately 7 mg of actual peptide — not 10 mg. Dose calculations based on label weight may overstate effective peptide delivered by ~30%. This caveat applies before the chirality problem.

**The chirality caveat for reconstituted product.** Even correctly reconstituted FOXO4-DRI from a grey-market supplier carries a layer of identity uncertainty that does not apply to simpler peptides. At the moment of reconstitution, the buyer cannot know whether the lyophilized powder is correctly D-inverted, partially L-substituted, or a mixture. Standard HPLC purity (whether stated at 98% or 99%) does not resolve this. The compound may or may not exhibit senolytic activity at any dose.

## Monitoring framework (experimental / off-label context only)

No published clinical guideline, society recommendation, or validated monitoring protocol exists for FOXO4-DRI in humans. If a clinician were tracking a patient using FOXO4-DRI in an explicitly experimental capacity under full informed consent, the following is a rational scaffold derived from mechanistic targets (p53 apoptotic axis, cardiac FOXO4 expression, hepatic/renal clearance of a large peptide) and the specific concerns flagged in the literature:

**Baseline (before starting):**

| Domain | Tests | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Cardiac | Troponin I or T, CK-MB, BNP, ECG | FOXO4 expressed in heart; p53 apoptotic activation in cardiomyocytes is the cardiotoxicity concern [9, animal]; baseline required before any inference |
| Malignancy clearance | Clinical history; age- and sex-appropriate cancer screening current; oncology clearance if ANY active or recent cancer | On-target p53 manipulation in oncology context is unpredictable; malignancy is the primary absolute contraindication |
| Hepatic | ALT, AST, GGT, bilirubin | No hepatic safety data from any human exposure; large peptide; basic safety panel |
| Renal | BUN, creatinine, eGFR | Renal clearance of a 5.4 kDa D-peptide is uncharacterized; Baar 2017 mouse data showed no BUN/creatinine signal at 5 mg/kg IP but this cannot be extrapolated to human subcutaneous at unknown bioavailability |
| Pulmonary | History of pulmonary hypertension; if present, cardiopulmonary evaluation | Born/Adnot 2023 *Circulation* adverse finding: senolytic clearance worsened pulmonary hemodynamics [10, animal]; specific absolute contraindication for PH |
| Complete blood count | CBC with differential | Platelet count; immune baseline |
| Immunogenicity | Baseline anti-drug antibody assay (if available) | D-amino acid scaffolds are novel antigens to the human immune system; immunogenicity is entirely uncharacterized [8, mechanism_review] |

**Follow-up (48–72 hours post-first dose):**

- Troponin I/T — earliest assessment for acute cardiomyocyte stress
- General symptom review (fatigue, myalgias, palpitations, injection-site reaction)

**Follow-up (7 days post-dose):**

- Troponin I/T (repeat; delayed myocardial injury window)
- ALT, AST
- CBC with differential
- Symptom diary review

**Follow-up (30 days post-dose):**

- Full hepatic/renal panel
- Anti-drug antibody assay
- CBC
- Cancer surveillance update (new symptoms, any palpable findings)

**Stopping rules (mechanism-derived, not validated):**

- Any troponin elevation above the institution's upper limit of normal at any time point → stop immediately; cardiology evaluation
- ALT or AST > 3× upper limit of normal at any on-cycle measurement
- Any new cancer diagnosis or suspicious finding during use → stop; oncology evaluation before any re-challenge
- Symptomatic palpitations, chest pain, or dyspnea at any time during or after dosing
- Persistent injection-site nodule, induration, or abscess (>2 weeks)
- Any sign of systemic immunogenic reaction (rash, urticaria, angioedema, anaphylaxis)

**Honest limit:** This framework is a rational mechanistic extrapolation. No human study has applied it. It is not a clinical protocol and is not a substitute for individualized medical evaluation. Given the ABSOLUTE absence of human PK, human safety, or human efficacy data for FOXO4-DRI, any application of this framework should be accompanied by a formal adverse-event reporting plan and ethics-board-level informed-consent documentation.
