# Section E — Human Clinical Evidence (GHK-Cu)

> **Scope note.** This section covers human studies of **topical GHK-Cu** (facial skin / photoaging, periorbital, hair, wound healing) plus any registered trials. The single most important honest finding is stated up front and repeated in the gaps section: **the entire human evidence base for GHK-Cu is TOPICAL (cosmetic and wound-healing). There is essentially NO human RCT — none — for INJECTABLE or systemic GHK-Cu.** The strongest cosmetic claims rest on **industry-sponsored conference posters and a book chapter that were never published as peer-reviewed journal articles**, mostly from small-n studies. Two of the best-documented *controlled* human studies (Mulder 1994 diabetic ulcers; Miller 2006 post-laser) are wound-healing, not anti-aging — and Miller 2006 was **negative** on its primary endpoint. Read the source tally and gaps before quoting any number.

## Claims

**Claim 1 — A 12-week topical GHK-Cu facial cream study in 71 photoaged women reported improved skin density/thickness, reduced laxity, improved clarity, and reduced fine lines/wrinkle depth.** [1, anecdote_aggregate→open_label]
- Design: 12-week topical application, facial skin; reported as before/after improvement across multiple instrumented and graded parameters. Whether it was placebo/vehicle-controlled or randomized is **not established** from any retrievable primary document — only secondary summaries exist.
- n: 71 women, "mild to advanced" photoaging.
- Blinding: not verifiable (no primary methods document retrievable).
- Control: not verifiable.
- Sponsor/COI: **Industry.** Authors Leyden JJ, Stephens TJ, **Finkey MB, Appa Y** (Neutrogena/Johnson & Johnson cosmetic-science affiliations), Barkovic S. Presented as an **American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) 60th Annual Meeting poster (Feb 2002, New Orleans)** — **conference abstract, not a peer-reviewed journal article, no PMID/DOI**.
- Result: positive for all reported anti-aging parameters; magnitude figures circulating online (e.g., "67% wrinkle-volume reduction," "20–30% firmness") trace to vendor/blog text and are **not** confirmed by any retrievable primary source — do not cite those numbers as established.
- **Tier-3 weight.** Treat as suggestive cosmetic signal, not high-quality controlled evidence. Cited authoritatively in the peer-reviewed Pickart & Margolina 2018 review [4] as reference [14], which is the verifiable trail to it.

**Claim 2 — A 12-week topical GHK-Cu eye cream study in 41 women with periorbital photodamage performed better than placebo and a vitamin-K cream for lines/wrinkles, overall appearance, and skin density/thickness.** [2, anecdote_aggregate→open_label]
- Design: 12-week periorbital application; comparator arms reported as **placebo + vitamin-K cream** (so a *controlled* comparison was claimed).
- n: 41 women.
- Blinding: not verifiable from a primary document.
- Control: placebo and vitamin-K cream (per the Pickart 2018 description).
- Sponsor/COI: **Industry.** Leyden JJ, Stephens TJ, Finkey MB, Barkovic S — companion AAD-poster-class work (same cosmetic-science group). **No PMID/DOI; not a peer-reviewed journal article.**
- Result: positive vs both comparators on reported parameters.
- **Tier-3 weight.** Same caveat as Claim 1; verifiable only through the secondary peer-reviewed review [4] (its reference [15]).

**Claim 3 — A 1-month pilot study (n=20, thigh skin) found a copper-binding peptide cream (GHK-Cu) increased dermal procollagen in 70% of subjects vs 50% for vitamin C, 40% for tretinoin/retinoic acid, and 50% for melatonin.** [3, open_label]
- Design: pilot clinical/histologic/ultrastructural study; topical creams applied to the **extensor surface of the thighs** (an internally active-comparator design — tretinoin as the reference); 1-month duration; outcome = dermal procollagen by biopsy/immunohistology. **Not randomized, not blinded** (described as a pilot, active-comparator).
- n: 20 healthy subjects (split into two 10-subject comparison sets per secondary descriptions).
- Blinding: none reported.
- Control: internal active comparators (tretinoin, vitamin C, melatonin) — no inert vehicle arm.
- Sponsor/COI: academic (Mount Sinai authors). Funding not clearly disclosed in retrievable record.
- Result: copper peptide produced procollagen increase in 70% of subjects — numerically the highest of the four arms — without tretinoin-type irritation.
- **Tier-2 (low).** Real published primary study but **no PMID** (published in *Disease Management and Clinical Outcomes*, a non-PubMed-indexed journal); DOI 10.1016/S1088-3371(98)00011-4. Histologic surrogate endpoint (procollagen), tiny n, no inert control.

**Claim 4 — A multicenter randomized, evaluator-blinded, vehicle-controlled trial found topical GHK-Cu gel ("lamin Gel") accelerated closure of diabetic neuropathic foot ulcers ~3× vs vehicle, with lower infection rates.** [5, rct]
- Design: **multicenter, randomized, evaluator-blinded, placebo(vehicle)-controlled** — the highest-quality controlled human design in the GHK-Cu literature. Topical gel.
- n: not stated in the abstract (multicenter; sample size not retrievable from abstract).
- Blinding: evaluator-blinded.
- Control: vehicle (placebo) gel + standardized wound care in both arms.
- Sponsor/COI: ProCyte Corp.-associated authorship (Patt, Duncan — the company commercializing the copper-peptide gel) → **industry/COI present**.
- Result: median plantar-ulcer closure 98.5% (GHK-Cu) vs 60.8% (vehicle), p<0.05; closure ~3× faster; infection 7% vs 34%, p<0.05.
- **Tier-1/2.** Genuine RCT, peer-reviewed (*Wound Repair Regen* 1994; PMID 17147644), but old, industry-linked, and **wound-healing — NOT anti-aging/cosmetic**.

**Claim 5 — A randomized trial of topical copper tripeptide complex after CO2 laser facial resurfacing (n=13 completers) found NO significant benefit on the primary endpoint (resolution of erythema); only patient-reported satisfaction was higher.** [6, rct]
- Design: **randomized, blinded-evaluator** comparison of post-laser skincare regimen **with vs without** GHK-Cu; objective erythema by computer analysis + blinded evaluators; wrinkle/appearance at 12 weeks.
- n: **13 patients completed** — very small.
- Blinding: blinded evaluators for objective measures.
- Control: standard post-laser regimen without GHK-Cu.
- Sponsor/COI: academic (Univ. of New Mexico / Indiana); product provided context not fully disclosed.
- Result: **NEGATIVE for the primary objective endpoint** — no statistically significant difference in erythema resolution, wrinkle, or appearance between groups; **only patient satisfaction was significantly higher** in the GHK-Cu group. This is the most rigorous cosmetic-adjacent RCT and it did **not** confirm an objective benefit.
- **Tier-1/2.** Real RCT, peer-reviewed (*Arch Facial Plast Surg* 2006; PMID 16847171). Critical counter-evidence — cite it whenever the cosmetic claims are presented.

**Claim 6 — A registered Phase 2 RCT of topical GHK-Cu gel for acute wound re-epithelialization is recruiting (not yet reporting results).** [7, rct]
- Design: **Phase 2, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, split-wound** study; topical GHK-Cu gel 0.1% w/w once daily vs vehicle; quadruple masking.
- n: 60 (estimated enrollment).
- Blinding: double-blind (identical gels).
- Control: vehicle gel (intra-subject split-wound).
- Sponsor/COI: **Hudson Biotech (industry).**
- Result: **none yet** — status Recruiting (start Feb 2026). Primary outcome = time to complete re-epithelialization over 21 days. Listed for completeness; carries no result weight.
- **Regulatory/registry record.** NCT07437586.

**Claim 7 (NEGATIVE / boundary claim) — There is essentially NO human randomized controlled trial of INJECTABLE or systemic GHK-Cu.** [confirmed by absence; see also 8]
- A clinicaltrials.gov query (GHK-Cu / copper tripeptide / glycyl-histidyl-lysine) returns no injectable/systemic GHK-Cu interventional trial; the only directly relevant registered GHK-Cu study is **topical** (NCT07437586) [7]. All published human GHK-Cu studies are topical (cosmetic or wound gel). Injectable/"subcutaneous" GHK-Cu use is clinic/community practice **without** supporting human RCTs. State this plainly anywhere injectable use is discussed.

**Claim 8 (boundary / mis-citation guard) — The frequently cited "GHK hair-growth RCT" (Lee 2016) is NOT evidence for GHK-Cu.** [8, rct — but off-target]
- Lee WJ et al. 2016 (*Ann Dermatol*; PMID 27489425) was a randomized controlled trial (n=45, male pattern hair loss) of **ALAVAX = a complex of 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) + glycyl-histidyl-lysine peptide**, not GHK-Cu (copper complex), and the active agent is the ALA/peptide combination. **Do not cite as standalone GHK-Cu efficacy.** No verifiable human RCT exists for **topical GHK-Cu monotherapy for hair growth**; hair claims rest on in-vitro/animal work and combination/microneedling protocols.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

1. **TOPICAL vs INJECTABLE — the headline.** ALL human GHK-Cu evidence is **topical** (cosmetic creams + wound-healing gels). **There is NO human RCT — and no published human study of any design — for INJECTABLE or systemic GHK-Cu.** Subcutaneous/injected GHK-Cu is clinic and community practice unsupported by human trials. Any benefit/safety claim for injectable GHK-Cu is an extrapolation from topical/in-vitro/animal data and must be labeled as such.

2. **The flagship cosmetic claims are NOT peer-reviewed primary research.** The 71-woman facial [1] and 41-woman eye-cream [2] studies — the backbone of GHK-Cu's anti-aging marketing — are **AAD conference posters with no PMID/DOI**, traceable only through a secondary review [4] whose lead author (Pickart) is the peptide's originator with a commercial interest. We could not retrieve a primary methods/results document for either; their controlled/blinded status for [1] is **unverifiable**. Treat as Tier-3.

3. **Industry sponsorship and COI are pervasive.** Cosmetic posters [1][2] = cosmetic-industry scientists; the diabetic-ulcer RCT [5] = ProCyte (the commercializing company); the new registered trial [7] = Hudson Biotech; the authoritative review [4] = the inventor. Independent, non-conflicted human efficacy data are essentially absent.

4. **Small n / surrogate endpoints.** Where designs are verifiable, samples are tiny (Abdulghani n=20; Miller n=13 completers) and/or endpoints are surrogate (procollagen by biopsy in [3]) rather than validated clinical aging outcomes.

5. **The most rigorous anti-aging-adjacent RCT was NEGATIVE.** Miller 2006 [6] (randomized, blinded evaluators) found **no objective benefit** of GHK-Cu after laser resurfacing; only subjective patient satisfaction improved. This directly tempers the uncontrolled positive cosmetic reports.

6. **Wound healing ≠ anti-aging.** The one clear positive RCT [5] is for **diabetic foot ulcers**, an indication and population distinct from cosmetic facial use; it is also 30 years old and industry-run. The new RCT [7] is wound re-epithelialization and has **no results yet**.

7. **Hair-growth human RCT does not exist for GHK-Cu monotherapy.** The widely-cited "GHK hair RCT" [8] tested an **ALA+GHK combination**, not GHK-Cu. Topical eyelash/hair claims in the broader literature rest on combination products, microneedling-delivery protocols, or in-vitro/animal data — not on a controlled trial of topical GHK-Cu alone.

8. **No regulatory drug approval.** GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide-1) is a permitted cosmetic ingredient; it is **not an approved drug** for any systemic or injectable indication. (Cross-ref Section on regulatory status.)

**Bottom line for the section:** Human evidence for GHK-Cu is modest, topical-only, small-n, heavily industry-linked, and built more on conference posters than peer-reviewed RCTs. The two real GHK-Cu RCTs with reported results split 1 positive (diabetic ulcers) / 1 negative (post-laser cosmetic). For injectable/systemic GHK-Cu, the correct statement is unequivocal: **no human RCT exists.**
