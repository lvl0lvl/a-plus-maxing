# Section D — Other Preclinical & Systemic Claims (GHK-Cu)

Scope: systemic/anti-aging gene-expression claims; lung/COPD gene-expression; nerve
regeneration; CNS (anti-anxiety, intracerebral hemorrhage, cognitive); the
"anti-cancer gene-expression" claims. DEEP, skeptical pass. The governing caution for
this section: **a large fraction of GHK-Cu's broad systemic narrative rests on
computational gene-expression signatures (Broad Connectivity Map / cMap) and on
narrative reviews authored by the molecule's originator (Pickart), not on demonstrated
efficacy.** Bioinformatic/expression claims are hypothesis-generating and are tagged and
caveated as such throughout.

---

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; species+n; explicit caveats)

**Anti-aging / genome-wide gene-expression**

1. GHK modulates the expression of a large fraction of human genes — the originator's
   framing is that GHK "resets" thousands of genes (often quoted as ~4,000 genes, "a
   third of the genome") toward a more youthful/healthy state [1, mechanism_review]
   [2, mechanism_review]. **CAVEAT (critical):** This figure is derived from the Broad
   Institute Connectivity Map (cMap) — a database of microarray responses of a few
   cultured human cell lines (e.g., MCF7) to 1,309 compounds at a single concentration.
   It is an *in vitro transcriptional fingerprint in cancer cell lines*, computationally
   tallied, **not** evidence that GHK rejuvenates tissue or extends lifespan in any
   organism. The "third of the genome" / "youthful reset" language is interpretive and
   originates from Pickart/Margolina narrative reviews, not from a controlled aging
   study. No anti-aging clinical endpoint (lifespan, biological-age biomarker) is
   demonstrated. [1, mechanism_review] [2, mechanism_review]

2. GHK is proposed to act systemically across many organs (skin, lung, bone, liver,
   stomach, GI, nervous system) via shared pathways — NF-κB suppression, proteasome
   activation, DNA-repair gene induction, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant programs
   [1, mechanism_review] [2, mechanism_review]. **CAVEAT:** These are review-level
   syntheses that assemble cMap signatures plus scattered primary findings into a
   unifying hypothesis. The breadth of the claim is inversely related to the depth of the
   per-organ evidence; most organ-specific assertions are signature-level, not
   efficacy-level.

**Lung / COPD (the strongest independent systemic claim)**

3. GHK reverses an emphysema-associated lung gene-expression signature. In explanted
   human lung tissue, 127 genes were significantly associated with regional emphysema
   severity; cMap analysis flagged GHK as a compound predicted to reverse that signature
   and induce a TGF-β–like, tissue-repair pattern [3, in_vitro]. **Independent of
   Pickart** (Boston University / Groningen / UBC / Penn group; Campbell, Spira, Hogg et
   al.). Tier-1 journal. [3, in_vitro]

4. In follow-up *in vitro* work within the same study, GHK (0.1–10 nM) applied to
   cultured **human** lung fibroblasts recapitulated TGF-β–induced expression patterns,
   organized the actin cytoskeleton, raised integrin β1, and restored collagen-I
   contraction/remodeling in fibroblasts from **COPD patients (n=5)** versus former
   smokers without COPD (n=5) [3, in_vitro]. **CAVEAT:** This is a small-n human-cell
   culture result, not an animal emphysema model or a human trial. It is the best
   mechanistic support for a lung claim but does not show GHK treats COPD in vivo.
   [3, in_vitro]

**Nerve regeneration (peripheral)**

5. GHK incorporated into collagen guidance tubes improved early peripheral-nerve
   regeneration: enhanced production of neurotrophic factors (NGF, NT-3, NT-4) and
   recruitment of hematogenous and Schwann cells in **rat** sciatic-nerve regeneration
   [4, animal]. **Independent of Pickart** (Ahmed, Basha, Gopinath, Muthusamy,
   Jayakumar; Indian biomaterials group). **CAVEAT:** Early-phase (~10 day) growth-factor
   upregulation in a conduit model; species = rat; the publication does not provide a
   clean treated-vs-control functional-recovery n in the abstract-level data retrieved.
   Supports a regenerative *mechanism*, not a clinical nerve-repair claim. [4, animal]

**CNS — anti-anxiety / behavior**

6. GHK produced an anxiolytic effect in rats. Intraperitoneal GHK (0.5, 5, 50 µg/kg,
   12 min pre-test) increased time in open arms of the elevated plus maze across all
   doses [5, animal]. **Independent of Pickart** (Bobyntsev et al., Kursk State Medical
   University, Russia; n per group not isolated from the retrieved abstract). **CAVEAT:**
   Single behavioral paradigm, acute dosing, small Russian-group literature; explicitly
   too thin to support any human anxiety/mood indication. [5, animal]

**CNS — intracerebral hemorrhage / neuroprotection**

7. GHK reduced neuronal apoptosis after intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) in rats. In a
   collagenase-induced ICH model, **Wistar rats** (72 total; 24/group; GHK 1 or
   10 mg/kg i.p. q24h ×3 pre-ICH) showed reduced caspase-3/TUNEL apoptosis, lower brain
   water content, improved neurological scores, raised VEGFA and lowered miR-339-5p
   [6, animal]. **Independent of Pickart** (Zhang/Wang/He, China Medical University).
   [6, animal]

8. GHK alleviated astrocyte injury after ICH in rats via Akt/miR-146a-3p/AQP4. **Wistar
   rats** (120 total; 24/group; GHK 1, 5, 10 µg/g i.p. q24h ×3) plus primary astrocyte /
   293T in-vitro work showed reduced brain water content, improved neurological deficits,
   reduced astrocyte injury, and downregulated AQP4 [7, animal]. Same independent group
   [7, animal]. **CAVEAT (7 & 8):** Two reports from one lab; prophylactic dosing (GHK
   given *before* hemorrhage), pathway-mechanism focus. Genuinely independent in-vivo
   neuroprotection signal, but single-group and not therapeutic-timing.

**CNS — cognitive decline (review/computational)**

9. GHK is claimed to up-modulate ~700+ genes relevant to nervous-system function,
   neuronal/glial/Schwann-cell biology, myelin, DNA repair and nerve outgrowth, with
   implied benefit for cognitive decline [8, mechanism_review]. **CAVEAT:** This is a
   Pickart/Margolina **cMap computational analysis**, not a primary neuro experiment and
   not a cognition study. Hypothesis-generating only; no cognitive endpoint measured.
   [8, mechanism_review]

**"Anti-cancer" gene-expression claims — handle with care**

10. GHK was identified as the single most active of 1,309 cMap compounds at reversing a
    54-gene "metastasis-prone" signature of early-stage mismatch-repair-proficient
    colorectal cancer; only GHK (1 µM) and the alkaloid securinine (18 µM) reversed the
    signature [9, in_vitro]. **Independent of Pickart** (Hong, Downey, Eu, Koh, Cheah;
    Singapore). **CAVEAT (critical):** This is a *bioinformatic perturbagen-matching*
    result — a predicted transcriptional reversal of a prognostic gene set, **NOT** a
    demonstration that GHK shrinks tumors, prevents metastasis, or treats cancer in cells,
    animals, or patients. It is a computational hypothesis. [9, in_vitro]

11. GHK up-regulates caspase, growth-regulatory, and DNA-repair "anti-cancer" gene
    programs, and modulates gene expression in MCF7 (breast) and PC3 (prostate) cancer
    cell lines [10, mechanism_review]. **CAVEAT (critical):** Pickart/Margolina cMap +
    GenePattern analyses. The authors **themselves concede** that "gene data by itself
    would not be enough to draw a conclusion" and report **no** cell-killing/viability
    assays — pure expression-signature work. Not anti-cancer efficacy. [10,
    mechanism_review]

12. GHK induces programmed cell death (apoptosis) in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells
    *in vitro* — 1.8–3.3-fold apoptosis increase at 1 and 10 nM GHK (higher with
    GHK-PEG) [11, in_vitro]. **Independent of Pickart** (Matalka, Ford, Unlap).
    **CAVEAT:** This is the **only** retrieved primary wet-lab anti-cancer-direction
    result (actual apoptosis assay, not signature). However it is a single cell line, a
    single small study, and published in a **low-tier / OMICS-group journal** (J
    Biotechnol Biomater; not PubMed-indexed) — weight accordingly. [11, in_vitro]

**Honest bottom line on cancer:** There is **no clinical, and effectively no in-vivo,
anti-cancer evidence** for GHK-Cu. The "anti-cancer" story is built on (a) computational
cMap signature-reversal predictions (claims 10, 11) and (b) one low-tier single-cell-line
apoptosis paper (claim 12). Representing GHK-Cu as an anti-cancer agent would
overstate the evidence by a wide margin.

## Lab-provenance note (Pickart/Margolina dominance?)

**Yes — heavy originator dominance on the systemic/anti-aging and anti-cancer-signature
narrative.** Of the 11 admissible sources:

- **4 are Pickart/Margolina-authored** ([1], [2], [8], [10]) and are the source of the
  expansive "resets a third of the genome," "youthful reset," multi-organ, and
  anti-cancer-gene framing. Three are explicitly narrative reviews; one ([10]) plus [8]
  are computational cMap analyses. Two ([1],[2],[10]) carry R&D Skin Biology /
  commercial-originator affiliation (Pickart founded/commercializes copper peptides).
- **7 are independent of Pickart** ([3] Boston U/Hogg/Spira lung; [4] Ahmed nerve;
  [5] Bobyntsev anxiolytic; [6],[7] Zhang ICH; [9] Hong colorectal; [11] Matalka
  neuroblastoma).

Critically, even the strongest *independent* systemic results lean computational: the
lung [3] and colorectal [9] headline findings are **cMap signature-reversal predictions**,
with only small-n human-cell or tissue-signature backing. The genuinely independent
*in-vivo* evidence is confined to: peripheral nerve (rat, [4]), anxiety behavior (rat,
[5]), and ICH neuroprotection (rat, [6],[7] — one group, prophylactic dosing). No
independent in-vivo anti-aging or anti-cancer efficacy exists in this section.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **No human clinical trials** for any Section-D systemic claim (anti-aging, lung/COPD,
  nerve, CNS, cancer). Everything here is computational, in-vitro, or rodent.
- **Anti-aging "genome reset" is the weakest-supported / most-overstated claim** relative
  to its prominence in marketing: it rests on cMap in-vitro fingerprints in cancer cell
  lines re-narrated by the originator. No lifespan, no biological-age, no controlled aging
  endpoint.
- **Anti-cancer claims are bioinformatic, not therapeutic** (see bottom-line above). The
  one wet-lab apoptosis paper [11] is single-cell-line and low-tier.
- **ICH neuroprotection** [6],[7] is genuinely interesting independent in-vivo work but
  is single-group, prophylactic-timing, and unreplicated externally.
- **One PMID I could not fetch-verify line-by-line** is [9] (PubMed reCAPTCHA blocked
  direct PMID confirmation; year/volume/pages confirmed via Springer listing and multiple
  secondary citations). The DOI (10.1007/s10585-010-9305-4) and bibliographic record are
  consistent across sources; I have flagged it rather than assert a verified PMID. If
  strict PMID verification is required, [9] should be re-confirmed against PubMed.
- **Did not pursue** vendor/anecdote material (excluded by policy; would not ground
  numbers anyway). Patent filings (e.g., US9586989B1 "Non-toxic skin cancer therapy")
  were seen but deliberately excluded — patents are not evidence.
- The Pickart "anti-cancer caspase" Journal of Analytical Oncology paper (DOI
  10.6000/1927-7229.2014.03.04.5) was found but **omitted as admissible** — low-tier,
  not PubMed-indexed, and redundant with [10]; mentioned here only for completeness.
