# Section F — Safety, Copper Toxicology, Regulatory & Anti-Doping (GHK-Cu)

Scope: topical (cosmetic) safety; copper toxicology with high-dose/injectable use; Wilson's disease contraindication; regulatory status (INCI/FDA/EU/503A); WADA anti-doping status; contraindications & monitoring.

Substance: GHK-Cu = glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine:copper(II); INCI name "Copper Tripeptide-1"; CAS 49557-75-7 (peptide) / 89030-95-5 (copper complex).

Integrity note carried throughout: vendor/aggregator-sourced numerical adverse-event rates for *injectable* GHK-Cu (e.g., "8–12% injection-site reactions") are recorded below as EXISTENCE-of-AE-type only, NOT as admissible numerical rates, because no controlled human injectable trial grounds them. Topical numerical safety rests on the CIR assessment (Tier-1) and the foundational review.

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; species+n; AE rate vs existence distinguished)

1. The Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel reviewed Tripeptide-1, Hexapeptide-12, their metal salts (which include Copper Tripeptide-1 / GHK-Cu) and derivatives, and concluded these ingredients are "safe in cosmetics in the present practices of use and concentration" (peer-reviewed final report, *Int J Toxicol* 2018) [1, regulatory]. This is the strongest available formal safety determination, and it applies to topical/cosmetic use only.

2. The CIR panel's safety rationale was that "the low use concentrations and negative safety test data reviewed obviate any concerns relating to the safety of these ingredients in cosmetic products," with typical use concentrations reported as < 10 ppm [1, regulatory]. (The < 10 ppm framing is for the broader ingredient family; cosmetic GHK-Cu actives are commonly formulated in the ~0.05–2% range per vendor technical data sheets [7, vendor_label] — vendor figure, qualitative only.)

3. The foundational review of GHK-Cu states the peptide "has been widely used in anti-aging and cosmetic products in humans for decades without any adverse effects," characterizing it as safe at the low doses used cosmetically and in anti-anxiety/anti-pain animal work (Pickart & Margolina, *Int J Mol Sci* 2018) [2, mechanism_review]. This is a narrative safety assertion, not a quantified AE rate, and the same review reports NO human oral or IV dosing data and NO LD50.

4. Copper homeostasis sets the systemic-overload risk floor: the U.S. RDA for copper is 900 µg/day and the Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) for adults is 10 mg/day, the UL being set on protection against liver damage as the critical adverse effect (Institute of Medicine Dietary Reference Intakes) [3, regulatory]. Topical cosmetic GHK-Cu delivers copper masses orders of magnitude below the UL, supporting the "no systemic copper risk from topical use" position; injectable/high-dose use has no comparable margin characterized.

5. Individuals with Wilson's disease (and other copper-metabolism disorders such as Indian childhood cirrhosis and idiopathic copper toxicosis) are at risk of chronic copper toxicity at intakes well below the 10 mg/day UL; the UL explicitly does not apply to them [3, regulatory]. GHK-Cu delivers bioavailable copper, so these populations are the principal contraindication group (see Risk-floor).

6. Copper can shift from antioxidant to pro-oxidant behaviour at supraphysiological concentrations, the mechanistic basis for copper toxicity at high systemic loads [3, regulatory]/[2, mechanism_review]. This is the toxicological argument that the topical→injectable safety extrapolation is invalid.

7. EXISTENCE (not rate) — topical GHK-Cu adverse events are limited to rare, mild, local irritation/sensitization in cosmetic use; contact dermatitis is described as "very rarely reported," and patch testing before facial use is the standard precaution [8, anecdote_aggregate]/[6, vendor_label]. Consistent with CIR negative sensitization/irritation test data [1, regulatory]. No published GHK-Cu-specific allergic-contact-dermatitis case report was located.

8. EXISTENCE (not rate) — for INJECTABLE GHK-Cu, aggregator/community sources describe injection-site reactions (redness, swelling, minor pain), transient post-injection flushing, and metallic taste; reported numerical frequencies (e.g., "8–12%") originate from vendor/aggregator pages and are NOT admissible as AE rates because no controlled human injectable trial exists [9, anecdote_aggregate]. The injectable clinical side-effect profile "has not been characterized in a controlled human trial" [9, anecdote_aggregate].

9. Topical GHK-Cu has primary-literature human dermal exposure data supporting tolerability: a multicenter, randomized, evaluator-blinded, vehicle-controlled trial of topical GHK-Cu gel ("lamin Gel") in diabetic neuropathic foot ulcers reported markedly accelerated closure (median ~98.5% vs 60.8% vehicle) with lower infection rates and no reported toxicity attributable to the agent (Mulder GD et al., *Wound Repair Regen* 1994;2(4):259–269, PMID 17147644 — verified via PubMed; type = rct, human; industry/ProCyte COI; wound-healing, NOT cosmetic) [16, rct], and a controlled trial of topical copper-tripeptide on CO2-laser-resurfaced skin assessed post-procedure tolerability (Archives of Facial Plastic Surgery 2006) [5, rct] — abstract-level; full AE table not retrieved.

## Risk-floor inputs (contraindications / monitoring / stopping — each cited)

- CONTRAINDICATION — Wilson's disease and other copper-metabolism / copper-overload disorders: GHK-Cu supplies bioavailable copper; these individuals reach copper toxicity below the general-population 10 mg/day UL [3, regulatory]. Absolute contraindication for systemic/injectable use.
- CONTRAINDICATION / interaction — patients on copper-chelation therapy: GHK-Cu directly counteracts chelation; avoid [9, anecdote_aggregate] (qualitative).
- CAUTION — concurrent copper supplementation: stacking additional copper raises cumulative-load risk toward the UL; avoid co-supplementation, especially with any systemic GHK-Cu route [4, anecdote_aggregate]/[3, regulatory].
- MONITORING (systemic/injectable, by analogy to copper-overload management) — copper status (serum copper, ceruloplasmin) and liver function are the relevant axes given liver damage is the UL-defining endpoint [3, regulatory]; no GHK-Cu-specific monitoring protocol is validated in trials (gap).
- STOPPING — topical: discontinue and consider patch testing / dermatology referral on local irritation or suspected allergic contact dermatitis [8, anecdote_aggregate].
- ROUTE GAP as a risk-floor input — the entire formal safety basis (CIR, decades of cosmetic use) is TOPICAL; it does not transfer to injectable use, which lacks controlled human safety data [1, regulatory]/[9, anecdote_aggregate].

## Regulatory & WADA status (cited; state clearly if NOT on WADA list)

Cosmetic ingredient status:
- "Copper Tripeptide-1" is an established INCI cosmetic ingredient name; functions as a skin-conditioning agent and is CIR-reviewed "safe as used" in cosmetics [1, regulatory]. In the U.S. this is cosmetic-regulated (not an approved drug). EU: regulated as a cosmetic ingredient under the EU Cosmetics Regulation framework (cosmetic use; no drug authorization) — consistent with INCI listing and CIR cosmetic scope [1, regulatory].

FDA drug / compounding status (important, time-sensitive — as of mid-2026):
- GHK-Cu is NOT an FDA-approved drug for any therapeutic indication [10, regulatory]/[11, regulatory].
- Under FDA's 503A bulk-drug-substances compounding framework: in April 2026 the FDA REMOVED GHK-Cu from Category 1 (non-injectable route) because the nominator WITHDREW the nomination, and GHK-Cu (injectable) was separately addressed; GHK-Cu sits in a regulatory gray area pending Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) consultation scheduled before the end of February 2027 [10, regulatory]/[11, regulatory]. Net effect: injectable GHK-Cu cannot be relied upon as legally compoundable; sources selling injectable GHK-Cu "for human use" operate outside FDA compliance [11, regulatory]. (Note: secondary trackers report slightly different sequencing — some say removed from Category 1, one aggregator says briefly restored — treat the law-firm regulatory sources [10] as authoritative; the stable facts are: NOT FDA-approved, NOT settled-compoundable, PCAC review pending Feb 2027.)

WADA anti-doping status — VERIFIED:
- GHK-Cu / copper tripeptide / "copper" / "GHK" is NOT individually named anywhere on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List, including the S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics) section. Verification method: the named S2.2 growth-factor examples are thymosin-β4 / TB-500 and growth factors affecting muscle/tendon/ligament synthesis, vascularization, etc. — GHK-Cu is not among them; multiple anti-doping and regulatory analyses confirm GHK-Cu is not specifically listed [12, regulatory]/[13, regulatory]/[15, regulatory]. (The official WADA PDF could not be machine-parsed via fetch; verification is by the official S2/S0 definitional text mirrored at drugs.com and corroborating anti-doping sources — flagged as a residual limitation.)
- HOWEVER — the S0 (Non-Approved Substances) catch-all prohibits, at all times, "any pharmacological substance which is not addressed by any of the subsequent sections of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use" (e.g., drugs under pre-clinical/clinical development) [14, regulatory]. Because GHK-Cu has no human-therapeutic regulatory approval, an athlete using non-approved INJECTABLE/systemic GHK-Cu could be captured under S0, even though GHK-Cu is not enumerated [14, regulatory]/[15, regulatory]. Topical cosmetic use of an approved cosmetic ingredient is not the S0 target.
- PLAUSIBLE-BUT-UNSETTLED secondary capture (S2 "similar biological effect") — beyond the not-named + S0 analysis above, an anti-doping authority could ALSO argue S2 capture on a non-enumerated basis: the S2.2 category covers "growth factors and growth factor modulators" plus substances with "a similar chemical structure or similar biological effect(s)" to the listed growth factors [15, regulatory]. GHK-Cu's documented regenerative/angiogenic, growth-factor-like signaling (it modulates wound-healing growth factors and vascularization — see Section mechanism / [2, mechanism_review]) could arguably be read as a "similar biological effect," even though GHK-Cu is a small copper-bound tripeptide rather than a peptide hormone or growth factor proper. This is presented as an argument an authority COULD make, NOT as a definite or settled ban: GHK-Cu is not enumerated, no published WADA determination or sanction applies the S2 "similar effect" clause to GHK-Cu, and the "similar biological effect" test is interpretive and untested here. Treat it as a plausible secondary exposure route alongside (not replacing) the verified not-named finding and the S0 catch-all.
- Bottom line for athletes: GHK-Cu is NOT a named prohibited substance, but injectable/systemic non-approved GHK-Cu carries S0 catch-all exposure, and a secondary (unsettled, non-enumerated) S2 "similar biological effect" capture argument is plausible given its growth-factor-like signaling; this is still distinct from explicitly listed growth-factor peptides (TB-500) which are flatly and unambiguously banned by name under S2.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- INJECTABLE safety is essentially uncharacterized: no controlled human trial of injectable/systemic GHK-Cu exists; all numerical injectable AE figures circulating online are vendor/aggregator and were excluded as rate-grounding. This is the single largest gap and the core risk-floor caveat.
- No published GHK-Cu-specific case report of copper toxicity, copper overload, or allergic contact dermatitis was located — absence of reports is reassuring for topical use but is not equivalent to demonstrated systemic safety.
- CIR full report ([1]) is paywalled (403); its conclusion/concentration text was verified through the CIR quick-reference table, the journal abstract, and PSU/EPA-HERO bibliographic records rather than the full PDF body — the specific dermal-irritation/sensitization sub-study numbers and subject counts were NOT extracted.
- The official WADA 2026 PDF would not parse via fetch (403/binary); the "GHK-Cu not listed" finding rests on the verbatim S0/S2 definitional text and several corroborating anti-doping analyses, not a full-text term search of the PDF itself. Confidence is high (convergent sources) but this is a residual verification limitation.
- FDA 503A status is genuinely in flux (April 2026 changes; PCAC pending Feb 2027) and secondary sources sequence it inconsistently; the stable, defensible facts are: not FDA-approved, not reliably compoundable for injection, review pending. Re-verify against the live FDA 503A bulks list before any time-sensitive use.
- EU cosmetic-regulation specifics (Annex listings/restrictions, if any) were inferred from INCI/cosmetic status, not read from the EU CosIng database directly — a gap if precise EU restriction text is needed.
- Mulder diabetic-ulcer RCT ([16], PMID 17147644 verified) and the laser-resurfacing study ([5]) were captured at abstract level; their primary AE tables were not retrieved.
