## Concentration/COI audit (Pickart/Margolina share estimate + method; commercial COI surfaced)

**COMMERCIAL COI — surfaced as first-class (mandatory regardless of share).**
The GHK-Cu *review and claims-synthesis* literature is anchored by **Loren Pickart, PhD (1938–2023)** — the discoverer of GHK in human plasma (Pickart PhD thesis, UCSF, 1973) — who was also the **commercial seller** of GHK-Cu products. Pickart founded **ProCyte Corp.** (took it to NASDAQ; copper-peptide skincare) and then **Skin Biology, Inc.** (Bellevue, WA; retail GHK-Cu cosmetics at reverseskinaging.com), the latter with his wife Charlene [11, regulatory/obituary; 12, vendor/company]. His principal review co-author **Anna Margolina** is affiliated with the **Skin Biology Research & Development Department** — i.e., the seller's own R&D arm [1, 2, mechanism_review]. This is a textbook discoverer-seller-reviewer triple role: the dominant secondary literature is authored by a party with a direct, ongoing financial interest in GHK-Cu sales. **This COI must be carried forward into every efficacy claim that rests on a Pickart/Margolina review.** Note the disclosure gap: at least one heavily-cited Pickart/Vasquez-Soltero/Margolina review (BioMed Res Int 2015, PMID 26236730) states "**The authors declare that there is no conflict of interests regarding the publication of this paper**" despite all three authors being affiliated with the commercial entity Skin Biology [9, mechanism_review] — i.e., the affiliation is printed but the COI is explicitly *denied*.

**Share estimate — method.** I distinguish two different literatures, because the share is very different in each:

- **(a) Review / claims-synthesis literature (the "story" of GHK-Cu):** Pickart and Margolina dominate. The most-cited, broadest "what GHK-Cu does" reviews are theirs: Pickart & Margolina 2018 (Int J Mol Sci, PMID 29986520) [1]; Pickart, Vasquez-Soltero & Margolina 2015 (BioMed Res Int, PMID 26236730) [9]; Pickart, Vasquez-Soltero & Margolina 2012 (cognitive/oxidative-stress review, PMC3359723) [10]; plus the gene-expression and anti-cancer reviews. **Estimated Pickart-nexus share of the broad-claims review literature ≈ 70–85%.** This CLEARS the ≥70% concentration-audit gate — the synthesized narrative is effectively single-source. Method: enumerated the widely-circulated "comprehensive review" papers surfaced across PubMed/PMC and secondary write-ups; the substantial majority trace to Skin Biology authors.

- **(b) Primary experimental literature (in vitro / animal / clinical):** Pickart-nexus is a **minority**. A PubMed scan of GHK copper-peptide wound-healing primary studies (~26 enumerated, sorted by date) shows ~3–4 Pickart-authored/co-authored entries (incl. the Reims collaboration **Maquart, Pickart et al. 1988**, where Pickart is one co-author of a French academic group [7]) against ~22 independent-group studies — Maquart/Reims (Maquart, Siméon, Wegrowski, Borel — PMID-indexed FEBS/J Clin Invest work) [7, 23-class], Korean groups (Choi, Kang, Park), Chinese hydrogel/scaffold groups (2020–2025), Polish liposome groups (Dymek, Ogórek), and orthopaedic groups (Fu SC et al., J Orthop Res 2015, PMID 25731775) [13/orthopaedic]. **Estimated Pickart-nexus share of primary experimental literature ≈ 15–25%.** This does NOT trip the gate.

**Net read for the concentration gate:** TRIPPED for the *claims/review* layer (~70–85%, single-source narrative + denied COI), NOT tripped for the *primary-evidence* layer (~15–25%, genuinely independent). The honest framing: GHK-Cu has a reasonably distributed primary literature but a **highly concentrated, commercially-conflicted interpretive/review layer.** Any synthesis that leans on the reviews inherits both the concentration and the COI.

**Dominant groups (named):**
- **Pickart nexus / Skin Biology** (Loren Pickart, Anna Margolina, Jessica M. Vasquez-Soltero) — Bellevue, WA — owns the review/claims layer; commercial seller.
- **Reims group (France)** — Maquart, Borel, Siméon, Wegrowski, Gillery, Monboisse — foundational independent collagen/MMP mechanism work (Pickart co-authored the 1988 FEBS paper) [7].
- **Korean dermatology groups** — Choi, Kang, Park — keratinocyte/stem-cell and lung-injury work.
- **Recent biomaterials groups (China/Poland/Portugal, 2020–2025)** — copper-peptide hydrogels, liposomal delivery, microsphere fillers (Dymek, Ogórek, Hu, Castro/3B's-Minho).

## Sourcing landscape (cosmetic vs injectable; purity caveats)

**Two distinct, non-interchangeable supply channels:**

1. **Cosmetic topical (regulated-ish, legitimate).** Sold as **INCI "Copper Tripeptide-1"** in serums/creams. The **Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) 2018 expert-panel safety assessment** (Johnson, Bergfeld, Belsito et al., Int J Toxicol 2018, doi 10.1177/1091581818807863) concluded Tripeptide-1 / Copper Tripeptide-1 and related ingredients are **"safe in cosmetics in the present practices of use and concentration,"** noting typical use concentrations are **<10 ppm** and that low use levels plus negative test data obviate safety concerns [3, regulatory]. Supplier-recommended cosmetic formulation concentrations cluster around **0.05–1%** (some raw-material vendors sell 5–10% solutions for formulators) [4, vendor_label]. Formulation caveat: the Cu²⁺ ion is redox-active, so the complex degrades with oxygen/light/incompatible actives — stability, not toxicity, is the main cosmetic concern [4, vendor_label]. This channel is for **topical use only**; CIR safety does NOT extend to injection.

2. **Injectable / "research chemical" gray market (UNREGULATED).** GHK-Cu is sold as **lyophilized powder vials (typ. 50 mg / 100 mg)** by research-chemical/peptide vendors marketed "for research use only — not for human consumption," with self-reported purity claims (commonly "≥98–99% by HPLC/MS," third-party COA "on request") [4, 14, vendor_label — gray-market flagged; purity/reconstitution math ONLY, never efficacy]. **GHK-Cu is not an FDA-approved drug for injection.** Documented gray-market risks (not vendor-specific, generic to the channel): endotoxin contamination, heavy-metal contamination (acutely relevant for a *copper-containing* complex — excess free/poorly-chelated copper is a real toxicity vector), incorrect sequence/identity, and unknown excipients/stabilizers [4, 14, vendor_label]. 

**Copper-complex stoichiometry / identity concern (chemistry-specific).** GHK-Cu is a **1:1 GHK:Cu²⁺ chelate** — not free copper and not free peptide. Two failure modes the channel does not reliably control: (i) **under-/over-coppering** — product sold as "GHK" (copper-free) vs "GHK-Cu" (copper-loaded) are chemically different, and a powder labeled "GHK-Cu" may carry variable or excess copper; (ii) **reconstitution chemistry** — the complex is pH- and redox-sensitive, so bacteriostatic-water reconstitution and storage (refrigerate, never freeze, protect from light/oxygen) materially affect what is actually delivered. Vendor COAs verifying *copper stoichiometry* (not just peptide HPLC purity) are rarely provided. The safest legitimate access route described for human use is a **licensed physician + FDA-registered compounding pharmacy** producing pharmaceutical-grade *topical* formulations, not self-sourced injectables [14, vendor_label/practitioner-framing].

## Prescribing-practice conventions (name+venue+date) [practitioner_protocol]

> Tier 2.7 — captured for **route/dose/cycle convention ONLY**; NOT evidence of efficacy. GHK-Cu has no FDA-approved injectable indication; these reflect aesthetic/longevity-clinic and peptide-educator conventions, not guideline medicine.

- **Subcutaneous (gray-market/longevity convention):** ~**0.5–1 mg SubQ 3–5×/week to start**, escalating to **1–2 mg/day**, injected into abdominal subcutaneous tissue lateral to the navel; cycled in **4–8 week blocks**; reconstituted vial refrigerated (never frozen) [4, practitioner_protocol — venue: peptide-vendor/educator protocol pages, accessed/updated 2026]. *Named, dated source:* **"GHK-Cu Dosing Guide," Peptide Dosing Protocols (PDP), publisher Garret Grant, last updated June 2026** — lists Injectable SubQ **1–2 mg/day**, conservative start 1.0 mg (5-on/2-off, 4 wk), standard 1.0–1.5 mg/day (4–6 wk), high-end 1.5–2.0 mg/day (6–8 wk); Topical **1–3% by weight**, 8–12 wk for skin / 12+ wk for hair [15, practitioner_protocol].
- **Procedure-adjunct (aesthetic/derm convention):** GHK-Cu applied **topically immediately post-procedure** (microneedling, laser resurfacing, chemical peel) to exploit transient barrier disruption for dermal delivery [4, practitioner_protocol].
- **Peptide-education / clinician-author venue:** **William A. Seeds, MD** — founder/chair, **International Peptide Society**; author *Peptide Protocols, Vol. 1* (2020) — peptide-therapy educator framework that includes GHK-Cu and emphasizes dosing peptides separately to preserve stability [practitioner_protocol — name/venue/date present; route/handling guidance only, NOT efficacy]. Seeds is also co-author of **two 2026 peer-indexed orthopaedic peptide reviews** that catalog GHK-Cu among injectable therapeutic peptides: **Rahman, Lee & Seeds 2026, "Therapeutic Peptides in Orthopaedics," J Am Acad Orthop Surg Glob Res Rev, PMID 41490200** [4, practitioner_protocol/regulatory-adjacent], and **Mayfield et al. 2026, "Injectable Peptide Therapy: A Primer," Am J Sports Med, PMID 41476424** [5]. NOTE: Seeds is a peptide-society commercial principal — treat his protocol/review output as practitioner_protocol, NOT independent efficacy.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **Quantitative share is an estimate, not a census.** I did not run a full bibliometric extraction of every GHK-Cu citation. The 70–85% (review layer) and 15–25% (primary layer) figures are calibrated from a PubMed wound-healing scan (~26 primary studies enumerated) + the surfaced comprehensive-review set. The *direction* (concentrated reviews, distributed primary evidence) is robust; the exact percentages are bounded ranges.
- **Tier-2.7 efficacy firewall held.** No efficacy claim in this section rests on a practitioner_protocol, vendor_label, or compounding sheet. GHK-Cu efficacy adjudication belongs to other sections using Tier 1–2 sources (e.g., CIR safety [3], Mulder RCT [13], independent primary studies).
- **PMID 41490200 / 41476424 (2026 Seeds-adjacent orthopaedic reviews):** existence and citation verified via PubMed index; full text was CAPTCHA-gated, so internal GHK-Cu dosing detail is reported only at the index/abstract level. Treated as practitioner_protocol, not independent efficacy.
- **Maquart 1988 attribution:** Pickart is a co-author of this independent-Reims-group paper; I counted it toward the "nexus collaboration" share, which slightly *inflates* the Pickart-nexus primary-literature share — i.e., the true independent-primary share may be marginally higher than stated. Conservative direction for the COI flag.
- **Copper stoichiometry / heavy-metal contamination in gray-market vials** is described at the channel-risk level from vendor/aggregator sources; I found no independent third-party assay study quantifying actual copper-content variance across research-chem GHK-Cu lots. Genuine evidence gap.
- **No vendor was endorsed.** All vendor_label sources used strictly for purity/reconstitution/sourcing framing, never efficacy, per integrity rules.
