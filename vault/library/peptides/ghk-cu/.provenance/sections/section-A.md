# Section A — Identity, Chemistry, Copper Complex, PK (GHK-Cu)

Scope: tripeptide identity (Gly-His-Lys), the Cu(II) complex (stoichiometry, coordination, stability constant, structure), chemistry/formulation, skin penetration / PK, and endogenous GHK biology (plasma levels, the ~200→80 ng/mL age-decline claim and its true source). GHK and GHK-Cu are distinguished throughout.

## Claims

### Identity of the free tripeptide (GHK)

1. GHK is the tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine (sequence Gly-His-Lys), IUPAC (2S)-6-amino-2-[[(2S)-2-[(2-aminoacetyl)amino]-3-(1H-imidazol-5-yl)propanoyl]amino]hexanoic acid, molecular formula C14H24N6O4, molecular weight 340.38 g/mol, CAS 49557-75-7 (PubChem CID 73587). [6, regulatory]

2. GHK was first isolated in 1973 by Loren Pickart from human plasma (albumin fraction) as the activity that caused aged human liver tissue to synthesize proteins in the manner of younger tissue; the activity was identified as the tripeptide Gly-His-Lys with strong affinity for Cu(II), readily forming the GHK-Cu complex. [1, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review] The original report is Pickart's 1973 University of California (San Francisco) doctoral dissertation. [1a, mechanism_review] (NOTE: contrary to many secondary/vendor write-ups, the 1973 finding was a PhD thesis, not a primary peer-reviewed Nature paper; see "Coverage gaps.")

3. GHK is endogenous to humans and is reported in plasma, saliva, and urine. [2, mechanism_review]

### The GHK-Cu copper(II) complex

4. GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide-1; INCI "Copper Tripeptide-1"; also called prezatide copper) is the 1:1 Cu(II) complex of GHK, molecular formula commonly written C14H22CuN6O4 (the dianionic peptide chelating one Cu²⁺); CAS 89030-95-5. [6, regulatory][7, regulatory]

5. In the Cu(II)GHK complex, copper is bound by a tridentate set of donor atoms — the N-terminal (glycine) amino nitrogen, the deprotonated amide nitrogen of the Gly–His peptide bond, and the histidine side-chain imidazole nitrogen — forming two fused chelate rings, with square-planar geometry and the fourth equatorial site occupied by a water molecule or other monodentate/ternary ligand. [3, in_vitro]

6. The Cu(II)GHK complex is 1:1 in stoichiometry and binds copper very tightly: a conditional formation constant of log cK(7.4) ≈ 12.62 at physiological pH (pH 7.4). [3, in_vitro] Separately, Pickart's review quotes GHK's copper-binding constant as log₁₀ = 16.44 versus albumin's high-affinity site at log₁₀ = 16.2, the basis for GHK abstracting Cu²⁺ from albumin. [1, mechanism_review] (These two numbers are different quantities — a pH-7.4 conditional constant vs. an overall/absolute stability constant cited by Pickart — and should not be conflated; the directly experimentally re-measured value in tier-1 inorganic-chemistry literature is the log cK(7.4) ≈ 12.62.) [3, in_vitro]

7. A 2:1 peptide:copper species, (GHK)₂-Cu, also exists in solution alongside GHK-Cu, and both, together with free GHK, are the species relevant to membrane transport (see Claim 11). [4, in_vitro][1, mechanism_review]

### Chemistry / formulation

8. GHK and GHK-Cu are hydrophilic molecules; this hydrophilicity is the fundamental barrier to passive transport across the stratum corneum and motivates delivery strategies (liposomes, microneedling, penetration enhancers). [8, in_vitro][5, in_vitro]

9. Liposomal encapsulation produces stable GHK-Cu-loaded liposomes (~100 nm) with prolonged release (64.7–84.9% over 24 h vs. near-complete release of free peptide within 4 h) and improved cell viability vs. free peptide, proposed to improve delivery to deeper skin layers. [8, in_vitro]

### Skin penetration / PK

10. In vitro across isolated human skin layers (glycyl-histidyl-lysine cuprate diacetate, 0.68% aqueous, flow-through diffusion cells, infinite dose), copper as the tripeptide permeated dermatomed human skin with a permeability coefficient of 2.43 ± 0.51 × 10⁻⁴ cm/h; 136.2 ± 17.5 µg/cm² copper permeated over 48 h and 97 ± 6.6 µg/cm² was retained as a stratum-corneum depot; the authors concluded copper as the tripeptide was delivered "in potentially therapeutically effective amounts." [9, in_vitro][human ex vivo skin] (This tier-1 finding is more favorable to penetration than common "copper peptides don't penetrate" framing; it is in vitro through dermatomed skin, not in vivo systemic PK.)

11. In a stratum-corneum model-membrane (liposome/Flynn-cell) ESI-MS study, free GHK and its copper complexes GHK-Cu and (GHK)₂-Cu were the species able to migrate across the membrane; the work characterizes the mechanism of Cu²⁺ transport by the peptide and the pH-dependent speciation of the complexes. [4, in_vitro]

12. Native-GHK preformulation/physicochemical characterization for dermal delivery has been performed (solubility, stability, partitioning relevant to wound-healing/anti-aging topical products). [10, in_vitro]

13. Honest PK limit: no admissible peer-reviewed human systemic pharmacokinetic study (plasma half-life, Cmax, clearance after topical/SC/IV dosing) was located for GHK-Cu in this search; widely repeated half-life figures (e.g., "~0.5–1 h after IV") appear only in vendor/blog material and are NOT grounded here. [coverage gap — see limits] [route-extrapolation across topical vs. systemic]

### Endogenous GHK and the age-related decline claim

14. Pickart's reviews state plasma GHK is "about 200 ng/mL (10⁻⁷ M) at age 20" and "declines to 80 ng/mL by age 60." [1, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review]

15. SOURCE OF THE DECLINE CLAIM (verified): in the 2015 review the 200→80 ng/mL statement appears uncited in the introduction — the sentence carries NO citation marker. The plausible unstated origin is Pickart L., "A Tripeptide from Human Serum Which Enhances the Growth of Neoplastic Hepatocytes and the Survival of Normal Hepatocytes," University of California (San Francisco), 1973 — i.e., Pickart's own doctoral dissertation, which is the review's reference [1] [1a], NOT a peer-reviewed primary plasma-measurement paper. The same figures recur across Pickart's later reviews [2] without an independent primary measurement. No independent peer-reviewed quantitation of this age trajectory was located (see "Coverage gaps"). [1, mechanism_review]

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

1. THE DECLINE CLAIM IS NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED. The famous "plasma GHK ~200 ng/mL at age 20 → ~80 ng/mL at age 60" figure appears uncited in the introduction of Pickart's own 2015 review (no citation marker on that sentence); its plausible unstated origin is his 1973 UCSF doctoral dissertation [1a], which is the review's reference [1]. It is repeated across his later reviews but I found NO independent, peer-reviewed primary measurement of this age trajectory (no cohort with methods, n, assay). Treat as a single-source, inventor-originated, grey-literature claim. The many vendor/blog/"50-year-trail" pages restate it without a better source, and at least one secondary page misattributes the 1973 work to a Nature paper — that attribution was NOT confirmed and appears incorrect.

2. NO ADMISSIBLE HUMAN SYSTEMIC PK. No peer-reviewed human PK study (half-life, Cmax, AUC, clearance after topical/SC/IV) was located. Commonly cited half-life numbers (~0.5–1 h IV) are vendor/blog-only and are explicitly NOT grounded here.

3. STABILITY-CONSTANT DUALITY. Two different "binding constant" numbers circulate: Pickart's log₁₀≈16.44 (overall, cited in review [1]) and the directly re-measured conditional log cK(7.4)≈12.62 at physiological pH [3]. They are different quantities; downstream sections should cite the tier-1 conditional value [3] for physiological-pH statements and flag Pickart's 16.44 as a review-quoted figure.

4. COMPLEX FORMULA / SALT FORM. The copper-complex molecular formula is represented inconsistently across registries/vendors (free complex vs. acetate vs. TFA salt). CAS 89030-95-5 is solid; the bare-formula C14H22CuN6O4 is the deprotonated 1:1 complex and is kept tier-2.

5. PENETRATION FRAMING CONFLICT. Tier-1 ex vivo data [9] show copper-as-tripeptide DOES permeate dermatomed human skin in potentially therapeutic amounts, while formulation papers [8] emphasize the hydrophilicity barrier for free peptide. These are reconcilable (depot in stratum corneum + slow flux; delivery enhanced by liposomes/microneedling) but Section B/clinical sections should not overstate either "fully penetrates" or "does not penetrate."

6. Endogenous biology beyond plasma levels (turnover, which protease/albumin pool releases GHK, saliva/urine quantitation) is asserted in reviews [1][2] but I did not locate primary quantitation; flagged for any deeper endogenous-GHK subsection.
