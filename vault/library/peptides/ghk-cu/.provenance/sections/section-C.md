# Section C — Preclinical Skin/Wound/Hair (GHK-Cu)

Scope: GHK-Cu (Copper tripeptide-1; glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine-Cu2+) preclinical evidence in
(i) fibroblast collagen/ECM synthesis in vitro, (ii) dermal wound-healing animal models,
(iii) hair-follicle / hair-growth preclinical, (iv) skin-barrier / remodeling preclinical.
Clinical (RCT/open-label) and systemic/non-skin organs are out of scope and only flagged where
they bound interpretation. Type-tags: in_vitro | animal | mechanism_review | rct | vendor_label |
anecdote_aggregate. Every animal claim carries species + n. vendor/anecdote NEVER ground numbers.

## Claims

### (i) Fibroblast collagen / ECM synthesis in vitro

1. GHK-Cu stimulates collagen synthesis in cultured fibroblasts; stimulation begins between
   10^-12 and 10^-11 M and is maximal at ~10^-9 M, independent of any change in cell number
   (i.e., not merely a proliferation artifact). [1, in_vitro]

2. GHK-Cu at low nanomolar concentrations increases mRNA and synthesis of multiple ECM
   components beyond collagen — including dermatan sulfate / chondroitin-sulfate proteoglycans
   (decorin) and glycosaminoglycans — in dermal fibroblast culture. [2, in_vitro]
   (Route note: the same Reims group's broader "synthesis + breakdown / remodeling" framing is
   developed in vivo in claim 8 — extrapolation from culture to wound chamber. [route→8])

3. GHK-Cu modulates the protease/anti-protease balance in skin fibroblasts: it increases MMP-2
   (gelatinase A) mRNA and secretion while also increasing TIMP-1 and TIMP-2, consistent with
   controlled ECM remodeling rather than pure deposition. [3, in_vitro]

4. In human adult dermal fibroblasts (HDFa), GHK-Cu at 0.01, 1, and 100 nM enhanced collagen
   and elastin production, with the response peaking at 1 nM and DECLINING at higher
   concentration (100 nM) — a non-monotonic, biphasic dose response; the effect tracked an
   increased TIMP:MMP mRNA ratio. [4, in_vitro]

5. Combined with hyaluronic acid (1:9 GHK-Cu : low-MW HA), GHK-Cu synergistically up-regulated
   collagen IV: ~25.4-fold increase in collagen IV in the fibroblast cell test and ~2.03-fold in
   an ex-vivo human skin test (also raised collagen I and VII). Effect size attaches to the
   COMBINATION, not GHK-Cu alone. [5, in_vitro]

6. GHK-Cu accelerates recovery/growth of IRRADIATED human fibroblasts toward normal
   population-doubling kinetics, and raises early bFGF and VEGF production by treated irradiated
   fibroblasts (serum-free model). [6, in_vitro]

7. GHK-Cu restores proliferative capacity and growth-factor output in irradiated human fibroblasts
   (independent confirmation of claim 6, same lab lineage, later cohort/cell context). [7, in_vitro]

### (ii) Dermal wound-healing animal models

8. In rat subcutaneous wire-mesh wound chambers, locally injected GHK-Cu produced a
   CONCENTRATION-DEPENDENT increase in chamber dry weight, DNA, total protein, collagen, and
   glycosaminoglycan content; collagen synthesis was stimulated to ~2x that of non-collagen
   proteins, and dermatan sulfate proportion rose — demonstrating increased ECM accumulation in
   vivo. [8, animal — rat; n not stated in abstract (HALT-soft, see gaps)]

9. In rat experimental wounds, GHK-Cu modulates MMP and TIMP expression/activation
   (the in-vivo counterpart of claim 3), supporting a remodeling rather than purely pro-fibrotic
   mechanism. [9, animal — rat; n not stated in abstract]

10. In a controlled open full-thickness wound model in rabbits (n = 18 New Zealand rabbits;
    3 groups: tripeptide-copper complex / zinc oxide / untreated control; one wound per dorsal
    side), topical GHK-Cu gave a significantly smaller unhealed wound area than zinc oxide on
    day 7, and than untreated control on days 7, 14, and 21. [10, animal — rabbit, n=18]

11. In rabbits, topical tripeptide-copper complex (± helium-neon laser) accelerated wound healing
    and blood-vessel formation versus controls (same Uludag group lineage; independent
    confirmation within rabbit model). [11, animal — rabbit]

12. NEGATIVE / NULL RESULT: in an IRRADIATED rat dorsal-flap model (2 x 8 cm flap; topical GHK-Cu
    gel vs control ointment twice daily x10 days), GHK-Cu showed NO significant difference in
    flap ischemia (mean ischemic area 5.0 vs 3.8 cm^2), blood-vessel number/area, or VEGF
    expression (VEGF intensity 0.34 vs 0.54, P=0.169) versus control. [12, animal — rat]
    This bounds the wound-healing claims: benefit is not universal across models (irradiated
    random-pattern flap viability was not rescued).

### (iii) Hair-follicle / hair-growth preclinical

13. The copper-tripeptide ANALOG AHK-Cu (alanyl-histidyl-lysine-Cu2+, NOT GHK-Cu) stimulated
    elongation of human hair follicles ex vivo and proliferation of dermal-papilla cells in vitro
    at 10^-12 to 10^-9 M, and shifted Bcl-2/Bax toward survival (caspase-3/PARP reduction not
    statistically significant). [13, in_vitro/ex_vivo — human follicle, analog]
    (Route note: AHK-Cu is an analog; do NOT transfer this number to GHK-Cu without qualification.
    Included because it is the most-cited "copper tripeptide + hair follicle" primary. [route→ analog])

14. A GHK-Cu delivery study (ionic-liquid microemulsion) reported ~3.18x increased cutaneous
    penetration of copper peptide in pig skin and, in mice, hair-follicle entry into anagen by
    ~day 6 with significantly higher hair density than minoxidil by day 28, with increased
    beta-catenin and VEGF (Wnt/beta-catenin pathway) on IHC and no change in testosterone/estradiol.
    [14, animal — mouse (n not stated in retrieved record) + ex_vivo pig skin]
    (Effect is for GHK-Cu-IN-MICROEMULSION; the formulation, not bare GHK-Cu, is the test article.)

### (iv) Skin-barrier / remodeling preclinical

15. The collective in-vitro + animal evidence (claims 1-11) describes GHK-Cu as a balanced ECM
    REMODELER — co-stimulating synthesis (collagen/elastin/GAG/proteoglycan) and the
    MMP/TIMP machinery — rather than a one-way collagen-deposition agent. [1,2,3,4,8,9, mixed]

> Out-of-scope adjacency (logged, NOT counted): GHK reduced bleomycin-induced PULMONARY (lung)
> fibrosis in mice (C57BL/6, n=10/group, i.p. 2.6/26/260 ug/mL/day) via TGFβ1/Smad and EMT
> suppression. Lung, not skin — excluded from Section C tally; relevant only as cross-organ
> anti-fibrotic mechanism. [ref OOS-1, animal — mouse]

- Reims biochemistry group (Borel / Maquart / Gillery, CNRS, Faculté de Médecine Reims, France):
  refs [1], [2], [3], [8]. This is the principal ACADEMIC originator of the GHK-Cu ECM literature.

  of Pickart.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

1. n NOT REPORTED in the abstracts I could verify for the two strongest in-vivo rat papers
   ([8] JCI 1993, [9] JID) — PubMed was reCAPTCHA-gated and several publishers returned HTTP 403,
   so I could not open full methods. Animal claims 8/9 therefore carry species but UNCONFIRMED n.
   This is a soft-HALT on those specific n values: cite the EFFECT, flag n as unverified.

2. Refs [2] and [9] were reached via index/review citation rather than a directly fetched primary
   record (publisher blocks). They are real, indexed papers but I did not read their full text;
   treat their numbers as supporting, not number-grounding.

3. Hair preclinical is genuinely THIN for bare GHK-Cu: the best ex-vivo human-follicle study [13]
   uses the AHK-Cu ANALOG, and the mouse study [14] tests a microemulsion FORMULATION, not bare
   peptide. There is no independently-replicated bare-GHK-Cu mouse anagen study in the retrieved set.
   Do not overstate hair claims.

4. PIG dermal wound model: the Pickart-review claim of granulation-tissue increase in pigs could not
   be tied to a directly verifiable standalone primary in this pass (surfaced only via Pickart-authored
   review [R1] / patents). NOT cited as a numbered claim — would need primary verification first.

5. Commercial entanglement at the root: the two highest-tier foundational papers ([1] FEBS, [8] JCI)
   carry Pickart and/or ProCyte (Patt/Trachy) co-authorship. The literature is not as cleanly
   "independent academic" as headline summaries imply; independence is strongest in the LATER
   replications (Koch, Uludag, Auckland, Minnesota, Seoul).

6. Mechanism papers and vendor pages (Spartan, Loti, Core Peptides, biotechpeptides, innerbody, etc.)
   appeared throughout searches and were EXCLUDED from grounding per integrity rules; none were used
   to source any number in this section.
