# GHK-Cu (Copper Tripeptide-1) — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how GHK-Cu is actually used, prescribed, compounded, dosed, and reconstituted in practice* — the concentration/dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions that circulate among cosmetic formulators, prescribers, compounding pharmacies, and the injectable gray market. It is **not** an efficacy document and must not be read as one. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (Tier 3), and `vendor_label` (Tier 4) sources are admissible **only** for concentration/dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) purity/reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy, mechanism, or adverse-event-rate claim, and none appears here for that purpose. Several cosmetic data sheets cited below (Avena Lab TDS; Empower preparation pages) *do contain* benefit/efficacy language in their bodies — that language is **explicitly excluded** here and cited **only** for administration/formulation data (use concentration, pH, storage, incompatibilities, application directions). The efficacy state (human evidence is **topical-only**, small-n, heavily industry/COI-linked, with one positive wound RCT and one negative post-laser RCT; **zero** human data of any design for injectable/systemic GHK-Cu) lives in the research report and is unaffected by anything below.
>
> **Two use channels, very different footing.** GHK-Cu has two real-world use channels and they are not symmetric. **(a) TOPICAL cosmetic** is the dominant, regulated, mainstream channel: "Copper Tripeptide-1" is an established INCI cosmetic ingredient, CIR-rated "safe as used" (2018), and is sold in mass-market and professional serums plus compounded topical preparations. **(b) INJECTABLE / SC** is a gray-market/community channel with **no human evidence and an adverse regulatory footing** (see §5). The bulk of GHK-Cu's actual use is topical; the injectable conventions are documented here for completeness, flagged as gray-market throughout.

## 1. Topical cosmetic use conventions (concentration ranges)

Topical is the modal real-world use. Reported finished-product use concentrations vary by an order of magnitude across sources because cosmetic formulators dose **far lower** (fractions of a percent / ppm) than community "peptide-guide" pages, which quote whole-percent figures.

- **Cosmetic-formulator convention (the authoritative band): ~0.001%–0.1% w/w (10–1,000 ppm) of active GHK-Cu in the finished product**, with daily-skincare products at the low end and "professional anti-aging serums / regenerative treatments" at the higher end. A higher-strength (5,000 ppm) raw solution is dosed into the finished formula at a **commonly 0.01–0.05%** final-product level [1, compounding_data_sheet][2, compounding_data_sheet]. This matches the research report's regulatory note that the broader Tripeptide-1/Cu family is used at **< 10 ppm typical** under the CIR determination — formulator practice clusters at the **fractions-of-a-percent / ppm** scale, not whole percent.
- **Compounded topical (prescription) convention: 0.5% w/w** — the strength at which the named 503A pharmacy Empower compounds both its GHK-Cu facial serum and scalp solution (§3) [3, compounding_data_sheet][4, compounding_data_sheet].
- **Community/"peptide-guide" convention (higher, less rigorous): ~1–3% by weight**, with several aggregators quoting 1–3% (and irritation reports said to rise above ~4%); one aggregator quotes a wider "2–10%" band [5, practitioner_protocol][6, practitioner_protocol]. These whole-percent figures are **far above** typical cosmetic-formulator use levels and should be read as community convention, not formulator standard.
- **Application frequency convention:** once or twice daily, applied to clean dry skin; scalp/hair use 1–2 mL of a 0.5% solution to affected areas once–twice daily [4, compounding_data_sheet][5, practitioner_protocol].

**Why the spread.** "1–3%" (community) vs "0.001–0.1%" (formulator) vs "0.5%" (compounded Rx) are not contradictory measurements of the same thing: formulator ppm figures describe the *active peptide* level a cosmetic chemist designs to, while many community "%" figures conflate the strength of a *raw solution* with the finished-product level, or simply over-state. The defensible statement is: **mainstream cosmetic formulation uses sub-0.1% (ppm-scale) active; compounded prescription topicals run ~0.5%; community guides quote 1–3%.**

## 2. Injectable / SC practitioner conventions (gray-market)

**No admissible named-prescriber protocol (name + venue + date) for injectable GHK-Cu was located** — see §2.2. What circulates is a convergent *community/aggregator* convention, tagged `practitioner_protocol` for dose/route/cycle only and carrying **no efficacy weight** (there is zero human injectable evidence).

### 2.1 The modal community injectable convention

- **Dose band:** **1–2 mg per dose** is modal; some aggregators state **1–3 mg** [5, practitioner_protocol][6, practitioner_protocol][7, practitioner_protocol].
- **Frequency:** either **daily** (often "5 days on / 2 days off") or **2–3× per week** for beginners [5, practitioner_protocol][6, practitioner_protocol].
- **A recurring named-pattern "30-day cycle":** 1 mg/day for days 1–15, then 2 mg/day for days 16–30, SC before bed, followed by a rest period — repeated across multiple aggregators but **not attributable to any named, dated prescriber** [6, practitioner_protocol][7, practitioner_protocol].
- **Cycle length / off-period:** **4–8 weeks** (some say 8–12 weeks) on, with **2–6 week** (commonly 4-week) off-periods; cycling is justified in community text by copper-overload / zinc-depletion concern — a *rationale*, not an efficacy or safety finding [5, practitioner_protocol][6, practitioner_protocol].
- **Route / site:** **subcutaneous**; abdomen ~2 inches lateral to the navel, rotated, also outer thigh / upper arm; "near the target area" for a specific wound/scar by convention [5, practitioner_protocol][7, practitioner_protocol].

All injectable figures are **convention/heuristic**: no human dose-finding study, indeed **no human study of any design**, exists for injectable GHK-Cu. Every number here is extrapolated from community practice and preclinical literature, not an established therapeutic dose.

## 4. Reconstitution / formulation notes

**Topical (formulation, admin only).** GHK-Cu is formulated into water-based serums/gels/lotions/creams; **add during cold-process (< 40 °C)** to preserve the copper complex; **optimal pH 5.0–7.0** to prevent copper-ion dissociation; the **Cu²⁺ ion is redox-active**, so stability (not toxicity) is the principal formulation concern. Documented **incompatibilities**: EDTA (turns green), octyl hydroxamic acid, allantoin, ergothioneine, carbomer, and skin-brightening agents (niacinamide, arbutin); also avoid combining with pure acids, retinoids, pure vitamin C, or strong antioxidants; carnosine can bind copper (purple discoloration). Store finished/raw cosmetic GHK-Cu refrigerated **4–8 °C** long-term, light-protected [1, compounding_data_sheet][2, compounding_data_sheet]. (Cross-ref: research report §formulation — liposomal encapsulation/microneedling are pursued because GHK-Cu is hydrophilic and crosses the stratum corneum poorly.)

**Injectable (gray-market reconstitution math, admin only).** Lyophilized GHK-Cu powder reconstituted with **bacteriostatic water**; a faint blue tint is normal (copper complex). Representative arithmetic that recurs across aggregators (purity/dilution bookkeeping only — no efficacy/safety implication):

## 5. Regulatory footing of the two channels (read with §1–§2)

The two channels diverge sharply and the asymmetry is itself a prescribing-practice fact:

- **Topical / non-injectable: regulated and compoundable.** "Copper Tripeptide-1" is an established INCI cosmetic ingredient (CIR 2018 "safe as used," topical only). On **2026-05-14 the FDA restored *non-injectable* GHK-Cu to 503A Category 1**, so compounders may work with GHK-Cu for **topical/non-injectable** preparations under the interim policy [11, regulatory]. This is the footing under which Empower compounds its 0.5% topical serum/scalp solution.
- **Injectable: adverse / unsettled.** Injectable GHK-Cu is **not FDA-approved** and is **not on the permissive Category-1 list**; secondary trackers report it as sitting in/around **Category 2** (injectable "safety risk," not legally compoundable for injection) or as removed-from-Category-2-on-nomination-withdrawal, with **PCAC consultation pending** (a 23–24 July 2026 meeting and a "before end of Feb 2027" consult are both reported) [11, regulatory]. Trackers sequence the events inconsistently; the **stable, defensible facts** are: **non-injectable = Category 1 (compoundable) as of 2026-05-14; injectable = not approved, not reliably compoundable, PCAC review pending**. (Cross-ref research report §4.2 — re-verify against the live FDA 503A bulks list before any time-sensitive use.) Vendors selling injectable GHK-Cu "for human use" operate outside FDA compliance; CIR "safe as used" does **not** extend to injection, which delivers bioavailable copper (Wilson's disease and other copper-metabolism disorders are contraindications per the research report).
