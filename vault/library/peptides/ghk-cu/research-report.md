# GHK-Cu (Copper Tripeptide-1, Gly-His-Lys:Cu²⁺)

## Metadata

- **class:** peptide (copper-chelating tripeptide / cosmetic active)
- **evidence_tier:** C — There is a genuinely independent, multi-lab in-vitro and animal literature for the *topical/dermal* uses (collagen synthesis, wound healing), one positive controlled human wound-healing RCT, and a formal cosmetic-safety determination — but the *human* evidence base is entirely **topical**, mostly small-n and industry-sponsored (including non-peer-reviewed conference posters), the single most rigorous cosmetic-adjacent RCT was **negative**, and the broad "anti-aging / gene-resetting / anti-cancer" narrative rests on computational predictions and reviews authored by the molecule's commercial originator. Topical-cosmetic evidence is modestly better than for the injectable use case, where it is effectively **zero**.
- **risk_tier:** experimental — Topical cosmetic use is CIR-rated "safe as used" (low risk), but **injectable/systemic GHK-Cu is an unapproved drug with no controlled human safety trial of any kind**; it delivers bioavailable copper, so it carries copper-overload risk and Wilson's disease (and other copper-metabolism disorders) is a contraindication. Not individually named on the WADA Prohibited List, but non-approved injectable use risks S0 catch-all capture (and arguably, but unsettled, S2 "similar biological effect").
- **status:** researching
- **last_verified:** 2026-06-19

> **Read this first — the honest evidence state.**
>
> GHK-Cu is the **copper(II) complex of the human plasma tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine (GHK; Gly-His-Lys)**. The molecule was discovered in 1973 by Loren Pickart as a plasma factor that made aged human liver tissue synthesize protein like younger tissue — but that original report was his **UCSF doctoral dissertation, not a peer-reviewed primary paper**. The widely circulated figure that endogenous plasma GHK falls from "~200 ng/mL at age 20 to ~80 ng/mL by age 60" traces only to that 1973 unpublished thesis; the sentence stating it in Pickart's own 2015 review carries **no citation marker at all**, and no independent peer-reviewed measurement of that age trajectory has been located. Treat it as a single-source, inventor-originated, grey-literature claim.
>
> The clinical picture is narrow. **Human evidence exists ONLY for TOPICAL use.** The cosmetic anti-aging "evidence" is mostly small-n and industry-sponsored, and its two flagship studies (a 71-woman facial cream study and a 41-woman eye-cream study) are **AAD conference posters that were never published as peer-reviewed journal articles**, retrievable only through a secondary review by the inventor. The two real GHK-Cu RCTs with reported results split **one positive** (Mulder 1994, diabetic neuropathic foot ulcers) and **one negative** (Miller 2006, post-CO₂-laser skin — no objective benefit, only patient satisfaction improved). There is **NO human study of any design — and no human RCT — for INJECTABLE or systemic GHK-Cu.** Injectable use is clinic/community practice unsupported by human trials.
>
> Much of the synthesizing "what GHK-Cu does" literature — roughly **70–85% of the review/claims layer** — originates from the **Pickart / Skin Biology commercial nexus** (the discoverer is also the patent-holder and the seller, via ProCyte and then Skin Biology). One heavily cited 2015 Pickart/Vasquez-Soltero/Margolina review even **declares "no conflict of interests"** despite all three authors being affiliated with the commercial entity Skin Biology. That said — and this distinction is the crux of the honest read — the **primary EXPERIMENTAL literature is mostly independent** (the Pickart-nexus share of efficacy primaries is only ≈0.11). The headline "GHK resets a large fraction of human genes / a third of the genome toward youth" (the specific ~31.2% figure and its thin 3-profile/single-dose basis are examined in §2.3) and the "anti-cancer" claims are **bioinformatic Connectivity-Map predictions in cancer cell lines**, not clinical — or in most cases even in-vivo — evidence.
>
> On regulation: "Copper Tripeptide-1" is an established **cosmetic** ingredient, CIR-rated "safe as used" (2018) for **topical** use only; **injectable GHK-Cu is an unapproved drug** in a gray-market/compounding limbo (FDA 503A status in flux as of April 2026, PCAC review pending Feb 2027). It is **not individually named** on the WADA Prohibited List, but injectable non-approved use risks **S0** capture and arguably an unsettled **S2** "similar biological effect" argument.
>
> This is a **goal-agnostic library entry**: operator/dosing-for-a-specific-person fields are deliberately left blank. The purpose is to state the evidence honestly, not to recommend use.

## 1. Identity, Chemistry & the Copper Complex

### 1.1 The free tripeptide (GHK)

GHK is the tripeptide **glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine** (sequence **Gly-His-Lys**), IUPAC name (2S)-6-amino-2-[[(2S)-2-[(2-aminoacetyl)amino]-3-(1H-imidazol-5-yl)propanoyl]amino]hexanoic acid. Its molecular formula is **C₁₄H₂₄N₆O₄**, molecular weight **340.38 g/mol**, **CAS 49557-75-7**, PubChem **CID 73587** [6]. GHK is endogenous to humans and is reported in plasma, saliva, and urine [3].

GHK was **first isolated in 1973 by Loren Pickart** from the albumin fraction of human plasma as the activity that caused aged human liver tissue to synthesize protein in the manner of younger tissue; the activity was identified as the tripeptide Gly-His-Lys with strong affinity for Cu(II), readily forming the GHK-Cu complex [1, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review]. **Provenance caveat (important):** the original 1973 report is **Pickart's University of California, San Francisco doctoral dissertation** — a PhD thesis, not a primary peer-reviewed Nature paper, despite that misattribution appearing in some secondary/vendor write-ups [2, mechanism_review].

### 1.2 The GHK-Cu copper(II) complex

GHK-Cu (INCI name **"Copper Tripeptide-1"**; also called **prezatide copper**) is the **1:1 Cu(II) complex** of GHK, molecular formula commonly written **C₁₄H₂₂CuN₆O₄** (the dianionic peptide chelating one Cu²⁺ ion), **CAS 89030-95-5** [6, regulatory][7, regulatory]. There is a deliberate **CAS split** to keep in mind throughout: **49557-75-7 is the free peptide**, **89030-95-5 is the copper complex** — these are not interchangeable, and a powder labeled "GHK" (copper-free) is a chemically different substance from "GHK-Cu" (copper-loaded).

In the Cu(II)·GHK complex, copper is bound by a **tridentate (3N) donor set** — the N-terminal (glycine) α-amino nitrogen, the deprotonated amide nitrogen of the Gly–His peptide bond, and the histidine side-chain imidazole nitrogen — forming **two fused chelate rings** with **square-planar geometry**, the fourth equatorial site occupied by a water molecule or other monodentate/ternary ligand [4, in_vitro]. This coordination was characterized by potentiometric titration and visible-absorption spectrophotometry in 0.15 M NaCl at 25 °C in the foundational physicochemical study [11, in_vitro].

A **2:1 peptide:copper species, (GHK)₂-Cu**, also exists in solution alongside the 1:1 GHK-Cu, and both — together with free GHK — are the species relevant to membrane transport (see §1.5) [5, in_vitro][1, mechanism_review].

### 1.3 The two copper-binding constants (kept distinct — do NOT conflate)

Two different "binding constant" numbers circulate for GHK and Cu²⁺, and conflating them is a recurring error. They are **different quantities**:

- **Overall / absolute stability constant: log₁₀ ≈ 16.44.** Pickart's reviews quote GHK's copper-binding constant as **log₁₀ = 16.44**, essentially equal to serum albumin's high-affinity site (**log₁₀ = 16.2**) and far above the dipeptide Gly-His-Cu complex (**log₁₀ ≈ 8.68**). This near-equality with albumin is the canonical rationale for the claim that GHK can both hold copper in circulation and abstract Cu²⁺ from albumin [1, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review]. The constant itself is a physicochemical (potentiometric/spectrophotometric) measurement [11, in_vitro]; the "delivery to higher-affinity cellular targets" framing layered on top of it is **interpretive, not a measured flux**.
- **Conditional formation constant at physiological pH: log cK(7.4) ≈ 12.62.** Independent tier-1 inorganic-chemistry work directly re-measured a **conditional formation constant of log cK(7.4) ≈ 12.62 at pH 7.4** [4, in_vitro].

For physiological-pH statements, the directly re-measured tier-1 conditional value (**log cK(7.4) ≈ 12.62**, [4]) is the more defensible figure; the **log₁₀ ≈ 16.44** value should be flagged as a Pickart-review-quoted overall/absolute constant. The two are kept firewalled throughout this report.

A note on the complex molecular formula: it is represented inconsistently across registries and vendors (free complex vs. acetate vs. TFA salt). The **CAS 89030-95-5** identifier is solid; the bare formula **C₁₄H₂₂CuN₆O₄** is the deprotonated 1:1 complex and is treated as a tier-2 representation [7, regulatory].

### 1.4 Chemistry / formulation

GHK and GHK-Cu are **hydrophilic** molecules, and this hydrophilicity is the fundamental barrier to passive transport across the stratum corneum — the reason delivery strategies (liposomes, microneedling, penetration enhancers) are pursued for topical products [8, in_vitro]. **Liposomal encapsulation** produces stable GHK-Cu-loaded liposomes (~100 nm) with prolonged release (**64.7–84.9% over 24 h** vs. near-complete release of free peptide within 4 h) and improved cell viability versus free peptide, proposed to improve delivery to deeper skin layers [8, in_vitro]. Native-GHK preformulation/physicochemical characterization (solubility, stability, partitioning) for dermal delivery has also been performed [10, in_vitro].

A formulation caveat specific to the copper complex: the **Cu²⁺ ion is redox-active**, so the complex degrades with oxygen, light, and incompatible actives — **stability, not toxicity, is the principal cosmetic-formulation concern** [60, vendor_label].

### 1.5 Skin penetration / PK

The penetration evidence is more favorable than the common "copper peptides don't penetrate" framing, with an important qualifier — it is *in vitro through human skin, not in vivo systemic PK*:

- In an **ex vivo human-skin** study (glycyl-histidyl-lysine cuprate diacetate, 0.68% aqueous, flow-through diffusion cells, infinite dose), copper as the tripeptide **permeated dermatomed human skin** with a permeability coefficient of **2.43 ± 0.51 × 10⁻⁴ cm/h**; **136.2 ± 17.5 µg/cm²** copper permeated over 48 h and **97 ± 6.6 µg/cm²** was retained as a stratum-corneum depot; the authors concluded copper as the tripeptide was delivered "in potentially therapeutically effective amounts" [9, in_vitro].
- In a stratum-corneum **model-membrane (liposome/Flynn-cell) ESI-MS study**, free GHK and its copper complexes GHK-Cu and (GHK)₂-Cu were the species able to migrate across the membrane; the work characterizes the mechanism of Cu²⁺ transport by the peptide and the pH-dependent speciation of the complexes [5, in_vitro].

These two framings — "delivers therapeutic copper amounts through skin" [9] and "hydrophilicity is a barrier" [8] — are reconcilable (a stratum-corneum depot plus slow flux, enhanced by liposomes/microneedling); neither "fully penetrates" nor "does not penetrate" should be overstated.

**Honest PK limit:** **no admissible peer-reviewed human systemic pharmacokinetic study** (plasma half-life, Cmax, clearance after topical/SC/IV dosing) was located for GHK-Cu. Widely repeated half-life figures (e.g., "~0.5–1 h after IV") appear only in vendor/blog material and are **not grounded here** [route-extrapolation across topical vs. systemic].

### 1.6 Endogenous GHK and the age-related decline figure

Pickart's reviews state that plasma GHK is **"about 200 ng/mL (10⁻⁷ M) at age 20"** and **"declines to 80 ng/mL by age 60"** [1, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review]. This figure is the cornerstone of GHK-Cu's anti-aging marketing — and it is **not independently verified**.

The verified source situation: in the 2015 review, the 200→80 ng/mL statement appears **uncited in the introduction — the sentence carries no citation marker at all**. Its plausible (but inferred, not stated) origin is **Pickart's own 1973 UCSF doctoral dissertation** [2, mechanism_review], which is the review's reference [1]. The same figures recur across his later reviews without an independent primary measurement, and **no independent peer-reviewed quantitation of this age trajectory** (a cohort with methods, n, and assay) was located [1, mechanism_review]. Treat the decline figure as a **single-source, inventor-originated, grey-literature claim**.

## 2. Mechanism of Action

GHK-Cu's proposed mechanisms fall into five linked buckets: (1) copper carriage/homeostasis, (2) ECM remodeling by fibroblasts, (3) broad gene-expression modulation, (4) antioxidant / anti-inflammatory activity, and (5) angiogenic, stem-cell and wound-healing signaling. The crucial honest caveat governs everything below: **the strongest mechanistic data are in vitro and animal; human evidence is largely limited to skin-appearance endpoints, so almost every cellular mechanism here is *extrapolated to humans rather than demonstrated in them*** [12, mechanism_review]. A second structural caveat: a large share of the "GHK resets gene expression" narrative traces to the Pickart group and rests on a small, single-platform microarray dataset (§2.3).

### 2.1 Copper delivery / homeostasis

The canonical mechanistic rationale is that GHK's copper affinity (**log K ≈ 16.44**, essentially equal to albumin's **16.2** and far above the dipeptide GH-Cu's **8.68**) lets it hold copper in circulation yet still hand it off to higher-affinity cellular targets [1, mechanism_review][11, in_vitro][12, mechanism_review]. The affinity itself is a measured physicochemical quantity [11]; the "delivery to higher-affinity targets" framing is **interpretive, not a measured flux** — there are no verified cellular copper-delivery kinetic/flux data; log K establishes affinity, not transport [11, in_vitro]. The square-planar 3N coordination (§1.2) was established by potentiometric titration and visible-absorption spectrophotometry [11, in_vitro].

### 2.2 ECM remodeling — collagen, elastin, GAG/proteoglycan, and the copper-driven MMP-2 effect

This is the **most independently grounded** mechanistic bucket:

- GHK-Cu **stimulates collagen synthesis in cultured fibroblasts at extremely low concentrations**: stimulation begins between **10⁻¹² and 10⁻¹¹ M**, peaks near **10⁻⁹ M**, and occurs **independently of any change in cell number** — i.e., a per-cell synthetic effect, not proliferation. The authors note a GHK triplet exists in the α2(I) chain of type I collagen, suggesting wound proteases could liberate endogenous GHK in situ [13, in_vitro].
- GHK-Cu acts on ECM **turnover, not just deposition**: in fibroblast culture it raised **MMP-2 (gelatinase A)** secretion and mRNA, and *also* increased the inhibitors **TIMP-1 and TIMP-2** — consistent with controlled remodeling rather than uncontrolled degradation. **Notably, the MMP-2 effect was reproduced by copper ions but NOT by GHK peptide alone, implicating the copper moiety** [15, in_vitro]. Copper additionally serves as the obligate cofactor for **lysyl oxidase** (the enzyme that cross-links collagen and elastin) — a homeostatic link between buckets (1) and (2), though this is a cofactor-supply *inference* rather than a directly measured GHK-Cu → lysyl-oxidase activation experiment [12, mechanism_review].
- In **3D skin-equivalent / organotypic** models, GHK-Cu increased keratinocyte proliferation and raised integrin α6/β1 and p63 positivity (p63 is a putative epidermal stem-cell marker) while making basal cells more cuboidal — interpreted as enhanced "stemness," with integrin changes confirmed by Western blot [19, in_vitro]. This is in-vitro/organotypic, not in-vivo human.

The **copper-vs-peptide attribution is unsettled**: MMP-2 induction was driven by copper, not the peptide [15]; some antioxidant quenching (4-HNE, §2.4) was the peptide *without* copper [12]. "GHK-Cu mechanisms" are a mix of copper-carrier effects and peptide-intrinsic effects, and few studies cleanly dissect the two.

### 2.3 Gene-expression modulation — the "resets a third of the genome" claim, represented carefully

The headline figure: using the Broad Institute **Connectivity Map (cMap)**, GHK is reported to change expression by ≥50% in **~31.2% of assayed human genes**, increasing expression in ~59% and suppressing it in ~41% of affected genes [12, mechanism_review][16, in_vitro].

**The critical basis behind that number** must be stated alongside it: it derives from **only 3 cMap microarray profiles — 2 from PC3 (prostate) and 1 from MCF7 (breast) cancer cell lines, all at a single 1 µM dose** on the Affymetrix Human Genome U133A array (the standard U133A spec is ~22,277 probe sets / ~13,000 genes; [16] specifies the platform and MAS5 processing but does not itself enumerate the probe-set count), **with no independent replication, no dose-response, and no non-cancer validation reported** [16, in_vitro]. The "gene-resetting toward a younger profile" / "a third of the genome" language is the **Pickart group's interpretive synthesis of this small dataset**, not a separate experimental result [12, mechanism_review][32, mechanism_review] (see §7.5).

The two most-cited downstream "reset" claims are **external cMap-prediction studies, not GHK assays**: (a) Hong et al. 2010 found GHK among the most active of 1,309 cMap compounds at reversing a 54-gene (74-probe-set) "metastasis-prone" signature in early colorectal cancer, predicted active at ~1 µM — **a prediction with no GHK treatment performed** [17, in_vitro]; (b) Campbell et al. 2012 identified a 127-gene emphysema-severity signature and predicted GHK would reverse it, then showed in cultured human fibroblasts that GHK recapitulated TGF-β-induced expression, organized the actin cytoskeleton, and raised integrin-β1 [18, in_vitro]. Both are computational predictions with limited wet-lab confirmation (see §7.6).

### 2.4 Antioxidant / anti-inflammatory

GHK shows **antioxidant activity** by quenching reactive carbonyl/lipid-peroxidation products: GHK (the **peptide alone, not the Cu complex**) was shown to quench α,β-4-hydroxy-trans-2-nonenal (4-HNE) [12, mechanism_review]. The Pickart reviews additionally attribute to GHK the blocking of Cu²⁺-dependent LDL oxidation and inhibition of iron release from ferritin, but those specific quantitative figures are carried in the secondary reviews citing older primary work that could not be independently verified within scope; they are reported here **only as review-level claims and do not ground numbers** [12, mechanism_review].

In an **animal model of acute lung injury**, GHK blocked activation of NF-κB p65 and p38 MAPK, raised superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity, and lowered TNF and IL-6 — an anti-inflammatory signature reported via the 2018 review citing a mouse study; treat as animal-level evidence summarized in review [12, mechanism_review].

### 2.5 Angiogenesis / stem-cell / wound-healing signaling

GHK and related peptides are described as **biphasic in vessel signaling** — promoting new-vessel outgrowth early in healing (linked to SPARC proteolysis) and restraining it later [12, mechanism_review]. No quantitative VEGF induction is reported in the reviewed mechanism sources; the angiogenic claim here is **mechanistic/qualitative, not a measured vessel-count or VEGF-fold result** [12, mechanism_review]. (Quantitative VEGF/vessel data do appear in some preclinical efficacy work — and a contradicting null — in §7.)

### 2.6 Honest in-vitro → human extrapolation caveat

Human mechanism evidence is essentially absent. Collagen/elastin/GAG, gene-expression, antioxidant, angiogenic and stem-cell mechanisms are demonstrated in vitro or in rodents; human data in the reviewed literature are skin-appearance endpoints (covered in §3, not §2). The honest split: **copper-binding and ECM mechanisms are independently grounded** (Lau & Sarkar binding chemistry [11], Maquart collagen [13], Siméon MMP-2 [15], Kang organotypic [19] — all non-Pickart or Reims-academic primaries); the broad **"gene-resetting / anti-aging" mechanism is predominantly Pickart-group and dataset-thin** (the 3-profile cMap re-analysis [16] plus narrative reviews [12, 32]).

## 3. Human Clinical Evidence (TOPICAL only)

**The single most important honest finding, stated up front and repeated:** the entire human evidence base for GHK-Cu is **TOPICAL** (cosmetic and wound-healing). There is **no human study of any design — and no human RCT — for INJECTABLE or systemic GHK-Cu.** The strongest cosmetic claims rest on **industry-sponsored conference posters and a book/review chapter that were never published as peer-reviewed journal articles**, mostly from small-n studies. The two best-documented *controlled* human studies are wound-healing and post-laser, not anti-aging — and the post-laser one was **negative** on its primary endpoint.

### 3.2 The cosmetic anti-aging studies (Tier-3 — posters, not peer-reviewed)

**A 12-week topical GHK-Cu facial cream study in 71 photoaged women** reported improved skin density/thickness, reduced laxity, improved clarity, and reduced fine lines/wrinkle depth [39, anecdote_aggregate→open_label]. Whether it was placebo/vehicle-controlled or randomized is **not establishable** — only secondary summaries exist. Authors Leyden, Stephens, Finkey, Appa, Barkovic carry Neutrogena/J&J cosmetic-science affiliations; it was presented as an **American Academy of Dermatology 60th Annual Meeting poster (Feb 2002)** — a **conference abstract with no PMID/DOI**. Magnitude figures circulating online (e.g., "67% wrinkle-volume reduction," "20–30% firmness") trace to **vendor/blog text and are not confirmed by any retrievable primary source** — do not cite them. The only verifiable trail is the peer-reviewed Pickart & Margolina 2018 review [12] (its ref 14).

**A 12-week topical GHK-Cu eye cream study in 41 women** with periorbital photodamage reported performing better than placebo and a vitamin-K cream for lines/wrinkles, overall appearance, and skin density/thickness [40, anecdote_aggregate→open_label]. Same AAD-poster-class companion work; **no PMID/DOI**; industry; verifiable only via the secondary review [12] (its ref 15). **Tier-3 weight.**

**A 1-month pilot study (n=20, thigh skin)** found a copper-binding peptide cream increased dermal procollagen in **70% of subjects vs 50% for vitamin C, 40% for tretinoin, and 50% for melatonin** [41, open_label]. This is a real published primary study but with **no PMID** (published in *Disease Management and Clinical Outcomes*, a non-PubMed-indexed journal; DOI 10.1016/S1088-3371(98)00011-4); it is non-randomized, unblinded, with internal active comparators (no inert vehicle), a histologic surrogate endpoint, and tiny n. **Tier-2 (low).**

### 3.3 The two controlled RCTs (one positive, one negative)

**Positive — Mulder 1994 (diabetic foot ulcers).** A **multicenter, randomized, evaluator-blinded, vehicle-controlled trial** of topical GHK-Cu gel ("lamin Gel") in diabetic neuropathic foot ulcers — **the highest-quality controlled human design in the GHK-Cu literature** — reported median plantar-ulcer closure **98.5% (GHK-Cu) vs 60.8% (vehicle)**, p<0.05, closure ~3× faster, and **infection 7% vs 34%**, p<0.05 [42, rct]. Caveats: sample size is not stated in the abstract; the authorship is **ProCyte Corp.-associated (industry/COI)**; it is 30 years old; and it is **wound-healing, NOT anti-aging/cosmetic**. Peer-reviewed (*Wound Repair Regen* 1994; PMID 17147644).

**Negative — Miller 2006 (post-CO₂-laser skin).** A **randomized, blinded-evaluator** comparison of a post-laser skincare regimen with vs. without GHK-Cu (objective erythema by computer analysis + blinded evaluators; n=13 completers) found **NO statistically significant difference in erythema resolution, wrinkle, or appearance** between groups; **only patient-reported satisfaction was significantly higher** in the GHK-Cu group [43, rct]. This is the most rigorous cosmetic-adjacent RCT and it **did not confirm an objective benefit** — cite it whenever the cosmetic claims are presented. Peer-reviewed (*Arch Facial Plast Surg* 2006; PMID 16847171).

### 3.4 The registered (no-results) trial

A **Phase 2, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, split-wound** study of topical GHK-Cu gel **0.1% w/w** once daily vs. vehicle for acute-wound re-epithelialization (quadruple masking; est. n=60; primary outcome = time to complete re-epithelialization over 21 days) is registered as **NCT07437586** (Sponsor: Hudson Biotech; status Recruiting, start Feb 2026). It carries **no result weight** — listed for completeness [44, rct/registry].

### 3.5 Two boundary findings (state these plainly)

- **No injectable/systemic human study exists.** A clinicaltrials.gov query (GHK-Cu / copper tripeptide / glycyl-histidyl-lysine) returns no injectable/systemic GHK-Cu interventional trial; the only directly relevant registered GHK-Cu study is topical (NCT07437586). All published human GHK-Cu studies are topical. Injectable/"subcutaneous" GHK-Cu use is clinic/community practice **without supporting human RCTs** [44, rct/registry].
- **The "GHK hair RCT" (Lee 2016) is NOT GHK-Cu evidence.** Lee WJ et al. 2016 (*Ann Dermatol*; PMID 27489425; n=45, male pattern hair loss) was an RCT of **ALAVAX — a complex of 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) + glycyl-histidyl-lysine peptide**, not the GHK-*Cu* copper complex, and the active agent is the ALA/peptide combination. **Do not cite as standalone GHK-Cu efficacy** [45, rct — off-target, EXCLUDED]. No verifiable human RCT exists for topical GHK-Cu monotherapy for hair growth.

### 3.6 Bottom line for the section

Human evidence for GHK-Cu is **modest, topical-only, small-n, heavily industry-linked, and built more on conference posters than peer-reviewed RCTs.** The two real GHK-Cu RCTs with reported results split **1 positive (diabetic ulcers) / 1 negative (post-laser cosmetic)**. For injectable/systemic GHK-Cu, the correct statement is unequivocal: **no human study of any design exists.**

## 4. Regulatory Status

### 4.1 Cosmetic ingredient status (topical)

**"Copper Tripeptide-1" is an established INCI cosmetic ingredient name**, functioning as a skin-conditioning agent. The **Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel (2018)** reviewed Tripeptide-1, Hexapeptide-12, their metal salts (which include Copper Tripeptide-1 / GHK-Cu) and derivatives, and concluded these ingredients are **"safe in cosmetics in the present practices of use and concentration"** (*Int J Toxicol* 2018) [46, regulatory]. The panel's rationale was that "the low use concentrations and negative safety test data reviewed obviate any concerns," with typical use concentrations reported as **< 10 ppm** for the broader ingredient family [46, regulatory]. In the U.S. this is **cosmetic-regulated, not an approved drug**; in the EU it is regulated as a cosmetic ingredient under the EU Cosmetics Regulation framework [46, regulatory]. **CIR safety applies to topical/cosmetic use only and does NOT extend to injection.**

### 4.2 FDA drug / compounding status (time-sensitive, mid-2026)

GHK-Cu is **NOT an FDA-approved drug** for any therapeutic indication [53, regulatory][54, regulatory]. Under FDA's **503A bulk-drug-substances compounding** framework, in **April 2026** the FDA **removed GHK-Cu from Category 1 (non-injectable route)** because the nominator **withdrew the nomination**, and GHK-Cu sits in a regulatory gray area pending **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC)** consultation scheduled **before the end of February 2027** [53, regulatory][54, regulatory]. Net effect: **injectable GHK-Cu cannot be relied upon as legally compoundable**, and sources selling injectable GHK-Cu "for human use" operate outside FDA compliance [54, regulatory]. Secondary trackers sequence the 503A events inconsistently (Category 1 vs 2 wording; one aggregator reports a brief restoration); the **stable, defensible facts** are: **not FDA-approved, not reliably compoundable for injection, PCAC review pending Feb 2027.** Re-verify against the live FDA 503A bulks list before any time-sensitive use.

### 4.3 WADA anti-doping status (NOT named + S0/S2 caveat)

- **Not individually named.** GHK-Cu / copper tripeptide / "copper" / "GHK" is **NOT individually named anywhere on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List**, including the **S2** (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics) section. The named S2.2 growth-factor examples are thymosin-β4 / TB-500 and growth factors affecting muscle/tendon/ligament synthesis, vascularization, etc. — GHK-Cu is not among them [55, regulatory][56, regulatory][58, regulatory]. (The official WADA PDF could not be machine-parsed; absence verified via the official S2/S0 definitional text and corroborating anti-doping analyses — a flagged residual limitation.)
- **S0 catch-all exposure.** The **S0 (Non-Approved Substances)** clause prohibits, at all times, "any pharmacological substance which is not addressed by any of the subsequent sections of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use" [57, regulatory]. Because GHK-Cu has no human-therapeutic regulatory approval, an athlete using **non-approved injectable/systemic GHK-Cu could be captured under S0**, even though GHK-Cu is not enumerated [57, regulatory][58, regulatory]. Topical cosmetic use of an approved cosmetic ingredient is not the S0 target.
- **Plausible-but-unsettled S2 "similar biological effect" argument.** Beyond the not-named + S0 analysis, an anti-doping authority *could also* argue S2 capture on a non-enumerated basis: S2.2 covers "growth factors and growth factor modulators" plus substances with "a similar chemical structure or similar biological effect(s)" to the listed growth factors [58, regulatory]. GHK-Cu's documented regenerative/angiogenic, growth-factor-like signaling could arguably be read as a "similar biological effect," even though GHK-Cu is a small copper-bound tripeptide, not a peptide hormone or growth factor proper [12, mechanism_review]. **This is presented as an argument an authority COULD make, NOT a definite or settled ban** — GHK-Cu is not enumerated, no published WADA determination or sanction applies the S2 "similar effect" clause to it, and the test is interpretive and untested here.
- **Bottom line for athletes:** GHK-Cu is **not a named prohibited substance**, but injectable/systemic non-approved GHK-Cu carries **S0** catch-all exposure, and a secondary (unsettled, non-enumerated) **S2** "similar biological effect" argument is plausible — still distinct from explicitly listed growth-factor peptides (e.g., TB-500), which are flatly banned by name under S2.

## 5. Concentration / Commercial-COI Audit (FIRST-CLASS)

This audit is surfaced as a first-class concern because GHK-Cu's interpretive literature is unusually concentrated and commercially conflicted — but the concentration is *layer-specific*, and conflating the two layers is the central analytical trap.

### 5.1 The discoverer–seller–reviewer triple role

The GHK-Cu **review and claims-synthesis literature is anchored by Loren Pickart, PhD (1938–2023)** — the discoverer of GHK in human plasma (PhD thesis, UCSF, 1973) — who was **also the commercial seller** of GHK-Cu products. Pickart founded **ProCyte Corp.** (copper-peptide skincare, taken to NASDAQ) and then **Skin Biology, Inc.** (Bellevue, WA; retail GHK-Cu cosmetics at reverseskinaging.com) [63, regulatory/biographical][64, vendor_label]. His principal review co-author **Anna Margolina** is affiliated with the **Skin Biology R&D Department** — the seller's own research arm [12, 59, mechanism_review]. This is a **textbook discoverer–seller–reviewer triple role**: the dominant secondary literature is authored by a party with a direct, ongoing financial interest in GHK-Cu sales, and this COI must be carried forward into every efficacy claim that rests on a Pickart/Margolina review.

**The denied-COI disclosure gap:** at least one heavily cited Pickart/Vasquez-Soltero/Margolina review (BioMed Res Int 2015, PMID 26236730) states **"The authors declare that there is no conflict of interests regarding the publication of this paper"** despite all three authors being affiliated with the commercial entity Skin Biology [1, mechanism_review] — i.e., the affiliation is printed but the COI is explicitly *denied*.

### 5.2 Two layers, two very different shares

The share differs dramatically between the *story* of GHK-Cu and the *experiments* on GHK-Cu:

- **(a) Review / claims-synthesis layer (the "story"): Pickart-nexus share ≈ 70–85%.** The most-cited, broadest "what GHK-Cu does" reviews are theirs — Pickart & Margolina 2018 (*Int J Mol Sci*, PMID 29986520) [12]; Pickart/Vasquez-Soltero/Margolina 2015 (*BioMed Res Int*, PMID 26236730) [1]; the 2012 cognitive/oxidative-stress review (PMC3359723) [3]; the 2014 "Resetting the Human Genome to Health" review (PMID 25302294) [32]; plus the gene-expression and anti-cancer reviews. The synthesized narrative is **effectively single-source** — this clears the ≥70% concentration-audit gate for the review layer.
- **(b) Primary experimental layer (in vitro / animal / clinical): Pickart-nexus share ≈ 0.11 (range 0.07–0.25).** Across the de-duplicated efficacy primaries (~28 distinct studies), the Pickart-authored/affiliated cluster is only ~2–3 entries — chiefly the Reims collaboration **Maquart, Pickart et al. 1988** (where Pickart is one co-author of a French academic group) [13] and the **Pickart/Margolina 2021 OBM cMap** re-analysis [16], with the **JCI-1993 rat paper** [14] counted nexus-*adjacent* via ProCyte (Patt/Trachy) co-authorship as a conservative over-count. The remaining ~22+ are genuinely independent: the Reims biochemistry group (Maquart, Siméon, Wegrowski, Borel), Korean dermatology groups, Chinese hydrogel/scaffold groups, Polish liposome groups, the Koch lab (Stanford), the Uludag veterinary group, Minnesota, Auckland, and orthopaedic groups [20–30].

### 5.3 The honest net read

The concentration gate is **TRIPPED for the claims/review layer (~70–85%, single-source narrative + denied COI), NOT tripped for the primary-evidence layer (≈0.11, genuinely independent).** GHK-Cu has a **reasonably distributed primary literature but a highly concentrated, commercially-conflicted interpretive/review layer.** Any synthesis that leans on the reviews inherits *both* the concentration and the COI; any synthesis that leans on the independent primaries does not. (Note: counting the Pickart-co-authored Maquart 1988 toward the nexus slightly *inflates* the nexus primary share — the true independent-primary share may be marginally higher, a conservative direction for the COI flag. The 70–85% and ≈0.11 figures are calibrated estimates from a PubMed wound-healing scan plus the surfaced comprehensive-review set, not a full bibliometric census; the *direction* is robust, the exact percentages are bounded ranges.)

## 6. Why This Lands at evidence_tier C

**evidence_tier C** reflects a deliberately split assessment:

**What pulls it up to C (rather than D):**

- A **genuinely independent, multi-lab in-vitro literature** for the core ECM mechanism — collagen synthesis replicated across Reims, Auckland, and the Koch (Stanford) labs in different cell systems [13, 21, 23, 24]; MMP-2/TIMP remodeling [15]; organotypic stemness [19].
- **In-vivo wound benefit replicated across species and independent labs** — rat (Reims [14]) and rabbit (Uludag [26, 27]).
- **One positive controlled human RCT** (Mulder 1994 diabetic ulcers [42]) — a real multicenter, randomized, evaluator-blinded, vehicle-controlled design. This is a genuine pillar but a **qualified** one, not a robust standalone: sample size is **not stated** in the abstract (limited/unverifiable n), it is **ProCyte-sponsored (industry COI)**, and it is **~30 years old** and unreplicated by a second positive RCT — exactly the caveats carried in §3.3.
- A **formal cosmetic-safety determination** (CIR 2018 "safe as used" [46]) and tier-1 human ex-vivo penetration data [9].

**What holds it down at C (and no higher):**

- The **human evidence is entirely topical**; the flagship cosmetic studies are **non-peer-reviewed posters** [39, 40]; samples are tiny; sponsorship/COI is pervasive.
- The single most rigorous cosmetic-adjacent RCT (Miller 2006 [43]) was **negative** on its objective primary endpoint.
- The animal wound signal is **model-dependent** — contradicted by the irradiated-rat-flap null (Parker 2013, §7.3) [28].
- The broad **anti-aging / gene-resetting / anti-cancer narrative is computational and inventor-authored**, not clinical (§2.3, §7.5–7.6).
- There is **zero human evidence of any kind for the injectable/systemic use case.**

**The key tier nuance:** topical-cosmetic evidence is **modestly better** than the injectable evidence, which is **none**. The C tier is generous to the topical case and would be charitable-to-the-point-of-overstatement if applied to systemic use.

## 7. Preclinical & In-Vitro Efficacy

### 7.1 Fibroblast collagen / ECM synthesis in vitro (multi-lab replicated)

- **Collagen synthesis** stimulated at 10⁻¹²–10⁻¹¹ M onset, peak ~10⁻⁹ M, independent of cell number [13, in_vitro — human fibroblasts].
- GHK-Cu increases mRNA and synthesis of **multiple ECM components beyond collagen** — dermatan sulfate / chondroitin-sulfate proteoglycans (decorin) and GAGs — in dermal fibroblast culture [20, in_vitro].
- **MMP-2 ↑ with TIMP-1/TIMP-2 ↑** (controlled remodeling; MMP-2 effect copper-driven) [15, in_vitro].
- In **human adult dermal fibroblasts (HDFa)**, GHK-Cu at 0.01/1/100 nM enhanced collagen and elastin, **peaking at 1 nM and declining at 100 nM** (non-monotonic/biphasic), tracking an increased TIMP:MMP ratio [21, in_vitro].
- **Combination effect:** with hyaluronic acid (1:9 GHK-Cu:low-MW HA), collagen IV rose **~25.4-fold** in the fibroblast test and **~2.03-fold** in an ex-vivo human-skin test (also ↑collagen I and VII) — **effect size attaches to the COMBINATION, not GHK-Cu alone** [22, in_vitro].
- In **irradiated human fibroblasts**, GHK-Cu accelerated recovery toward normal population-doubling kinetics and raised early bFGF and VEGF (Koch lab, Stanford) [23, in_vitro]; an independent confirmation extended this in irradiated fibroblasts [24, in_vitro].

**Replication note:** in-vitro collagen/ECM stimulation is **genuinely multi-lab** — Reims [13, 15, 20], Auckland [21], and Koch/Stanford [23, 24] across different cell systems.

### 7.2 Dermal wound-healing animal models (replicates rat + rabbit)

- **Rat subcutaneous wire-mesh wound chambers:** locally injected GHK-Cu produced a **concentration-dependent** increase in chamber dry weight, DNA, total protein, collagen, and GAG content; collagen synthesis ~2× non-collagen protein; ↑type I/III collagen mRNA; ↑dermatan sulfate; a control tripeptide had no effect (specificity) [14, animal — rat; **n not stated in abstract**]. A companion rat report covers MMP/TIMP modulation in vivo [25, animal — rat; n not confirmed].
- **Rabbit full-thickness open wounds (n=18 New Zealand rabbits; 3 groups: GHK-Cu / zinc oxide / untreated):** topical GHK-Cu gave a significantly smaller unhealed wound area than zinc oxide on day 7, and than untreated control on days 7, 14, and 21 [26, animal — rabbit, n=18]. A same-group follow-up (± helium-neon laser) reported accelerated healing and blood-vessel formation [27, animal — rabbit].

### 7.3 The contradicting null (bounds the wound claims)

**NEGATIVE / NULL RESULT — irradiated rat dorsal-flap model** (2×8 cm flap; topical GHK-Cu gel vs control ointment twice daily ×10 days): GHK-Cu showed **NO significant difference** in flap ischemia (mean ischemic area 5.0 vs 3.8 cm²), blood-vessel number/area, or VEGF expression (VEGF intensity 0.34 vs 0.54, P=0.169) versus control [28, animal — rat]. This bounds the wound-healing claims: **benefit is not universal across models** — irradiated random-pattern flap viability was not rescued. The overall animal signal is therefore **model-dependent**: positive in rat chambers and rabbit open wounds, null in the irradiated flap. An independent orthopaedic animal study points the same way — GHK-Cu(II) only **"transiently improved" healing outcome** in a tendon/ligament-repair model, i.e., the benefit was not durable [62, animal — independent group]. Taken together, the wound/repair signal is **real but not robust**: it varies by tissue model and, where positive, can be transient rather than sustained.

### 7.4 Hair (thin; analog-based)

Hair preclinical is **genuinely thin for bare GHK-Cu**:

- The best ex-vivo human-follicle study tests the **ANALOG AHK-Cu (alanyl-histidyl-lysine-Cu²⁺), NOT GHK-Cu** — it stimulated elongation of human hair follicles ex vivo and dermal-papilla-cell proliferation at 10⁻¹²–10⁻⁹ M and shifted Bcl-2/Bax toward survival [29, in_vitro/ex_vivo — human follicle, **analog**]. Do not transfer this number to GHK-Cu without qualification.
- A GHK-Cu **ionic-liquid microemulsion** study reported ~3.18× increased cutaneous penetration in pig skin and, in mice, hair-follicle anagen entry by ~day 6 with higher hair density than minoxidil by day 28 (↑β-catenin/VEGF, Wnt pathway) — but the **test article is GHK-Cu-IN-microemulsion, not bare peptide**, and mouse n was not in the retrieved record [30, animal — mouse + ex_vivo pig skin]. There is **no independently-replicated bare-GHK-Cu mouse anagen study** in the retrieved set.

### 7.5 Systemic / anti-aging claims (honestly framed as cMap predictions / reviews)

The "GHK resets thousands of genes (~4,000 / a third of the genome) toward a youthful state" and multi-organ (skin/lung/bone/liver/stomach/GI/nervous-system) framing originates from **Pickart/Margolina narrative reviews** assembling **cMap transcriptional fingerprints** (the 3-profile, single-dose, cancer-cell-line dataset of §2.3) plus scattered primaries into a unifying hypothesis [32, mechanism_review][12, mechanism_review][16, in_vitro]. **No anti-aging clinical endpoint (lifespan, biological-age biomarker) is demonstrated; no controlled aging study exists.** The breadth of the claim is inversely related to the depth of the per-organ evidence.

The independent systemic signals that *do* exist are limited and mostly small:

- **Lung/COPD (the strongest independent systemic claim):** in explanted human lung, 127 genes tracked emphysema severity; cMap flagged GHK to reverse the signature, and follow-up *in vitro* work showed GHK (0.1–10 nM) on cultured human lung fibroblasts from **COPD patients (n=5)** vs former smokers without COPD (n=5) recapitulated TGF-β-induced expression, organized actin, raised integrin-β1, and restored collagen-I contraction [18, in_vitro — independent of Pickart, tier-1 journal]. Small-n human-cell culture, not an animal emphysema model or human trial.
- **Peripheral nerve:** GHK in collagen guidance tubes improved early peripheral-nerve regeneration (↑NGF/NT-3/NT-4, Schwann-cell recruitment) in rat sciatic nerve [33, animal — rat, independent].
- **Anxiety behavior:** intraperitoneal GHK (0.5/5/50 µg/kg) increased open-arm time in the rat elevated plus maze [34, animal — rat, independent] — single paradigm, acute dosing, too thin for any human indication.
- **Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) neuroprotection:** in collagenase-induced ICH, **Wistar rats (72 total, 24/group; GHK 1 or 10 mg/kg i.p. ×3 pre-ICH)** showed reduced caspase-3/TUNEL apoptosis, lower brain water content, improved neurological scores, ↑VEGFA, ↓miR-339-5p [35, animal — rat, independent]; a second report (**120 rats, 24/group; 1/5/10 µg/g i.p.**) showed reduced astrocyte injury via Akt/miR-146a-3p/AQP4 [36, animal — rat, same independent group]. Genuinely independent in-vivo neuroprotection, but **single-group, prophylactic (pre-hemorrhage) dosing, unreplicated externally.**
- **Cognitive decline:** ~700+ nervous-system genes claimed up-modulated — a **Pickart/Margolina cMap computational analysis**, not a primary neuro experiment and not a cognition study [37, mechanism_review].
- **Lung anti-fibrosis (out-of-skin-scope, logged):** GHK reduced bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice (C57BL/6, n=10/group, i.p.) via TGFβ1/Smad and EMT suppression [31, animal — mouse]; relevant only as a cross-organ anti-fibrotic mechanism.

### 7.6 "Anti-cancer" claims (bioinformatic predictions, not therapy)

The "anti-cancer" story is built almost entirely on **computation, not therapy**:

- GHK was identified as the **single most active of 1,309 cMap compounds** at reversing a 54-gene "metastasis-prone" signature of early colorectal cancer (only GHK at ~1 µM and securinine at 18 µM reversed it) [17, in_vitro — independent]. **This is a bioinformatic perturbagen-matching prediction — NOT a demonstration that GHK shrinks tumors, prevents metastasis, or treats cancer; no GHK treatment was performed in that study.**
- GHK is claimed to up-regulate caspase/growth-regulatory/DNA-repair "anti-cancer" gene programs in MCF7 and PC3 cells [16, in_vitro] — but the **authors themselves concede that "gene data by itself would not be enough to draw a conclusion"** and report **no cell-killing/viability assays** [16/32].
- The **only** retrieved primary wet-lab anti-cancer-direction result is a single low-tier study: GHK induced apoptosis (1.8–3.3-fold, higher with GHK-PEG) in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells at 1 and 10 nM [38, in_vitro — independent, but single cell line, single small study, non-PubMed-indexed OMICS-group journal].

**Honest bottom line on cancer:** there is **no clinical, and effectively no in-vivo, anti-cancer evidence** for GHK-Cu. Representing GHK-Cu as an anti-cancer agent would overstate the evidence by a wide margin. (Patent filings such as US9586989B1 "Non-toxic skin cancer therapy" were seen but are **not evidence** and are deliberately excluded.)

## 8. Safety, Copper Toxicology & Contraindications

### 8.1 Topical (CIR-safe, low risk)

The **CIR Expert Panel (2018)** determined Copper Tripeptide-1 and its ingredient family **"safe in the present practices of use and concentration"** in cosmetics, citing low use concentrations (< 10 ppm family-wide) and negative safety test data [46, regulatory]. Topical adverse events are limited to **rare, mild, local irritation/sensitization**; contact dermatitis is described as "very rarely reported," and patch testing before facial use is the standard precaution [51, anecdote_aggregate][49, vendor_label]. **No published GHK-Cu-specific allergic-contact-dermatitis case report was located** [51, anecdote_aggregate]. The foundational review additionally asserts (narratively, not as a quantified AE rate) that the peptide "has been widely used in anti-aging and cosmetic products for decades without any adverse effects" — but the same review reports **no human oral or IV dosing data and no LD50** [12, mechanism_review].

### 8.2 Copper toxicology and the systemic-overload risk floor

Copper homeostasis sets the systemic risk floor: the U.S. **RDA for copper is 900 µg/day**, and the **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) for adults is 10 mg/day**, the UL being set on protection against **liver damage** as the critical adverse effect [47, regulatory]. **Topical** cosmetic GHK-Cu delivers copper masses **orders of magnitude below the UL**, supporting "no systemic copper risk from topical use." **Injectable/high-dose use has no comparable margin characterized.** Copper can shift from antioxidant to **pro-oxidant** behavior at supraphysiological concentrations — the mechanistic basis for copper toxicity at high systemic loads, and the toxicological argument that the **topical→injectable safety extrapolation is invalid** [47, regulatory][12, mechanism_review].

### 8.3 Contraindications, interactions, monitoring, stopping

- **CONTRAINDICATION — Wilson's disease** and other copper-metabolism/copper-overload disorders (Indian childhood cirrhosis, idiopathic copper toxicosis): these individuals reach copper toxicity **below** the 10 mg/day UL, which explicitly does not apply to them; GHK-Cu supplies bioavailable copper, so they are the **principal contraindication group** — an **absolute contraindication for systemic/injectable use** [47, regulatory].
- **CONTRAINDICATION / interaction — copper-chelation therapy:** GHK-Cu directly counteracts chelation; avoid [52, anecdote_aggregate] (qualitative).
- **CAUTION — concurrent copper supplementation:** stacking additional copper raises cumulative-load risk toward the UL; avoid co-supplementation, especially with any systemic route [48, anecdote_aggregate][47, regulatory].
- **MONITORING (systemic/injectable, by analogy to copper-overload management):** serum copper, ceruloplasmin, and liver function are the relevant axes (liver damage being the UL-defining endpoint); **no GHK-Cu-specific monitoring protocol is validated in trials** [47, regulatory].
- **STOPPING (topical):** discontinue and consider patch testing / dermatology referral on local irritation or suspected allergic contact dermatitis [51, anecdote_aggregate].

### 8.4 Injectable safety: uncharacterized, and the repudiated "8–12%"

**Injectable safety is essentially uncharacterized:** no controlled human trial of injectable/systemic GHK-Cu exists. Aggregator/community sources describe injection-site reactions (redness, swelling, minor pain), transient post-injection flushing, and metallic taste — but these are recorded here as **existence-of-AE-type only, NOT as admissible rates**. The frequently cited **"8–12% injection-site reactions" figure originates from vendor/aggregator pages and is NOT admissible** as an AE rate, because no controlled human injectable trial grounds it; the injectable side-effect profile "has not been characterized in a controlled human trial" [52, anecdote_aggregate]. (This is the AE-existence-not-rate discipline applied: the number is quoted only to reject it.) No published GHK-Cu-specific case report of copper toxicity, copper overload, or allergic contact dermatitis was located — reassuring for topical use, but **absence of reports is not demonstrated systemic safety.**

Supporting tolerability data exist only at the topical/dermal-exposure level: the Mulder 1994 diabetic-ulcer RCT reported no toxicity attributable to the agent [42, rct] and the Miller 2006 post-laser study assessed post-procedure tolerability [43, rct] — both abstract-level for AE tables, both **topical**.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics, Dose, Route & Formulation

### 9.1 Penetration / PK data (topical; no systemic human PK)

The only admissible PK-adjacent data are **topical/ex-vivo penetration**: permeability coefficient **2.43 ± 0.51 × 10⁻⁴ cm/h**, **136.2 ± 17.5 µg/cm²** copper permeated over 48 h, **97 ± 6.6 µg/cm²** retained as a stratum-corneum depot through dermatomed human skin [9, in_vitro]; and the model-membrane finding that free GHK, GHK-Cu, and (GHK)₂-Cu are the membrane-permeant species [5, in_vitro]. There is **no admissible peer-reviewed human systemic PK** (half-life, Cmax, AUC, clearance) by any route; circulating "~0.5–1 h IV" half-life figures are vendor/blog-only and are **not grounded** [route-extrapolation across topical vs. systemic].

### 9.2 Concentration regimes — keep the three distinct

Three distinct, correctly-attributed concentration regimes circulate and must not be conflated:

- **CIR regulatory framing:** typical cosmetic use concentrations **< 10 ppm** (ingredient-family-wide) [46, regulatory].
- **Vendor cosmetic-active formulation:** commonly **~0.05–2%** (some raw-material vendors sell 5–10% solutions for formulators — e.g., a 5000 ppm / 0.5% cosmetic-grade technical data sheet [50, vendor_label]) [60, vendor_label] — vendor figure, qualitative only.
- **Practitioner topical-dosing convention:** **1–3% by weight** [65, practitioner_protocol] — convention, not efficacy.

### 9.3 Practitioner dose/route conventions (tagged practitioner_protocol — NOT efficacy)

> **Tier 2.7 — captured for route/dose/cycle convention ONLY; NOT evidence of efficacy.** GHK-Cu has no FDA-approved injectable indication; these reflect aesthetic/longevity-clinic and peptide-educator conventions, not guideline medicine.

- **Subcutaneous (gray-market/longevity convention):** a named, dated source — **"GHK-Cu Dosing Guide," Peptide Dosing Protocols (PDP), publisher Garret Grant, last updated June 2026** — lists **Injectable SubQ 1–2 mg/day** (conservative start 1.0 mg, 5-on/2-off, 4 wk; standard 1.0–1.5 mg/day, 4–6 wk; high-end 1.5–2.0 mg/day, 6–8 wk), injected into abdominal subcutaneous tissue, cycled in 4–8 week blocks, vial refrigerated (never frozen); **Topical 1–3% by weight**, 8–12 wk for skin / 12+ wk for hair [65, practitioner_protocol]. A broader vendor/educator convention describes ~0.5–1 mg SubQ 3–5×/week to start, escalating to 1–2 mg/day [60, practitioner_protocol].
- **Procedure-adjunct (aesthetic/derm convention):** GHK-Cu applied **topically immediately post-procedure** (microneedling, laser, peel) to exploit transient barrier disruption for dermal delivery [60, practitioner_protocol].
- **Peptide-educator framework:** **William A. Seeds, MD** (founder/chair, International Peptide Society; author *Peptide Protocols, Vol. 1*, 2020) emphasizes dosing peptides separately to preserve stability [67, practitioner_protocol]; Seeds is co-author of two 2026 peer-indexed orthopaedic peptide reviews cataloging GHK-Cu among injectable therapeutic peptides — Rahman, Lee & Seeds 2026 (*JAAOS Glob Res Rev*, PMID 41490200) [66] and Mayfield et al. 2026 (*Am J Sports Med*, PMID 41476424) [61]. Seeds is a peptide-society commercial principal — treat all of this as **practitioner_protocol, NOT independent efficacy.**

### 9.4 Formulation / stability / reconstitution

The redox-active Cu²⁺ makes the complex **pH- and oxygen/light-sensitive**: **stability is the main cosmetic concern, not toxicity** [60, vendor_label]. For the injectable gray-market channel, two chemistry-specific failure modes the channel does not reliably control are **(i) under-/over-coppering** (product labeled "GHK-Cu" may carry variable or excess copper, and "GHK" copper-free is a different substance) and **(ii) reconstitution chemistry** (bacteriostatic-water reconstitution, refrigeration, protection from light/oxygen materially affect what is delivered) [64, vendor_label]. Vendor COAs verifying **copper stoichiometry** (not just peptide HPLC purity) are rarely provided. Documented gray-market risks include endotoxin and heavy-metal contamination — acutely relevant for a **copper-containing** complex, where excess free/poorly-chelated copper is a real toxicity vector — plus incorrect sequence/identity and unknown excipients [60, 64, vendor_label]. The safest legitimate route described for human use is a **licensed physician + FDA-registered compounding pharmacy producing pharmaceutical-grade *topical* formulations**, not self-sourced injectables [64, vendor_label].

## 10. N=1 / Trial-Design Considerations (goal-agnostic)

This is a goal-agnostic library entry; the following are design considerations, not a protocol or recommendation.

- **Route is the first fork.** Topical and injectable GHK-Cu are evidentially worlds apart: topical has a formal safety determination, penetration data, and one positive RCT; injectable has **no human data of any kind**. Any self-experiment should be explicit about which it is testing — they do not transfer.
- **Blinding and vehicle control are decisive here.** The cleanest cosmetic-adjacent RCT (Miller 2006) found a benefit on *subjective* satisfaction but **none on objective endpoints** — a textbook illustration that unblinded before/after impressions of GHK-Cu are unreliable. Any meaningful N=1 needs an objective, instrumented endpoint and ideally a vehicle-controlled split-site (e.g., split-face/split-wound) design, as the registered NCT07437586 trial uses.
- **Copper load is the safety axis to monitor for any systemic route.** The UL (10 mg/day, liver endpoint) and the Wilson's-disease contraindication are the hard constraints; serum copper/ceruloplasmin and liver function are the analogous monitoring axes, though no validated GHK-Cu-specific protocol exists.
- **Product identity is a live confound for injectables.** Copper stoichiometry, peptide identity, endotoxin, and heavy-metal content are uncontrolled in the gray-market channel and would confound any injectable N=1 independent of biology.
- **Endpoints to avoid over-reading:** gene-expression/cMap "reset" framing, anti-aging "youthful reset," and anti-cancer claims are **not** demonstrated outcomes — they are computational predictions and cannot serve as N=1 endpoints.

## 11. Bottom Line — Supported vs Marketed

**Supported by human data (modest, topical):**

- **Topical cosmetic use** has *modest real support*: a genuinely independent multi-lab in-vitro collagen/ECM literature, replicated rat+rabbit wound benefit, a formal cosmetic-safety determination (CIR "safe as used"), tier-1 human ex-vivo penetration data, and one positive controlled human wound-healing RCT (diabetic ulcers — **but n-unstated, ProCyte-sponsored, ~30 years old, and unreplicated; a qualified pillar, not a robust one**). The honest qualifier: the cosmetic anti-aging evidence specifically is small-n, industry-sponsored, partly non-peer-reviewed posters, and the single most rigorous cosmetic-adjacent RCT was **negative** on objective endpoints.

**Marketed but NOT supported by human data:**

- **Injectable / systemic GHK-Cu** — no human study of any design exists; unapproved; copper-overload risk; Wilson's = contraindication.
- **Anti-aging "gene reset" / "resets a third of the genome toward youth"** — computational cMap fingerprints in cancer cell lines, re-narrated by the commercial originator; no lifespan, biological-age, or controlled aging endpoint.
- **Anti-cancer** — bioinformatic perturbagen-matching predictions plus one low-tier single-cell-line apoptosis paper; no clinical and effectively no in-vivo evidence.
- **Hair growth (GHK-Cu monotherapy)** — thin and analog/formulation-based; the widely cited "hair RCT" tested an ALA+GHK combination, not GHK-Cu.

**One-sentence bottom line:** Topical GHK-Cu has modest, genuinely independent support for skin/wound use (one positive but qualified RCT — n-unstated, ProCyte-sponsored, ~30 years old, unreplicated — plus multi-lab in-vitro/animal data and CIR "safe as used") — but injectable/systemic GHK-Cu and the anti-aging-gene-reset and anti-cancer claims are unsupported by any human data, the broad interpretive narrative is dominated (~70–85%) by its commercial discoverer, and copper-overload risk makes Wilson's disease an absolute contraindication for any systemic use.
