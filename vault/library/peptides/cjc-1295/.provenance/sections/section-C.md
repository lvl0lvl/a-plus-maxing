## Section C — Preclinical + concentration audit

**Scope note / DAC discipline.** This section concerns the discovery-stage preclinical pharmacology of the albumin-conjugated GHRH analogue. Two distinct molecules must never be conflated: **CJC-1295 WITH DAC** (Drug Affinity Complex) carries a Nε-3-maleimidopropionamide-of-lysine moiety at the C-terminus that covalently binds the free thiol of albumin Cys34 in vivo, giving a multi-day half-life; **CJC-1295 WITHOUT DAC** ("Modified GRF 1-29", "Mod GRF 1-29") is the same tetrasubstituted GRF(1-29) backbone *without* the maleimido linker, with a half-life on the order of ~30 minutes. The preclinical primaries below (Jette 2005, Alba 2006) studied the **DAC-bearing albumin-conjugate**. The half-life of the no-DAC peptide is not measured in those primaries and is reported here only from review/structural sources; numbers are never cross-attributed.

### Molecular background (mechanism)

The CJC-1295 backbone is a tetrasubstituted analogue of human GH-releasing factor hGRF(1-29): D-Ala at position 2 (blocks DPP-IV cleavage), plus Gln8, Ala15 and Leu27 substitutions that further resist plasma proteolysis [3][4]. `mechanism_review` The "DAC" feature is an additional Nε-3-maleimidopropionamide derivative of a C-terminal lysine; this maleimide reacts with the single free thiol (Cys34) of serum albumin to form a covalent bioconjugate in situ, which is what extends residence time from minutes to days [1][4]. `mechanism_review` Both the conjugate and native GRF act through the same GHRH receptor (GHRHR) on anterior-pituitary somatotrophs, stimulating adenylyl cyclase / cAMP signalling, GH gene transcription and pulsatile GH release [3][4]. `mechanism_review`

Reported half-lives by species/context (NOT cross-attributed):
- CJC-1295 WITH DAC, humans: estimated 5.8–8.1 days (clinical PK; reported in Section D's clinical primary, restated by review [4]). `mechanism_review`
- CJC-1295 WITH DAC, rat conjugate: plasma-detectable beyond 72 h after a single sc dose [1]. `animal`
- CJC-1295 WITHOUT DAC (Mod GRF 1-29): ~30 min — review/structural framing only, no preclinical PK primary located in this pass. `mechanism_review`

### Primary preclinical pharmacology

**[1] Jette et al., Endocrinology 2005 — rat discovery/PK study (ConjuChem).**
- Species/route: normal male Sprague-Dawley rats, subcutaneous administration. `animal` (species = rat, Sprague-Dawley)
- Design: three maleimido derivatives of hGRF(1-29) were synthesised and bioconjugated to human serum albumin; CJC-1295 (the tetrasubstituted form bearing the Nε-3-maleimidopropionamide-lysine at the C-terminus) was identified as the lead. `in_vitro` + `animal`
- In vitro: all three albumin conjugates showed enhanced stability against dipeptidyl-peptidase-IV (DPP-IV) versus the unconjugated peptide, and remained bioactive in a GH-secretion assay in cultured rat anterior-pituitary cells (direct evidence of GRF-receptor activation by the conjugate) [1]. `in_vitro`
- In vivo (rat): a single sc injection of CJC-1295 produced an acute rise in plasma GH; CJC-1295 gave an ~4-fold increase in GH area-under-the-curve over a 2-h window versus an equimolar dose of hGRF(1-29) [1]. `animal`
- PK (rat): the albumin-bound conjugate appeared in plasma by ~15 min, remained in circulation beyond 24 h, and was still detectable beyond 72 h [1]. `animal`
- **Caveat on n:** the openly accessible abstract/PDF text did not state an exact animals-per-group count or the precise mg/kg doses; per the species+n rule, the GH-AUC and PK findings above are reported as rat findings (species verified) but the explicit per-group n could not be verified from the sources opened and is therefore NOT asserted as a number. The full-text Methods (behind the OUP paywall) would carry it.

**[2] Alba et al., Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab 2006 — GHRH-knockout mouse efficacy study (academic-led, ConjuChem-supplied compound).**
- Model/route: GHRH-knockout (GHRHKO) mice, the GH-deficient dwarf model; CJC-1295 given subcutaneously. `animal` (species = mouse, GHRHKO strain)
- Design and n: three groups of 1-week-old GHRHKO mice were treated for 5 weeks with 2 µg CJC-1295 per dose at intervals of 24 h, 48 h, or 72 h, against placebo controls [2]. `animal` (n: enumerated as three dose-interval groups + placebo; the paper reports group-level body-weight/length and pituitary outcomes — exact animals-per-arm beyond "three groups" was not quoted in the abstract opened, so only the verified design is asserted).
- Findings [2]: `animal`
  - Daily (24 h) dosing **normalised** body weight and body length and normalised femur/tibia length (bone/longitudinal growth) and body composition.
  - 48 h and 72 h dosing raised body weight and length above placebo but did NOT fully normalise growth — a clear dose-frequency gradient.
  - CJC-1295 increased pituitary GH mRNA and total pituitary RNA, with immunohistochemical evidence of somatotroph proliferation — i.e. a trophic effect on the pituitary, not only acute secretion.
- This is the strongest preclinical *efficacy* (growth/bone) signal: it shows the DAC conjugate can drive normal somatic growth in a GH-deficient animal when dosed often enough.

### Supporting / structural-regulatory source

**[3] Henninge et al., Drug Testing and Analysis 2010 — independent identification.** `regulatory` Independent (Norwegian anti-doping lab; not ConjuChem) LC-HRMS/MS identification of CJC-1295 as a 29-amino-acid C-terminally amidated GRF analogue in an illicit preparation, confirming the molecular identity of the marketed compound and its WADA-prohibited status. Used here to corroborate identity/structure, not for efficacy numbers.

**[4] Mavrych et al., Frontiers in Aging 2026 — narrative review.** `mechanism_review` Independent academic review summarising the GHRHR/cAMP mechanism, the DAC albumin-binding half-life extension (6–8 days WITH DAC), and explicitly citing Alba 2006 for the "daily CJC-1295 normalises growth, body composition and metabolic function in GH-deficient mice" preclinical claim. Used for mechanism framing only.
