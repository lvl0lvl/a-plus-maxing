## Section D — Safety, adverse effects, disputed fatalities

> **Scope & integrity note.** This section separates three evidence classes that are routinely conflated in the lay literature: (1) adverse events actually *reported in the human trial* of CJC-1295 with DAC (Teichman 2006); (2) the disputed "CJC-1295 deaths" claim, which is adjudicated against a primary news-of-record source below; and (3) *class-level* growth-hormone (GH) / GHRH-analogue risks that are theoretically applicable to CJC-1295 but were **not** observed in any CJC-1295-specific trial. Type-tags mark which is which. The defining pharmacokinetic fact governs the entire safety discussion: **CJC-1295 *with DAC* has a measured terminal half-life of 5.8–8.1 days [1], whereas CJC-1295 *without DAC* ("Modified GRF 1-29") has a half-life of roughly 30 minutes [2].** Because DAC-bound drug circulates for days, any adverse effect it provokes is correspondingly *long-lived and not rapidly reversible* — the long exposure amplifies, rather than mitigates, every safety concern below. With-DAC and without-DAC findings are never cross-attributed.

### Adverse events from the human trial (with-DAC)

The only published human safety dataset for CJC-1295 with DAC is Teichman et al. 2006, two randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind ascending-dose trials in healthy adults aged 21–61 years, of 28- and 49-day duration [1] `rct`.

- The trial's headline safety conclusion is that CJC-1295 "was safe and relatively well tolerated, particularly at doses of 30 or 60 µg/kg," and that **"no serious adverse reactions were reported"** during the studies [1] `rct`.
- A single subcutaneous dose raised mean plasma IGF-1 by 1.5–3-fold for 9–11 days, and after multiple doses IGF-1 stayed above baseline for up to 28 days — confirming that the pharmacodynamic (and therefore the safety) footprint of a with-DAC dose persists for weeks [1] `rct`. This is the mechanistic core of the "amplified" concern: an idiosyncratic reaction cannot simply be "stopped" by withholding the next injection.
- Reported treatment-emergent adverse events in this trial were generally mild and transient and clustered into injection-site reactions (local redness/swelling/pain), transient flushing or warmth, and headache [1] `rct`. **Caveat on numbers:** specific per-event incidence figures (e.g., the "80% injection-site reactions" / "22% flushing" figures circulating in secondary write-ups) could *not* be verified against the primary abstract, which reports only the qualitative tolerability statement above; those percentages are therefore treated as **UNVERIFIED** and are not asserted here as trial-derived rates.

No higher-quality CJC-1295-with-DAC human safety data exist. CJC-1295 is **not FDA-approved**, has **no USP/NF monograph**, and is **not** described by any FDA-approved drug label [3] `regulatory`. Consequently there is no `vendor_label` or approved-label AE table for this compound — a point that itself constrains how confidently any AE profile can be stated.

### The "CJC-1295 deaths" claim — what the evidence actually shows

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED as to the *event*, but UNVERIFIED / NOT ESTABLISHED as to *causation*.** A real participant death did occur in a real CJC-1295 (with-DAC) trial, and there is a primary news-of-record source for it — so the often-dismissed claim is *not* pure internet myth. However, the specific causal assertion ("CJC-1295 killed someone") was never established and was contradicted by the trial's own attending-physician assessment.

What the source of record actually documents [4] `regulatory`/`cohort`-adjacent (clinical-trial event report via medical news of record, aidsmap, 31 July 2006, author Edwin J. Bernard):

- The compound was **ConjuChem's CJC-1295 with DAC**, in a **multicentre, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind Phase II trial** of **192 participants with HIV-related visceral obesity (lipodystrophy)** across sites in North and South America, dosing weekly escalating subcutaneous regimens (low arm 60→90→120 µg/kg; high arm 60→120→240 µg/kg; or placebo) [4] `regulatory`.
- **ConjuChem halted the study on 17 July 2006** following the death of one participant at a study site in **Argentina** [4] `regulatory`.
- At the time of reporting, **"the cause of death and its relationship to the study drug" was under investigation and was not officially disclosed** [4] `regulatory`. Subsequent reporting attributes the working assessment of the attending physician to asymptomatic coronary artery disease with plaque rupture and coronary occlusion, judged the most likely explanation and **not** drug-related; the trial was nonetheless terminated as a precaution [5] `cohort`-adjacent.
- The widely repeated and lurid detail — that "a man died a few hours after receiving his eleventh CJC-1295 injection" — is explicitly described in the source of record as an **anecdotal, unconfirmed report from a trial participant, not from the company or medical officials** [4] `anecdote_aggregate` (qualitative lead only; no numerical death rate is or can be derived from it).

**Synthesis.** The honest reading is: (a) one death in a 192-person with-DAC trial is a fact with a credible source; (b) causation to CJC-1295 was never demonstrated and the on-scene clinical judgement pointed to pre-existing coronary disease; (c) the program was discontinued and CJC-1295 never advanced to Phase 3 or approval, so no regulatory causality adjudication (e.g., an FDA review or a published autopsy/causality analysis) was ever produced [3][4] `regulatory`. Therefore the claim "CJC-1295 causes/caused deaths" must be reported as **UNVERIFIED as to causation** — true that a trial death and study halt occurred, false-to-overstated that the drug was shown to be the cause. Any wiki rendering that states CJC-1295 "has killed people" as established fact is **not supported by the evidence** and should be corrected. (This single trial death is *with-DAC only*; it cannot be attributed to the without-DAC "Mod GRF 1-29" form, which has no comparable controlled-trial mortality event of record.)

### GH-axis class risks (theoretical / class-derived — NOT observed in the CJC-1295 trial)

CJC-1295 acts by raising endogenous GH and downstream IGF-1. Its long-term risk *ceiling* is therefore reasonably bounded by the well-characterised adverse-effect profile of GH excess and exogenous GH therapy. These are **class risks**, applied by mechanism — they were **not** seen in Teichman 2006 and must not be presented as CJC-1295 trial findings.

From the largest long-term GH safety dataset, KIMS/overview of 15,809 GH-treated adults (Johannsson et al., *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 2022) `cohort`/`mechanism_review` [6]:

- **Fluid-retention complex** — arthralgia (4.6% any / 2.6% treatment-related), peripheral edema (3.8% / 3.1%), myalgia (1.4% / 0.7%), paresthesia (1.1% / 0.6%), and **carpal tunnel syndrome (1.2% / 0.8%)** [6] `cohort`. These effects are dose-related and IGF-1-mediated and typically improve with dose reduction `mechanism_review`.
- **Impaired glucose tolerance / insulin resistance** — GH is directly diabetogenic (increased lipolysis, increased insulin resistance) `mechanism_review`; in the KIMS cohort, treatment-related type 2 diabetes occurred in ~0.4% with a small median fasting-glucose rise (+0.28 mmol/L) [6] `cohort`.
- **Headache** — reported both in the GH class and in the CJC-1295 trial itself [1][6].
- **Theoretical IGF-1–mediated proliferation / cancer concern (ASSOCIATIONAL — flag).** High circulating IGF-1 is associated in epidemiology with neoplasia risk and poorer prognosis `mechanism_review`; this is a biologically plausible concern for any agent that chronically elevates IGF-1. It must be flagged as **associational, not causal**: in the actual long-term GH cohort, overall de-novo cancer incidence was **not** increased (SIR 0.92, 95% CI 0.83–1.01), with no increase for prostate, colon, or breast cancer, and pituitary tumour recurrence (2.7%) comparable to controls [6] `cohort`. The honest framing: theoretical concern is real, but the best long-term human data on GH-axis stimulation do not demonstrate an excess-cancer signal. No CJC-1295-specific carcinogenicity data exist.
- **Pituitary desensitization / tachyphylaxis under continuous exposure.** Continuous (non-pulsatile) GHRH exposure causes somatotroph desensitization at the receptor level, independent of GH-pool depletion (Rittmaster et al., *Neuroendocrinology* 1987, rat anterior pituitary cells) [7] `animal (rat, in vitro)`. This is the most CJC-1295-with-DAC-specific theoretical concern: because the DAC form maintains *days-long, relatively flat* drug exposure rather than physiologic pulses, it is mechanistically positioned to blunt its own GH response over time. The clinical magnitude and time-course in humans on CJC-1295-with-DAC are **not established**.

### Contraindications, monitoring, and stopping criteria (populated from class evidence)

These are derived from GH-axis class risk and the trial halt above; they are **practitioner-level / mechanism-derived**, not from a CJC-1295 label (none exists [3]).

- **Contraindications / strong cautions** `practitioner_protocol`/`mechanism_review`: active or recent malignancy (IGF-1 elevation is biologically undesirable in active cancer, even though excess-cancer signal is unproven [6]); pre-existing diabetes or impaired glucose tolerance (GH is diabetogenic [6]); pregnancy and breastfeeding (no safety data); and — given the trial's lone death occurred against a backdrop of presumed occult coronary disease [4][5] — caution in those with significant cardiovascular disease/uninvestigated cardiac risk.
- **Baseline + on-treatment monitoring** — **serum IGF-1 interpreted against age- and sex-specific reference ranges** (the objective downstream pharmacodynamic readout; keep within, not above, the normal range), and **fasting glucose plus HbA1c** for glycaemic drift `practitioner_protocol`/`mechanism_review`. These are standard, objective, validated assays; no CJC-1295-specific validated biomarker is required or exists.
- **Stopping criteria** `practitioner_protocol`: IGF-1 rising above the age/sex reference range; new or worsening glucose intolerance (HbA1c rise, new hyperglycaemia); new fluid-retention symptoms (edema, paresthesia, carpal-tunnel symptoms, arthralgia); any new or suspicious mass / malignancy; and any new cardiac symptoms. The days-long with-DAC half-life means a stopping decision does **not** produce immediate washout — an added reason to stop early and conservatively [1].
