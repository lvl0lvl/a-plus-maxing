## Section B — Human pharmacology & efficacy

### Defining distinction (carried into every claim below)

"CJC-1295" denotes two pharmacologically non-interchangeable molecules sharing the same tetra-substituted GHRH(1-29) backbone:

- **CJC-1295 WITH DAC** ("Drug Affinity Complex"): carries a maleimidopropionic-acid linker that covalently binds circulating albumin in vivo, producing a terminal half-life on the order of **days**. This is the only version that has ever entered a registered human trial. **All human pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data in this section are for the WITH-DAC molecule** [1][2].
- **CJC-1295 WITHOUT DAC** ("Modified GRF 1-29" / "Mod GRF 1-29"): the same four substitutions but no albumin-binding maleimide linker. Without the linker the analogue is not albumin-tethered, so it lacks the multi-day depot of the DAC molecule [4]. There is **no registered human trial and no admissible human efficacy or PK study** of this variant (see "No-DAC human data" below).

PK/PD and trial outcomes are never cross-attributed between the two.

### The pivotal human study: Teichman 2006 (CJC-1295 with DAC)

The only peer-reviewed human pharmacology study of CJC-1295 is Teichman, Neale, Lawrence, Gagnon, Castaigne & Frohman, *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 2006;91(3):799–805 (PMID 16352683) `[rct]` [1]. It comprises **two randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, ascending-dose Phase 1 trials** in **healthy adults aged 21–61**, of 28-day and 49-day duration respectively [1].

Design [1] `[rct]`:
- **Trial 1** — four ascending **single** subcutaneous doses.
- **Trial 2** — **multiple** doses given weekly or biweekly (two to three administrations).
- Doses of **30 and 60 µg/kg** are identified in the abstract as particularly well tolerated [1] `[rct]`.

Pharmacodynamics — single dose [1] `[rct]`:
- Mean plasma **GH rose 2- to 10-fold**, dose-dependently, and the elevation was **sustained for ≥6 days** from a single injection.
- Mean plasma **IGF-1 rose 1.5- to 3-fold**, sustained for **9–11 days**.

Pharmacodynamics — multiple dose [1] `[rct]`:
- With repeated weekly/biweekly dosing, **mean IGF-1 remained above baseline for up to 28 days**.

Pharmacokinetics [1] `[rct]`:
- Estimated **terminal half-life 5.8–8.1 days**. This day-scale half-life — together with the maintenance of elevated GH for ≥6 days after one injection — is the mechanistic fingerprint of the **albumin-bound (DAC) molecule**: the foundational rat pharmacology (Jetté 2005) shows CJC-1295 covalently attaches to circulating serum albumin within 15 min and persists in plasma beyond 72 h, the depot that produces multi-day GH release; an unconjugated peptide cannot reproduce this, which is the basis for assigning this entire human dataset to the WITH-DAC compound `[animal]` [1][4].

Safety [1] `[rct]`:
- **No serious adverse reactions** were reported; the compound was described as "safe and relatively well tolerated," particularly at 30–60 µg/kg. This is a small healthy-volunteer Phase 1 safety signal only — not a long-term safety dataset.

**What Teichman 2006 does and does not show.** It demonstrates a robust, durable **biomarker** response (GH and IGF-1 secretion) to an albumin-bound GHRH analogue in healthy adults. It contains **no clinical efficacy endpoint** — no body-composition, lean-mass, fat-loss, wound-healing, or anti-aging outcome was measured or met. "IGF-1 went up" is a pharmacodynamic biomarker, not a demonstration of clinical benefit.

### Other human data: the ConjuChem clinical program and its discontinuation

CJC-1295 was developed by **ConjuChem Biotechnologies** (Montreal) under the program name **DAC:GRF**. A search of ClinicalTrials.gov for CJC-1295 / DAC:GRF / ConjuChem returns exactly **one registered human trial of the CJC-1295 GHRH analogue** [2]:

1. **NCT00267527** — "A Multicenter, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled, Double-Blind, Phase 2 Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of CJC 1295 Administered for 12 Weeks in HIV Infected Patients With HIV Associated Visceral Obesity." Sponsor: ConjuChem. Condition: HIV infection with HIV-associated visceral obesity. Phase 2. Enrollment 120 (planned). Started December 2005; **status: Terminated**. **No results are posted** on the registry `[regulatory]` [2].

That is the complete enumeration: **N = 1 registered CJC-1295 trial.** (Other ConjuChem ClinicalTrials.gov records — NCT00638716, NCT00674466, NCT01514149 and related entries in Type 2 diabetes — are for **CJC-1134-PC**, an albumin-conjugated exendin-4/GLP-1 agonist, a *different* molecule; they are explicitly **not** CJC-1295 and are excluded here.)

**Indication pursued and discontinuation.** ConjuChem's clinical program targeted **HIV-associated visceral obesity / lipodystrophy**, not generic GH deficiency, "anti-aging," or athletic body-composition uses [2]. The Phase 2 trial (NCT00267527) was **halted on 17 July 2006 following the death of a participant** at a study site in Argentina, reported to have occurred after the eleventh CJC-1295 injection; at the time of reporting the cause and its relationship to the study drug were under investigation, and ConjuChem **discontinued the development program** thereafter `[regulatory]` [3]. (Note a registry/press discrepancy: ClinicalTrials.gov lists planned enrollment of 120 [2] while contemporaneous reporting cited ~192 enrolled across North and South America [3]; the registry figure is taken as the controlled value.) No CJC-1295 trial has been registered since; there is no FDA-approved product and no Phase 3 program `[regulatory]` [2].

**Human efficacy verdict.** No CJC-1295 human trial has **ever reported a met clinical efficacy endpoint** for body composition, lean mass, fat/visceral-fat loss, wound healing, or anti-aging. The single efficacy-powered trial (NCT00267527, HIV visceral obesity) was terminated with **no posted results** [2], and the only published human study (Teichman 2006) measured **biomarkers only**, not clinical outcomes [1]. Claims of "10–15% visceral-fat reduction" or "modest lean-mass gains" circulate on commercial peptide sites but trace to no peer-reviewed publication or posted trial result and are therefore **not admissible as efficacy evidence**.

### GH / IGF-1 pharmacodynamics in humans — interpretation

The human PD picture is internally consistent and limited to the WITH-DAC molecule: a single subcutaneous dose drives a **multi-day, dose-dependent rise in GH (2–10×) and a downstream IGF-1 rise (1.5–3×) lasting ~9–11 days**, with repeated dosing holding IGF-1 above baseline for ~4 weeks [1] `[rct]`. Mechanistically this is consistent with sustained GHRH-receptor agonism at the somatotroph from an albumin-tethered depot, giving a continuous "bleed" of GHRH signal rather than the pulsatile pattern of native secretion — the depot mechanism directly demonstrated in the rat bioconjugate study `[animal]` [4]. Whether such *non-pulsatile, chronically elevated* GH/IGF-1 is therapeutically desirable or carries downstream risk was **not** answered in any completed human trial — the only trial designed to test a clinical consequence was terminated [2].

### No-DAC ("Modified GRF 1-29") human data

There is **no admissible human trial of CJC-1295 without DAC**. ClinicalTrials.gov returns no registered Mod GRF 1-29 study; no peer-reviewed human PK or efficacy trial of the no-DAC variant was located on tier-1 hosts. The defining pharmacology of the shared GRF(1-29) backbone — four substitutions (D-Ala²/Gln⁸/Ala¹⁵/Leu²⁷) conferring DPP-IV resistance and enhanced bioactivity — comes from the rat bioconjugate study, but in that work the long half-life is produced specifically by the **albumin linker** that the no-DAC variant lacks `[animal]` [4]. **No human efficacy of any kind has been demonstrated for the no-DAC molecule.** The albumin-bioconjugate proof-of-concept that defined CJC-1295 (Jetté et al., *Endocrinology* 2005) was a **rat** study identifying CJC-1295 as the long-lasting albumin-binding GRF analogue — animal, not human, and supportive of the WITH-DAC PK mechanism only `[animal]` [4].

### Human-matrix analytical characterization (anti-doping; not efficacy)

CJC-1295 is on the WADA Prohibited List (S2 peptide hormones), and two peer-reviewed analytical-chemistry groups have characterized it in human biological matrices. Knoop et al. (*Anal Bioanal Chem* 2016) developed an immunoaffinity-LC-HRMS/MS method targeting GHRH analogues including CJC-1295 (and CJC-1293) plus metabolites in human plasma, validated in spiked blank plasma `[in_vitro]` [5]. Memdouh et al. (*Drug Test Anal* 2021) extended detection to urine, validating identification of both CJC-1295 and CJC-1295-with-DAC in fortified human urine at the ~1 ng/mL anti-doping threshold `[in_vitro]` [6]. These establish that the molecule and its DAC form are analytically distinguishable and detectable in human samples, and corroborate the metabolite/structure description — but **they contribute no in vivo PK, no PD, and no efficacy data** (samples were spiked, not from dosed humans), and are flagged here only as human-matrix characterization.

### Bottom line for Section B

- **One** published human study (Teichman 2006, Phase 1, healthy adults, with-DAC) — robust GH/IGF-1 **biomarker** elevation, day-scale half-life, small short-term safety signal; **no clinical endpoint** [1].
- **One** registered Phase 2 efficacy trial (NCT00267527, HIV visceral obesity, with-DAC) — **terminated after a participant death, no results posted, program discontinued** [2][3].
- **Zero** human trials demonstrating efficacy for body composition, fat loss, wound healing, or anti-aging — for either molecule.
- **Zero** admissible human studies of the no-DAC ("Mod GRF 1-29") variant.
