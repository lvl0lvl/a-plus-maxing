## Section E — Regulatory & sport status

### Defining distinction: WITH DAC vs WITHOUT DAC ("Mod GRF 1-29")

CJC-1295 exists in two physically distinct molecules that the regulatory and gray-market literature frequently conflate. **CJC-1295 *with* DAC** carries a Drug Affinity Complex (a maleimidoproprionic-acid moiety that binds covalently to serum albumin), extending the half-life to roughly 6–8 days. **CJC-1295 *without* DAC** is chemically the modified GHRH(1-29) sequence, marketed as **"Modified GRF 1-29" / "Mod GRF 1-29" / "CJC-1295 no-DAC,"** with a half-life of only ~30 minutes [1][4]. The no-DAC form is routinely sold as a nominally separate "research chemical," and informal-channel vials frequently mislabel or interchange which variant they actually contain — a documented identity/quality problem that compounds the already-unapproved status of both forms [4] (`regulatory`/`practitioner_protocol`). For every regulatory determination below, FDA and WADA treat the DAC and no-DAC species as the same class; renaming the no-DAC form does not change its legal standing (`regulatory`).

### FDA approval status — never approved

CJC-1295 has **never been approved by FDA** (no NDA, no BLA, no approved label, no agency-recognized indication) and is not approved by any other national regulator [1][2] (`regulatory`). It was developed by **ConjuChem** in the mid-2000s as a candidate for growth-hormone deficiency, HIV-associated lipodystrophy, and muscle-wasting; Phase 1/Phase 2 data were published in *JCEM* around 2005–2006 [2] (`mechanism_review`). The development program was **discontinued** by the sponsor and never passed to a successor pursuing registration, so as of 2026 there is no active approval pathway [2] (`regulatory`). Because it has no approval, no pharmacy may dispense it as a finished drug, and it cannot be lawfully marketed for any therapeutic claim [1][2] (`regulatory`).

### FDA 503A compounding status — CRITICAL detail

Section 503A of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act permits compounding from a bulk drug substance only if that substance is (a) the subject of an applicable USP/NF monograph, (b) a component of an FDA-approved drug, or (c) on FDA's positive **503A Bulks List**. Pending final rulemaking, FDA maintains an **interim list** with two buckets: **Category 1** (eligible for use while under evaluation) and **Category 2** ("may present significant safety risks" — *do not compound*), per FDA's interim compounding policy and the agency's published Category 2 page [7] (`regulatory`). CJC-1295 has never qualified under (a) or (b), so its compounding eligibility turns entirely on the list.

The documented sequence for CJC-1295 is:

1. **September 2023** — FDA placed multiple peptides, including CJC-1295, into **interim Category 2** ("may present significant safety risks"), as reflected on FDA's published Category 2 bulk-substances page [7] (`regulatory`).
2. **December 4, 2024 PCAC meeting** — The Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee evaluated CJC-1295 for inclusion on the positive 503A Bulks List. The meeting and the exact substances under review are established by the **primary Federal Register meeting notice, FR Doc. 2024-24828 (89 FR, published October 25, 2024)**, which announces the December 4, 2024 PCAC meeting and lists all five CJC-1295 forms nominated: **CJC-1295 (free base), CJC-1295 acetate, CJC-1295 with drug affinity complex (DAC) (free base), CJC-1295 DAC acetate, and CJC-1295 DAC trifluoroacetate** — alongside AOD-9604-related and Thymosin alpha-1-related substances [8] (`regulatory`). At that meeting **FDA recommended against inclusion, and the committee voted AGAINST adding CJC-1295** to the positive list (corroborated by the FDA advisory-committee meeting page for December 4, 2024 [9] and by the legal-industry summary [3]) (`regulatory`).
3. **April 16, 2026 — Federal Register Doc. 2026-07361** — The **primary Federal Register notice (FR Doc. 2026-07361, published April 16, 2026)** is the PCAC meeting notice that establishes a public docket and schedules the **July 23–24, 2026 PCAC meeting** to consider **seven** peptides for the positive 503A Bulks List: **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, and MOTS-c on July 23; Emideltide/DSIP, Semax, and Epitalon on July 24** [10] (`regulatory`). Concurrent with this scheduling, FDA removed **twelve** peptides from interim Category 2; the canonical removed-12 are: **BPC-157, TB-500 (Tβ4 fragment), KPV, MOTS-c, DSIP (Emideltide), Semax, Epitalon, GHK-Cu, Melanotan-II, Dihexa, PEG-MGF, and LL-37 (cathelicidin)** [7][3][5] (`regulatory`). The July 23–24, 2026 PCAC meeting reviews the first seven (per FR Doc. 2026-07361 [10]); the remaining five (GHK-Cu, Melanotan-II, LL-37, Dihexa, PEG-MGF) are slated for a later meeting before the end of February 2027 [3][5] (`regulatory`). **CJC-1295 is NOT among the removed twelve, is NOT mentioned anywhere in FR Doc. 2026-07361, and is NOT on either the July-2026 or February-2027 PCAC agenda** [10][3][5] (`regulatory`).

> **Primary-source note (FR Doc. 2026-07361 scope):** The April 16, 2026 Federal Register document (FR Doc. 2026-07361), retrieved from govinfo.gov (FR-2026-04-16), is specifically the **PCAC July 23–24, 2026 meeting notice** and on its face enumerates only the **seven** peptides on that agenda; it does not itself print the full "twelve removed from Category 2" tally. The twelve-peptide *removal* from interim Category 2 is a parallel FDA 503A interim-bulks-list action reflected on FDA's own Category 2 page [7]; the enumerated removed-12 and the second (Feb-2027) meeting slate are reported by the legal advisories [3][5]. Both the primary FR notice [10] and FDA's Category 2 page [7] independently confirm that **CJC-1295 is absent** from every part of this action.

**Bottom line on current 503A status:** CJC-1295 is **not** on the positive 503A Bulks List (PCAC declined to recommend it in December 2024 [8][9]), is **not** among the twelve peptides addressed by the April 2026 Federal Register / Category-2 action [10][7], and is **not** on the announced PCAC agendas [10]. Whether it is best described as still sitting in interim Category 2 or as having been removed into an unclassified "regulatory gap" cannot be uniformly confirmed across the sources reviewed: some practitioner/compounding trackers state it remains in Category 2 after the December 2024 vote, while at least one source states the molecule now sits in a no-formal-classification gap pending future review [4]. **What is firmly established from the primary record: CJC-1295 is not eligible for routine 503A compounding and was not part of the April 2026 reinstatement of twelve peptides** [10][7] (`regulatory`). **Removal from Category 2 ≠ FDA approval, and inclusion on the positive 503A list ≠ FDA approval; neither status was conferred on CJC-1295, and even for the twelve reinstated peptides, list movement does not constitute approval of safety or efficacy** [3][5] (`regulatory`).

A federal lawsuit over the Category 2 peptide placements named four substances specifically — **AOD-9604, CJC-1295, Ipamorelin acetate, and Thymosin Alpha-1** — situating CJC-1295 squarely inside the contested compounding-enforcement landscape rather than any approved channel [5] (`regulatory`).

### WADA status — prohibited at all times

CJC-1295 is **prohibited at all times (in- and out-of-competition)** under the **WADA 2026 Prohibited List** (entered into force 1 January 2026), within class **S2. Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances, and Mimetics** [6] (`regulatory`). The List header for S2 states verbatim: *"All prohibited substances in this class are prohibited at all times (in- and out-of-competition)"* and that they are non-Specified Substances [6]. Within S2, subsection **S2.2.4 ("Growth hormone releasing factors")** names CJC-1295 explicitly: *"growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) and its analogues (e.g. CJC-1293, CJC-1295, sermorelin and tesamorelin)"* [6] (`regulatory`). The prohibition is therefore both by explicit naming and by class coverage, and it applies equally to the DAC and no-DAC ("Mod GRF 1-29") forms because both are GHRH analogues (`regulatory`). Validated LC-MS/MS anti-doping assays detect CJC-1295 and CJC-1295-DAC in urine at the WADA-relevant ~1 ng/mL level, with multi-day detection windows [1] (`mechanism_review`).

### Gray-market reality

Despite no approval and no 503A eligibility, CJC-1295 (both DAC and no-DAC "Mod GRF 1-29") is widely sold online labeled **"for research use only / not for human consumption,"** a disclaimer that does not confer legal status for human use and does not exempt sellers from FDA enforcement against misbranded/unapproved new drugs [1][4] (`regulatory`). The no-DAC form's separate "research chemical" branding, combined with documented vial mislabeling between DAC and no-DAC variants in informal supply chains, creates both a regulatory gray zone and a product-identity/safety hazard for end users [4] (`practitioner_protocol`).
