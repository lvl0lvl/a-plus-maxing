# CJC-1295

## Metadata
- **class:** peptide (synthetic, tetrasubstituted analogue of human growth-hormone-releasing factor hGRF(1-29) = GHRH(1-29)).
- **evidence_tier: C** — the entire admissible evidence base is **one** published human pharmacology study (Teichman 2006, Phase 1, biomarker-only) plus **two** corporate-originated preclinical primaries (Jetté 2005 rat, Alba 2006 mouse); there is **zero** human efficacy evidence (the only efficacy-powered trial was terminated with no results posted), and 100% of the discovery-stage efficacy/PK primaries trace to ConjuChem involvement.
- **risk_tier: experimental** — unapproved drug with no controlled long-term human safety dataset; never FDA-approved; the only registered efficacy trial halted after a participant death; days-long with-DAC half-life means any adverse effect is slow to reverse; not lawfully compoundable (503A); prohibited at all times in sport (WADA S2.2.4).
- **status:** researching
- **last_verified:** 2026-06-20

> **Read this first — THE DEFINING FACT.** "CJC-1295" is not one molecule, it is **two**, and almost every number attached to the name is meaningless until you say which. **CJC-1295 WITH DAC** ("Drug Affinity Complex") carries a C-terminal maleimido-lysine that covalently binds circulating albumin (at albumin Cys34) in vivo, giving a measured terminal half-life of **~5.8–8.1 days** — this is the molecule in the only published human trial (Teichman 2006) and the only one ConjuChem ever put into a registered efficacy trial. **CJC-1295 WITHOUT DAC = "Modified GRF 1-29" / "Mod GRF 1-29"** is the *same* tetrasubstituted GRF(1-29) backbone but **without** the albumin-binding linker, so it is cleared in roughly **30 minutes** and is the form stacked daily with ipamorelin in gray-market practice. **PK, efficacy, and trial outcomes are NEVER cross-attributed between the two.** Now the honest evidence state: the only human **efficacy** trial — ConjuChem's with-DAC Phase II in HIV-associated visceral obesity, **NCT00267527** — was **TERMINATED on 17 July 2006 after a participant died** (causation to the drug was **never established**; the attending physician's working assessment was pre-existing occult coronary disease). **No CJC-1295 trial of any kind has ever met a body-composition, anti-aging, or wound-healing efficacy endpoint.** The robust GH (2–10×) and IGF-1 (1.5–3×) rises that are real and durable are **biomarkers, not proven clinical benefit.** CJC-1295 is **not FDA-approved**; it is **NOT** among the twelve peptides removed from interim Category 2 by FR Doc 2026-07361 (April 2026); the PCAC **voted against** adding it to the positive 503A Bulks List on **4 December 2024**; and WADA **S2.2.4** names it by name as prohibited at all times. ## TL;DR

CJC-1295 is a long-acting GHRH(1-29) analogue developed by ConjuChem in the mid-2000s. The single most important thing to understand is the **two-molecule split**: the **with-DAC** form binds albumin and lasts days; the **no-DAC** form ("Modified GRF 1-29") lasts minutes. The with-DAC molecule has a genuinely impressive *pharmacodynamic* signature in healthy adults — one subcutaneous dose raises GH 2–10-fold for ≥6 days and IGF-1 1.5–3-fold for 9–11 days, with a 5.8–8.1-day half-life (Teichman 2006). But that is a **biomarker** result. The only trial ever designed to test a **clinical** outcome (HIV-associated visceral obesity, NCT00267527) was **terminated after a death**, posted no results, and the entire development program was discontinued; CJC-1295 never reached Phase 3 or approval. The preclinical base is thin and corporate-originated (a rat discovery/PK study and a GHRH-knockout-mouse growth study, both ConjuChem-touched). The "CJC-1295 deaths" claim that circulates online is **partially true and widely overstated**: a real trial death occurred, but causation to the drug was never demonstrated. Class-level GH-axis risks (fluid retention, glucose intolerance, theoretical IGF-1/proliferation concern) apply by mechanism but were not observed in the one CJC-1295 trial. Regulatorily it is unapproved, not lawfully compoundable, and explicitly WADA-prohibited. It is best read as a **mechanistically real but clinically unproven** experimental compound whose marketed body-composition and anti-aging benefits rest on no completed human efficacy trial.

## 1. Identity, Chemistry & Pharmacokinetics

### 1.1 The two-molecule split (read before any number)

"CJC-1295" denotes two structurally distinct entities that are constantly conflated, and essentially every numerical claim is uninterpretable until this is resolved [2]:

- **CJC-1295 WITH DAC** ("DAC:GRF"): a tetrasubstituted hGRF(1-29) analogue carrying an added **Nε-3-maleimidopropionamide-lysine** at the C-terminus, which covalently binds circulating serum albumin in vivo, conferring a half-life on the order of days (**~5.8–8.1 d** measured in humans) [1][2].
- **CJC-1295 WITHOUT DAC** = "Modified GRF 1-29" / "Mod GRF 1-29": the *same* tetrasubstituted GRF(1-29) backbone but **lacking** the maleimido-Lys / DAC moiety, so it does not conjugate albumin and has a short (**~30 min** order) half-life; it is the form commonly stacked with the ghrelin-receptor agonist ipamorelin [13]. (The ~30-min figure is a review/consensus value, not a measured human-trial value — flagged accordingly [13].)

The PK data below (Teichman 2006, Ionescu 2006) are for the **DAC** form and must not be attributed to the no-DAC "Mod GRF 1-29." Vendor mislabeling — selling "Mod GRF 1-29" as "CJC-1295 without DAC" while quoting the DAC half-life — is rampant; this is a structural caveat, not a cited primary number.

### 1.2 The backbone and the exactly-four substitutions

CJC-1295 is a synthetic analogue of **human GH-releasing factor, residues 1-29** (hGRF(1-29) = GHRH(1-29)) — the same biologically active 1-29 N-terminal fragment that defines sermorelin and retains full GH-releasing activity [2]. The canonical identification paper (Jetté et al., *Endocrinology* 2005) describes CJC-1295 as "a tetrasubstituted form of hGRF(1-29) with an added N-epsilon-3-maleimidopropionamide derivative of lysine at the C terminus" [2]. The **exactly four** substitutions, relative to native hGRF(1-29), are:

1. **D-Ala at position 2** (Ala2 → D-Ala) — blocks dipeptidyl-peptidase-IV (DPP-IV) cleavage at the N-terminus, the principal route of GHRH inactivation.
2. **Gln at position 8** (Asn8 → Gln) — removes the asparagine prone to deamidation.
3. **Ala at position 15** — improves bioactivity/stability.
4. **Leu at position 27** (Met27 → Leu) — removes the oxidation-sensitive methionine.

This matches the "tetrasubstituted" descriptor [2]. **Verification limit (carried honestly):** the published Jetté abstract verifies the "tetrasubstituted" status and the maleimido-Lys addition, but the residue-by-residue enumeration is the widely reported set consistent with the DPP-IV-resistance design logic rather than spelled out residue-by-residue in the abstract text itself [2].

### 1.3 The DAC mechanism: covalent albumin bioconjugation

The "DAC" is the C-terminal **Nε-3-maleimidopropionamide-lysine** group [2]. After subcutaneous injection the electrophilic **maleimide reacts with the single free thiol of albumin — Cys34** — forming a stable covalent **thiosuccinimide (thioether) bond** and producing a peptide-albumin bioconjugate in vivo [2][4]. The chemistry is well characterised in the broader bioconjugate literature: maleimidopropionic-acid modification "allows a specific and stable covalent attachment to cysteine 34 of albumin to form a thiosuccinimide bond," and the conserved free Cys34 (Cys34 in human) is "the only free thiol group in this protein" across rodents, dogs, rabbits, monkeys, and humans [4]. Because albumin circulates for a long time and the ~67 kDa conjugate is too large for renal filtration and is shielded from peptidases, the bioconjugate dramatically extends the analogue's half-life relative to the free peptide [4]. Jetté et al. confirmed the in-vivo consequence: CJC-1295 was "present in plasma beyond 72 h" in rats [2].

The contrast is the crux: the **no-DAC** Mod GRF 1-29 has the same four backbone substitutions but no maleimido-Lys, never forms the albumin conjugate, and is cleared in roughly half an hour [13]. **The long half-life is a property of the DAC, not of the substitutions.** Two consequences follow that the marketplace routinely gets wrong: (1) quoting the 5.8–8.1-day half-life for a "no-DAC" product is a category error — the substitutions alone confer protease resistance, not a multi-day depot; and (2) the albumin conjugate (~67 kDa) is too large for renal filtration and is shielded from peptidases, which is *why* the DAC form persists, whereas the bare ~3.6 kDa peptide is filtered and degraded on a minutes timescale [4][13].

### 1.4 Pharmacokinetics — human (with-DAC only)

Teichman et al. 2006 reported, from two Phase 1 ascending-dose trials in healthy adults, an estimated **terminal half-life of 5.8–8.1 days** for CJC-1295 with DAC, with a single dose producing dose-dependent GH elevation sustained ≥6 days and IGF-1 elevation for 9–11 days [1] (full PD detail in §3). This day-scale half-life — together with maintenance of elevated GH for ≥6 days after one injection — is the mechanistic fingerprint of the albumin-bound molecule; an unconjugated peptide cannot reproduce it, which is the basis for assigning this entire human dataset to the with-DAC compound [1][2].

### 1.5 Pharmacokinetics — no-DAC ("Modified GRF 1-29")

There is **no admissible human PK study** of the no-DAC variant. The **~30-minute** half-life is a review/consensus value reflecting clearance of the unconjugated peptide and is not a measured human-trial figure [13]. No human efficacy of any kind has been demonstrated for the no-DAC molecule.

### 1.6 Compounded/marketed physicochemical descriptors (sourcing context only)

A historical compounding catalog (Tailor Made Compounding) lists a CJC-1295 monograph with molecular weight **3647.15**, molecular formula **C165H269N47O46**, purity ">98% (HPLC on request)" [27] `[compounding_data_sheet]`. These are sourcing/identity descriptors, not efficacy data, and reflect a pre-restriction compounding offering (see §9). Independent anti-doping identification (Henninge 2010) corroborates the marketed compound as a 29-amino-acid C-terminally amidated GRF analogue [12].

### 1.7 Coverage gaps (Section 1)
- The residue-by-residue four-substitution list is consistent with the design logic but not all spelled out in the Jetté abstract — flagged as a verification limit [2].
- The no-DAC ~30-min half-life is review/consensus only — no measured human PK primary located [13].
- No quantitative aqueous-stability constant for either form was retrieved in this pass.

## 2. Mechanism of Action

**Bottom line up front:** CJC-1295 is a **GHRH-receptor agonist**, not a ghrelin agonist. It acts at the GHRH-R on pituitary somatotrophs → Gs/cAMP/PKA/CREB → GH release and synthesis → hepatic IGF-1. The with-DAC form's albumin tether converts a normally pulsatile GHRH signal into days-long continuous exposure, which preserves pulse *shape* but markedly raises basal/trough GH.

### 2.1 Receptor and signalling

CJC-1295 is an agonist at the **growth-hormone-releasing-hormone receptor (GHRH-R)**, a **class B (secretin-like) GPCR** expressed mainly on **anterior-pituitary somatotropes** [5]. Jetté et al. showed this directly: the hGRF(1-29)-albumin conjugates "were bioactive in a GH secretion assay in cultured rat anterior pituitary cells," i.e. they activate the GRF receptor on somatotrophs [2].

The downstream cascade is canonical Gs signalling: GHRH-R → Gαs → **adenylyl cyclase** → **cAMP** → **PKA**, which phosphorylates and activates the transcription factor **CREB** [5]. This drives two outputs — acute **release** of pre-formed GH from secretory granules and de-novo **synthesis** (CREB may induce Pit-1, raising GH gene expression and replenishing GH stores) [5]. Released GH then "stimulates the production of insulin-like growth factor I (IGF-I) in the liver" — the hepatic IGF-1 arm [5].

### 2.2 Contrast with ghrelin-receptor secretagogues (the basis of the ipamorelin stack)

CJC-1295 is **not** a ghrelin agonist. GH secretagogues such as **ipamorelin** act at a *different* receptor — the **GHS/ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a)** — and Raun et al. showed "ipamorelin, like GHRP-6, stimulates GH release via a GHRP-like receptor," with "a selectivity for GH release similar to that displayed by GHRH" [6]. The two receptors and G-protein routes (GHRH-R → Gs/cAMP/PKA versus GHSR-1a → Gq/PLC/IP3/Ca²⁺) are distinct, which is the **mechanistic basis for the GHRH-analogue + GHRP "stack"**: a GHRH-R agonist (CJC-1295/Mod GRF) and a ghrelin-receptor agonist (ipamorelin) hit the somatotrope through complementary, non-redundant pathways, producing a larger combined GH response than either alone [5][6].

### 2.3 Pulsatility vs continuous DAC exposure (the honest nuance)

A central concern with a long-acting GHRH analogue is whether continuous stimulation collapses the pulsatile GH rhythm. The primary human data say it largely does **not collapse pulse shape — but it raises basal tone substantially.** Ionescu & Frohman (2006) sampled GH every 20 min overnight in healthy men before and 1 week after a single 60 or 90 µg/kg CJC-1295 (DAC) injection: "GH secretion was increased after CJC-1295 administration with preserved pulsatility. The frequency and magnitude of GH secretory pulses were unaltered. However, basal (trough) GH levels were markedly increased (7.5-fold; P < 0.0001)" and contributed to an overall GH increase (mean +46%; P < 0.01) and IGF-I increase (+45%; P < 0.001) [3]. **The honest reading:** pulse architecture is preserved, but the DAC's continuous albumin-tethered exposure raises **trough/basal** GH ~7.5-fold, and it is this trough elevation — not a change in pulse amplitude or frequency — that drives the IGF-1 rise [3]. "Preserved pulsatility" is true for pulse shape but does **not** mean the GH profile is unchanged.

### 2.4 Mechanistic synthesis (what the receptor story does and does not buy)

Putting the pieces together: CJC-1295 is a competent GHRH-R agonist that drives the full physiologic output of the somatotrope (acute GH release plus de-novo GH synthesis via CREB/Pit-1), and in the GH-deficient mouse this is sufficient to normalise somatic growth when dosed often enough [2][5][7]. The DAC's contribution is purely pharmacokinetic — it converts a minutes-scale signal into a days-scale one by tethering the analogue to albumin [2][4] — and the Ionescu sampling shows the physiologic price of that conversion is a large rise in **trough** GH rather than a distortion of pulse shape [3]. None of this is a demonstration of clinical benefit: a coherent receptor→GH→IGF-1 cascade and a durable biomarker rise are exactly what an effective GHRH agonist should produce, but whether *chronically elevated, non-pulsatile* GH/IGF-1 is therapeutically desirable — or whether it carries the desensitization/metabolic downsides developed in §6 — was never answered in a completed human trial [3][8]. The mechanism is real; the clinical consequence is unestablished.

### 2.5 Coverage gaps (Section 2)
- All direct receptor/bioassay evidence is rat pituitary (Jetté) or healthy-male sampling (Ionescu); human somatotrope-level signalling is inferred from the GH/IGF-1 readout, not measured at the receptor.
- The desensitization implication of continuous exposure is developed in §6 (theoretical) — no human time-course on CJC-1295-with-DAC desensitization exists.
- The "larger combined GH response" from the GHRH+GHRP stack is established mechanistically (two distinct receptors/pathways) and in classic GH-secretagogue pharmacology [6]; no CJC-1295-specific human stack-vs-monotherapy efficacy comparison exists.

## 3. Human Clinical Evidence

**State this plainly:** there is **one** published human pharmacology study (biomarker-only) and **one** registered efficacy trial (terminated, no results). **No human efficacy endpoint has ever been met**, and there is **zero** admissible human study of the no-DAC variant.

### 3.1 The pivotal human study: Teichman 2006 (with-DAC)

The only peer-reviewed human pharmacology study is Teichman, Neale, Lawrence, Gagnon, Castaigne & Frohman, *JCEM* 2006;91(3):799–805 (PMID 16352683) [1] `[rct]`. It comprises **two randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, ascending-dose Phase 1 trials** in healthy adults aged 21–61, of 28- and 49-day duration [1].

Design [1]:
- **Trial 1** — four ascending **single** subcutaneous doses.
- **Trial 2** — **multiple** doses given weekly or biweekly (two to three administrations).
- Doses of **30 and 60 µg/kg** identified as particularly well tolerated.

Pharmacodynamics — single dose [1]:
- Mean plasma **GH rose 2- to 10-fold**, dose-dependently, sustained **≥6 days** from one injection.
- Mean plasma **IGF-1 rose 1.5- to 3-fold**, sustained **9–11 days**.

Pharmacodynamics — multiple dose [1]:
- With repeated weekly/biweekly dosing, **mean IGF-1 remained above baseline for up to 28 days**.

Pharmacokinetics [1]:
- Estimated **terminal half-life 5.8–8.1 days**.

Safety [1]:
- **No serious adverse reactions** reported; "safe and relatively well tolerated," particularly at 30–60 µg/kg. This is a small healthy-volunteer Phase 1 safety signal — not a long-term dataset (see §6).

**What Teichman 2006 does and does not show.** It demonstrates a robust, durable **biomarker** response (GH and IGF-1 secretion) to an albumin-bound GHRH analogue in healthy adults. It contains **no clinical efficacy endpoint** — no body-composition, lean-mass, fat-loss, wound-healing, or anti-aging outcome was measured or met. "IGF-1 went up" is a pharmacodynamic biomarker, not a demonstration of clinical benefit.

### 3.2 The only efficacy trial: NCT00267527 (terminated)

CJC-1295 was developed by **ConjuChem Biotechnologies** (Montreal) under the program name **DAC:GRF**. A search of ClinicalTrials.gov returns exactly **one** registered human trial of the CJC-1295 GHRH analogue [8]:

- **NCT00267527** — "A Multicenter, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled, Double-Blind, Phase 2 Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of CJC 1295 Administered for 12 Weeks in HIV Infected Patients With HIV Associated Visceral Obesity." Sponsor: ConjuChem. Phase 2. Enrollment 120 (planned). Started December 2005; **status: Terminated**. **No results posted** [8].

That is the complete enumeration: **N = 1 registered CJC-1295 trial.** (Other ConjuChem ClinicalTrials.gov records — NCT00638716, NCT00674466, NCT01514149 — are for **CJC-1134-PC**, a *different* albumin-conjugated exendin-4/GLP-1 molecule, and are excluded.)

**Discontinuation.** The Phase 2 trial was **halted on 17 July 2006 following the death of a participant** at a site in Argentina, reported to have occurred after the eleventh injection; at the time of reporting, cause and relationship to the study drug were under investigation, and ConjuChem **discontinued the development program** thereafter [9]. (Registry/press discrepancy: ClinicalTrials.gov lists planned enrollment of 120 [8] while contemporaneous reporting cited ~192 enrolled across the Americas [9]; the registry figure is taken as the controlled value.) No CJC-1295 trial has been registered since; there is no FDA-approved product and no Phase 3 program [8]. The death and causation question are adjudicated in full in §6.

### 3.3 Human efficacy verdict

**No CJC-1295 human trial has ever reported a met clinical efficacy endpoint** for body composition, lean mass, fat/visceral-fat loss, wound healing, or anti-aging. The single efficacy-powered trial (NCT00267527) was terminated with no posted results [8], and the only published human study (Teichman 2006) measured biomarkers only [1]. Claims of "10–15% visceral-fat reduction" or "modest lean-mass gains" circulate on commercial peptide sites but trace to no peer-reviewed publication or posted trial result and are **not admissible as efficacy evidence**.

### 3.4 GH/IGF-1 pharmacodynamics in humans — interpretation

The human PD picture is internally consistent and limited to the with-DAC molecule: a single subcutaneous dose drives a multi-day, dose-dependent rise in GH (2–10×) and a downstream IGF-1 rise (1.5–3×) lasting ~9–11 days, with repeated dosing holding IGF-1 above baseline for ~4 weeks [1]. Mechanistically this is sustained GHRH-receptor agonism at the somatotrope from an albumin-tethered depot — a continuous "bleed" of GHRH signal rather than the pulsatile pattern of native secretion, the depot directly demonstrated in the rat bioconjugate study [2]. The reading that matters for a wiki user: this is the *expected* signature of an effective long-acting GHRH agonist, and it confirms the molecule engages its target durably — but it is a biomarker trajectory, not a clinical outcome. Whether such non-pulsatile, chronically elevated GH/IGF-1 is therapeutically desirable or carries downstream risk was **not** answered in any completed human trial; the only trial designed to test a clinical consequence (NCT00267527) was terminated [8].

### 3.5 No-DAC human data: none

There is **no admissible human trial of CJC-1295 without DAC** — no registered study, no peer-reviewed human PK or efficacy trial of the no-DAC variant on tier-1 hosts. The albumin-bioconjugate proof-of-concept that defined CJC-1295 (Jetté 2005) was a **rat** study, animal not human [2].

### 3.6 Human-matrix analytical characterization (anti-doping, not efficacy)

Two analytical groups have characterized CJC-1295 in human biological matrices: Knoop et al. (*Anal Bioanal Chem* 2016) developed an immunoaffinity-LC-HRMS/MS method for GHRH analogues including CJC-1295 in human plasma, validated in spiked blank plasma [10]; Memdouh et al. (*Drug Test Anal* 2021) extended detection to urine, validating identification of both CJC-1295 and CJC-1295-with-DAC in fortified human urine at the ~1 ng/mL anti-doping threshold [11]. These establish that the two forms are analytically distinguishable and detectable, but the **samples were spiked, not from dosed humans** — they contribute no in-vivo PK, no PD, and no efficacy data [10][11].

## 4. Preclinical Evidence

**Scope/DAC discipline:** the preclinical primaries (Jetté 2005, Alba 2006) studied the **DAC-bearing albumin conjugate**. The no-DAC peptide's half-life is not measured in those primaries and is reported only from review/structural sources; numbers are never cross-attributed.

**Reported half-lives by species/context (NOT cross-attributed):**

| Form | Context | Half-life / residence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| WITH DAC | humans (clinical PK) | 5.8–8.1 days | Teichman 2006 [1] |
| WITH DAC | rat conjugate | plasma-detectable beyond 72 h after a single sc dose | Jetté 2005 [2] |
| WITHOUT DAC (Mod GRF 1-29) | review/structural framing | ~30 min (no preclinical PK primary located) | review [13] |

### 4.1 Jetté et al., *Endocrinology* 2005 — rat discovery/PK (ConjuChem)

- **Species/route:** normal male Sprague-Dawley rats, subcutaneous [2] `animal`.
- **Design:** three maleimido derivatives of hGRF(1-29) were synthesised and bioconjugated to human serum albumin; CJC-1295 (the tetrasubstituted form bearing the Nε-3-maleimidopropionamide-lysine) was identified as the lead [2].
- **In vitro:** all three albumin conjugates showed enhanced DPP-IV stability versus the unconjugated peptide and remained bioactive in a GH-secretion assay in cultured rat anterior-pituitary cells (direct GRF-receptor activation) [2].
- **In vivo (rat):** a single sc injection produced an acute rise in plasma GH; CJC-1295 gave an **~4-fold increase in GH AUC** over a 2-h window versus an equimolar dose of hGRF(1-29) [2].
- **PK (rat):** the albumin-bound conjugate appeared in plasma by **~15 min**, remained in circulation beyond 24 h, and was detectable **beyond 72 h** [2].
- **Caveat on n:** the openly accessible abstract/PDF did not state an exact animals-per-group count or precise mg/kg doses; per the species+n rule, the GH-AUC and PK findings are reported as rat findings (species verified) but the explicit per-group n is **not asserted** — the paywalled full-text Methods would carry it.

### 4.2 Alba et al., *Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab* 2006 — GHRH-knockout mouse efficacy

- **Model/route:** GHRH-knockout (GHRHKO) mice, the GH-deficient dwarf model; CJC-1295 given subcutaneously [7] `animal`.
- **Design:** three groups of 1-week-old GHRHKO mice treated for 5 weeks with **2 µg CJC-1295 per dose at intervals of 24 h, 48 h, or 72 h**, against placebo [7]. (Exact animals-per-arm beyond "three groups" was not quoted in the abstract opened, so only the verified design is asserted.)
- **Findings [7]:**
  - **Daily (24 h) dosing normalised** body weight, body length, femur/tibia length (longitudinal growth) and body composition.
  - 48 h and 72 h dosing raised body weight and length above placebo but did **not** fully normalise growth — a clear dose-frequency gradient.
  - CJC-1295 increased pituitary GH mRNA and total pituitary RNA, with immunohistochemical evidence of somatotroph proliferation — a trophic effect, not only acute secretion.
- This is the strongest preclinical **efficacy** (growth/bone) signal: the DAC conjugate can drive normal somatic growth in a GH-deficient animal when dosed often enough — and it confirms that **dosing interval matters even for a long-acting agent** [7].

### 4.3 Supporting / structural source

Henninge et al., *Drug Test Anal* 2010 [12] `regulatory`: an independent (Norwegian anti-doping lab; not ConjuChem) LC-HRMS/MS identification of CJC-1295 as a 29-amino-acid C-terminally amidated GRF analogue in an illicit preparation, confirming the marketed compound's identity and WADA-prohibited status. Used to corroborate identity/structure, not efficacy. Mavrych et al., *Frontiers in Aging* 2026 [13] `mechanism_review` is an independent narrative review used for the GHRHR/cAMP mechanism framing and the DAC half-life (6–8 d with DAC); not a primary.

- Two primaries total; exact per-arm n not extractable from accessible text for either (paywalled Methods).
- The no-DAC backbone SAR (older hGRH work) exists in the wider literature but was outside the DAC-conjugate efficacy/PK scope and is not counted here.

## 6. Safety & Adverse Effects

> **Scope note.** Three evidence classes are kept separate: (1) adverse events actually reported in the human trial (Teichman 2006); (2) the disputed "CJC-1295 deaths" claim, adjudicated below; and (3) *class-level* GH/GHRH-analogue risks that are theoretically applicable but were **not** observed in any CJC-1295 trial. The defining PK fact governs the whole discussion: **with-DAC has a measured half-life of 5.8–8.1 days [1]; without-DAC ~30 minutes [13]** — so any adverse effect the DAC form provokes is long-lived and not rapidly reversible, which **amplifies** every concern below. With-DAC and without-DAC findings are never cross-attributed.

### 6.1 Adverse events from the human trial (with-DAC)

The only published human safety dataset is Teichman 2006 [1] `rct`:
- Headline conclusion: CJC-1295 "was safe and relatively well tolerated, particularly at doses of 30 or 60 µg/kg," and **"no serious adverse reactions were reported"** [1].
- Because a single dose holds IGF-1 elevated for weeks [1], the safety footprint of a with-DAC dose persists — an idiosyncratic reaction cannot be "stopped" by withholding the next injection.
- Reported treatment-emergent AEs were generally mild/transient: injection-site reactions (redness/swelling/pain), transient flushing/warmth, and headache [1]. **Caveat on numbers:** specific per-event incidence figures (e.g., the "80% injection-site / 22% flushing" figures in secondary write-ups) could **not** be verified against the primary abstract, which reports only the qualitative tolerability statement; those percentages are treated as **UNVERIFIED** and are not asserted as trial-derived rates.

No higher-quality CJC-1295-with-DAC human safety data exist. CJC-1295 is **not FDA-approved**, has **no USP/NF monograph**, and is described by no FDA-approved label [14] `regulatory` — so there is no approved-label AE table, and the confidence with which any AE profile can be stated is correspondingly constrained.

**The amplification logic (specific to the DAC form).** The central safety asymmetry between the two molecules is pharmacokinetic, not pharmacodynamic. Because the with-DAC drug is covalently tethered to albumin and circulates for **days** (measured t½ 5.8–8.1 d [1]), any adverse effect it provokes — whether an idiosyncratic reaction, a metabolic shift, or a fluid-retention symptom — is **long-lived and not rapidly reversible**. A single dose holds IGF-1 above baseline for 9–11 days [1], so withholding the next injection does not produce prompt washout. This is the mechanistic core of the "amplified concern" framing: the long exposure does not mitigate the class risks below, it extends the window over which they can manifest and slows any attempt to back out of them. The no-DAC form, cleared in ~30 minutes [13], does not share this property — but it also has no controlled-trial safety dataset of its own, and its findings are never cross-attributed.

### 6.2 The "CJC-1295 deaths" claim — what the evidence shows

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED as to the *event*, but UNVERIFIED / NOT ESTABLISHED as to *causation*.** A real participant death did occur in a real CJC-1295 (with-DAC) trial, and there is a primary news-of-record source — so the claim is *not* pure internet myth. But the causal assertion ("CJC-1295 killed someone") was never established and was contradicted by the trial's own attending-physician assessment.

What the source of record documents [9] `regulatory`/`anecdote_aggregate` (for the unconfirmed detail only):
- The compound was **ConjuChem's CJC-1295 with DAC**, in a **multicentre, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind Phase II trial** of **192 participants** with HIV-related visceral obesity across the Americas, dosing weekly escalating subcutaneous regimens (low arm 60→90→120 µg/kg; high arm 60→120→240 µg/kg; or placebo) [9].
- **ConjuChem halted the study on 17 July 2006** following the death of one participant at a study site in **Argentina** [9].
- At the time of reporting, **"the cause of death and its relationship to the study drug" was under investigation and was not officially disclosed** [9]. Subsequent reporting attributes the attending physician's working assessment to **asymptomatic coronary artery disease with plaque rupture and coronary occlusion**, judged most likely and **not drug-related**; the trial was terminated as a precaution [15] `cohort`-adjacent.
- The lurid detail — "a man died a few hours after his eleventh CJC-1295 injection" — is explicitly described in the source of record as an **anecdotal, unconfirmed report from a trial participant, not from the company or medical officials** [9]; no numerical death rate can be derived from it.

**Synthesis.** (a) One death in a 192-person with-DAC trial is a fact with a credible source; (b) causation to CJC-1295 was never demonstrated and the on-scene clinical judgement pointed to pre-existing coronary disease; (c) the program was discontinued and CJC-1295 never advanced to Phase 3 or approval, so no regulatory causality adjudication (FDA review, published autopsy/causality analysis) was ever produced [14][9]. Therefore the claim "CJC-1295 causes/caused deaths" must be reported as **UNVERIFIED as to causation** — true that a trial death and study halt occurred, false-to-overstated that the drug was shown to be the cause. Any wiki rendering that states CJC-1295 "has killed people" as established fact is **not supported by the evidence**. (This death is **with-DAC only**; it cannot be attributed to the without-DAC "Mod GRF 1-29" form, which has no comparable controlled-trial mortality event of record.)

### 6.3 GH-axis class risks (theoretical / class-derived — NOT observed in the CJC-1295 trial)

CJC-1295 raises endogenous GH and downstream IGF-1, so its long-term risk *ceiling* is reasonably bounded by the adverse-effect profile of GH excess and exogenous GH therapy. These are **class risks**, applied by mechanism — they were **not** seen in Teichman 2006. From the largest long-term GH safety dataset, KIMS/overview of 15,809 GH-treated adults (Johannsson et al., *JCEM* 2022) [16] `cohort`:

- **Fluid-retention complex** — arthralgia (4.6% any / 2.6% treatment-related), peripheral edema (3.8% / 3.1%), myalgia (1.4% / 0.7%), paresthesia (1.1% / 0.6%), **carpal tunnel syndrome (1.2% / 0.8%)** [16]. Dose-related and IGF-1-mediated; typically improve with dose reduction.
- **Impaired glucose tolerance / insulin resistance** — GH is directly diabetogenic; in KIMS, treatment-related type 2 diabetes occurred in ~0.4% with a small median fasting-glucose rise (+0.28 mmol/L) [16].
- **Headache** — reported both in the GH class and in the CJC-1295 trial [1][16].
- **Theoretical IGF-1-mediated proliferation / cancer concern (ASSOCIATIONAL — flag).** High circulating IGF-1 is epidemiologically associated with neoplasia risk — biologically plausible for any chronic IGF-1 elevator — but must be flagged as **associational, not causal**: in the long-term GH cohort, overall de-novo cancer incidence was **not** increased (SIR 0.92, 95% CI 0.83–1.01), with no increase for prostate, colon, or breast cancer, and pituitary tumour recurrence (2.7%) comparable to controls [16]. No CJC-1295-specific carcinogenicity data exist.
- **Pituitary desensitization / tachyphylaxis under continuous exposure.** Continuous (non-pulsatile) GHRH exposure causes somatotroph desensitization at the receptor level, independent of GH-pool depletion (Rittmaster et al., *Neuroendocrinology* 1987, rat anterior-pituitary cells) [17] `animal (rat, in vitro)`. This is the most CJC-1295-with-DAC-specific theoretical concern: the DAC form maintains days-long, relatively flat exposure rather than physiologic pulses, so it is mechanistically positioned to blunt its own GH response over time. The clinical magnitude/time-course in humans is **not established**.

### 6.4 Contraindications, monitoring, and stopping criteria (class-derived; no CJC-1295 label exists)

These are derived from GH-axis class risk and the trial halt; they are practitioner-level / mechanism-derived, not from a CJC-1295 label (none exists [14]).

- **Contraindications / strong cautions** `practitioner_protocol`/`mechanism_review`: active or recent malignancy (IGF-1 elevation undesirable in active cancer, even with unproven excess-cancer signal [16]); pre-existing diabetes or impaired glucose tolerance (GH is diabetogenic [16]); pregnancy and breastfeeding (no safety data); and — given the trial's lone death occurred against presumed occult coronary disease [9][15] — caution in significant or uninvestigated cardiovascular disease.
- **Baseline + on-treatment monitoring:** **serum IGF-1 interpreted against age- and sex-specific reference ranges** (the objective downstream pharmacodynamic readout — keep within, not above, the normal range), and **fasting glucose plus HbA1c** for glycaemic drift `practitioner_protocol`/`mechanism_review`. These are standard, objective, validated assays; no CJC-1295-specific validated biomarker exists or is required.
- **Stopping criteria** `practitioner_protocol`: IGF-1 rising above the age/sex reference range; new or worsening glucose intolerance; new fluid-retention symptoms (edema, paresthesia, carpal-tunnel symptoms, arthralgia); any new or suspicious mass / malignancy; any new cardiac symptoms. The days-long with-DAC half-life means a stopping decision does **not** produce immediate washout — an added reason to stop early and conservatively [1].

- Per-event AE incidence in the CJC-1295 trial is qualitative only; the circulating percentages are unverified.
- Causality of the trial death was never adjudicated by a regulator or published autopsy; the not-drug-related assessment is the attending physician's working judgement [9][15].
- All quantitative AE rates are borrowed from the GH class (KIMS), not from CJC-1295 itself.

## 7. Regulatory Status

### 7.1 FDA approval — never approved

CJC-1295 has **never been approved by FDA** (no NDA, no BLA, no approved label, no recognized indication) and is not approved by any other national regulator [18][19] `regulatory`. ConjuChem developed it in the mid-2000s for GH deficiency, HIV-associated lipodystrophy, and muscle-wasting; the program was **discontinued** and never passed to a successor pursuing registration, so as of 2026 there is no active approval pathway [19]. Because it has no approval, no pharmacy may dispense it as a finished drug and it cannot be lawfully marketed for any therapeutic claim [18][19].

### 7.2 FDA 503A compounding — the critical detail

Section 503A permits compounding from a bulk substance only if it has a USP/NF monograph, is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or is on FDA's positive **503A Bulks List**. Pending final rulemaking, FDA maintains an interim list with **Category 1** (eligible while under evaluation) and **Category 2** ("may present significant safety risks" — do not compound) [14] `regulatory`. CJC-1295 has never qualified under monograph/approved-drug routes, so eligibility turns entirely on the list. The documented sequence:

1. **September 2023** — FDA placed multiple peptides, including CJC-1295, into **interim Category 2** [14].
2. **4 December 2024 PCAC meeting** — the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee evaluated CJC-1295. The meeting and substances under review are established by the **primary Federal Register notice, FR Doc. 2024-24828 (89 FR, published 25 October 2024)**, which lists all five CJC-1295 forms nominated: **CJC-1295 (free base), CJC-1295 acetate, CJC-1295 with DAC (free base), CJC-1295 DAC acetate, and CJC-1295 DAC trifluoroacetate** — alongside AOD-9604- and Thymosin alpha-1-related substances [24]. At that meeting **FDA recommended against inclusion and the committee voted AGAINST adding CJC-1295** to the positive list (corroborated by the FDA advisory-committee meeting page for 4 December 2024 [25] and a legal-industry summary [20]) [24][25].
3. **16 April 2026 — Federal Register Doc. 2026-07361** — the **primary FR notice** that establishes a public docket and schedules the **23–24 July 2026 PCAC meeting** to consider **seven** peptides for the positive list: **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, and MOTS-c on July 23; Emideltide/DSIP, Semax, and Epitalon on July 24** [26]. Concurrently, FDA removed **twelve** peptides from interim Category 2; the canonical removed-12 are **BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, DSIP (Emideltide), Semax, Epitalon, GHK-Cu, Melanotan-II, Dihexa, PEG-MGF, and LL-37** [14][20][22]. The July 23–24 meeting reviews the first seven (per FR Doc. 2026-07361 [26]); the remaining five are slated for a later meeting before end-February 2027 [20][22]. **CJC-1295 is NOT among the removed twelve, is NOT mentioned anywhere in FR Doc. 2026-07361, and is NOT on either PCAC agenda** [26][20][22].

> **Primary-source note (FR Doc. 2026-07361 scope):** the April 16 2026 FR document, retrieved from govinfo.gov, is the PCAC July 23–24 2026 meeting notice and on its face enumerates only the **seven** agenda peptides; it does not itself print the full "twelve removed from Category 2" tally. The twelve-peptide removal is a parallel FDA 503A interim-bulks action reflected on FDA's own Category 2 page [14]; the enumerated removed-12 and the Feb-2027 slate are reported by the legal advisories [20][22]. Both the primary FR notice [26] and FDA's Category 2 page [14] independently confirm **CJC-1295 is absent** from every part of this action.

**Bottom line on 503A:** CJC-1295 is **not** on the positive 503A Bulks List (PCAC declined to recommend it in December 2024 [24][25]), is **not** among the twelve peptides addressed by the April 2026 action [26][14], and is **not** on the announced agendas [26]. **Honest caveat (preserved):** whether it is best described as *still sitting in interim Category 2* or as having been moved into an *unclassified "regulatory gap"* cannot be uniformly confirmed across sources — some practitioner/compounding trackers say it remains in Category 2 after the December 2024 vote, while at least one source states it now sits in a no-formal-classification gap pending future review [21]. **What is firmly established from the primary record: CJC-1295 is not eligible for routine 503A compounding and was not part of the April 2026 reinstatement of twelve peptides** [26][14]. **Removal from Category 2 ≠ FDA approval, and inclusion on the positive list ≠ FDA approval; neither status was conferred on CJC-1295** [20][22].

A federal lawsuit over the Category 2 placements named four substances specifically — **AOD-9604, CJC-1295, Ipamorelin acetate, and Thymosin Alpha-1** — situating CJC-1295 inside the contested compounding-enforcement landscape rather than any approved channel [22].

### 7.3 WADA — prohibited at all times

CJC-1295 is **prohibited at all times (in- and out-of-competition)** under the **WADA 2026 Prohibited List** (in force 1 January 2026), within class **S2. Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances, and Mimetics** [23] `regulatory`. The S2 header states "All prohibited substances in this class are prohibited at all times (in- and out-of-competition)" as non-Specified Substances. Within S2, subsection **S2.2.4 ("Growth hormone releasing factors")** names CJC-1295 explicitly: "growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) and its analogues (e.g. CJC-1293, CJC-1295, sermorelin and tesamorelin)" [23]. The prohibition is therefore both by explicit naming and by class coverage, applying equally to the DAC and no-DAC forms (both are GHRH analogues). Validated LC-MS/MS assays detect CJC-1295 and CJC-1295-DAC in urine at the ~1 ng/mL level with multi-day detection windows [18].

### 7.4 Gray-market reality

Despite no approval and no 503A eligibility, CJC-1295 (both forms) is widely sold online labeled "for research use only / not for human consumption" — a disclaimer that confers no legal status for human use and does not exempt sellers from FDA enforcement against misbranded/unapproved new drugs [18][21]. The no-DAC form's separate "research chemical" branding, plus documented vial mislabeling between DAC and no-DAC variants in informal supply chains, creates both a regulatory gray zone and a product-identity/safety hazard [21] `practitioner_protocol`.

## 8. Why this lands at evidence_tier C

The grade is deliberate and conservative. CJC-1295 lands at **C** for converging reasons:

1. **No human efficacy evidence of any design.** The only efficacy-powered trial (NCT00267527) was terminated with no posted results [8]; the only published human study (Teichman 2006) is biomarker-only [1]. A higher tier is impossible without met clinical endpoints.
2. **GH/IGF-1 elevation is a biomarker, not a clinical outcome.** The 2–10× GH and 1.5–3× IGF-1 rises are real and durable but are pharmacodynamic readouts, not demonstrated body-composition/anti-aging/wound benefit [1][3].
3. **Severe evidentiary concentration.** 100% (inclusive) of the two preclinical efficacy/PK primaries trace to ConjuChem, and the human efficacy/PK layer also traces to ConjuChem (§5). No independent efficacy/PK primary exists.
4. **A terminated trial with a death (causation unproven).** The one efficacy trial halted after a participant death; while causation was never established, this is the opposite of a reassuring clinical record [8][9].
5. **No controlled long-term human safety dataset.** All quantitative AE rates are borrowed from the GH class (KIMS), not from CJC-1295 [16].

What keeps CJC-1295 at C rather than lower is the genuine, mechanistically coherent, durable **biomarker** response in a real (if small) human Phase 1 trial plus an internally consistent preclinical growth signal. What prevents anything higher is the total absence of human efficacy data, the corporate concentration of the evidence base, and the terminated efficacy trial. **C = pharmacodynamically real, clinically unproven.**
