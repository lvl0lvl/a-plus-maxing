# SS-31 / Elamipretide — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how elamipretide is actually prescribed, accessed, dosed, and monitored in practice* — the approved prescribing pathway for Barth syndrome, the compounding restrictions that flow from FDA approval, and the off-label grey-market reality that predates and coexists with approval. Source-tagging rule: `regulatory` (Tier 1) and `human_RCT` (Tier 2) ground safety/monitoring parameters. `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4), and `vendor_label` (Tier 4) are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and purity/identity math respectively. **None** of these lower tiers grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** Elamipretide received **accelerated FDA approval on October 30, 2025** under the brand name **FORZINITY** (NDA215244, Stealth BioTherapeutics Inc.) — the first approved treatment for **Barth syndrome**. This is a real, active pharmaceutical prescribing pathway: 40 mg SC once daily, adults and pediatric patients ≥30 kg with genetically confirmed Barth syndrome. It is an accelerated approval; continued authorization is contingent upon a confirmatory trial. **This approval does NOT authorize elamipretide for any other indication.** Off-label longevity, mitochondrial anti-aging, and performance uses of "SS-31" remain unapproved and are accessed entirely via the grey market (research-chemical vendors) or, in a legally contested channel, via 503A compounding pharmacies — the latter being restricted by the "essentially a copy" doctrine described below.
>
> **The approved drug and grey-market peptide are structurally identical but NOT the same product.** Forzinity is a pre-filled, ready-to-use 280 mg/3.5 mL (80 mg/mL) sterile solution manufactured under pharmaceutical GMP. Grey-market "SS-31" is a lyophilized powder of unverified purity from research-chemical vendors, reconstituted by the end-user. No equivalence of quality, sterility, or potency should be assumed.

## Approved Prescribing: FORZINITY (Barth Syndrome)

### Indication

"FORZINITY is a mitochondrial cardiolipin binder indicated to improve muscle strength in adult and pediatric patients with Barth syndrome weighing at least 30 kg." [1, regulatory]

**Population specifics:** adults and pediatric patients ≥12 years, ≥30 kg body weight, genetically confirmed Barth syndrome (TAZ mutation). Approved under accelerated approval based on improvement in knee extensor muscle strength (intermediate clinical endpoint, not a direct functional outcome) from the TAZPOWER crossover trial (n=12). [1, regulatory]

**Confirmatory trial condition:** "Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in a confirmatory trial(s)." [1, regulatory] Stealth BioTherapeutics is required to conduct a post-approval randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial confirming that the observed muscle strength improvement translates to meaningful patient benefit. [2, regulatory]

### Dosing and Administration

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Dose (eGFR ≥30 mL/min) | 40 mg SC once daily | [1, regulatory] |
| Dose (eGFR <30 mL/min, not on dialysis) | 20 mg SC once daily | [1, regulatory] |
| Route | Subcutaneous injection | [1, regulatory] |
| Frequency | Once daily, at the same time each day | [1, regulatory] |
| Injection sites | Abdomen (≥2 inches from navel) or outer thigh; rotate sites | [1, regulatory] |
| Formulation | 280 mg/3.5 mL (80 mg/mL) sterile solution, pre-filled; ready-to-use (NO reconstitution required) | [1, regulatory] |

No oral formulation exists; elamipretide is a tetrapeptide susceptible to GI degradation and is not bioavailable by the oral route.

Administration may be performed by the adult patient or a trained caregiver after proper injection technique training. [1, regulatory]

### Specialty Pharmacy Distribution

Forzinity is not available through conventional retail pharmacies. **AnovoRx Specialty Pharmacy** was selected as the exclusive commercial distribution partner in the United States via the **Mito Assist™ patient support program**, launched October 9, 2025. Commercial product became available through AnovoRx by December 2025. [2, regulatory]

Mito Assist provides:
- Co-pay assistance and patient assistance for uninsured/underinsured patients
- At-home subcutaneous injection training
- Dedicated case management
- Insurance coverage and prior-authorization navigation

Contact: 1-833-458-9099 [2, regulatory]

Patients previously on the Stealth expanded-access program (≥30 kg) are contacted directly by their HCPs and AnovoRx case managers for transition to commercial therapy. [2, regulatory]

**No REMS program** was required as a condition of approval. [1, regulatory]

### Contraindications

Serious hypersensitivity to elamipretide or any excipient (including benzyl alcohol). [1, regulatory]

Not approved for neonates: fatal reactions from benzyl alcohol toxicity have been reported in low-birth-weight neonates (<2,500 g) and preterm neonates (<34 weeks gestational age) who received benzyl-alcohol-containing IV drugs. [1, regulatory]

### Warnings, Precautions, and Mandatory PI Monitoring

#### Injection Site Reactions (ISR)

ISR is the primary and near-universal adverse event. In the TAZPOWER trial: **100% of elamipretide-treated patients (12/12) vs. 67% of placebo patients (8/12)** experienced any local administration reaction. Injection site erythema occurred in **100% vs. 25%** of patients respectively. [1, regulatory]

Other ISR manifestations: pain, induration, pruritus, bruising, urticaria. [1, regulatory]

**PI-required monitoring:** Assess injection sites at each clinical visit. Teach and enforce site rotation (abdomen, outer thigh, alternating sides). Document all ISRs.

#### Eosinophilia

"Increases in absolute eosinophil counts were noted frequently in studies where duration of administration of FORZINITY was 30 days or greater. Eosinophil counts generally peaked around 90 days after initial exposure (mean increase from baseline ~0.5 to 0.6 × 10³/µL) and returned to baseline levels after 6 to 12 months of continuous exposure or after discontinuation of FORZINITY." [1, regulatory]

**PI-required monitoring:** Monitor absolute eosinophil count (AEC) at baseline and periodically during treatment, with increased vigilance around the 90-day peak window. No explicit stopping-rule threshold is given in the PI, but eosinophilia concurrent with respiratory or dermatologic symptoms warrants assessment for eosinophilic organ involvement.

#### Hypersensitivity Reactions

"Hypersensitivity reactions, including serious allergic reactions requiring emergency medical intervention, have been reported in patients receiving FORZINITY. These reactions have included skin manifestations such as rash, papular lesions, and eczematous dermatitis, as well as respiratory symptoms including cough." Reactions can occur minutes to months after initiation. [1, regulatory]

**PI-required monitoring:** Patients and caregivers should be counseled to recognize and immediately report hypersensitivity signs (rash, urticaria, respiratory symptoms, angioedema). Maintain capacity for emergency intervention if serious hypersensitivity develops.

#### Renal Function

eGFR-based dose adjustment is required: reduce to 20 mg SC once daily for eGFR <30 mL/min in patients not on dialysis. [1, regulatory] No dose adjustment guidance is provided for dialysis patients in the PI.

**PI-implied monitoring:** Obtain baseline eGFR before initiating Forzinity; reassess periodically, particularly in patients with known CKD or fluctuating renal function.

#### Reproductive Safety (Pregnancy / Lactation)

No human pregnancy or lactation data are available for elamipretide. The Forzinity PI does not contain a specific pregnancy category statement, but absence of data is itself the safety signal. Use is not recommended in pregnancy. Counsel women of childbearing potential before initiating therapy. This caveat is particularly relevant in the off-label setting where prescribers may encounter reproductive-age women outside the ultra-rare BTHS approved population. [1, regulatory]

### PI Monitoring Summary Table

| Parameter | Timing | Threshold / Action |
|-----------|--------|--------------------|
| ISR assessment | Each clinical visit | Site rotation; document |
| Absolute eosinophil count (AEC) | Baseline + periodic (heightened ~day 90) | Investigate if eosinophilia + symptoms |
| eGFR | Baseline + periodic | Reduce to 20 mg/day if eGFR <30 mL/min (not on dialysis) |

## Compounding Status After FDA Approval: A Restricted Pathway

### The "Essentially a Copy" Doctrine (503A)

Under FDCA §503A, a 503A compounding pharmacy may not compound a drug product that is "essentially a copy of a commercially available drug product" unless a prescriber determines that the compounded version produces a **significant clinical difference** for a specific individual patient (e.g., removal of an excipient to which the patient is allergic, a different dosage form or strength the approved product does not offer). [3, regulatory]

With Forzinity's October 2025 approval, elamipretide is now a commercially available approved drug. A 503A pharmacy compounding elamipretide in the same active ingredient, strength, and route (40 mg SC) for non-Barth-syndrome use — the common grey-market scenario — would be compounding an essentially-a-copy product. Whether this is lawful depends on whether a documented significant-clinical-difference determination exists for the specific patient. **No blanket authorization to compound elamipretide for off-label longevity use was created by Forzinity's approval.** [3, regulatory]

Critically, the Forzinity formulation is a pre-filled ready-to-use solution at 80 mg/mL. A prescriber requesting a lower-concentration lyophilized SS-31 formulation (e.g., 5–10 mg vials for reconstitution at lower doses) for a specific clinical reason (e.g., a patient who requires a lower dose than the approved 40 mg, or who cannot tolerate benzyl alcohol preservative) could arguably satisfy the significant-clinical-difference requirement. But this analysis must be documented per-patient, not assumed wholesale.

**For 503B outsourcing facilities:** 503B facilities may only compound drugs on the Category 1 outsourcing bulk substances list or FDA-approved drugs in shortage. Forzinity is not in shortage. Outsourcing elamipretide at scale under 503B authority post-approval would require inclusion on the 503B bulks list, which has not occurred. [3, regulatory]

## Off-Label / Grey-Market Reality ("SS-31" as Research Chemical / Longevity Peptide)

### The Two Realities

The prescribing landscape for elamipretide is split:

**Reality 1 — Approved pharmaceutical (Forzinity):** A rigorously manufactured ready-to-use sterile solution, dispensed exclusively through AnovoRx by physician prescription for genetically confirmed Barth syndrome patients ≥30 kg. This is a legitimate, regulated, pharmaceutical-grade pathway.

**Reality 2 — Grey-market / research chemical / compounded "SS-31":** The same active peptide sequence, sold as a lyophilized research powder by research-chemical vendors under "not for human use" disclaimers, or obtained as compounded preparations from 503A pharmacies whose authority to do so post-approval is legally contested. This pathway serves anti-aging / longevity / mitochondrial performance interests that have no approved indication.

The gulf between these two realities is substantial: the approved product is GMP-manufactured, sterility-tested, and of verified identity and potency. Research-chemical SS-31 is manufactured to varying specifications, lacks FDA oversight, and purity claims depend entirely on third-party CoA quality, which varies.

There is **no human dose-finding trial for off-label SS-31 use in healthy adults or longevity populations** and no validated therapeutic dose for any non-Barth indication. ## Practitioner Monitoring Framework (Off-Label / Compounded Context)

If a clinician is managing a patient using compounded or grey-market SS-31 off-label, the following monitoring framework is grounded in: (a) the Forzinity PI's mandatory monitoring requirements (the same molecule, so ISR, eosinophilia, hypersensitivity, and eGFR concerns apply regardless of regulatory context) [1, regulatory]; and (b) mechanism-consistent laboratory surveillance (mitochondrial function, oxidative stress biomarkers). This is a rational-extrapolation framework, not a validated clinical guideline.

**Baseline (before starting):**

| Domain | Tests | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Renal | eGFR, creatinine, BUN | Dose-reduction threshold in PI (eGFR <30 → 20 mg); establish baseline |
| Hematology | CBC with differential (AEC baseline) | Eosinophilia warning: AEC peaks ~day 90; baseline required to detect rise |
| Hypersensitivity history | Allergy history, prior peptide reactions | Contraindication: serious hypersensitivity to elamipretide |
| Metabolic / mitochondrial | CMP, fasting glucose, lactate (optional in PMM patients) | Establish metabolic baseline; relevant if using for mitochondrial indication |
| Injection site | Physical inspection baseline | ISR will occur (100% in TAZPOWER); document baseline skin status |

**Follow-up:**

| Timing | Tests / Assessment |
|--------|--------------------|
| Day 30 | AEC (eosinophilia begins; pre-peak surveillance) |
| Day 90 | AEC (peak eosinophil count window per PI) |
| Week 4–6 | ISR assessment, injection site inspection; hypersensitivity signs review |
| 6–12 months | AEC (expected normalization; confirm) |
| Periodic | eGFR (recheck if baseline borderline or CKD history) |

**Stopping rules (rational, not validated):**

- Serious hypersensitivity: emergency intervention + permanent discontinuation
- AEC rise concurrent with respiratory symptoms, dermatologic findings, or organ-specific eosinophilia signs → withhold + evaluate
- Persistent worsening ISR (abscess, ulceration, spreading erythema beyond 2 cm) → reassess injection technique + consider dose holiday
- eGFR drops below 30 mL/min in a patient not previously dose-adjusted → reduce to 20 mg equivalent if applicable

**Honest limit:** The TAZPOWER trial had n=12 with a rare pediatric/young adult Barth syndrome population. Monitoring parameters above extrapolate the PI's requirements to a healthy-adult off-label context that was never studied. The eosinophilia signal, ISR incidence, and eGFR adjustment threshold are derived from this small Barth population. Applicability to the off-label longevity user is assumed but not established.
