# Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro) — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 non-English-literature survey for **Selank**, companion to `vault/library/peptides/selank/research-report.md`. Non-English peer-reviewed sources are admissible under the SAME tier rules as English; primary publication language is noted on every entry. No citation is fabricated; items found only as secondhand mentions with no locatable primary record are reported as "none located," not cited.

**Critical admissibility distinction enforced throughout (LANGUAGE, not author).** A study **authored by a Russian (or other non-Anglophone) investigator but published in English** — including the **Springer-translated journals** — is NOT a "genuinely non-English source": it is already reachable by, and catalogued in, the English layer. For Selank this distinction is the *central* methodological point, because Selank is **Russian in origin** (IMG RAS / Zakusov Institute), so a large fraction of its corpus is Russian-AUTHOR but English-PUBLISHED. Only works whose **primary publication language is Russian (or Chinese, German, etc.)** count here as genuine non-English primaries.

**Headline result:** unlike most peptides (where the non-English survey is a clean null), Selank has a **SUBSTANTIVE genuine Russian-LANGUAGE primary literature** — the IMG-RAS / Zakusov / Serbsky-Korsakov corpus that is the actual evidentiary backbone of the compound. **5 genuine Russian-LANGUAGE primaries** are catalogued below (3 clinical, 1 preclinical, 1 pharmacokinetic), plus 1 Russian-language secondary review used for citation tracing. By contrast, **Chinese** and **German** surveys returned **null/sparse** findings. This is the inverse of the typical Western-origin peptide: Selank's non-English (Russian) layer is where the primary evidence *lives*, not a derivative footnote.

## The LANGUAGE-vs-AUTHOR distinction (Springer-translated = author-not-language)

This is the load-bearing methodological call for Selank. A large portion of the Selank corpus is **Russian-AUTHOR but English-PUBLISHED** and is therefore catalogued in the English layer (research report §§1–4), **NOT** counted as a non-English primary:

- **Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine (BEBM)** — a **Springer-translated** journal. Russian-authored Selank work appearing here (e.g., Zozulya 2001 [1], Sokolov 2002 [2], Kolik 2014 [15], Kolik 2019 [9], Konstantinopolsky/Kolik 2022 [21]) is **English-LANGUAGE** at the point of indexing/access. These are **author-not-language** items → English layer.
- **Neuroscience and Behavioral Physiology** (Kozlovskaya 2003 [13]) and **Doklady Biological Sciences** (Inozemtseva 2008 [4]) — likewise English-translated/English-published → English layer.
- **Frontiers in Pharmacology** (Volkova 2016 [16]; Filatova 2017 [5]) — Russian (IMG-RAS) authors, but native **English-language** open-access journal → English layer.

By contrast, the five numbered items in the Russian section above are **Russian-LANGUAGE originals** (the three *Zh. Nevrol. Psikhiatr. im. Korsakova* items appear in PubMed only as **bracketed-title English translations of Russian originals**; the *Zh. Vyssh. Nerv. Deyat.* and *Bioorg. Khim.* items are Russian-language). Those are the genuine non-English primaries. Conflating "Russian author" with "Russian language" would either (a) wrongly inflate this layer with English-published BEBM/Frontiers papers, or (b) wrongly dismiss the layer as a null. Neither is correct — the discipline is applied per-item by **publication language**.
