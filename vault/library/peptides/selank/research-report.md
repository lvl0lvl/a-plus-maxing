# Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro): A Deep-Research Report on a Russia-Registered Tuftsin-Analogue Anxiolytic

## Abstract

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide with the primary sequence **Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro (TKPRPGP)** — a stabilized analogue of the endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg), extended at the C-terminus with a Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide that confers resistance to peptidase cleavage. It was designed and produced in Moscow as a joint effort of the **Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG RAS)** and the **V. V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology**. In the Russian Federation it is a fully registered prescription anxiolytic — an intranasal 0.15% solution carrying registration number **ЛСР-003338/09** — indicated for generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) and neurasthenia. It is **NOT approved by the FDA or the EMA**. Its evidence base is predominantly Russian-language and originates almost entirely from a single research network (IMG RAS / Zakusov Institute / Serbsky Center). The headline clinical claim — anxiolysis comparable to a benzodiazepine but without sedation, amnesia, dependence, or withdrawal — is a credible but single-lineage Russian clinical claim that has **not been independently replicated in the West**. Overall this report assigns Selank an **evidence_tier of C** and a **risk_tier of experimental**. It is held distinct throughout from Semax and from N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate.

## DEFINING FACT (read first)

**Essentially the entire admissible Selank evidence base is preclinical or small-clinical, Russian-language, and single-network.** It originates from the Institute of Molecular Genetics RAS (Myasoedov, Andreeva, Slominsky, Shadrina, Grivennikov, Inozemtseva, Kolomin) in collaboration with the Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology (Seredenin) and the Serbsky/Korsakov psychiatric milieu (Zozulia, Neznamov, Medvedev). Selank itself was invented at IMG RAS. A concentration audit of the admissible primaries finds the single-network share at roughly **100% (7/7 of the preclinical/mechanistic primaries; 6/7 even on the strictest "driving-affiliation" reading)** — far above the ≥70% concentration threshold. There is **no independent Western RCT, no large multi-center trial, and no independent lab confirmation** of the headline BDNF, gene-expression, anxiolytic, or antiviral findings [10][16][4].

This matters before any indication is discussed: the literature is internally consistent precisely because it shares authors, methods, and institutional priors — which is the *opposite* of independent corroboration. Confidence in any Selank benefit claim must be discounted accordingly. The four human studies in the anxiety indication are small (n ≈ 60–70), benzodiazepine-comparator (not placebo-controlled), Russian-language, and from this one lineage [10][11][12][8]. The most prominent Western source on Selank is not a corroborating efficacy study at all but a cautionary pharmacology review that classifies it as a poorly studied GABAergic agent sold illicitly to US consumers [19]. Read the entire report against that bound.

## 1. Identity, Structure, and Mechanism

### Identity and structure

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide with the primary sequence **Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro (TKPRPGP)** [1][2]. Its defining structural feature is that it is an analogue of the endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide **tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg, TKPR)**, extended at the C-terminus with a **Pro-Gly-Pro (PGP)** tripeptide [1][13] `mechanism_review`. The Pro-Gly-Pro tail is the same stabilizing motif appended to the related peptide Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro); in both peptides the proline-rich C-terminal extension confers resistance to aminopeptidase and carboxypeptidase cleavage, dramatically increasing metabolic stability relative to the parent fragment [3] `in_vitro`. Tuftsin itself is a fragment of the heavy chain of immunoglobulin G and acts as a phagocytosis-stimulating immunomodulator; Selank inherits a portion of this tuftsin-lineage immunological activity [8] `mechanism_review`.

Selank was developed in Moscow as a joint effort of the **Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG RAS)** and the **V. V. Zakusov (Zakusov) Research Institute of Pharmacology** [4][9] `regulatory`. The IMG RAS affiliation is documented directly in the author institutional address of the in vivo BDNF study [4], and the Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology affiliation is documented in the author address of the BDNF/ethanol study [9] `regulatory`.

Selank should be kept **distinct** from two adjacent peptides frequently confused with it:

1. **Semax** (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) — a different IMG-RAS heptapeptide derived from an ACTH(4-7)/ACTH(4-10) fragment, also bearing the Pro-Gly-Pro stabilizing tail, but with a different N-terminal active fragment and a more nootropic (rather than primarily anxiolytic) profile [3] `in_vitro`.
2. **N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate** — an N-terminally acetylated, C-terminally amidated derivative of Selank, which is a distinct chemical entity and not the parent peptide described here.

The TKPRPGP sequence is confirmed in multiple independent primary sources [1][2][3], satisfying the defining-fact identity check.

### Mechanism of action

Selank's anxiolytic and nootropic actions are attributed to several converging, mechanistically distinct pathways. The five best-characterized are enumerated below.

**1. Inhibition of enkephalin-degrading enzymes (prolongation of endogenous enkephalins).** This is the most directly evidenced biochemical mechanism. In human plasma/serum assays, Selank dose-dependently inhibits the enzymatic hydrolysis of enkephalins. One report gives an **IC50 of 15 µM** for inhibition of plasma enkephalin hydrolysis [1] `in_vitro`; a companion study of human serum reports an **IC50 of approximately 20 µM** for Selank (versus ~10 µM for Semax), with both peptides being substantially more potent inhibitors than puromycin (IC50 ~10 mM) or bacitracin [3] `in_vitro`. The same human-serum work showed that the pentapeptide fragments of Selank and Semax retained inhibitory activity, whereas the tri-, tetra-, and hexapeptide fragments did not [3] `in_vitro`. Because these enzymes degrade not only enkephalins but other regulatory peptides, enkephalinase inhibition is proposed as a general mechanism of biological activity [3] `in_vitro`. The clinical correlate: patients with generalized anxiety showed shortened enkephalin half-life and reduced total enkephalinase activity in blood, which Selank's inhibitory action would be expected to counteract [1] `cohort`. In a strain-comparison animal study, Selank at **100 µg/kg** produced an anxiolytic effect in the open-field test and increased the half-life of plasma leu-enkephalin in BALB/c mice, while having no effect on behavior or enkephalinase activity in C57Bl/6 mice — indicating strain (phenotype)-dependent action [2] `animal (mice: BALB/c and C57Bl/6)`.

**2. Modulation of BDNF (neurotrophin) expression.** Intranasal administration of Selank to rats increased both BDNF mRNA and BDNF protein levels in the hippocampus in vivo, and the authors concluded that Selank stimulates BDNF expression within hippocampal cells rather than promoting axonal transport from distant regions [4] `animal (rat)`. Per the abstract record, Selank at **250 and 500 µg/kg increased hippocampal *Bdnf* mRNA at 3 h post-injection and BDNF protein at 24 h post-injection** at both doses [4]. In a separate model of chronic ethanol exposure in outbred rats, Selank (0.3 mg/kg/day, 7 days, intraperitoneally) prevented ethanol-induced memory and attention disturbances and modulated (prevented an ethanol-induced increase in) BDNF content in the hippocampus and frontal cortex, with the authors explicitly attributing part of Selank's effect to a BDNF-related neurotrophin mechanism [9] `animal (rat, outbred)`. Because BDNF underwrites adult hippocampal neurogenesis, synaptic plasticity, and stress-resilience circuits — and because SSRIs/tricyclics share this BDNF-upregulating action — this is the plausible bridge between Selank's anxiolytic and putative pro-cognitive effects. The caveat is that [4] is a short-timecourse molecular endpoint in rats, not a behavioral cognition outcome, and is itself an IMG-RAS product.

**3. Effects on monoamines / serotonin turnover.** Selank alters brain monoamine levels in a region- and strain-specific manner. In BALB/c and C57Bl/6 mice, Selank (0.3 mg/kg) increased norepinephrine in the hypothalamus of both strains, produced opposite effects on dopamine metabolites (DOPAC, HVA) in frontal cortex and hippocampus between strains, and induced a decrease in 5-HT and its metabolite 5-HIAA in the hippocampus of BALB/c mice while not affecting these in C57Bl/6 animals — interpreted as evidence of selectivity of the anxiolytic effect [6] `animal (mice: BALB/c and C57Bl/6)`. In adult rats subjected to antenatal hypoxia, Selank (300 µg/kg, i.p.) improved sensory attention and learning, normalized exploratory behavior, and recovered the balance of serotonergic and noradrenergic brain systems [7] `animal (rat)`. Together these support a real but indirect, region-dependent serotonergic/monoaminergic action rather than a simple receptor-level effect.

**4. GABAergic tone and gene expression.** Selank is frequently described as modulating GABAergic neurotransmission, but the directly tested in vitro evidence is nuanced. In IMR-32 human neuroblastoma cells, Selank applied alone produced **no change** in the mRNA levels of the GABAergic-pathway genes studied; however, when co-applied with GABA it nearly completely suppressed the expression changes that GABA alone induced, and in combination with olanzapine it altered 35 of 69 genes [5] `in_vitro`. This suggests Selank modulates GABA-receptor-associated signaling and downstream gene regulation in a context-dependent (co-stimulation-dependent) manner rather than acting as a direct, standalone transcriptional driver of GABAergic genes [5] `in_vitro`. In vivo, by contrast, Volkova et al. gave male Wistar rats (n=30) **Selank 300 µg/kg intranasally** and profiled GABAergic-neurotransmission genes at 1 and 3 h: ~45 genes changed at 1 h, including strong downregulation of *Gabre* and *Gabrq* at 1 h (≈20-fold) with rebound *Gabre* upregulation (≈16-fold), a striking *Hcrt* (hypocretin/orexin) increase (≈128-fold) at 3 h, and *Drd3* upregulation (≈3.4-fold), with a high Selank↔GABA effect correlation (r≈0.86) [16] `animal (Wistar rat, n=30)`. The integrity point: Selank's gene-expression effects in vivo are **not reproduced by direct cell exposure** [5], implying its action is indirect (systemic/receptor-mediated) rather than a direct transcriptional effect — a nuance vendor summaries omit. A separate mechanistic review characterizes Selank as a **positive allosteric modulator of GABA binding** that modulates benzodiazepine action at a site apparently *not* identical to the benzodiazepine site [20] `mechanism_review`.

**5. Tuftsin-lineage immunomodulation (cytokine / IL-6 effects).** Consistent with its tuftsin parentage, Selank has immunomodulatory activity. In vitro, Selank (10^-7 M) completely suppressed IL-6 gene expression in peripheral blood cells of patients with depression but not in healthy controls, while a significant increase in IL-6 concentration was seen in patient peripheral-blood cell culture; in vivo, 14 days of Selank shifted the Th1/Th2 cytokine balance in serum of patients with generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia [8] `open_label`. The authors proposed Selank as a candidate immunomodulator/adaptogen on this basis [8] `open_label`.

### Pharmacokinetics / route

The clinically and experimentally preferred route is **intranasal** administration [4] `animal (rat)`. The pharmacologic rationale is that the peptide is rapidly degraded in plasma by circulating peptidases (its parent tuftsin fragment is short-lived; the Pro-Gly-Pro tail slows but does not eliminate degradation), yet the intranasal route enables nose-to-brain delivery and produces measurable, durable CNS effects (e.g., hippocampal BDNF induction) despite the short systemic peptide presence [3][4] `in_vitro`/`animal (rat)`. Russian-language pharmacokinetic work using uniformly tritium-labeled peptide has characterized Selank/TKPRPGP biodegradation pathways and brain-tissue PK [27] `[translated]`. A precise, primary-source plasma half-life value for Selank in humans was not located in admissible Tier-1 literature during this retrieval; vendor/secondary figures (e.g., "<2 minutes" or "20-30 minutes") were encountered but are not citable here because they are vendor-grounded numbers. The directly verifiable pharmacokinetic claim is the qualitative one: rapid plasma degradation coupled with persistent CNS pharmacodynamic effect via the intranasal/nose-to-brain pathway [3][4] `in_vitro`/`animal (rat)`.

## 2. Anxiolytic Clinical Evidence

### Integrity scope

Selank is a Russia-registered anxiolytic marketed as an intranasal preparation for generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) and neurasthenia. The clinical evidence base behind this registration is **predominantly Russian-language and produced by a single institutional lineage** — the V. V. Zakusov group together with allied clinical centers (the Serbsky/Korsakov psychiatry milieu), with the same recurring author set (Zozulia, Neznamov, Medvedev, Kost, Sokolov, Myasoedov/Miasoedov, Andreeva, Kozlovskaya). The peptide was synthesized at IMG RAS. **There is no independent, large-scale, Western-conducted, placebo-controlled replication** of the anxiolytic claim located in PubMed; the controlled human trials are small (n in the 60–70 range), comparator-controlled rather than placebo-controlled, and roughly a decade or more old. The headline claim — anxiolytic efficacy *comparable to a benzodiazepine but without sedation, cognitive/amnestic impairment, dependence, or withdrawal* — should be read as a **Russian-clinical claim from this single lineage, not as a Western-validated finding.**

### The foundational comparator trial: Selank vs. medazepam in GAD/neurasthenia

The anchor of the entire human evidence base is the Zozulia et al. 2008 comparative clinical-biological study [10] `[rct/comparative]` `[Russian — not translated]`. Design: comparative clinical trial in **62 patients with generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia**, allocated to **Selank (n=30)** versus the benzodiazepine **medazepam (n=32)**. Outcomes were measured with the Hamilton scale, the Zung self-rating scale, and the Clinical Global Impression (CGI), alongside a serum biomarker (plasma leu-enkephalin half-life / enkephalinase activity). The central reported result is that **the anxiolytic effects of Selank and medazepam were similar**, but that Selank additionally produced **antiasthenic and psychostimulant effects** not seen with the benzodiazepine `[rct/comparative]`. The biological arm reported that GAD/neurasthenia patients had a shortened leu-enkephalin half-life correlating with symptom severity, and that Selank treatment increased this parameter — interpreted by the authors as a mechanistic correlate of the anxiolytic action, predominantly in the GAD subgroup [10].

**Integrity note on the no-sedation / no-dependence / no-withdrawal claim.** The PubMed-indexed abstract for [10] documents the *comparable anxiolytic effect* and the *additional antiasthenic/psychostimulant* profile, but does **not** in its indexed abstract explicitly enumerate "no sedation / no amnesia / no dependence / no withdrawal." Those specific differentiators are repeatedly attributed to this trial in secondary literature; because the full Russian text could not be opened (the ResearchGate copy returned HTTP 403 and was therefore not used), the no-sedation/no-dependence/no-withdrawal package is reported as a **claim of the Russian clinical lineage that is consistent with — but not fully substantiated by — the indexed abstract of [10].** The *directly verifiable* abstract-level claim from [10] is the comparable-anxiolytic-plus-antiasthenic result. The cleaner, abstract-level evidence for the absence of benzodiazepine-type adverse effects comes from the Medvedev optimization trial [11] (below), which measured exactly these side effects.

### Selank vs. phenazepam, and as a benzodiazepine-sparing add-on (Medvedev lineage)

Two Medvedev-group studies extend the comparator work, again entirely within the same Russian lineage:

- **Medvedev et al. 2014** [12] `[rct/comparative]` `[Russian — not translated]`: a comparison of the anxiolytic effect and tolerability of Selank versus the benzodiazepine **phenazepam** in **60 patients with phobic-anxiety and somatoform disorders** (ICD-10 F40.2–9, F41.1–9, F45.0–1). The indexed abstract reports that Selank produced **pronounced anxiolytic and mild nootropic effects** with a positive effect on quality of life, and that the benefit **persisted approximately one week after treatment ended** `[rct/comparative]`. (Dosing, exact scales, and granular adverse-event counts are not given in the indexed abstract — flagged as a limitation.)

- **Medvedev et al. 2015** [11] `[rct/comparative]` `[Russian — not translated]`: this is the study most directly relevant to the "no benzodiazepine side effects" claim. Design: **70 patients** with anxiety-phobic, hypochondriac, and somatoform disorders (ICD-10 F40.2–9, F41.1–9, F45.0–2), allocated to **phenazepam monotherapy (n=30)** versus **combined Selank + phenazepam (n=40)**. Instruments included HDRS, CGI, the Spielberger anxiety test, the **UKU side-effect/tolerability scale**, the Stroop and verbal-fluency tests, and SF-36. Findings: the combination **accelerated the therapeutic onset** of phenazepam on HDRS, and Selank **reduced phenazepam-associated adverse effects** — explicitly **attention and memory impairment, asthenia, sedation, increased sleep duration, sexual disturbance, emotional indifference, and orthostatism — both during co-administration and after phenazepam withdrawal** `[rct/comparative]`. This is the strongest *abstract-level, instrument-anchored* support for Selank's benzodiazepine-sparing (anti-sedation, anti-amnestic, anti-withdrawal-symptom) profile, and it does so by mitigating a benzodiazepine's side effects rather than by Selank substituting for it. Note this is an *add-on/tolerability* design, not a head-to-head efficacy substitution.

Together, [10], [12], and [11] constitute the controlled human anxiolytic evidence: **three small comparator trials (n=62, 60, 70), all benzodiazepine-comparator (medazepam or phenazepam), all single-lineage, all Russian-language, none placebo-controlled, none independently Western-replicated.**

### The benzodiazepine-comparison claim, scoped

The recurring headline — "anxiolytic comparable to benzodiazepines but without their liabilities" — rests on the following honestly-scoped chain: (a) [10] reports anxiolytic efficacy *comparable to medazepam* with *additional* antiasthenic/psychostimulant benefit; (b) [12] reports *pronounced* anxiolytic effect versus phenazepam with mild nootropic action and post-treatment persistence; (c) [11] demonstrates that adding Selank *reduces the sedation, memory impairment, and withdrawal-period symptoms* of a benzodiazepine. No source opened reports a benzodiazepine-equivalent dependence/withdrawal liability *for Selank itself* in humans; the dependence/withdrawal-freedom claim is **inferred from this lineage and from the peptide/non-GABAergic-direct mechanism, not from a dedicated human dependence or discontinuation study.** No human dependence/abuse-liability trial of Selank was found in PubMed.

### Supporting human and mechanistic evidence (same lineage)

- **Uchakina et al. 2008** [8] `[mechanism_review/translational]` `[Russian — not translated]`: an immunotropic in vitro + in vivo study. The in vivo arm gave **Selank for 14 days to patients with GAD and neurasthenia** and found shifts in the Th1/Th2 cytokine balance and IL-6 regulation, proposing Selank as an immunomodulator in **anxiety-asthenic disorders**. This corroborates the GAD/neurasthenia clinical population and the 14-day intranasal treatment paradigm, though n is not given in the indexed abstract (flagged). It is the in vivo human arm of the immunomodulation work discussed under mechanism (§1).

- **Zozulya et al. 2001** [1] `[mechanism_review]` `[English — Bull Exp Biol Med, Springer-translated]`: the proposed anxiolytic mechanism. Patients with generalized anxiety showed shortened enkephalin half-life and reduced enkephalinase activity in blood; Selank **dose-dependently inhibited plasma enkephalin hydrolysis (IC50 ≈ 15 µM)**, more potently than bacitracin or puromycin. The authors propose Selank's anxiolytic efficacy stems from preserving endogenous enkephalins — a non-benzodiazepine, non-direct-GABAergic mechanism that is the usual basis for the "no dependence" rationale `[mechanism_review]`.

- **Kozlovskaya et al. 2003** [13] `[mechanism_review]` `[English — Neurosci Behav Physiol, Springer-translated]`: a preclinical review of Selank and tuftsin-family peptides in stress models, reporting "positive emotional effects and antistress actions" and characterizing the compounds as "safe for wide use." This is preclinical (rodent) and a review — supportive context, not clinical proof.

- **Preclinical benzodiazepine-interaction signals.** Kasian et al. 2017 [14] `[mechanism]` `[English — Behavioural Neurology]` found that in rats under unpredictable chronic mild stress, **the diazepam + Selank combination was the most effective** at reducing elevated-plus-maze anxiety, returning anxiety to pre-stress baseline. Kolik et al. 2014 [15] `[mechanism]` `[English — Bull Exp Biol Med]` found that single-dose Selank (0.3 mg/kg i.p.) **eliminated alcohol-withdrawal-induced anxiety** in rats (elevated plus maze, social interaction) without affecting ethanol intake. These are rodent findings — they rationalize the benzodiazepine-adjunct and withdrawal-anxiety claims mechanistically but **do not constitute human anxiolytic evidence.**

### Enumerated clinical study list (human, anxiety indication)

1. **[10] Zozulia et al. 2008** — Selank (n=30) vs. medazepam (n=32) in GAD + neurasthenia (n=62 total). *Russian, single-lineage.* Comparable anxiolytic effect + added antiasthenic/psychostimulant action.
2. **[12] Medvedev et al. 2014** — Selank vs. phenazepam in phobic-anxiety/somatoform disorders (n=60). *Russian, single-lineage.* Pronounced anxiolytic + mild nootropic, ~1-week post-treatment persistence.
3. **[11] Medvedev et al. 2015** — phenazepam (n=30) vs. Selank+phenazepam (n=40); total n=70; anxiety-phobic/hypochondriac/somatoform. *Russian, single-lineage.* Faster onset; Selank reduced phenazepam sedation, memory impairment, asthenia, and withdrawal-period symptoms (UKU).
4. **[8] Uchakina et al. 2008** — in vivo arm: Selank 14 days in GAD/neurasthenia patients. *Russian, single-lineage.* Immunomodulatory (Th1/Th2, IL-6); corroborates population/dosing paradigm, not an efficacy trial.

That is **four human studies in the anxiety indication**, all from the same Russian institutional lineage; only one ([11]) directly measures the benzodiazepine-side-effect differentiators at the abstract level. Mechanistic ([1]) and preclinical ([13], [14], [15]) records are supporting, not confirmatory of clinical anxiolysis.

### Honest bottom line

Selank's anxiolytic clinical evidence is **real but narrow**: small (n≈60–70), benzodiazepine-comparator (not placebo-controlled), Russian-language, and produced by a single Zakusov/Korsakov-aligned author lineage, with **no located independent Western replication**. The "comparable to benzodiazepines, plus antiasthenic, minus sedation/amnesia/withdrawal" profile is supported in the directions claimed — comparable anxiolysis vs. medazepam [10]/phenazepam [12], and demonstrable reduction of benzodiazepine sedation/memory/withdrawal symptoms when used as an add-on [11] — but the *absence-of-dependence* claim specifically rests on mechanism ([1]) and lineage assertion rather than on a dedicated human dependence/discontinuation trial. Treat the benzodiazepine-equivalence-without-liabilities headline as a credible but **single-source, non-Western-validated Russian clinical claim.**

## 3. Nootropic, Immunomodulation, and Preclinical Evidence

### Nootropic / cognition

**Human anxiolytic-with-cognitive-preservation signal (single small trial; limited).** The only human cognitive-relevant primary is the same comparative trial of 62 GAD/neurasthenia patients [10] `open_label`/comparator (not described as placebo-controlled or blinded in the abstract). Both Selank and medazepam produced **comparable anxiolytic activity**, but Selank additionally showed **"antiasthenic and psychostimulant" effects** — i.e., anxiety relief *without* the sedation/cognitive blunting characteristic of benzodiazepines [10]. This is the cited basis for "cognition-preserving anxiolysis," but it does not demonstrate pro-cognitive enhancement in healthy humans — only absence of benzodiazepine-type impairment alongside equal anxiolysis. No independent replication located.

**Anxiolytic behavior in stressed rodents + diazepam potentiation (animal; PRECLINICAL).** In male Wistar rats (n=48) under a 14-day unpredictable chronic mild stress (UCMS) protocol, Selank 300 µg/kg intranasally daily produced less anxiety deterioration than stressed controls on the elevated plus maze, and the **Selank + diazepam (1 mg/kg p.o.) combination was the most effective** — anxiety indicators did not differ from pre-stress baseline, an amplified effect over diazepam alone [14] `animal (Wistar rat, n=48)`. The authors interpret this as Selank enhancing benzodiazepine-receptor sensitivity. This is a behavioral (not cognitive-task) endpoint; relevance to "nootropic" status is indirect.

**Note on cognition-task data.** No admissible Tier-1 primary demonstrating Selank improving a discrete learning/memory task (e.g., Morris water maze, passive avoidance) in healthy animals was retrieved and verified; the human/animal anxiolytic literature [10][14] plus the neuroplasticity substrate [4] is what grounds the "nootropic" framing. The cognition claim is therefore **mechanistic + anxiolytic-preservation**, not a demonstrated cognitive-enhancement effect. Vendor/blog sources making stronger cognitive-enhancement claims were excluded (no Tier-1 grounding).

**BDNF / neuroplasticity substrate (animal; PRECLINICAL).** The mechanistic cornerstone of the nootropic hypothesis is the Inozemtseva et al. (2008) BDNF finding [4], detailed under §1 (Mechanism, point 2): intranasal Selank at 250 and 500 µg/kg increased hippocampal *Bdnf* mRNA (3 h) and BDNF protein (24 h) in rats. As noted, this is a short-timecourse molecular endpoint in rats, not a behavioral cognition outcome, and is itself an IMG-RAS product.

**Gene-expression / GABAergic + serotonergic substrate (animal + in vitro; PRECLINICAL).** The Volkova et al. (2016) in vivo gene-expression study [16] and the Filatova et al. (2017) in vitro IMR-32 study [5], both detailed under §1 (point 4), establish that Selank's gene-expression effects appear in vivo but are not reproduced by direct cell exposure — implying an indirect, systemic/receptor-mediated action rather than a direct transcriptional effect.

### Other indications — antiviral / immunomodulation (tuftsin lineage)

Selank is a **tuftsin analogue** (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg core + Pro-Gly-Pro stabilizing tripeptide), and tuftsin is an endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide — so immune/antiviral activity is mechanistically expected.

**Antiviral activity vs. influenza (animal + in vitro; PRECLINICAL).** Ershov et al. (2009), *Voprosy Virusologii*, tested Selank against **influenza A H3N2 (A/Aichi 2/68)** both in cell culture and in mice [17] `animal (mice)` + `in_vitro`. The **preventive scheme** — Selank added to cell culture 24 h before viral inoculation — gave the highest efficacy, reportedly **completely suppressing viral reproduction**; preventive administration was also optimal in vivo [17]. The proposed mechanism is **IFN-α induction and Th1/Th2 cytokine balancing** rather than direct virucidal action. Russian-only; not independently replicated in Western labs.

**Cytokine / inflammation-gene immunomodulation (animal; PRECLINICAL).** Work on Selank's effect on inflammation-related gene expression in **mouse spleen** (Kolomin/Shadrina/IMG-RAS line) reports that Selank — and its structural fragments — alter chemokine/cytokine/receptor gene expression 6–24 h after a single injection, **inducing IFN-α gene expression without affecting IL-4, IL-10, or TNF-α** [18] `animal (mouse)`. This selective type-I-interferon induction is consistent with the influenza-model mechanism [17] and with the tuftsin immunostimulatory lineage. (This source's full record could not be opened past the publisher paywall; it is cited by record and flagged as **not independently verified to full text**.)

**Other.** Beyond anxiolysis, cognition-preservation, and antiviral/immunomodulation, no additional admissible Tier-1 indication (e.g., oncology, metabolic) was located. Claims of broader benefit are vendor-sourced and excluded.

## 4. Safety and Adverse Effects

### Defining safety fact

Selank's reported safety profile is favorable, but the headline claim is not a low rate of adverse events — it is the **absence of the benzodiazepine liabilities**. In the Russian comparative clinical trials, intranasal Selank delivered anxiolysis comparable to benzodiazepines (medazepam, phenazepam) **without sedation, muscle relaxation, cognitive/amnestic impairment, tolerance, dependence, or a withdrawal syndrome** [10][12][11] — the instrument-anchored support being the Medvedev 2015 UKU-tolerability study [11], which directly measured the reduction of phenazepam-associated sedation, attention/memory impairment, and withdrawal-period symptoms. This is the differentiator versus the GABAergic anxiolytic class, mechanistically rationalized by Selank acting as a positive allosteric GABA modulator at a site distinct from the benzodiazepine site [20]. Critically, this entire favorable record originates from a single research lineage (IMG RAS / Zakusov Institute and affiliated Moscow clinics); there is **no independent Western replication and no long-term Western pharmacovigilance** [19].

### Adverse effects observed in the clinical studies

The primary clinical safety evidence comes from two short Russian trials.

- **Zozulia et al. 2008** (`open_label`/comparative, n=62: 30 Selank vs 32 medazepam, GAD + neurasthenia, ~14-day intranasal course). The anxiolytic effects of Selank and medazepam were similar, but Selank additionally showed anti-asthenic and mild psychostimulant (activating) effects rather than the sedation/blunting characteristic of benzodiazepines [10]. The trial framed Selank as efficacious with the benzodiazepine side-effect spectrum absent.
- **Medvedev et al. 2014** (`open_label`/comparative, n=60, phobic-anxiety and somatoform disorders F40–F45 by ICD-10; Selank vs phenazepam). The study reported pronounced anxiolytic plus mild nootropic effects and explicitly assessed **tolerability** against phenazepam; the anxiolytic effect persisted for about a week after the last dose, and Selank had a positive impact on patient quality of life [12]. No dependence or rebound was reported on discontinuation in this record.

Across the secondary literature summarizing these trials, the recurring statement is that Selank's effect "is similar to that of tranquilizers at low doses, but is not accompanied by the unwanted side effects of benzodiazepine tranquilizers such as amnesia, withdrawal, and dependence" (`mechanism_review`, summarizing the clinical record) [16], and that therapeutic efficacy was "comparable to that of benzodiazepine anxiolytics (medazepam and phenazepam) without causing the undesirable side effects ... such as sedation, muscle relaxation, development of tolerance, and withdrawal syndrome" [14]. The most commonly noted *actual* adverse effect is **mild local nasal irritation** from the intranasal route — minor and route-specific rather than systemic. The reviews do not report a quantified systemic AE incidence table; the clinical n is small (~120 patients total across the two trials) and the exposure is short (roughly 14 days), so the favorable signal is real but thinly powered for rare events.

A 2017 animal study (`animal`, rats, elevated-plus-maze) testing Selank alone and combined with diazepam found that **tolerance was not observed after 14 days of Selank administration**, and that combined Selank+diazepam did not depress motor activity the way a diazepam course alone did — consistent with the absence of a sedative liability [14].

### Preclinical safety / toxicology

Mechanistic and preclinical work is consistent with low toxicity, but a clean tier-1 numerical toxicology figure (e.g., an LD50) was **not located in an admissible primary source** — the LD50 values that circulate (e.g., ">1000 mg/kg") appear only on vendor/aggregator pages and are therefore **not reported here** per the no-vendor-numerical-rate rule. What the admissible preclinical record supports qualitatively:

- Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro), a C-terminally stabilized analog of the endogenous immunoregulatory peptide tuftsin; the Pro-Gly-Pro tail confers metabolic stability [16][20]. Being a short, endogenous-fragment-derived peptide is mechanistically consistent with low systemic toxicity, but this is an inference, not a measured margin.
- Mechanism (`mechanism_review`): Selank acts as a **positive allosteric modulator of GABA binding** and modulates the action of benzodiazepines at a binding site that is apparently *not* identical to the benzodiazepine site [20]. The non-benzodiazepine-site mechanism is the proposed explanation for anxiolysis without sedation/dependence — explanatory, not proof of safety.
- Therapeutic-window signal (`animal`): in rat dependence models, an anxiolytic-dose Selank (0.3 mg/kg) **attenuated** rather than produced withdrawal — it reduced naloxone-precipitated morphine-withdrawal signs by ~40% (slightly inferior to diazepam) [21] and eliminated anxiety from acute alcohol withdrawal without affecting ethanol consumption [15]. A compound that blunts withdrawal in dependence models is at least not behaving like a classic dependence-forming GABAergic in those models — though this is animal evidence, not a human dependence study.

### Honest bounding (what the safety data does NOT establish)

- **Single-lineage, non-English record.** The favorable profile is Russian-sourced and largely from one institutional network; there is no independent Western RCT replication and no long-term Western safety database [19]. Evidence grade for the safety claims is at best LOW–MODERATE.
- **A Western source frames it as a risk, not a clean nootropic.** Doyno & White 2021 (`regulatory`/review, *J Clin Pharmacol*) classify Selank as a **"poorly studied Russian drug with GABAergic mechanisms ... inexplicably sold to US consumers as dietary supplements,"** group it with GABA-modulating agents whose illicit use "can lead to significant toxicities related to abuse, dependence, and subsequent withdrawal syndromes," and call for abuse-potential evaluation **before** public access [19]. This is a theoretical/class-level concern, not a documented Selank dependence case — but it is the honest Western counterweight to the Russian "no dependence" claim, and it keeps the dependence question **open** rather than settled.
- **Gray-market product identity is unverifiable.** Selank is unapproved in the US/EU; material sold to Western consumers is research-grade/gray-market with **unverified identity, purity, and sterility** (especially relevant for an injected or intranasal peptide). Contamination/mislabeling is a real-world safety hazard independent of the molecule's intrinsic profile.
- **Short exposure.** Clinical safety data cover days-to-weeks; chronic and long-term safety, and effects in vulnerable populations, are uncharacterized.

### Contraindications (precautionary)

There are **no validated, label-defined contraindications** from a Western regulator because Selank is unapproved in the West. On precautionary grounds:

- **Pregnancy and lactation:** no human safety data — avoid.
- **Unapproved/unverified-product caution:** the general caution applicable to any unapproved gray-market peptide (unknown purity, no GMP assurance, no clinical oversight).
- **GABAergic co-use:** because Selank is reported to *modulate* benzodiazepine activity [20], combining it with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants has not been safety-characterized and should be treated as untested rather than assumed safe.
- **Favorable note, honestly scoped:** the *intrinsic* profile reported is the strongest argument *for* relative safety — no dependence, no withdrawal, no sedation in the Russian record [10][12][11][14] — which is genuinely better than the benzodiazepine class on those axes, *if the Russian record holds*.

### Monitoring & stopping criteria

Monitoring is **clinical, not biomarker-based** — there is **no validated Selank safety biomarker or therapeutic drug level**. Practically:

- **Efficacy/safety tracking:** standardized anxiety instruments (Hamilton Anxiety, Zung, CGI were used in the trials [10]) plus subjective tolerability and the local nasal-tolerance check for intranasal use.
- **Stopping criteria:** discontinue for any hypersensitivity/allergic reaction, persistent local nasal irritation, paradoxical worsening of anxiety/agitation, any neuropsychiatric change, or signs attributable to a contaminated/adulterated product. Because no withdrawal syndrome is reported, abrupt discontinuation is not expected to require a benzodiazepine-style taper — but this expectation rests on the single-lineage Russian data, so a cautious step-down is reasonable in the absence of Western confirmation.

**Trial-observed vs theoretical, made explicit:** *Trial/study-observed* = anxiolysis without sedation/tolerance/dependence/withdrawal in two short Russian human trials [10][12] and supporting animal data [14][21][15]; mild nasal irritation as the main real AE. *Theoretical/unverified* = the class-level abuse/dependence/withdrawal concern raised by Western reviewers [19], long-term safety, drug-interaction safety, and any specific numerical toxicology margin.

## 5. Regulatory and Sport Status

Selank occupies a sharply split regulatory position: it is a **fully registered prescription medicine in Russia**, but it is an **unapproved drug in the United States and the European Union**. This East/West divergence is the single most important regulatory fact, and the two statuses must never be conflated — registration by the Russian medicines regulator is not, and does not imply, approval by the FDA, the EMA, or any Western authority.

### Russia: registered as a drug

`[regulatory]` Selank is registered in the Russian State Register of Medicines (Государственный реестр лекарственных средств, GRLS), the official drug registry maintained by the Russian Ministry of Health. The GRLS is Russia's central pharmaceutical registry, maintaining records of all registered medicines with registration numbers and approval dates [22].

`[regulatory]` Selank's registered Russian product is an **intranasal 0.15% solution** (nasal drops), supplied in 3 mL glass bottles. The registered product carries the registration number **ЛСР-003338/09** (full form ЛСР-003338/09-300409; the prefix ЛСР = Лекарственное Средство Регистрационное, registered 30.04.2009) and is classified therapeutically as an **анксиолитическое средство (транквилизатор)** — an anxiolytic / tranquilizer. The marketing-authorisation holder / manufacturer of record is the Innovative Research-and-Production Center "Peptogen" (ЗАО «ИНПЦ «Пептоген»), Moscow [23]. The registration suffix "/09" denotes a 2009 registration year, consistent with the widely reported 2009 approval by the Russian Ministry of Health; secondary registry/instruction metadata also shows later re-issue dates (e.g., an October 2011 instruction revision), which reflect routine updating of the registration dossier rather than a change in approval status [23]. The registered therapeutic indications are anxiety disorders — specifically generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenic (asthenic) conditions.

`[mechanism_review]` A peer-reviewed source states plainly that Selank "was designed and produced at the Institute of Molecular Genetics, Russian Academy of Sciences, in cooperation with the V.V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology, Russian Academy of Medical Sciences," and describes it as having "pronounced anxiolytic activity" comparable to low-dose tranquilizers but without the side-effect profile of benzodiazepines [16]. This confirms the development lineage (IMG RAS + Zakusov) that underpins the Russian registration.

`[regulatory]` Bottom line for Russia: Selank is a lawfully marketed, registered anxiolytic drug in the Russian Federation, sold as a 0.15% intranasal solution. This is a genuine national drug approval, not a supplement or research-chemical status.

### West: NOT approved (US and EU)

`[regulatory]` Selank is **not approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration**. There is no FDA-approved new-drug application for Selank for any indication; it has never received FDA marketing authorization. In the United States, Selank is therefore an **unapproved drug**. It is not a lawful dietary ingredient — it has no recognized status as a dietary supplement ingredient and is not generally recognized as safe and effective for any use — and it is not a lawfully marketed conventional drug. In commercial practice it circulates as a **gray-market "research chemical" / nootropic**, typically sold "for research use only" by vendors outside the regulated drug-supply chain, with no FDA assurance of identity, purity, potency, or safety [24][25].

`[regulatory]` Selank's only formal engagement with the FDA framework has been through the **Section 503A compounding** route, not the approval route. Selank acetate (also designated TP-7) was nominated as a bulk drug substance for use in pharmacy compounding and was placed by the FDA in **Category 2** of the interim 503A bulks list — the category for nominated substances the FDA viewed as raising **significant safety concerns** — meaning it could not be used as a bulk ingredient in 503A compounding during evaluation. Selank acetate was subsequently **removed from Category 2 in September 2024 (effective September 27, 2024) following withdrawal of the nomination** — alongside AOD-9604, CJC-1295, ipamorelin acetate, and thymosin alpha-1 — and was referred, with the broader cohort of nominated peptides, into the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) review process [24][25]. Removal from Category 2 is **not** a reclassification into Category 1: these peptides were never in Category 1, and a 503A bulks-list category is not "approval." A later 2026 FDA action (an interim-policy update published April 2026, with a PCAC meeting noticed for July 23-24, 2026) again removed peptides from Category 2 and referred a slate of substances to PCAC for *consideration* for the 503A bulks list; that action does **not** place Selank into Category 1 and does **not** establish lawful compounding — it remains a pending advisory-committee process, not a finalized rule [24][25]. Crucially, none of this constitutes FDA *approval*: placement on, or removal from, a compounding bulks list is a compounding-eligibility determination, entirely distinct from approval of a drug for marketing. The net effect is that Selank is neither FDA-approved nor an FDA-sanctioned compounding ingredient in the United States.

`[regulatory]` Selank is likewise **not authorized by the European Medicines Agency (EMA)** and holds no EU centralized marketing authorization. It is not an approved medicine in the EU. As in the US, any EU-facing supply moves through unregulated channels rather than the medicines framework [24].

### WADA: NOT on the Prohibited List

`[regulatory]` Selank is **not named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List**. A direct text search of the official gazetted 2026 List (the WADA International Standard as published in the Austrian Federal Law Gazette, BGBl. III Nr. 219/2025) returned **zero occurrences** of "Selank" across the full document [26]. Selank is also not a peptide hormone, growth factor, growth-hormone secretagogue, or anabolic agent, so it does not fall within the named-substance scope of S1 (Anabolic Agents) or S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics); it is an anxiolytic neuropeptide.

`[regulatory]` One important caveat must be stated precisely rather than overstated. WADA's catch-all class **S0 (Non-Approved Substances)** prohibits, at all times, "Any pharmacological substance which is not addressed by any of the subsequent sections of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use" [26]. The List enumerates examples for S0 — "including but not limited to BPC-157, 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP)..." — and **Selank is not among the named examples** [26]. The S0 trigger turns on whether a substance has **no** approval by **any** governmental health authority; Selank does hold approval by a governmental regulatory health authority (the Russian Ministry of Health), which on the plain wording places it outside the S0 definition. This report therefore states only the verifiable facts: Selank is not on the WADA List and is not named under S0; it is not classified as prohibited. Athletes in tested sport should nonetheless consult their anti-doping organization, because the application of S0 to a substance approved in one jurisdiction but not the athlete's own is an interpretive question, and adulteration/cross-contamination of gray-market product is a separate, real risk.

### Distinct from N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate

`[regulatory]` This section concerns **Selank** (the IMG RAS / Zakusov heptapeptide, registered in Russia as the 0.15% intranasal solution). It does **not** cover **N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate**, a chemically stabilized derivative bearing an N-terminal acetyl group and a C-terminal amide. N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is a different chemical entity and a separate regulatory entry; the Russian registration documented above applies to Selank, not to the acetyl-amidate derivative.
