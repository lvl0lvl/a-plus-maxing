## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### Identity and structure

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide with the primary sequence **Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro (TKPRPGP)** [1][2]. Its defining structural feature is that it is an analogue of the endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide **tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg, TKPR)**, extended at the C-terminus with a **Pro-Gly-Pro (PGP)** tripeptide [1][9] `mechanism_review`. The Pro-Gly-Pro tail is the same stabilizing motif appended to the related peptide Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro); in both peptides the proline-rich C-terminal extension confers resistance to aminopeptidase and carboxypeptidase cleavage, dramatically increasing metabolic stability relative to the parent fragment [3] `in_vitro`. Tuftsin itself is a fragment of the heavy chain of immunoglobulin G and acts as a phagocytosis-stimulating immunomodulator; Selank inherits a portion of this tuftsin-lineage immunological activity (see Mechanism, below) [8] `mechanism_review`.

Selank was developed in Moscow as a joint effort of the **Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG RAS)** and the **V. V. Zakusov (Zakusov) Research Institute of Pharmacology** [4][9] `regulatory`. The IMG RAS affiliation is documented directly in the author institutional address of the in vivo BDNF study [4], and the Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology affiliation is documented in the author address of the BDNF/ethanol study [9] `regulatory`.

Selank should be kept **distinct** from two adjacent peptides frequently confused with it:
1. **Semax** (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) — a different IMG-RAS heptapeptide derived from an ACTH(4-7) fragment, also bearing the Pro-Gly-Pro stabilizing tail, but with a different N-terminal active fragment and a more nootropic (rather than primarily anxiolytic) profile [3] `in_vitro`.
2. **N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate** — an N-terminally acetylated, C-terminally amidated derivative of Selank, which is a distinct chemical entity and not the parent peptide described here.

The TKPRPGP sequence is confirmed in multiple independent primary sources [1][2][3], satisfying the defining-fact identity check.

### Mechanism of action

Selank's anxiolytic and nootropic actions are attributed to several converging, mechanistically distinct pathways. The five best-characterized are enumerated below.

**1. Inhibition of enkephalin-degrading enzymes (prolongation of endogenous enkephalins).** This is the most directly evidenced biochemical mechanism. In human plasma/serum assays, Selank dose-dependently inhibits the enzymatic hydrolysis of enkephalins. One report gives an **IC50 of 15 µM** for inhibition of plasma enkephalin hydrolysis [1] `in_vitro`; a companion study of human serum reports an **IC50 of approximately 20 µM** for Selank (versus ~10 µM for Semax), with both peptides being substantially more potent inhibitors than puromycin (IC50 ~10 mM) or bacitracin [3] `in_vitro`. The same human-serum work showed that the pentapeptide fragments of Selank and Semax retained inhibitory activity, whereas the tri-, tetra-, and hexapeptide fragments did not [3] `in_vitro`. Because these enzymes degrade not only enkephalins but other regulatory peptides, enkephalinase inhibition is proposed as a general mechanism of biological activity [3] `in_vitro`. The clinical correlate: patients with generalized anxiety showed shortened enkephalin half-life and reduced total enkephalinase activity in blood, which Selank's inhibitory action would be expected to counteract [1] `cohort`. In a strain-comparison animal study, Selank at **100 µg/kg** produced an anxiolytic effect in the open-field test and increased the half-life of plasma leu-enkephalin in BALB/c mice, while having no effect on behavior or enkephalinase activity in C57Bl/6 mice — indicating strain (phenotype)-dependent action [2] `animal (mice: BALB/c and C57Bl/6)`.

**2. Modulation of BDNF (neurotrophin) expression.** Intranasal administration of Selank to rats increased both BDNF mRNA and BDNF protein levels in the hippocampus in vivo, and the authors concluded that Selank stimulates BDNF expression within hippocampal cells rather than promoting axonal transport from distant regions [4] `animal (rat)`. In a separate model of chronic ethanol exposure in outbred rats, Selank (0.3 mg/kg/day, 7 days, intraperitoneally) prevented ethanol-induced memory and attention disturbances and modulated (prevented an ethanol-induced increase in) BDNF content in the hippocampus and frontal cortex, with the authors explicitly attributing part of Selank's effect to a BDNF-related neurotrophin mechanism [9] `animal (rat, outbred)`.

**3. Effects on monoamines / serotonin turnover.** Selank alters brain monoamine levels in a region- and strain-specific manner. In BALB/c and C57Bl/6 mice, Selank (0.3 mg/kg) increased norepinephrine in the hypothalamus of both strains, produced opposite effects on dopamine metabolites (DOPAC, HVA) in frontal cortex and hippocampus between strains, and induced a decrease in 5-HT and its metabolite 5-HIAA in the hippocampus of BALB/c mice while not affecting these in C57Bl/6 animals — interpreted as evidence of selectivity of the anxiolytic effect [6] `animal (mice: BALB/c and C57Bl/6)`. In adult rats subjected to antenatal hypoxia, Selank (300 µg/kg, i.p.) improved sensory attention and learning, normalized exploratory behavior, and recovered the balance of serotonergic and noradrenergic brain systems [7] `animal (rat)`. Together these support a real but indirect, region-dependent serotonergic/monoaminergic action rather than a simple receptor-level effect.

**4. GABAergic tone and gene expression.** Selank is frequently described as modulating GABAergic neurotransmission, but the directly tested in vitro evidence is nuanced. In IMR-32 human neuroblastoma cells, Selank applied alone produced **no change** in the mRNA levels of the GABAergic-pathway genes studied; however, when co-applied with GABA it nearly completely suppressed the expression changes that GABA alone induced, and in combination with olanzapine it altered 35 of 69 genes [5] `in_vitro`. This suggests Selank modulates GABA-receptor-associated signaling and downstream gene regulation in a context-dependent (co-stimulation-dependent) manner rather than acting as a direct, standalone transcriptional driver of GABAergic genes [5] `in_vitro`. More broadly, several of the cited studies frame Selank's behavioral effects as arising from broad gene-expression regulation in anxiety-relevant brain regions [4][9] `animal (rat)`.

**5. Tuftsin-lineage immunomodulation (cytokine / IL-6 effects).** Consistent with its tuftsin parentage, Selank has immunomodulatory activity. In vitro, Selank (10^-7 M) completely suppressed IL-6 gene expression in peripheral blood cells of patients with depression but not in healthy controls, while a significant increase in IL-6 concentration was seen in patient peripheral-blood cell culture; in vivo, 14 days of Selank shifted the Th1/Th2 cytokine balance in serum of patients with generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia [8] `open_label`. The authors proposed Selank as a candidate immunomodulator/adaptogen on this basis [8] `open_label`.

### Pharmacokinetics / route

The clinically and experimentally preferred route is **intranasal** administration [4] `animal (rat)`. The pharmacologic rationale is that the peptide is rapidly degraded in plasma by circulating peptidases (its parent tuftsin fragment is short-lived; the Pro-Gly-Pro tail slows but does not eliminate degradation), yet the intranasal route enables nose-to-brain delivery and produces measurable, durable CNS effects (e.g., hippocampal BDNF induction) despite the short systemic peptide presence [3][4] `in_vitro`/`animal (rat)`. A precise, primary-source plasma half-life value for Selank in humans was not located in admissible Tier-1 literature during this retrieval; vendor/secondary figures (e.g., "<2 minutes" or "20-30 minutes") were encountered but are not citable here because they are vendor-grounded numbers. The directly verifiable pharmacokinetic claim is the qualitative one: rapid plasma degradation coupled with persistent CNS pharmacodynamic effect via the intranasal/nose-to-brain pathway [3][4] `in_vitro`/`animal (rat)`.
