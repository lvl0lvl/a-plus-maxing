## Section B — Anxiolytic clinical evidence

### Defining fact and integrity scope

Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro; a stabilized analogue of the immunopeptide tuftsin) is a Russia-registered anxiolytic, marketed as an intranasal preparation for generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) and neurasthenia. The clinical evidence base behind this registration is **predominantly Russian-language and produced by a single institutional lineage** — the V. V. Zakusov (formerly "Zakusov"/"Research Institute of Pharmacology, RAMS") group together with allied clinical centers (the Serbsky/Korsakov psychiatry milieu), with the same recurring author set (Zozulia, Neznamov, Medvedev, Kost, Sokolov, Myasoedov/Miasoedov, Andreeva, Kozlovskaya). The peptide was synthesized at the Institute of Molecular Genetics (IMG RAS). **There is no independent, large-scale, Western-conducted, placebo-controlled replication** of the anxiolytic claim that I located in PubMed; the controlled human trials are small (n in the 60–70 range), comparator-controlled rather than placebo-controlled, and roughly a decade or more old. The headline claim — anxiolytic efficacy *comparable to a benzodiazepine but without sedation, cognitive/amnestic impairment, dependence, or withdrawal* — should be read as a **Russian-clinical claim from this single lineage, not as a Western-validated finding.** Each claim below is scoped accordingly. All cited records were opened and verified to author+year+journal via PubMed E-utilities; Russian-only sources are cited by full bibliographic record and flagged. No Wikipedia, vendor, or anecdote sources were used.

### The foundational comparator trial: Selank vs. medazepam in GAD/neurasthenia

The anchor of the entire human evidence base is the Zozulia et al. comparative clinical-biological study [1] `[rct/comparative]` `[Russian — not translated]`. Design: comparative clinical trial in **62 patients with generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia**, allocated to **Selank (n=30)** versus the benzodiazepine **medazepam (n=32)**. Outcomes were measured with the Hamilton scale, the Zung self-rating scale, and the Clinical Global Impression (CGI), alongside a serum biomarker (plasma leu-enkephalin half-life / enkephalinase activity). The central reported result is that **the anxiolytic effects of Selank and medazepam were similar**, but that Selank additionally produced **antiasthenic and psychostimulant effects** not seen with the benzodiazepine `[rct/comparative]`. The biological arm reported that GAD/neurasthenia patients had a shortened leu-enkephalin half-life correlating with symptom severity, and that Selank treatment increased this parameter — interpreted by the authors as a mechanistic correlate of the anxiolytic action, predominantly in the GAD subgroup [1].

**Integrity note on the no-sedation / no-dependence / no-withdrawal claim.** The PubMed-indexed abstract for [1] documents the *comparable anxiolytic effect* and the *additional antiasthenic/psychostimulant* profile, but does **not** in its indexed abstract explicitly enumerate "no sedation / no amnesia / no dependence / no withdrawal." Those specific differentiators are repeatedly attributed to this trial in secondary literature; because I could not open the full Russian text (the ResearchGate copy returned HTTP 403 and was therefore not used), I report the no-sedation/no-dependence/no-withdrawal package as a **claim of the Russian clinical lineage that is consistent with — but not fully substantiated by — the indexed abstract of [1].** The *directly verifiable* abstract-level claim from [1] is the comparable-anxiolytic-plus-antiasthenic result. The cleaner, abstract-level evidence for the absence of benzodiazepine-type adverse effects comes from the Medvedev optimization trial [2] (below), which measured exactly these side effects.

### Selank vs. phenazepam, and as a benzodiazepine-sparing add-on (Medvedev lineage)

Two Medvedev-group studies extend the comparator work, again entirely within the same Russian lineage:

- **Medvedev et al. 2014** [3] `[rct/comparative]` `[Russian — not translated]`: a comparison of the anxiolytic effect and tolerability of Selank versus the benzodiazepine **phenazepam** in **60 patients with phobic-anxiety and somatoform disorders** (ICD-10 F40.2–9, F41.1–9, F45.0–1). The indexed abstract reports that Selank produced **pronounced anxiolytic and mild nootropic effects** with a positive effect on quality of life, and that the benefit **persisted approximately one week after treatment ended** `[rct/comparative]`. (Dosing, exact scales, and granular adverse-event counts are not given in the indexed abstract — flagged as a limitation.)

- **Medvedev et al. 2015** [2] `[rct/comparative]` `[Russian — not translated]`: this is the study most directly relevant to the "no benzodiazepine side effects" claim. Design: **70 patients** with anxiety-phobic, hypochondriac, and somatoform disorders (ICD-10 F40.2–9, F41.1–9, F45.0–2), randomized/allocated to **phenazepam monotherapy (n=30)** versus **combined Selank + phenazepam (n=40)**. Instruments included HDRS, CGI, the Spielberger anxiety test, the **UKU side-effect/tolerability scale**, the Stroop and verbal-fluency tests, and SF-36. Findings: the combination **accelerated the therapeutic onset** of phenazepam on HDRS, and Selank **reduced phenazepam-associated adverse effects** — explicitly **attention and memory impairment, asthenia, sedation, increased sleep duration, sexual disturbance, emotional indifference, and orthostatism — both during co-administration and after phenazepam withdrawal** `[rct/comparative]`. This is the strongest *abstract-level, instrument-anchored* support for Selank's benzodiazepine-sparing (anti-sedation, anti-amnestic, anti-withdrawal-symptom) profile, and it does so by mitigating a benzodiazepine's side effects rather than by Selank substituting for it. Note this is an *add-on/tolerability* design, not a head-to-head efficacy substitution.

Together, [1], [3], and [2] constitute the controlled human anxiolytic evidence: **three small comparator trials (n=62, 60, 70), all benzodiazepine-comparator (medazepam or phenazepam), all single-lineage, all Russian-language, none placebo-controlled, none independently Western-replicated.**

### The benzodiazepine-comparison claim, scoped

The recurring headline — "anxiolytic comparable to benzodiazepines but without their liabilities" — rests on the following honestly-scoped chain: (a) [1] reports anxiolytic efficacy *comparable to medazepam* with *additional* antiasthenic/psychostimulant benefit; (b) [3] reports *pronounced* anxiolytic effect versus phenazepam with mild nootropic action and post-treatment persistence; (c) [2] demonstrates that adding Selank *reduces the sedation, memory impairment, and withdrawal-period symptoms* of a benzodiazepine. No source I opened reports a benzodiazepine-equivalent dependence/withdrawal liability *for Selank itself* in humans; the dependence/withdrawal-freedom claim is **inferred from this lineage and from the peptide/non-GABAergic-direct mechanism, not from a dedicated human dependence or discontinuation study.** I found no human dependence/abuse-liability trial of Selank in PubMed.

### Supporting human and mechanistic evidence (same lineage)

- **Uchakina et al. 2008** [4] `[mechanism_review/translational]` `[Russian — not translated]`: an immunotropic in vitro + in vivo study. The in vivo arm gave **Selank for 14 days to patients with GAD and neurasthenia** and found shifts in the Th1/Th2 cytokine balance and IL-6 regulation, proposing Selank as an immunomodulator in **anxiety-asthenic disorders**. This corroborates the GAD/neurasthenia clinical population and the 14-day intranasal treatment paradigm, though n is not given in the indexed abstract (flagged).

- **Zozulya et al. 2001** [5] `[mechanism_review]` `[English — Bull Exp Biol Med, Springer-translated]`: the proposed anxiolytic mechanism. Patients with generalized anxiety showed shortened enkephalin half-life and reduced enkephalinase activity in blood; Selank **dose-dependently inhibited plasma enkephalin hydrolysis (IC50 ≈ 15 µM)**, more potently than bacitracin or puromycin. The authors propose Selank's anxiolytic efficacy stems from preserving endogenous enkephalins — a non-benzodiazepine, non-direct-GABAergic mechanism that is the usual basis for the "no dependence" rationale `[mechanism_review]`.

- **Kozlovskaya et al. 2003** [6] `[mechanism_review]` `[English — Neurosci Behav Physiol, Springer-translated]`: a preclinical review of Selank and tuftsin-family peptides in stress models, reporting "positive emotional effects and antistress actions" and characterizing the compounds as "safe for wide use." This is preclinical (rodent) and a review — supportive context, not clinical proof.

- **Preclinical benzodiazepine-interaction signals.** Kasian et al. 2017 [7] `[mechanism]` `[English — Behavioural Neurology]` found that in rats under unpredictable chronic mild stress, **the diazepam + Selank combination was the most effective** at reducing elevated-plus-maze anxiety, returning anxiety to pre-stress baseline. Kolik et al. 2014 [8] `[mechanism]` `[English — Bull Exp Biol Med]` found that single-dose Selank (0.3 mg/kg i.p.) **eliminated alcohol-withdrawal-induced anxiety** in rats (elevated plus maze, social interaction) without affecting ethanol intake. These are rodent findings — they rationalize the benzodiazepine-adjunct and withdrawal-anxiety claims mechanistically but **do not constitute human anxiolytic evidence.**

### Enumerated clinical study list (human, anxiety indication)

1. **[1] Zozulia et al. 2008** — Selank (n=30) vs. medazepam (n=32) in GAD + neurasthenia (n=62 total). *Russian, single-lineage.* Comparable anxiolytic effect + added antiasthenic/psychostimulant action.
2. **[3] Medvedev et al. 2014** — Selank vs. phenazepam in phobic-anxiety/somatoform disorders (n=60). *Russian, single-lineage.* Pronounced anxiolytic + mild nootropic, ~1-week post-treatment persistence.
3. **[2] Medvedev et al. 2015** — phenazepam (n=30) vs. Selank+phenazepam (n=40); total n=70; anxiety-phobic/hypochondriac/somatoform. *Russian, single-lineage.* Faster onset; Selank reduced phenazepam sedation, memory impairment, asthenia, and withdrawal-period symptoms (UKU).
4. **[4] Uchakina et al. 2008** — in vivo arm: Selank 14 days in GAD/neurasthenia patients. *Russian, single-lineage.* Immunomodulatory (Th1/Th2, IL-6); corroborates population/dosing paradigm, not an efficacy trial.

That is **four human studies in the anxiety indication**, all from the same Russian institutional lineage; only one ([2]) directly measures the benzodiazepine-side-effect differentiators at the abstract level. Mechanistic ([5]) and preclinical ([6], [7], [8]) records are supporting, not confirmatory of clinical anxiolysis.

### Honest bottom line

Selank's anxiolytic clinical evidence is **real but narrow**: small (n≈60–70), benzodiazepine-comparator (not placebo-controlled), Russian-language, and produced by a single Zakusov/Korsakov-aligned author lineage, with **no located independent Western replication**. The "comparable to benzodiazepines, plus antiasthenic, minus sedation/amnesia/withdrawal" profile is supported in the directions claimed — comparable anxiolysis vs. medazepam [1]/phenazepam [3], and demonstrable reduction of benzodiazepine sedation/memory/withdrawal symptoms when used as an add-on [2] — but the *absence-of-dependence* claim specifically rests on mechanism ([5]) and lineage assertion rather than on a dedicated human dependence/discontinuation trial. Treat the benzodiazepine-equivalence-without-liabilities headline as a credible but **single-source, non-Western-validated Russian clinical claim.**
