## Section D — Safety & adverse effects

### Defining safety fact

Selank's reported safety profile is favorable, but the headline claim is not a low rate of adverse events in the abstract — it is the **absence of the benzodiazepine liabilities**. In the Russian comparative trials, intranasal Selank delivered anxiolysis comparable to benzodiazepines (medazepam, phenazepam) **without sedation, muscle relaxation, cognitive/amnestic impairment, tolerance, dependence, or a withdrawal syndrome** `[1][2][3]`. This is the differentiator versus the GABAergic anxiolytic class. Critically, this entire favorable record originates from a single research lineage (the Institute of Molecular Genetics / Zakusov Institute, Russian Academy of Sciences, and affiliated Moscow clinics); there is **no independent Western replication and no long-term Western pharmacovigilance** `[5]`. Every claim below should be read against that bound.

### Adverse effects observed in the clinical studies

The primary clinical safety evidence comes from two short Russian trials.

- **Zozulia et al. 2008 (`open_label`/comparative, n=62: 30 Selank vs 32 medazepam, GAD + neurasthenia, ~14-day intranasal course).** The anxiolytic effects of Selank and the benzodiazepine medazepam were similar, but Selank additionally showed anti-asthenic and mild psychostimulant (activating) effects rather than the sedation/blunting characteristic of benzodiazepines `[3]`. The trial framed Selank as efficacious with the benzodiazepine side-effect spectrum absent.
- **Medvedev et al. 2014 (`open_label`/comparative, n=60, phobic-anxiety and somatoform disorders F40–F45 by ICD-10; Selank vs phenazepam).** The study reported pronounced anxiolytic plus mild nootropic effects and explicitly assessed **tolerability** against phenazepam; the anxiolytic effect persisted for about a week after the last dose, and Selank had a positive impact on patient quality of life `[1]`. No dependence or rebound was reported on discontinuation in this record.

Across the secondary literature summarizing these trials, the recurring statement is that Selank's effect "is similar to that of tranquilizers at low doses, but is not accompanied by the unwanted side effects of benzodiazepine tranquilizers such as amnesia, withdrawal, and dependence" (`mechanism_review`, summarizing the clinical record) `[2]`, and that therapeutic efficacy was "comparable to that of benzodiazepine anxiolytics (medazepam and phenazepam) without causing the undesirable side effects ... such as sedation, muscle relaxation, development of tolerance, and withdrawal syndrome" `[4]`. The most commonly noted *actual* adverse effect is **mild local nasal irritation** from the intranasal route — minor and route-specific rather than systemic. The reviews do not report a quantified systemic AE incidence table; the clinical n is small (~120 patients total across the two trials) and the exposure is short (roughly 14 days), so the favorable signal is real but thinly powered for rare events.

A 2017 animal study (`animal`, rats, elevated-plus-maze) testing Selank alone and combined with diazepam found that **tolerance was not observed after 14 days of Selank administration**, and that combined Selank+diazepam did not depress motor activity the way a diazepam course alone did — consistent with the absence of a sedative liability `[4]`.

### Preclinical safety / toxicology

Mechanistic and preclinical work is consistent with low toxicity, but a clean tier-1 numerical toxicology figure (e.g., an LD50) was **not located in an admissible primary source** — the LD50 values that circulate (e.g., ">1000 mg/kg") appear only on vendor/aggregator pages and are therefore **not reported here** per the no-vendor-numerical-rate rule. What the admissible preclinical record supports qualitatively:

- Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro), a C-terminally stabilized analog of the endogenous immunoregulatory peptide tuftsin; the Pro-Gly-Pro tail confers metabolic stability `[2][6]`. Being a short, endogenous-fragment-derived peptide is mechanistically consistent with low systemic toxicity, but this is an inference, not a measured margin.
- Mechanism (`mechanism_review`): Selank acts as a **positive allosteric modulator of GABA binding** and modulates the action of benzodiazepines at a binding site that is apparently *not* identical to the benzodiazepine site `[6]`. The non-benzodiazepine-site mechanism is the proposed explanation for anxiolysis without sedation/dependence — explanatory, not proof of safety.
- Therapeutic-window signal (`animal`): in rat dependence models, an anxiolytic-dose Selank (0.3 mg/kg) **attenuated** rather than produced withdrawal — it reduced naloxone-precipitated morphine-withdrawal signs by ~40% (slightly inferior to diazepam) `[7]` and eliminated anxiety from acute alcohol withdrawal without affecting ethanol consumption `[8]`. A compound that blunts withdrawal in dependence models is at least not behaving like a classic dependence-forming GABAergic in those models — though this is animal evidence, not a human dependence study.

### Honest bounding (what the safety data does NOT establish)

- **Single-lineage, non-English record.** The favorable profile is Russian-sourced and largely from one institutional network; there is no independent Western RCT replication and no long-term Western safety database `[5]`. Evidence grade for the safety claims is at best LOW–MODERATE.
- **A Western source frames it as a risk, not a clean nootropic.** Doyno & White 2021 (`regulatory`/review, *J Clin Pharmacol*) classify Selank as a **"poorly studied Russian drug with GABAergic mechanisms ... inexplicably sold to US consumers as dietary supplements,"** group it with GABA-modulating agents whose illicit use "can lead to significant toxicities related to abuse, dependence, and subsequent withdrawal syndromes," and call for abuse-potential evaluation **before** public access `[5]`. This is a theoretical/class-level concern, not a documented Selank dependence case — but it is the honest Western counterweight to the Russian "no dependence" claim.
- **Gray-market product identity is unverifiable.** Selank is unapproved in the US/EU; material sold to Western consumers is research-grade/gray-market with **unverified identity, purity, and sterility** (especially relevant for an injected or intranasal peptide). Contamination/mislabeling is a real-world safety hazard independent of the molecule's intrinsic profile.
- **Short exposure.** Clinical safety data cover days-to-weeks; chronic and long-term safety, and effects in vulnerable populations, are uncharacterized.

### Contraindications (precautionary)

There are **no validated, label-defined contraindications** from a Western regulator because Selank is unapproved in the West. On precautionary grounds:
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** no human safety data — avoid.
- **Unapproved/unverified-product caution:** the general caution applicable to any unapproved gray-market peptide (unknown purity, no GMP assurance, no clinical oversight).
- **GABAergic co-use:** because Selank is reported to *modulate* benzodiazepine activity `[6]`, combining it with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants has not been safety-characterized and should be treated as untested rather than assumed safe.
- **Favorable note, honestly scoped:** the *intrinsic* profile reported is the strongest argument *for* relative safety — no dependence, no withdrawal, no sedation in the Russian record `[1][2][3][4]` — which is genuinely better than the benzodiazepine class on those axes, if the Russian record holds.

### Monitoring & stopping criteria

Monitoring is **clinical, not biomarker-based** — there is **no validated Selank safety biomarker or therapeutic drug level**. Practically:
- **Efficacy/safety tracking:** standardized anxiety instruments (Hamilton Anxiety, Zung, CGI were used in the trials `[3]`) plus subjective tolerability and the local nasal-tolerance check for intranasal use.
- **Stopping criteria:** discontinue for any hypersensitivity/allergic reaction, persistent local nasal irritation, paradoxical worsening of anxiety/agitation, any neuropsychiatric change, or signs attributable to a contaminated/adulterated product. Because no withdrawal syndrome is reported, abrupt discontinuation is not expected to require a benzodiazepine-style taper — but this expectation rests on the single-lineage Russian data, so a cautious step-down is reasonable in the absence of Western confirmation.

**Trial-observed vs theoretical, made explicit:** *Trial/study-observed* = anxiolysis without sedation/tolerance/dependence/withdrawal in two short Russian human trials `[1][3]` and supporting animal data `[4][7][8]`; mild nasal irritation as the main real AE. *Theoretical/unverified* = the class-level abuse/dependence/withdrawal concern raised by Western reviewers `[5]`, long-term safety, drug-interaction safety, and any specific numerical toxicology margin.
