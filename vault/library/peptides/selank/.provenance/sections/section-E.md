## Section E — Regulatory & sport status

Selank occupies a sharply split regulatory position: it is a **fully registered prescription medicine in Russia**, but it is an **unapproved drug in the United States and the European Union**. This East/West divergence is the single most important fact for anyone evaluating Selank, and the two statuses must never be conflated — registration by the Russian medicines regulator is not, and does not imply, approval by the FDA, the EMA, or any Western authority.

### Russia: registered as a drug

`[regulatory]` Selank is registered in the Russian State Register of Medicines (Государственный реестр лекарственных средств, GRLS), the official drug registry maintained by the Russian Ministry of Health. The GRLS is Russia's central pharmaceutical registry, maintaining records of all registered medicines with registration numbers and approval dates, publishing safety information, and managing the state register of maximum retail prices [1].

`[regulatory]` Selank's registered Russian product is an **intranasal 0.15% solution** (nasal drops), supplied in 3 mL glass bottles. The registered product carries the registration number **ЛСР-003338/09** (full form ЛСР-003338/09-300409; the prefix ЛСР = Лекарственное Средство Регистрационное, registered 30.04.2009) and is classified therapeutically as an **анксиолитическое средство (транквилизатор)** — an anxiolytic / tranquilizer. The marketing-authorisation holder / manufacturer of record is the Innovative Research-and-Production Center "Peptogen" (ЗАО «ИНПЦ «Пептоген»), Moscow [2]. The registration suffix "/09" denotes a 2009 registration year, consistent with the widely reported 2009 approval by the Russian Ministry of Health; secondary registry/instruction metadata also shows later re-issue dates (e.g., an October 2011 instruction revision), which reflect routine updating of the registration dossier rather than a change in approval status [2]. The registered therapeutic indications are anxiety disorders — specifically generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenic (asthenic) conditions.

`[mechanism_review]` The compound itself is a synthetic heptapeptide analog of the endogenous tetrapeptide tuftsin (the Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg fragment of the human immunoglobulin G heavy chain), extended with a C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide for metabolic stability. A peer-reviewed source states plainly that Selank "was designed and produced at the Institute of Molecular Genetics, Russian Academy of Sciences, in cooperation with the V.V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology, Russian Academy of Medical Sciences," and describes it as having "pronounced anxiolytic activity" comparable to low-dose tranquilizers but without the side-effect profile of benzodiazepines [3]. This confirms the development lineage (IMG RAS + Zakusov) that underpins the Russian registration.

`[regulatory]` Bottom line for Russia: Selank is a lawfully marketed, registered anxiolytic drug in the Russian Federation, sold as a 0.15% intranasal solution. This is a genuine national drug approval, not a supplement or research-chemical status.

### West: NOT approved (US and EU)

`[regulatory]` Selank is **not approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration**. There is no FDA-approved new-drug application for Selank for any indication; it has never received FDA marketing authorization. In the United States, Selank is therefore an **unapproved drug**. It is not a lawful dietary ingredient — it has no recognized status as a dietary supplement ingredient and is not generally recognized as safe and effective for any use — and it is not a lawfully marketed conventional drug. In commercial practice it circulates as a **gray-market "research chemical" / nootropic**, typically sold "for research use only" by vendors outside the regulated drug-supply chain, with no FDA assurance of identity, purity, potency, or safety [4][5].

`[regulatory]` Selank's only formal engagement with the FDA framework has been through the **Section 503A compounding** route, not the approval route. Selank acetate (also designated TP-7) was nominated as a bulk drug substance for use in pharmacy compounding and was placed by the FDA in **Category 2** of the interim 503A bulks list — the category for nominated substances the FDA viewed as raising **significant safety concerns** — meaning it could not be used as a bulk ingredient in 503A compounding during evaluation. Selank acetate was subsequently **removed from Category 2 in September 2024 (effective September 27, 2024) following withdrawal of the nomination** — alongside AOD-9604, CJC-1295, ipamorelin acetate, and thymosin alpha-1 — and was referred, with the broader cohort of nominated peptides, into the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) review process [4][5]. Removal from Category 2 is **not** a reclassification into Category 1: these peptides were never in Category 1, and a 503A bulks-list category is not "approval." A later 2026 FDA action (an interim-policy update published April 2026, with a PCAC meeting noticed for July 23-24, 2026) again removed peptides from Category 2 and referred a slate of substances to PCAC for *consideration* for the 503A bulks list; that action does **not** place Selank into Category 1 and does **not** establish lawful compounding — it remains a pending advisory-committee process, not a finalized rule [4][5]. Crucially, none of this constitutes FDA *approval*: placement on, or removal from, a compounding bulks list is a compounding-eligibility determination, entirely distinct from approval of a drug for marketing. The net effect is that Selank is neither FDA-approved nor an FDA-sanctioned compounding ingredient in the United States.

`[regulatory]` Selank is likewise **not authorized by the European Medicines Agency (EMA)** and holds no EU centralized marketing authorization. It is not an approved medicine in the EU. As in the US, any EU-facing supply moves through unregulated channels rather than the medicines framework.

### WADA: NOT on the Prohibited List

`[regulatory]` Selank is **not named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List**. A direct text search of the official gazetted 2026 List (the WADA International Standard as published in the Austrian Federal Law Gazette, BGBl. III Nr. 219/2025) returned **zero occurrences** of "Selank" across the full document [6]. Selank is also not a peptide hormone, growth factor, growth-hormone secretagogue, or anabolic agent, so it does not fall within the named-substance scope of S1 (Anabolic Agents) or S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics); it is an anxiolytic neuropeptide.

`[regulatory]` One important caveat must be stated precisely rather than overstated. WADA's catch-all class **S0 (Non-Approved Substances)** prohibits, at all times, "Any pharmacological substance which is not addressed by any of the subsequent sections of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use (e.g. drugs under pre-clinical or clinical development or discontinued, designer drugs, substances approved only for veterinary use)" [6]. The List enumerates examples for S0 — "including but not limited to BPC-157, 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP)..." — and **Selank is not among the named examples** [6]. The S0 trigger turns on whether a substance has **no** approval by **any** governmental health authority; Selank does hold approval by a governmental regulatory health authority (the Russian Ministry of Health), which on the plain wording places it outside the S0 definition. This report therefore states only the verifiable facts: Selank is not on the WADA List and is not named under S0; it is not classified as prohibited. Athletes in tested sport should nonetheless consult their anti-doping organization, because the application of S0 to a substance approved in one jurisdiction but not the athlete's own is an interpretive question, and adulteration/cross-contamination of gray-market product is a separate, real risk.

### Distinct from N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate

`[regulatory]` This section concerns **Selank** (the IMG RAS / Zakusov heptapeptide, registered in Russia as the 0.15% intranasal solution). It does **not** cover **N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate**, a chemically stabilized derivative bearing an N-terminal acetyl group and a C-terminal amide. N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is a different chemical entity and a separate regulatory entry; the Russian registration documented above applies to Selank, not to the acetyl-amidate derivative.

### Status summary (enumerated)

The regulatory positions verified in this section are, by jurisdiction (N = 4 distinct status determinations):

1. **Russia** — REGISTERED prescription drug; intranasal 0.15% solution; reg. no. ЛСР-003338/09; anxiolytic; ~2009 [1][2].
2. **United States (FDA)** — NOT approved; unapproved drug / gray-market research chemical; Selank acetate (TP-7) was 503A Category 2, removed Sept 2024 (eff. Sept 27, 2024) after nomination withdrawal and referred to PCAC; NOT placed in Category 1 and NOT a sanctioned compounding ingredient — never approved [4][5].
3. **European Union (EMA)** — NOT approved; no EU marketing authorization [4].
4. **WADA (2026)** — NOT on the Prohibited List; not named under S0; not classified as prohibited [6].
