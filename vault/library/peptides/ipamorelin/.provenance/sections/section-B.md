# Section B — Mechanism of Action (Ipamorelin)

Ipamorelin (Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2) is a synthetic pentapeptide growth-hormone secretagogue (GHS) that acts as an agonist at the growth-hormone-secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHS-R1a, the ghrelin receptor). Its defining mechanistic claim is *selective* GH release: it raises GH with a selectivity comparable to GHRH, without the ACTH/cortisol/prolactin co-stimulation seen with earlier GHRPs (GHRP-6, GHRP-2, hexarelin). The receptor-level signaling and the selectivity profile are well characterized; downstream IGF-1 elevation in humans is mechanistically expected (GH→hepatic IGF-1) but Ipamorelin-specific chronic human IGF-1 data are thin.

## Claims (numbered; inline [N, tag]; model/species+n)

1. Ipamorelin is a pentapeptide, sequence Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2, developed as a GH secretagogue [1, animal/in_vitro]. (in vitro: primary rat pituitary cells; in vivo: anesthetized Sprague-Dawley rats; conscious swine)

2. Ipamorelin is a selective agonist of the ghrelin/GHS-R1a receptor — it binds the same GHS-R1a receptor as ghrelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, and ibutamoren [5, mechanism_review]. As a GHS, it acts at the pituitary somatotroph (and hypothalamus) to drive GH release [3, mechanism_review].

3. GHS-R1a is a seven-transmembrane G-protein-coupled receptor; agonist binding couples primarily to Gαq/11, activating phospholipase C (PLC), which cleaves PIP2 into IP3 and DAG. IP3 mobilizes intracellular Ca2+ from ER stores; DAG activates PKC, which inhibits K+ channels, depolarizing the membrane and triggering voltage-gated Ca2+ influx. The resulting rise in intracellular Ca2+ drives GH-granule exocytosis from pituitary somatotrophs [3, mechanism_review]. (note: this is the canonical GHS-R1a transduction pathway; ipamorelin engages this shared receptor mechanism — claim 2.)

4. GHS-R1a exhibits unusually high constitutive (ligand-independent) activity; β-arrestin recruitment desensitizes G-protein signaling and targets the receptor for clathrin-mediated endocytosis [3, mechanism_review].

5. GHS-R/ghrelin-receptor expression is concentrated in the brain — greatest in the pituitary and in hypothalamic NPY/AgRP neurons — with lower peripheral expression (pancreas, spleen, kidney, adrenal) [4, mechanism_review].

6. **Selective GH release (foundational claim):** In rats, Ipamorelin did NOT release ACTH or cortisol at levels significantly different from GHRH, even at doses >200-fold above the GH ED50; and no significant effect on prolactin, FSH, LH, or TSH was seen for the secretagogues tested. By contrast, GHRP-6 and GHRP-2 elevated ACTH and cortisol. This makes Ipamorelin "the first selective GH secretagogue" [1, animal]. (anesthetized Sprague-Dawley rats and conscious swine; abstract did not state per-group n)

7. GH-releasing potency/efficacy (animal/in vitro): in vitro on primary rat pituitary cells, EC50 = 1.3 ± 0.4 nmol/L, Emax = 85 ± 5%; in pentobarbital-anesthetized rats, ED50 = 80 ± 42 nmol/kg, Emax ≈ 1545 ± 250 ng/mL; in conscious swine, ED50 = 2.3 nmol/kg. Potency/efficacy were comparable to GHRP-6 [1, animal/in_vitro].

8. **Human pharmacodynamics — pulsatile/single-pulse GH release:** In healthy male volunteers, a 15-min IV infusion of Ipamorelin produced a single discrete episode (pulse) of GH release peaking at ~0.67 h, declining exponentially to negligible levels at all doses — i.e., it elicits a single physiologic-style GH pulse rather than a sustained plateau [2, open_label/rct]. (40 healthy males, 8 per dose, 5 IV doses 4.21–140.45 nmol/kg; randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-escalation)

9. Human PK/PD: dose-proportional kinetics; terminal half-life ~2 h; clearance 0.078 L/h/kg; Vss 0.22 L/kg; half-maximal GH stimulation SC50 ≈ 214 nmol/L; modeled with an indirect-response (turnover) model [2, open_label/rct]. (n=40 healthy males) — the short half-life and single-pulse PD are consistent with pulsatile, self-limiting GH stimulation.

10. Selectivity vs other GHRPs (mechanistic synthesis): all bind GHS-R1a on somatotrophs, but GHRP-6/GHRP-2 also drive ACTH/cortisol elevation and GHRP-6/hexarelin appetite/prolactin effects; Ipamorelin's structure confers near-exclusive GH-selective action with HPA-axis sparing [1, animal][5, mechanism_review]. (Direct head-to-head ACTH/cortisol superiority is grounded in [1] (animal); the receptor-sharing/selectivity framing in [5].)

11. Downstream IGF-1: GH released by Ipamorelin is expected to stimulate hepatic IGF-1 (standard GH→IGF-1 axis); however, the strongest verified primary sources here characterize acute GH release and PK/PD, not chronic Ipamorelin-driven IGF-1 in humans. The body-composition/IGF-1 framing in reviews is weaker for Ipamorelin specifically [5, mechanism_review]. (HONEST LIMIT — see gaps)

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **Animal-vs-human selectivity:** The keystone "no cortisol/ACTH/prolactin/aldosterone" selectivity claim is grounded chiefly in Raun 1998 [1], which is RAT (and swine) data; aldosterone specifically is asserted in secondary/vendor sources, not verified in a primary human paper here. Human data [2] confirm GH PD/PK and single-pulse release but the abstract-level data accessed did not quantify a human ACTH/cortisol/prolactin panel head-to-head. Treat "HPA-axis sparing in humans" as mechanistically plausible and animal-supported, not definitively human-proven at the primary-source level reviewed.

- **IGF-1 downstream (human, chronic):** No verified primary source here measures sustained Ipamorelin-driven IGF-1 rises or body-composition endpoints in humans. The GH→IGF-1 axis is established generally, but Ipamorelin-specific chronic IGF-1 data were not located in admissible Tier-1/2 sources. Reviews [5] explicitly flag limited Ipamorelin-specific human evidence and even raise a GH-independent weight-gain finding in rodents — a caution against overstating the GH/IGF-1 story.

- **Pulsatile vs sustained:** [2] shows a single self-limiting GH pulse after acute IV dosing in humans — supports "pulsatile/physiologic" framing for acute dosing. Whether repeated dosing preserves natural pulsatility or risks somatotroph desensitization/receptor downregulation (β-arrestin internalization noted in [3]) is not directly characterized for Ipamorelin in the verified sources.

- **GHRP head-to-head:** Direct comparative human trials of Ipamorelin vs GHRP-6/hexarelin on the full hormone panel were not located in admissible sources; the comparison rests on Raun 1998 [1] (animal, same study) plus the receptor-sharing framing in [5].

- **Selectivity mechanism at molecular level:** Why a shared GHS-R1a agonist (Ipamorelin) avoids ACTH/cortisol co-release that GHRP-6/2 cause is not fully resolved mechanistically in the verified sources (biased agonism / differential downstream coupling is plausible but not confirmed here).
