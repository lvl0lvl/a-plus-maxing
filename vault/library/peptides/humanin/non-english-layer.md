# Humanin — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for Humanin (HN; mitochondrial-derived 24-amino-acid peptide, MT-RNR2 gene). Non-English sources are admissible under the same tier rules as English; language is noted on each entry. Every citation below was located via a retrievable identifier (PMID / DOI / stable journal URL). No citation is fabricated.

Key admissibility distinction applied throughout: a study **authored by researchers at a Japanese, Chinese, or Russian institution but published in English** (e.g., a PubMed-indexed English article in Molecular Neurobiology, Scientific Reports, or an international society journal) is NOT counted as a "genuinely non-English source" — it is already reachable by the English layer. Only articles whose **primary publication language is Russian, Chinese, or Japanese** are counted here as genuine non-English primaries. Humanin's discovery origin (Keio University, Tokyo, 2001) makes the Japanese and Chinese landscapes especially important to scrutinize: extensive institutional activity exists in both countries, but the question is whether any of it was published in the non-English press.

## Japanese (surveyed; citations or explicit "none located" + search terms/databases)

**Result: 2 genuinely Japanese-language admissible primaries located.** Both are from the originator lab (Nishimoto group, Keio University School of Medicine) and published in Japanese-language medical journals. Both are review/commentary articles, not in-vivo or clinical primary experiments.

### J1 — Niikura T, Hashimoto Y, Tajima H, Ito Y, Nishimoto I. 2003.

**Japanese Title (from PubMed):** [Neuronal cell death by Alzheimer's disease-relevant insults and its rescue]

**Journal:** 日本老年医学会雑誌 (*Nihon Ronen Igakkai Zasshi* / Japanese Journal of Geriatrics)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2003 Jan; 40(1):36–40

**PMID:** 12649845 | **DOI:** 10.3143/geriatrics.40.36

**Language:** Japanese (PubMed indexed as "[Article in Japanese]")

**Study type:** Review/invited article. Covers neuronal death mechanisms in FAD mutant models and the discovery of humanin as a suppressor of FAD mutant-induced and Aβ-induced neuronal death. No new in-vivo experimental data.

**Key mechanistic content:** Humanin "suppresses neuronal cell death caused by all known FAD mutants and Aβ while remaining inactive against unrelated neuronal death triggers"; mechanism described as "antagonism to neuronal death, not modulation of Aβ production."

**Population note:** Review; no animal or human subjects directly in this paper. Underlying data = cell-based (neuron culture) from cited English primaries.

**HNG-analog flag:** No HNG data in this article.

**Translation provenance:** Abstract and PubMed metadata rendered in English by NCBI; Japanese-language main text not retrieved but confirmed "[Article in Japanese]" by PubMed language indexing.

### J2 — Niikura T, Nishimoto I. 2004.

**Japanese Title:** アルツハイマー病における神経細胞死機序と細胞死抑制因子Humanin

**English translation of title:** Neuronal Cell Death Mechanisms in Alzheimer's Disease and the Cell Death Inhibitory Factor Humanin

**Journal:** 慶應医学 (*Keio Igaku* / Keio Journal of Medicine)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2004 Mar; 81(1):9–16

**Stable identifier:** CiNii CRID 1050845762328562944; NII論文ID 120001129269; ISSN 03685179. Full text also indexed in 医中誌 (Ichu-shi medical database) and IRDB. No DOI on the CiNii record; stable URL: https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1050845762328562944

**Language:** Japanese (CiNii language tag: Japanese; journal is a Japanese-language medical journal of Keio University School of Medicine)

**Study type:** Invited review (綜説). Covers FAD gene mutation-induced neuronal death, the cDNA screening methodology used to discover humanin, humanin as a secretable cell death suppression factor, structure–activity relationships, in-vivo expression, and binding molecule regulation.

**Key mechanistic content:** The review summarizes the 2001–2003 body of evidence from the Nishimoto lab, including: specificity of HN's cell death suppression activity (active against FAD mutants and Aβ, not unrelated insults); primary structure requirements (C-terminal region essential for activity); secretory nature of HN; and in-vivo expression in surviving neurons of AD brain.

**Population note:** Review; no new experimental subjects. Underlying data from English primaries (cell-based / transgenic mouse).

**HNG-analog flag:** Article predates HNG characterization as a separate compound; HNG not discussed.

**Translation provenance:** Title and section headings transcribed from CiNii metadata in Japanese. Abstract content summarized from CiNii record and WebFetch retrieval; main text language confirmed as Japanese by journal identity and CiNii language tag.

### Japanese landscape boundary note

Humanin was discovered at Keio University (Tokyo) in 2001. The originator lab (Ikuo Nishimoto and Yuichi Hashimoto) published their core 2001–2004 mechanistic work entirely in English (PNAS, Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications, Proc Natl Acad Sci, Journal of Neuroscience, Molecular Neurobiology). The two items above are the only Japanese-language publications the Nishimoto group produced on humanin — both are invited Japanese-language reviews summarizing work already published in English, not new primary experiments.

### C2 — Li LM, Qiao JT, Zhang C. 2009.

**Chinese Title (from PubMed):** [Effects of humanin on elevation of intracellular calcium concentration induced by beta-amyloid peptide(31-35) in cultured cortical neurons]

**Journal:** 生理学报 (*Sheng Li Xue Bao* / Acta Physiologica Sinica)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2009; 61(2):127–131

**PMID:** 19377823

**Language:** Chinese (PubMed: "[Article in Chinese]")

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vitro cell culture)

**Study population:** Cultured cortical neurons (rat, not specified strain); in vitro; no animal dose/group size recoverable from abstract.

**Key findings:** Humanin pretreatment significantly reduced the elevation of intracellular calcium concentration ([Ca2+]i) induced by Aβ(31-35) in cortical neurons; effect dose- and time-dependent; implicates calcium homeostasis disruption as a mechanism of Aβ neurotoxicity that humanin counters.

### C3 — Jin H, Hu HT, Wang WX, Feng GF, Li YY, Yang WN. 2010.

**Chinese Title (from PubMed):** [Effect of [Gly14]-Humanin on Abeta(25-35)-induced PC12 cell apoptosis]

**Journal:** 细胞与分子免疫学杂志 (*Xi Bao Yu Fen Zi Mian Yi Xue Za Zhi* / Chinese Journal of Cellular and Molecular Immunology)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2010 May; 26(5):427–430

**PMID:** 20423647

**Language:** Chinese (PubMed: "[Article in Chinese]")

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vitro cell line)

**Study population:** Rat PC12 cells (pheochromocytoma cell line); in vitro apoptosis model; HNG/humanin gene construct transfection + Aβ(25-35) exposure.

**Key findings:** HNG ([Gly14]-Humanin) overexpression significantly elevated PC12 cell viability and reduced apoptosis rates under Aβ(25-35) challenge; nuclear staining confirmed apoptotic morphology suppression; HNG-expressing cells maintained normal morphology vs. fragmented nuclei in controls. **HNG-analog flag: YES** — this is primary experimental data on HNG (the S14G superagonist analog).

**Translation provenance:** Abstract rendered in English by NCBI; main text confirmed as "[Article in Chinese]" by PubMed language indexing.

## Russian (surveyed; citations or explicit "none located" + search terms/databases)

**Result: 2 genuinely Russian-language admissible primaries located.** Both are original primary research articles published in Russian-language medical/genetics journals indexed in PubMed with confirmed "[Article in Russian]" or Russian-language journal identities.

### R1 — Maksimov VV, Arman IP, Tarantul VZ. 2006.

**Russian Title (from PubMed abstract):** [Identification of the proteins interacting with neuroprotective peptide humanin in a yeast two-hybrid system]

**Journal:** Генетика (*Genetika* / Russian Journal of Genetics)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2006 Feb; 42(2):274–277

**PMID:** 16583711

**Language:** Russian (PubMed English abstract available; main text in Russian; *Genetika* is the Russian-language flagship genetics journal of the Russian Academy of Sciences)

**Affiliation:** Institute of Molecular Genetics, Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow.

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (molecular biology)

**Experimental model:** Yeast two-hybrid system; human fetal brain cDNA library screening.

**Key findings:** Identified seven proteins that specifically interacted with humanin in the two-hybrid assay, aimed at elucidating the molecular basis of humanin's neuroprotective activity. This is the first (and, in the indexed literature, only) Russian primary molecular characterization of humanin binding partners.

**HNG-analog flag:** No HNG data; uses wild-type humanin as bait construct.

**Population note:** In vitro / yeast system + human fetal brain cDNA library (no animal or human subjects).

**Translation provenance:** Abstract rendered in English by NCBI; main text in Russian (journal *Genetika* publishes in Russian); Russian-language status confirmed by PubMed journal record.

### R2 — Zhloba AA, Subbotina TF, Molchan NS, Polushin YS. 2018.

**Russian Title (from PubMed):** [The level of circulating humanin in patients with ischemic heart disease]

**Journal:** Клиническая лабораторная диагностика (*Klin Lab Diagn* / Clinical Laboratory Diagnostics)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2018; 63(8):466–470

**PMID:** 30726649 | **DOI:** 10.18821/0869-2084-2018-63-8-466-470

**Language:** Russian (PubMed: "[Article in Russian]"; *Klin Lab Diagn* is a Russian-language clinical laboratory journal)

**Affiliation:** Saint Petersburg (Polushin YS is affiliated with the Military Medical Academy, St. Petersburg).

**Study type:** Primary observational / clinical study (human)

**Study population:** 59 patients with coronary heart disease (CHD); compared to healthy individuals. Human clinical sample.

**Key findings:** Circulating humanin levels in CHD patients: markedly reduced to ~250 ng/L vs. 1,110 ng/L (IQR 800–1,500) in healthy controls. Inverse correlation between humanin level and patient age. Low humanin co-occurred with elevated lactic acid and decreased homoarginine — indicating impaired mitochondrial aerobic metabolism in CHD. Authors proposed humanin as a potential new biomarker of metabolic compromise in ischemic heart disease.

**HNG-analog flag:** No HNG; measures endogenous circulating humanin in human serum.

**Population note:** Human (n=59 CHD patients). This is the only Russian-language human clinical humanin study in the indexed literature.

**Translation provenance:** Abstract rendered in English by NCBI; title given in PubMed as English translation of the Russian title; main text confirmed as "[Article in Russian]" by PubMed language indexing and journal identity.

## Originator-country note

Humanin was discovered at **Keio University, Tokyo** (Yuichi Hashimoto in Ikuo Nishimoto's lab, 2001), making Japan the originator country. Two Japanese-language primaries (J1, J2) were found — both invited reviews from the originator lab summarizing work already published in English. No new Japanese-language in-vivo or clinical primary was published by the originator group: their experimental work was published exclusively in English-language international journals (PNAS, J Neurosci, Biochem Biophys Res Commun, Mol Neurobiol). The non-English originator-country literature is therefore two review articles, contributing context but no experimental data beyond the English corpus.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**Japanese (2 admissible — both reviews):** The two Japanese-language articles are invited reviews from the Nishimoto/Niikura group at Keio, summarizing their own discovery-era English-published work. No new experimental findings appear in them. They establish: (a) the originator group considered humanin important enough to communicate to the Japanese-language medical audience early (2003–2004); (b) the Japanese-language scientific press carried humanin coverage from the start. They add no evidence not already in the English layer.

**Chinese (4 admissible — 1 review + 3 primaries):** This is the substantive yield. The three Chinese-language primary studies (C2, C3, C4) provide:
- Calcium homeostasis data: humanin suppresses Aβ(31-35)-induced intracellular Ca2+ elevation in cortical neurons (C2; in vitro)
- HNG apoptosis suppression: HNG overexpression protects PC12 cells from Aβ(25-35)-induced apoptosis (C3; in vitro) **— this is the only non-English HNG primary**
- HNG in-vivo behavioral data: HNG dose-dependently prevents Aβ(31-35)-induced spatial learning/memory impairment in rats (C4; in vivo) **— this is the only non-English in-vivo animal primary**

C4 is particularly significant: it constitutes the only Chinese-language in-vivo animal primary on HNG and demonstrates dose-dependent behavioral protection in a rat hippocampal model. These studies are from a Shanxi Medical University / Lanzhou University cluster that appears to have focused on the neuronal calcium and apoptosis pathways in the 2006–2012 window.

**Russian (2 admissible — both original primaries):** The two Russian primaries add distinct evidence not available in the same form from the English literature:
- Yeast two-hybrid binding partner identification (R1; ING RAS 2006): a targeted molecular screen identifying seven humanin-interacting proteins in human fetal brain. This approach is complementary to the receptor-binding studies in the English layer (FPRL1/FPR2, CNTFR, IGFBP3) and adds proteins not previously implicated.
- Human clinical circulating humanin data in CHD (R2; MMA St. Petersburg 2018): humanin serum levels in 59 CHD patients (~250 ng/L) vs. healthy controls (~1,110 ng/L), with age inverse-correlation. This is the only Russian-language human clinical humanin study and adds cardiovascular biomarker data that is complementary to the English-layer clinical studies on humanin in human aging populations.

The non-English layer therefore contributes three categories of evidence absent or sparse in the English corpus: in-vivo HNG behavioral protection in rats (Chinese), molecular binding partner identification via yeast two-hybrid (Russian), and human CHD circulating humanin levels (Russian).

## Translation handling notes

All non-English titles and abstracts reviewed in this pass were rendered by NCBI (PubMed English abstracts for Chinese and Russian articles) or by CiNii metadata for Japanese articles. Main texts of the non-English primaries were not fully retrieved (binary PDF or database-access restrictions). No numeric or mechanistic claim was extracted from a non-English main text without an English-language PubMed abstract to confirm it. All quantitative claims (e.g., humanin levels in R2; behavioral protection in C4) derive from the English-language PubMed abstracts of those articles. Japanese-language article content for J1 and J2 was confirmed as "[Article in Japanese]" by PubMed language indexing and CiNii language tag respectively; content summarized from available English-language abstracts and CiNii metadata.
