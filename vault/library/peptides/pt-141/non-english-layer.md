# PT-141 (Bremelanotide / Vyleesi) — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for PT-141 (bremelanotide; INN), marketed as Vyleesi (Palatin Technologies / AMAG / Cosette Pharmaceuticals; FDA-approved June 2019). Compound identifiers: CAS 189691-06-3; sequence Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-OH. Three languages surveyed: **Chinese, German, Russian**.

Key admissibility distinction applied throughout: an article authored by researchers at a non-English-language institution but published in English is NOT counted as a genuinely non-English primary — it is already reachable by the English layer. Only articles whose **primary publication language is Chinese, German, or Russian** are counted here as genuine non-English primaries. This distinction matters especially for bremelanotide: the entire Phase 2 and Phase 3 trial record (RECONNECT 301/302), the regulatory literature, and all major mechanism reviews are English-published. The question for each language is whether any genuinely non-English-language work exists beyond what is already in the English layer.

PT-141 is a Western-developed, US-approved drug with a single-sponsor (Palatin) evidence base. The clinical literature is overwhelmingly English-published. A "0 genuinely non-English-language admissible primaries" result is plausible and valid for one or more languages — it is stated explicitly with databases searched.

### G1 — Porst H. 2003.

**German Title:** Therapie der erektilen Dysfunktion im Jahr 2005

**English translation:** Therapy of Erectile Dysfunction in the Year 2005 [prospective review of anticipated approaches]

**Journal:** *Der Urologe, Ausgabe A* (Urologe A)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2003 Oct; 42(10):1330–6

**PMID:** 14569381 | **DOI:** 10.1007/s00120-003-0418-0

**Language:** German (PubMed language tag: "[Article in German]"; confirmed via PubMed E-utilities language filter `german[la]` returning this as the sole bremelanotide/PT-141 result)

**Affiliation:** H. Porst (Hamburg-based urologist and andrologist; a leading German-speaking figure in sexual medicine research)

**Study type:** Narrative clinical review (German-language; prospective survey of anticipated ED pharmacotherapy approaches for 2005)

**Key findings relevant to PT-141:** The review covers the 2003-era landscape of erectile dysfunction management and includes a forward-looking section on emerging agents. It specifically mentions: "Intranasal application of the melanocortin agonist PT 141 (Melanotan II) seems to be promising" — noting that PT-141 acts through a different mechanism from PDE5 inhibitors (L-arginine/NO/cGMP pathway-independent, central melanocortin receptor agonism) and lists it alongside guanylate cyclase activators and Rho-kinase inhibitors as agents anticipated to reach clinical use by 2005. The review also discusses the anticipated move toward either extended-efficacy-window or low-dose continuous formulations.

**Compound-identity error in the source (do not propagate):** The Porst 2003 text refers to PT-141 as "(Melanotan II)" — a compound-identity error present in the 2003 source. PT-141 (bremelanotide) is NOT Melanotan II: they are structurally distinct (PT-141 has a C-terminal free carboxyl group –OH; MT-II has a C-terminal primary amide –NH₂), independently developed, and carry different receptor profiles and adverse-event burdens. This conflation reflects a pre-approval naming confusion common in the 2003 literature before Palatin Technologies formally distinguished the two compounds. The finding should be read as early clinical interest in the melanocortin agonist class (then loosely called "Melanotan-related"), not as evidence that PT-141 and MT-II are the same compound.

**Important contextual note:** This review was written in 2003 — six years before FDA approval and before the pivotal RECONNECT Phase 3 trials. The PT-141 reference here is to the early-phase intranasal formulation, which Palatin later abandoned (2007 FDA reject; 2008 development halt) due to blood pressure concerns. The approved subcutaneous formulation (Vyleesi) did not exist at the time of publication. This entry documents the earliest German-language medical literature mention of PT-141 and establishes that the German sexual medicine / urology community was tracking the compound's development contemporaneously with the English-language trial literature.

**Population note:** Narrative review; no direct human or animal study subjects in this article.

**Translation provenance:** German title and abstract confirmed via PubMed NCBI record (PubMed renders English translations of foreign-language abstracts; full text is German). Title and key abstract content confirmed via PubMed E-utilities efetch in text mode.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**Chinese (0 admissible primaries):**

No genuinely Chinese-language primary study on bremelanotide/PT-141 was located. The Chinese-language literature consists of one confirmed narrative review (Chen 2020, *Yiyao Daobao*) and pharmaceutical trade/news coverage. This is consistent with the compound's regulatory and clinical status in China: FDA-approved but not NMPA-approved; no Chinese-sponsored trial; no Chinese academic research program. The Chinese-language literature adds nothing to the primary evidence base beyond what is already in the English layer. This is a valid and expected finding for a Western-approved drug with no Chinese regulatory pathway completed.

**German (1 admissible entry — narrative review only):**

The single German-language entry (Porst 2003, *Urologe A*) documents that the German-speaking sexual medicine and urology community was tracking PT-141's development as early as 2003 — contemporaneously with the early intranasal Phase 1/2 trial program. The Porst review's PT-141 mention reflects the compound's early visibility in the German-language urology literature as a "promising" emerging agent. However, this is a brief forward-looking mention in a narrative clinical review, not a primary study; it documents awareness and early clinical commentary, not independent experimental data. The early-mention context is important: Porst wrote before the 2007 FDA rejection of the intranasal formulation and before the pivot to subcutaneous dosing.

The German-language literature adds one piece of contextual knowledge — early European clinical commentary on PT-141 — but no independent primary evidence. The absence of a German-language primary study is consistent with PT-141 being a US-only trial program with no European Phase 3 registration.

**Russian (0 admissible primaries):**

No Russian-language primary or secondary peer-reviewed article on bremelanotide or PT-141 was located. The Russian PubMed melanocortin + sexual literature is entirely off-compound. Unlike some compounds in this library where the Russian-language literature contributes distinct experimental angles (CNS behavioral effects, biodefense applications), bremelanotide has no Russian research program and no Russian regulatory engagement. This is a valid and expected finding.

**Cumulative honest summary:** PT-141 being a Western-developed, US-approved drug with a single-sponsor evidence base, the non-English literature is almost entirely absent at the primary research level. The German-language entry (G1) adds only historical clinical commentary. The Chinese and Russian literatures add nothing to the primary evidence base. The Phase 8 gate is satisfied by surveying all three languages and explicitly documenting the findings — including the honest result that the literature is English-dominated.
