# N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate

## Metadata

- **class:** peptide (grey-market synthetic modified analog; terminally modified derivative of Selank, a Russian-registered synthetic heptapeptide Tuftsin analog)

## Summary

N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is the N-terminally acetylated, C-terminally amidated grey-market analog of **Selank** — the Russian-registered synthetic heptapeptide with sequence **Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro** (TKPRPGP; molecular formula C₃₃H₅₇N₁₁O₉; MW 751.89 g/mol; PubChem CID 11765600), itself a stabilized analog of the endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide **Tuftsin** developed at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG RAS) under Nikolai F. Myasoedov [1, mechanism_review]. This compound represents the thinnest-evidence entry in the peptide library. Six load-bearing facts define it:

**One: N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has zero analog-specific primary evidence.** A systematic search of PubMed, EuropePMC, Semantic Scholar, and indexed scientific databases identifies no clinical trials, no preclinical studies, and no pharmacokinetic characterization of this compound as a distinct chemical entity. Vendors themselves acknowledge the gap: "Studies on N-Acetyl Selank are lacking" and "no human studies have been performed on NA-Selank" [26, vendor_label], [27, anecdote_aggregate]. Everything cited for the analog's claimed effects is Selank parent data, extrapolated by assumption of pharmacological equivalence. That assumption is not validated.

**Two: The Selank parent's own evidence base is ~87–93% single-lineage.** Of approximately fifteen identifiable primary research groups and publications constituting the Selank evidence base, thirteen to fourteen carry authorship from IMG RAS and/or the V.V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology (RAMS) — the two co-developer institutions [18, mechanism_review]. Only the Medvedev/RUDN University group represents a clearly independent clinical lineage. The high-concentration flag threshold (≥70% single institute/lineage) is exceeded with substantial margin [19, mechanism_review]. This is not independent science; it is almost entirely author-continuous with the inventing institution and its direct co-development partner.

**Three: Selank's human evidence is small, Russian-only, and open-label.** The two principal human comparator trials enrolled 62 and 60 patients respectively, both conducted at Russian institutions, published in the Russian-language journal *Zhurnal Nevrologii i Psikhiatrii im. S.S. Korsakova*, and involving inventor co-authorship [4, rct], [9, open_label]. The one randomized comparator (Zozulia 2008, 30 Selank vs. 32 medazepam) [4, rct] is tier-2 at best: small n, open-label in its indexed abstract description, Russian regulatory context, and inventor co-authorship create a publication-incentive structure that Western regulatory reviewers would not accept as pivotal evidence. No Western RCT exists for Selank. The "regulatory peptide" paradigm under which Selank was developed is not a recognized mainstream-Western pharmacological framework.

**Four: The binding-epitope risk is real and analog-specific.** The Tuftsin receptor-binding literature establishes that the free α-amine of the N-terminal Thr residue is a key recognition element for Tuftsin-family receptor engagement [2, mechanism_review]. N-terminal acetylation eliminates this free amine. This means the N-acetyl modification could structurally disrupt the very binding epitope that mediates the parent compound's pharmacological activity — making the retention of Selank's effects in the analog not merely unverified but mechanistically uncertain even on first principles. No analog-specific binding assay has been published to resolve this.

**Five: Safety is Selank-extrapolated, unverified for the analog, and carries specific risks.** There is no direct safety data for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate. The Selank parent's clinical record is favorable in its reported tolerability across three small Russian trials [4, rct], [9, open_label], [20, rct], but carries no long-term safety data, no placebo-controlled AE reporting, and was conducted in clinical anxiety patients — not in healthy users seeking cognitive enhancement. Two risks are specific to the analog: (a) the grey-market formulation and unregulated manufacturing mean sterility and identity cannot be confirmed; and (b) repeated subcutaneous injection of a terminally modified peptide not endogenous to the organism carries immunogenicity risk not present in the registered intranasal Selank preparation. Additionally, the psychostimulant/activating effect documented as therapeutic in anxious patients is a two-sided signal in healthy off-label users: it is the pro-cognitive, focus-supporting quality that some healthy nootropic users actively seek, and yet the same activation can manifest as increased anxiety, sleep disruption, or agitation in users without the baseline anxiety disorder that characterized the studied population — a population-direction reversal where the same effect is sought in one context and adverse in another [4, rct].

**Six: Regulatory status and WADA are both unambiguous in one direction and uncertain in another.** Selank is Russia-registered (nasal spray, Selanc brand) for generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia, but is not FDA-approved or EMA-approved. N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is not approved anywhere in the world — not even in Russia. For WADA status: a 2026 peer-reviewed review in *Int J Mol Sci* states Selank and Semax are prohibited, classified as S2 or unapproved substances [21, mechanism_review]; however, (a) this claim could not be independently confirmed against the primary WADA Prohibited List document in this research run, and (b) S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances) is categorically non-obvious for a GABAergic anxiolytic — **S0 (Non-Approved Substances)** is the more clearly applicable category for any non-approved research peptide. Both uncertainties are load-bearing. Athletes subject to anti-doping rules must verify with their National Anti-Doping Organization (NADO) or GlobalDRO.com before any use.

The central honest tension: a grey-market terminal-modification analog with zero direct evidence of any kind, whose entire putative evidence base is a parent compound literature that is itself single-institute, Russian-only, small, open-label, and whose mechanism may be partially disrupted by the very modification designed to improve it.

## 1. Identity: Selank, the Tuftsin Family, and the Analog

### 1.1 The Tuftsin Foundation

Tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg) is an endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide derived by two-step proteolytic cleavage from the Fc region of IgG. First characterized by Najjar and colleagues, its structure–function pharmacology was systematically reviewed by Fridkin and Gottlieb [2, mechanism_review], who established it as a phagocytosis-stimulating tetrapeptide acting on specific receptors on mononuclear phagocytes and polymorphonuclear granulocytes. The critical pharmacological feature of Tuftsin for understanding the N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate analog is the receptor-binding epitope: the free α-amine of the N-terminal Thr residue is implicated as a key recognition element for Tuftsin-family receptor engagement [2, mechanism_review]. This structural fact has direct and non-trivial consequences for the acetylated analog discussed throughout this entry.

Tuftsin's limitation as a therapeutic agent is rapid enzymatic degradation — aminopeptidases cleave the N-terminal amine within minutes in plasma, and carboxypeptidases attack the C-terminal Arg. Its plasma half-life is measured in minutes, severely limiting any CNS bioavailability and making therapeutic use impractical without modification.

### 1.2 Selank: The Stabilized Heptapeptide Parent

Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro / TKPRPGP) extends the natural Tuftsin sequence with a C-terminal **Pro-Gly-Pro** tripeptide. This extension was designed at IMG RAS in the 1980s–1990s principally to resist the rapid enzymatic degradation that destroys Tuftsin within minutes in plasma — the proline-rich C-terminal tail impedes carboxypeptidase access and simultaneously enhances CNS bioavailability [3, in_vitro]. Selank has been registered in Russia as a pharmaceutical nasal spray (trade name Selanc, 0.15% intranasal solution) for generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia, making it one of only approximately 14 Russian-registered peptide drugs in the global ~70-drug registered peptide pharmaceutical space [18, mechanism_review].

As a Tuftsin analog, Selank retains the parent's immunomodulatory properties while gaining CNS-active effects through the Pro-Gly-Pro extension — a dual pharmacological profile that the originating research group developed as the defining feature of the "regulatory peptide" class [19, mechanism_review]. The Pro-Gly-Pro extension is not merely a stability strategy; it appears to be a functionally important pharmacophore element for at least some CNS mechanisms. In rat models, Tuftsin (the parent tetrapeptide alone) produced no brainstem serotonin enhancement, while Selank did — suggesting the C-terminal extension is necessary for at least the serotonergic component of the profile [8, animal].

The pharmacokinetics of Selank itself are incompletely characterized in the published literature. No peer-reviewed English-language study has published a confirmed intranasal half-life or bioavailability figure for Selank specifically. Following intranasal administration in mice, the compound is metabolized to a pentapeptide (TKPRP), tripeptide (TKP), and dipeptides (RP and GP), indicating sequential enzymatic cleavage [22, animal]. The rapid metabolite profile implies a short intact-peptide half-life. Vendor and grey-market sources cite ~92% intranasal bioavailability and a 20–30 minute half-life, but these figures trace to unpublished or Russian-language data not independently verified in indexed English-language sources. The nearest published pharmacokinetic data for a structurally related Russian tuftsin-analog heptapeptide is Boyko et al. (1998), a Russian-language rat PK study at the Institute of Pharmacology, RAMS, detecting the compound in plasma for 7–10 minutes [14, animal] — consistent with rapid enzymatic clearance but not directly measuring Selank.

### 1.3 N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate: The Analog and Its Two Modifications

N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate consists of the Selank heptapeptide sequence with two terminal chemical modifications:

**N-terminal acetylation:** An acetyl group (CH₃CO–) is added to the alpha-amine of the N-terminal Thr residue, replacing the free amine with an acetamide. Aminopeptidases — serine proteases that require a free N-terminal amino group for substrate recognition — are blocked by this modification. The protective effect is not trivial: an in vitro study using short peptide analogs in human serum found that N-terminal acetylation roughly doubled half-life in this particular system, with the doubly modified (acetyl-peptide-amide) form achieving the largest-than-additive benefit [12, in_vitro]. The magnitude is sequence- and charge-context-dependent; these reference values are from structurally unrelated antimicrobial peptides and establish mechanistic plausibility and rough order of magnitude, not a measurement for Selank specifically.

**C-terminal amidation:** The terminal carboxylate (–COOH) on the C-terminal Pro residue is replaced with a primary amide (–CONH₂). Carboxypeptidases require the free C-terminal carboxylate for recognition; amidation blocks this attack point and simultaneously removes a negative charge that may limit membrane partitioning. An N-acetylation study using supramolecular peptide nanofilaments found dramatically extended plasma half-lives for acetylated sequences (8.6–20.7 hours versus rapid degradation of non-acetylated forms), though the protective effect was charge-dependent rather than universal [13, in_vitro]. Again, structurally distinct peptides; order-of-magnitude context only.

**The binding-epitope uncertainty — the analog's most important unresolved pharmacological question.** The same N-terminal acetylation that blocks aminopeptidase attack eliminates the free α-amine of the Thr residue identified by Fridkin and Gottlieb [2, mechanism_review] as part of the Tuftsin receptor-binding epitope. Acetylating the N-terminal amine converts it from a primary amine (–NH₂) to an amide (–NHCOCH₃), fundamentally altering the electrostatic and steric presentation at the receptor interface. This means the stabilization benefit and the receptor-recognition mechanism are in potential conflict: N-terminal acetylation simultaneously protects the compound from aminopeptidase cleavage AND may disrupt the structural feature that makes Tuftsin-family compounds pharmacologically active at their receptors.

This is not a theoretical objection that the literature has ruled out. It is an unresolved empirical question: does N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate retain Selank's receptor-binding affinity, and at what fraction of the parent's potency? No published binding study, functional assay, or in vivo pharmacological comparison between Selank and its N-acetylated form has been conducted. The assumption of pharmacological equivalence underlying every vendor claim about this compound is therefore unvalidated at the receptor-interaction level and potentially contradicted by first-principle structural analysis.

A published investigation of N-terminal acetylation of Semax (the Russian nootropic ACTH-analog, discussed further in Section 3) found that acetylating Semax's free N-terminus altered copper(II) coordination geometry and eliminated redox reactivity — structural consequences at the molecular level that the authors did not translate into pharmacokinetic data but that illustrate how acetylation changes the molecule in ways that are not trivially "just stability" [15, in_vitro]. The Semax and Selank backbones are distinct, but the principle is consistent with the structural concern raised here for Selank's Tuftsin epitope.

**Critical finding:** A systematic search of PubMed, PubChem, EuropePMC, and the broader indexed scientific literature identified **zero peer-reviewed primary studies specifically on N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate** as a distinct compound. No clinical trial. No animal model. No pharmacokinetic study. No binding assay. Vendors acknowledge this explicitly: "There is a lack of research regarding the potential effects of N-Acetyl Selank Amidate" [26, vendor_label]. The compound's entire evidence base is Selank parent data, extrapolated by assumption. Every section below that appears to cite evidence for "effects" is citing evidence for the parent compound that is being extrapolated — not demonstrated — for the analog.

## 2. Selank Mechanisms: What the Parent's Literature Shows

Because N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has no independent mechanistic literature, everything in this section describes Selank's proposed mechanism in the Selank literature. The analog's mechanism is **assumed** to be equivalent; that assumption has not been tested.

### 2.1 Enkephalin-Degrading Enzyme Inhibition

The earliest and most directly human-biology-relevant proposed mechanism is inhibition of enkephalin-degrading enzymes — specifically aminopeptidase N (APN) and dipeptidyl peptidase IV (DPP-IV) — in plasma and CNS. Zozulya et al. (2001) [3, in_vitro] demonstrated in a mixed human/biochemical study that patients with GAD show shortened enkephalin half-life in plasma, and that Selank (TKPRPGP) inhibited enkephalin catabolism in a dose-dependent manner in vitro (IC₅₀ approximately 15 µM), exceeding the inhibitory potency of reference peptidase inhibitors bacitracin and puromycin at comparable concentrations. The parallel clinical observation in the 62-patient trial showed that Selank treatment increased enkephalin half-life parameters correlating with symptom improvement on the Hamilton and Zung scales [4, rct].

This mechanistic model proposes that Selank prolongs the action of endogenous anxiolytic opioid peptides — leu-enkephalin, met-enkephalin — without directly binding opioid receptors. The model is attractive because it provides a parsimonious account of how a peptide too large to readily cross the blood-brain barrier could nonetheless exert CNS effects: by inhibiting peripheral and mucosal enkephalin-degrading enzymes, it extends the effective half-life of circulating endogenous opioid peptides that then act on CNS targets. The mechanism is not established at the molecular detail level, and DPP-IV inhibition specifically would require careful separation from the glucose-metabolism effects of DPP-IV that are well-characterized in the diabetes literature — a separation the Selank literature does not perform.

### 2.2 GABAergic Modulation

Multiple converging lines of Selank research point toward modulation of GABAergic neurotransmission, though the mechanism appears indirect or conditional rather than direct GABA-A receptor binding.

Volkova et al. (2016) [5, animal] used rat frontal cortex transcriptomics (n=30 male Wistar rats, divided into control, Selank, and GABA groups of 10 each) and found that Selank administration produced significant changes in the expression of 45 GABA-related genes at 1 hour post-injection and 22 genes at 3 hours, including marked upregulation of *Gabre* (ε subunit), *Gabrq* (θ subunit), and *Hcrt* (hypocretin/orexin), with individual fold-changes ranging from approximately 13-fold to 128-fold for the most-affected genes. The strong positive correlation between Selank and GABA effects at 1 hour (r = 0.86) supports GABAergic system modulation as the operative framework. Notably, Selank did not produce the amnestic or sedative side effects typical of direct GABA-A agonists (benzodiazepines, barbiturates) — consistent with an indirect or allosteric modulating mechanism rather than receptor agonism.

A follow-up in vitro study in IMR-32 human neuroblastoma cells [6, in_vitro] found that Selank alone produced no significant changes in GABA-related gene expression, but markedly potentiated the gene-expression effects of co-administered olanzapine — a finding that suggests context-dependence. Selank's GABAergic influence may be conditional on co-stimulation or existing receptor tone rather than constitutively active in isolation. Electrophysiological evidence from ex vivo rat hippocampal CA1 pyramidal neuron slices showed increased amplitude and discharge rate of spontaneous inhibitory postsynaptic currents (sIPSCs) with Selank at 1–8 µM [7, animal], consistent with net enhancement of inhibitory GABAergic tone at the synaptic level, but obtained in an acute slice preparation that does not recapitulate the complexity of intact CNS regulation.

Both the in vivo gene-expression study [5, animal] and the in vitro neuroblastoma study [6, in_vitro] were published in *Frontiers in Pharmacology*, an indexed peer-reviewed journal that is also a high-volume open-access megajournal with above-average acceptance rates. These sources are not predatory, but they are explicitly tier-2 (lower-trust open-access, flagged in the bibliography) rather than tier-1 specialty-journal sources, and mechanistic claims from them should be weighted accordingly.

### 2.3 Serotonergic and Monoaminergic Effects

Semenova et al. (2010) [8, animal] demonstrated in 87 Wistar rats using a serotonin depletion model (PCPA treatment) that Selank at 300 µg/kg enhanced 5-HT metabolism in the brainstem at 30 minutes post-injection, with memory-consolidation benefits measured at 24 hours, 7 days, and 30 days follow-up. Critically, Tuftsin alone (the parent tetrapeptide) produced no brainstem serotonin enhancement — the C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro extension is necessary for this effect, establishing functional differentiation between parent and analog at the mechanistic level, and suggesting that the Pro-Gly-Pro tail is not merely a stability appendage but a pharmacophore for at least the serotonergic pathway.

Narkevich et al. (2008) [16, animal] used a strain-comparative mouse study (BALB/C and C57Bl/6, 0.3 mg/kg Selank) to document strain-specific monoamine effects: increased norepinephrine in the hypothalamus of both strains; increased dopamine metabolites (DOPAC, HVA) in C57Bl/6 but decreased them in BALB/C; decreased 5-HT and 5-HIAA in the hippocampus of BALB/C only. This strain-dependence is pharmacologically significant: Selank's monoamine effects interact with baseline genetic neurotransmitter tone and are not uniform across genetic backgrounds. Extrapolation from inbred mouse strain results to individual human users — who vary in monoaminergic baseline far more than two inbred strains — requires substantial caution.

### 2.4 BDNF Upregulation

Intranasal administration of Selank regulates BDNF expression in the rat hippocampus in vivo, as demonstrated by Inozemtseva et al. (2008) [17, animal] using reverse-transcriptase PCR and immunoenzyme assay of BDNF mRNA and protein in rat hippocampal tissue following intranasal Selank. This is primary molecular evidence for neurotrophin regulation as a downstream mechanism. BDNF upregulation in the hippocampus is consistent with the antiasthenic and nootropic aspects of Selank's reported clinical profile: BDNF supports synaptic plasticity, long-term potentiation, and hippocampal neurogenesis — mechanisms that would plausibly underpin cognitive and mood-related effects.

Kolik et al. (2019) [11, animal] corroborated this in a rat model of chronic ethanol exposure (30 weeks), finding that Selank prevented ethanol-induced dysregulation of BDNF in both the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex. While an ethanol-impairment model is not directly translatable to performance or anxiety effects in healthy users, it confirms BDNF regulation as a genuine pharmacological target of Selank in the CNS rather than an epiphenomenon.

### 2.5 Immunomodulatory Activity

As a Tuftsin analog, Selank retains partial immunomodulatory activity. Uchakina et al. (2008) [10, open_label] examined Selank's immunomodulatory effects alongside anxiolytic properties in patients with anxiety-asthenic disorders (published in *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S.S. Korsakova*, 2008; PMID 18577961), documenting modulation of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and a Th1/Th2 cytokine balance shift. Sample size is not reported in the indexed abstract and could not be retrieved from open-access routes. This immunomodulatory dimension distinguishes Selank from conventional anxiolytics: a GABAergic anxiolytic operating via enkephalin systems would not typically alter cytokine profiles, suggesting a bifurcated mechanism or a contribution from the Tuftsin-receptor engagement pathway that the C-terminal extension does not eliminate.

A mechanism review by Vyunova, Andreeva, Shevchenko, and Myasoedov (2018) [1, mechanism_review] in *Protein and Peptide Letters* synthesizes the pharmacokinetics, receptor interactions (GABAergic, serotonergic, enkephalinergic), and the structural basis of Selank's activity in English. This is the primary English-language mechanism synthesis for the compound family, though Myasoedov (inventor) is again an author — a recurrent conflict-of-interest pattern in this literature.

## 3. Anxiolytic and Nootropic Clinical Evidence for Selank (the Analog's Extrapolated Foundation)

### 3.1 Framing: Everything Here Is Parent Evidence

The human evidence reviewed in this section belongs entirely to the Selank parent compound, administered intranasally as a registered Russian pharmaceutical. N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has no human clinical evidence. No Selank finding in this section is attributed to the analog as demonstrated — it is extrapolated at best, and possibly disrupted by the N-terminal acetylation at the receptor-binding epitope level.

### 3.2 Foundational Comparative Efficacy Study (Zozulia et al., 2008)

The most-cited primary efficacy study enrolled **62 adults** — 30 receiving intranasal Selank and 32 receiving medazepam (a benzodiazepine) — with generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia [4, rct]. Conducted in Russia and published in Russian, the study assessed patient state with the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HARS), Zung Self-Rating Anxiety Scale, and Clinical Global Impression (CGI), alongside serum enkephalin activity. Both drugs produced comparable anxiolytic effects on rating scales. Selank additionally demonstrated antiasthenic and mild psychostimulant effects not seen with medazepam — a nootropic profile component. Selank-treated patients showed normalization of enkephalin half-life parameters that correlated with symptom improvement, consistent with the enkephalin-degrading enzyme inhibition mechanism described in Section 2.1. No sedation, tolerance, or dependence was documented in the study period.

This study is designated `rct` in the bibliography because it is a randomized two-arm comparator trial, but its tier-2 status reflects the substantial limitations: small n (30 vs. 32); Russian-language publication in a specialist Russian psychiatry journal not indexed in Western clinical databases with the same priority as NEJM, Lancet, or JAMA; no placebo arm (active-comparator design only); inventor co-authorship (Miasoedov NF is a listed author in the 14-member author list); blinding methodology not described in the indexed English abstract; conducted within the Russian regulatory framework that ultimately registered Selank — a context that creates structural publication incentives. These limitations do not render the study worthless, but they render it insufficient as standalone evidence by Western regulatory standards. It is proof-of-concept with methodological constraints.

### 3.3 Selank Versus Phenazepam (Medvedev et al., 2014)

A second comparative trial enrolled **60 patients** with phobic-anxiety and somatoform disorders (ICD-10: F40.2–F41.9, F45.0–1), spanning adolescents through middle age, comparing intranasal Selank against phenazepam — a Russian benzodiazepine with higher potency than medazepam [9, open_label]. Published in the same *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* journal in 2014, with Myasoedov NF again as a co-author, this study found pronounced anxiolytic and mild nootropic effects for Selank, with the anxiolytic benefit persisting approximately one week after the last dose. Positive effect on quality-of-life metrics was reported.

The one-week post-dose persistence finding is the most pharmacologically interesting claim from this study: for a short-half-life peptide, persistent therapeutic effect after discontinuation implies downstream neuroadaptation rather than direct receptor occupancy during benefit. However, this "neuroadaptation" is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism — in an open-label, inventor-co-authored study, expectation bias and regression to mean are equally plausible explanations for sustained subjective improvement after an unblinded therapeutic intervention. This study is classified `open_label` tier-2 because it lacks a described blinding protocol despite being a comparator trial.

The Medvedev/RUDN authorship is notable as representing the only clearly independent clinical lineage outside IMG RAS and the Zakusov Institute. Medvedev's co-author (Andriushchenko/Israelian) affiliates with RUDN University in Moscow — not a developer institution — making this study and the paired 2015 optimization paper [20, rct] the only approximately independent human clinical data in the entire Selank evidence base.

### 3.4 Optimization Study: Selank Combined with Phenazepam (Siuniakov et al., 2015)

A third Russian clinical trial — 70 patients distributed between Selank monotherapy (n=30) and Selank combined with phenazepam (n=40) — found that adding Selank to phenazepam reduced the benzodiazepine's own adverse effects [20, rct]. Selank attenuated attention and memory impairment, asthenia, sedation, increased sleep duration, sexual disturbances, emotional indifference, and orthostatic effects associated with phenazepam, while maintaining comparable anxiolytic efficacy. Quality of life was assessed by SF-36 and cognitive function by Stroop and verbal fluency tests; tolerability was assessed with the UKU Side Effect Rating Scale. This study is the principal source supporting the idea that Selank may be useful as an adjunct to benzodiazepine treatment for anxiety — a different framing than standalone anxiolytic use.

Classified `rct` based on described group assignment, but with the same structural limitations as the Zozulia study: small n, Russian language, Russian institution, no described double-blinding.

### 3.5 EEG Characterization: Response Subpopulations (Syunyakov et al., 2012)

A pharmaco-EEG study in **20 adults aged 24–52** with DSM-IV GAD characterized two response subtypes during intranasal Selank treatment at 2,700 µg/day [29, open_label]. Forty percent of patients showed rapid response within 1–3 days, with HARS total score reducing from approximately 20 to approximately 7 (p < 0.01); sixty percent showed gradual response by day 14. Rapid responders had greater baseline asthenic and cognitive symptom burden and stronger EEG reactivity to a single dose. This study provides mechanistic phenotyping rather than a placebo-controlled efficacy test. It was published as a conference abstract in *European Psychiatry* (the journal of the European Psychiatric Association) — an indexed Western-European outlet, which represents a higher-visibility signal than the Russian-language journals for this corpus, and the DOI is confirmed via Crossref (title, authors, year all match). Full text is Elsevier-paywalled and was not retrievable via open routes.

For the N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate analog specifically, the tier is not tier-2 — it is **absent**.

## 4. Modification Rationale, the Selank Already-Stabilized Backbone, and the Semax Sibling

### 4.1 The Parent's Own Stability Design

Before the grey-market modification, the originating researchers at IMG RAS already built metabolic stabilization into Selank's backbone. The C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide appended to Tuftsin's four-residue sequence was specifically designed to resist aminopeptidase attack on the N-terminus and slow carboxypeptidase access via the proline-terminated tail [5, animal]. Volkova et al. describe Selank as "elongated at the C terminus via the addition of three natural L-amino acids" to "improve its metabolic stability and yield a relatively longer duration" [5, animal]. All seven listed authors are affiliated with IMG RAS, Moscow.

The N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate modification layers on top of this already-stabilized backbone. The terminal modifications offer incremental exopeptidase protection at both termini — the N-terminal acetyl blocks the free-amine attack point, and the C-terminal amide blocks the carboxylate recognition site. But the net expected half-life gain is modest given that the already proline-rich termini of Selank make it more resistant to terminal cleavage than a typical heptapeptide: the dominant degradation pathway for a compound already equipped with proline-rich termini is internal peptide bond hydrolysis, which neither terminal modification affects. The compound's stability advantage over the parent is real in principle and unverified in measurement — and is smaller in magnitude than the same modification would produce on an unprotected linear peptide.

**No analog-specific pharmacokinetic data exist.** There are no published half-life, plasma stability, mucosal permeability, or blood-brain barrier penetration measurements for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate as a distinct compound. The claimed longer half-life is mechanistically reasonable but empirically undemonstrated [26, vendor_label], [27, anecdote_aggregate]. The nearest relevant published data — a rat PK study of a related Russian tuftsin-analog heptapeptide by Boyko et al. (1998) [14, animal] at the Institute of Pharmacology, RAMS — detected the compound in plasma for 7–10 minutes, establishing the baseline context for this compound class but not measuring Selank or its N-acetyl analog specifically.

### 4.2 The Russian "Regulatory Peptide" Class

Selank and Semax are the two flagship compounds from the same Soviet-era research program at what was then the Research Institute of Molecular Genetics of the USSR Academy of Sciences, run under the direction of academicians Igor Ashmarin and Nikolai Myasoedov beginning in the mid-1980s. The intellectual framework was the "regulatory peptide" or "neuropeptide analog" paradigm: identify a short naturally occurring regulatory peptide with established biological activity, then engineer a modified sequence that resists enzymatic clearance while preserving or enhancing receptor interaction [18, mechanism_review]. Deygin et al. (Pharmaceutics, 2022) document that Russia has registered approximately 14 of the roughly 70 global registered peptide drugs (~20%), with Selank and Semax among the 14 [18, mechanism_review]. This is a real programmatic achievement, but it is a Russian regulatory achievement, not a demonstration of the independent-replication-based evidence that Western regulatory frameworks require.

**Semax specifically.** Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) is an analog of the ACTH(4-10)/ACTH(4-7) fragment — a distinct peptide origin, a distinct primary pharmacological profile (primarily nootropic/cognitive stimulating, with BDNF upregulation as a proposed key mechanism), and a distinct receptor pharmacology from Selank. Like Selank, it terminates in the Pro-Gly-Pro stabilizing tail. Semax has Russian and Ukrainian registration for ischemic stroke, encephalopathy, and optic nerve atrophy. Neither Semax nor Selank is FDA-approved or EMA-approved [18, mechanism_review]. The grey-market research-chemical market has spawned parallel "N-Acetyl-...-Amidate" analogs of both — N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate and N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate — sold under the explicit naming convention to signal terminal stabilization modifications. The naming convention is a grey-market commercial invention, not nomenclature adopted by the originating Russian research groups [27, anecdote_aggregate].

Semax's evidence record belongs to Semax, not to Selank or to either analog. Evidence for one should not be attributed to the other.

### 4.3 The Single-Lineage Concentration: What ~87–93% Means

A full enumeration of the identifiable Selank primary literature yields the following institutional affiliation distribution [19, mechanism_review]:

| Myasoedov / Andreeva / Volkova / Shadrina et al. | Synthesis, BDNF, GABAergic gene expression, mechanism reviews | IMG RAS, Moscow |
| Inozemtseva / Dolotov / Grivennikov et al. | BDNF hippocampus, intranasal delivery | IMG RAS, Moscow |

Of approximately fifteen identifiable primary research groups/publications, thirteen to fourteen carry authorship from IMG RAS and/or the Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology — the two institutions that jointly developed and co-patented Selank. The Medvedev/RUDN group (two publications) is the only clearly independent clinical lineage outside the developer institutions. This gives an **IMG RAS + Zakusov lineage share of approximately 87–93%** of the primary Selank evidence base [19, mechanism_review]. The concentration flag threshold of ≥70% single institute/lineage is exceeded with substantial margin. **Verdict: HIGH concentration.** This is not independent science — it is an inventor-continuous literature where the same group developed, patented, conducted Phase I/II/III trials in Russia, and reviewed the evidence for the compounds it invented.

Western independent replication of the Selank/Semax evidence is essentially absent. Neither compound has undergone a randomized controlled trial conducted under FDA or EMA standards, in a Western patient population, with independent data monitoring [27, anecdote_aggregate]. The "regulatory peptide" paradigm as a scientific framework — the notion that short tuftsin or ACTH fragments with Pro-Gly-Pro stabilization produce clinically meaningful anxiolysis or nootropic effects — has not been adopted into any Western drug development program or clinical guideline. This does not constitute proof of inefficacy; the mechanistic hypotheses (GABAergic modulation, BDNF upregulation, enkephalin regulation) are scientifically plausible. But absence of independent replication is a real epistemic limitation that must be held proportionately.

## 5. Safety, Pharmacokinetics, Route, and Monitoring

### 5.1 The Fundamental Safety Framing

There is **no direct safety data for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate.** Every safety inference in this section is extrapolated from the Selank parent compound. The modifications — N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation — alter enzymatic recognition sites and are claimed to extend half-life; whether these modifications change the adverse-effect profile (including immune responses, receptor interaction profile, or metabolite spectrum) is entirely unstudied.

This extrapolation gap is not a minor methodological caveat to be absorbed into a confidence interval. It is the central safety unknown for this compound. The analog produces a different molecular surface than the parent at both termini — surfaces that interact with enzymatic recognition, immune recognition, and potentially receptor recognition. None of these interactions has been characterized for the analog.

### 5.2 Selank's Clinical Safety Record

Three small Russian clinical trials constitute the entire published human safety record for Selank — the parent compound from which safety is being extrapolated.

**Zozulia et al. 2008 (n=62):** The indexed abstract does not report specific adverse events, suggesting none were notable enough to headline in the comparator-to-medazepam framing [4, rct]. The consistent absence of sedation and the presence of antiasthenic/psychostimulant effects distinguish Selank's profile from classical benzodiazepines.

**Medvedev et al. 2014 (n=60):** Compared "tolerability" between Selank and phenazepam but the indexed abstract does not specify adverse event rates [9, open_label]. The one-week post-dose persistence of effect was interpreted as favorable, but in an open-label design this duration of reported benefit could reflect expectation bias and does not constitute a pharmacovigilance finding.

**Siuniakov et al. 2015 (n=70):** This is the most informative tolerability study because Selank was added to phenazepam and the UKU Side Effect Rating Scale was applied [20, rct]. Selank attenuated multiple phenazepam adverse effects — attention/memory impairment, asthenia, sedation, increased sleep duration, sexual disturbances, emotional indifference, orthostatic effects — without adding its own. Quality-of-life metrics improved. This study provides the strongest evidence for Selank's benign safety profile in a monitored clinical context, while still being a small, Russian, limited-blinding trial.

A 2026 review in *Int J Mol Sci* summarizes Selank as "non-sedative, non-addictive" with uncommon adverse effects described as mild daytime drowsiness or dry mouth [21, mechanism_review]. This is an MDPI review journal (lower-trust, higher acceptance rate) and its safety characterization should be treated as a secondary summary, not a primary source.

**Fundamental limitations of the safety record as a whole:** All trials are Russian-conducted, small (n ≤ 70), published in Russian-language journals, with no long-term safety data (beyond weeks), no placebo-controlled AE reporting with pre-specified endpoints, no Western regulatory safety data, and conducted in clinical anxiety patients not representative of broader healthy populations who might use the grey-market analog.

### 5.3 Cardiovascular Safety Signal

An acute study in anaesthetized cats administered intravenous Selank at 300 µg/kg and found a transient blood pressure decrease of approximately 32% in the first 1–3 minutes post-injection, normalizing thereafter; heart rate and respiratory rate were unaffected; cerebral blood flow increased approximately 24% transiently [23, animal]. This is an acute intravenous study in a terminal animal model and does not directly inform the clinical intranasal or subcutaneous route, but it is the only formal cardiovascular safety signal data available for this compound family and warrants awareness in users with baseline hypotension or concurrent antihypertensive use.

### 5.4 Pharmacokinetics of Selank and the Modification Claim

Following intranasal administration in mice, Selank's primary metabolites are the pentapeptide TKPRP, tripeptide TKP, and dipeptides RP and GP — products of sequential enzymatic cleavage [22, animal]. Intranasal delivery was established as the optimal route for glyproline-class CNS compounds across a four-route comparison (intranasal, intraperitoneal, intragastric, intravenous) in rats [24, animal]. Route-dependent pharmacological differences are documented: intranasal Selank affects NMDA receptor density (+23% binding sites in frontal cortex), while intraperitoneal administration preferentially affects GABAergic signaling (+38% GABA receptor binding in frontal cortex) — indicating distinct biotransformation pathways that produce different pharmacodynamic profiles depending on route [25, animal]. This route-pharmacodynamic interaction is pharmacologically significant: the grey-market analog is primarily used via subcutaneous injection or nasal spray, and these are not equivalent pharmacological interventions even for the parent compound, let alone for the untested analog.

No published half-life value for intact Selank appears in peer-reviewed English-language sources identified in this search. The rapid metabolite profile confirmed in brain tissue [22, animal] implies a short intact-peptide half-life consistent with Boyko et al.'s finding of 7–10 minute plasma detection for a related tuftsin-analog heptapeptide in rats [14, animal].

For N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate specifically: the N-terminal acetylation blocks aminopeptidase cleavage, and C-terminal amidation blocks carboxypeptidase cleavage — protecting both termini from the primary enzymatic attack points. This is the structural rationale for the claimed extended half-life. The rationale is biochemically plausible and consistent with the general peptide modification literature [12, in_vitro], [13, in_vitro], [26, vendor_label]. **It is unverified in any published PK study for this specific compound.** No pharmacokinetic data (half-life, C_max, T_max, bioavailability, brain penetration) exists in the published literature for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate.

### 5.5 Dosing and Route: Studied vs. Grey-Market

**Selank (studied doses and route):** The intranasal route is Selank's registered formulation and the route studied in human trials. Human trial doses are not specified in the available English-language abstracts — the full Russian-language text would be required for exact clinical dose values. The registered Russian formulation is a 0.15% intranasal drop solution (1.5 mg/mL). Animal study doses include 0.3 mg/kg/day intraperitoneally in rats [11, animal], [25, animal]; the acute cardiovascular cat study used 300 µg/kg IV [23, animal] — a research dose, not a therapeutic reference.

**N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate (grey-market — no citable dose):** Grey-market sources describe subcutaneous injection or nasal spray as the two common routes. No dosing number from any vendor or community source is cited in this entry because vendor_label and anecdote_aggregate sources cannot ground a clinical dose number per this document's sourcing standards. The compound exists commercially in lyophilized powder vials and pre-formulated nasal sprays from an array of US-based research-peptide vendors [26, vendor_label], [27, anecdote_aggregate], with varying volume and concentration by vendor. The claimed longer half-life relative to Selank is the stated rationale for preferring subcutaneous injection, which is argued to produce more sustained exposure. This claim is structurally plausible but not measured.

**Route-specific cautions for the analog:**
- If subcutaneous injection is used, sterility is entirely dependent on the manufacturing quality of an unregulated research-chemical supplier. The FDA's warning letters against peptide vendors — including US Chem Labs (February 2024) and Summit Research Peptides (December 2024) — cited unsubstantiated drug claims and dosing guidance rendering these products unapproved new drugs and misbranded [28, vendor_label].
- Unlike the registered Selank nasal drop formulation, N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has no pharmaceutical-grade manufacturing standard, no lot-testing requirement, and no regulatory body verifying identity, purity, or sterility.
- The registered intranasal route for Selank was also the route studied. Switching to subcutaneous injection changes the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profile in ways documented for the parent [25, animal] and entirely uncharacterized for the analog.

### 5.6 Regulatory Status

**Selank — Russia:** Registered by the Russian Federation Ministry of Health under the trade name Selanc (0.15% nasal drops) for generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia, with registration finalized around 2009 [19, mechanism_review], [4, rct]. Available in Russian and some CIS-country pharmacies as a prescription product.

**Selank — FDA/EMA:** NOT approved. No Investigational New Drug (IND) application on record in the FDA's publicly searchable drug database. Selank acetate (TP-7) was placed on the FDA's Category 2 interim 503A bulk substances list (significant safety risk designation, barring compounding-pharmacy use), and was removed from Category 2 in September 2024 after the nominator withdrew the nomination — it was not cleared; it moved to pending-evaluation status awaiting PCAC review [28, vendor_label]. Selank cannot be legally compounded in the United States because it is not an FDA-approved active pharmaceutical ingredient and is not on the FDA's bulk drug substances list for compounding.

**N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate:** NOT approved anywhere in the world — not in Russia, not in the European Union, not in the United States, not in any other jurisdiction. No pharmaceutical company has filed a development programme for this analog. There is no IND, NDA, EMA marketing authorisation, or equivalent. In the United States it occupies a research-chemical grey zone: not a scheduled controlled substance (no DEA Schedule listing identified), but also not a legally marketable drug for human use. Purchasing, possessing, and self-administering it occurs entirely outside any regulatory framework.

**WADA — doubly hedged:**

*Layer 1 — Source confirmation uncertainty:* A 2026 peer-reviewed review in *Int J Mol Sci* (PMC13164565) states that "WADA prohibits both [Semax and Selank], classifying them as peptides acting on the hormonal axis (category S2) or unapproved substances, for their potential neuroenhancement effect" [21, mechanism_review]. This claim cites reference 85 in that article; the full reference 85 was not accessible in the retrieved truncated reference list, and direct access to the WADA 2025/2026 Prohibited List PDF returned empty content in this research run. The prohibition claim must be treated as: reported in a 2026 peer-reviewed source but NOT independently confirmed against the primary WADA Prohibited List document in this research.

*Layer 2 — Category-assignment uncertainty:* Even if Selank/this analog is prohibited, classifying it under S2 ("Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics") is categorically non-obvious. WADA S2 was designed for compounds that modulate the hormonal axis — EPO, growth hormone, IGF-1, and their releasing factors. Selank is a GABAergic/anxiolytic heptapeptide derived from tuftsin with no documented hormonal axis activity; applying S2 requires a stretch of the category's stated rationale. The far more clearly applicable category for a non-approved research peptide with no regulatory standing is **WADA S0 ("Non-Approved Substances")**, which prohibits in-competition use of any pharmacological substance not approved by any governmental regulatory authority for human therapeutic use — a description that unambiguously covers N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate. S0 applies regardless of whether S2 does or does not apply. The review's framing should not be read as establishing S2 as the confirmed operative category; it may reflect the authors' own uncertainty.

*Athlete guidance:* Any individual subject to anti-doping rules must verify directly with their National Anti-Doping Organization (NADO) or via GlobalDRO.com before use. Do not rely on this document or the MDPI review's S2 framing as a definitive categorization.

### 5.7 Contraindications, Monitoring, and Stopping Criteria

**Absolute contraindications (extrapolated from Selank's mechanism and standard unregistered peptide principles):**
- Pregnancy and lactation: No safety data for Selank; contraindicated by default for any unregistered investigational compound in any individual who could become or is pregnant
- Known hypersensitivity to Selank, Tuftsin, or any component of grey-market formulations
- Concurrent use with benzodiazepines or other GABAergic CNS depressants without medical supervision: Selank potentiates GABAergic signaling; pharmacodynamic interaction is plausible even if additive sedation was reportedly mild in the phenazepam combination trial [20, rct]
- Active psychiatric conditions requiring monitored pharmacotherapy: self-administration of an unregistered research chemical in lieu of evidence-based care carries risks beyond the compound itself

**Population-direction reversal risk (specific to healthy users):** The psychostimulant/activating effect documented in the Zozulia 2008 trial [4, rct] has two distinct framings depending on the user population. In anxiety patients (the studied clinical population), it is THERAPEUTIC — it counteracts benzodiazepine-induced sedation and asthenia that are unwanted in that setting. This is also the effect that some healthy off-label users actively seek: the activating, pro-cognitive, focus-supporting dimension of Selank's profile is precisely what draws nootropic users to the compound, and for some that outcome may occur. However, in healthy users without baseline anxiety disorder, the same activation signal can instead manifest as unwanted anxiety, sleep disruption, or agitation — and the direction of effect in a non-anxious user cannot be assumed from clinical trial data obtained in anxious patients. This is the population-direction reversal: the effect that is therapeutic in one population may be adverse in another. Both the sought benefit and the possible adverse version arise from the same pharmacological signal; users cannot be certain which expression they will experience in the absence of the baseline anxiety disorder that characterized the studied population.

**Immunogenicity risk (specific to subcutaneous injection):** Repeated subcutaneous injection of a terminally modified peptide with no endogenous counterpart carries an immune-sensitization risk not present in intranasal Selank use and not addressed in any published safety data. The N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation create a molecular surface not recognized by normal self-tolerance mechanisms. Progressive injection-site reactions — increasing induration, urticaria, or systemic symptoms with repeated dosing — should be treated as a possible immune response to a non-self peptide hapten, not merely as a sterility failure, and constitute an absolute stopping criterion.

**Monitoring suggestions (minimum safety, not protocol-validated):**

No validated efficacy biomarker exists for Selank or this analog. The markers listed below are either general safety labs (CBC, CMP) or objective adjuncts for safety-and-mechanism-adjacent surveillance — none is a validated efficacy endpoint for anxiolytic or nootropic claims.

- Symptom diary tracking anxiety scores (e.g., GAD-7), cognition, sleep quality, and any adverse effects weekly; the one-week post-dose persistence effect from Medvedev 2014 [9, open_label] means benefit and adverse effects may outlast dosing periods
- Blood pressure: given the transient hypotensive signal in the anaesthetized cat cardiovascular study [23, animal], baseline and periodic blood-pressure checks are prudent, particularly in users with baseline hypotension or concurrent antihypertensives
- General safety labs at baseline: CBC, CMP (renal and hepatic function); not because hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity has been identified, but because no human safety data rules it out and a baseline establishes reference values if symptoms emerge
- Injection-site assessment if subcutaneous route is used: check for redness, induration, or abscess; any sign of infection is a stopping criterion given the sterility uncertainty of grey-market supplies
- Mental health monitoring: the documented psychostimulant effect could theoretically trigger or unmask anxiety in susceptible individuals; worsening anxiety should prompt discontinuation and reassessment
- **[[biomarkers/hs-crp]] (hs-CRP) — objective inflammatory/immune-sensitization surveillance:** The immunogenicity risk from repeated subcutaneous injection of a terminally modified non-endogenous peptide (described above) is the most specific safety signal for this route. hs-CRP is the appropriate objective inflammatory marker to track this risk: a rising hs-CRP trend on the injection schedule — absent an intercurrent infection or other identifiable cause — is the earliest laboratory signal of an immune-sensitization or subclinical inflammatory response to the peptide. Baseline hs-CRP before first injection, rechecked at 4–6 weeks of repeated dosing, is the minimum surveillance window. This is not an efficacy marker; it is an immune-safety adjunct for a risk the Selank clinical trials (intranasal route, endogenous-sequence-only modification) never had occasion to study.
- **[[biomarkers/cortisol]] — HPA/stress-axis mechanism-adjacent surveillance:** Selank's anxiolytic mechanism operates partly on the GABAergic and enkephalinergic axes, both of which interact with HPA-axis stress responses. Cortisol is not a validated efficacy biomarker for Selank; the Selank literature does not track it. However, as a mechanism-adjacent marker of the HPA/stress axis that the anxiolytic claim implicates, morning serum or salivary cortisol provides an objective correlate of stress-axis state. Significant HPA disruption (suppression or elevation trending outside normal range) in the context of use would be a clinically relevant observation, particularly given the healthy-user population-direction-reversal risk. This is a surveillance adjunct, not an efficacy endpoint.
- **[[biomarkers/hrv]] (Heart Rate Variability) — autonomic/anxiety surveillance:** HRV is a non-invasive continuous index of autonomic nervous system balance and is correlated with anxiety states, GABAergic tone, and parasympathetic activity — all relevant to Selank's proposed mechanism axis. Tracking HRV via consumer wearable (e.g., resting-state HRV at wake) provides an objective, time-series window on autonomic/anxiety state changes over the dosing period. Clinically meaningful HRV deterioration in the context of use (worsening autonomic dysregulation) would be an early objective signal complementing the subjective GAD-7 symptom diary. This is an autonomic surveillance adjunct, not a validated efficacy biomarker for Selank or any anxiolytic peptide.

**Stopping criteria:**
- Worsening anxiety or emergence of panic symptoms
- Any injection-site infection signs (fever, redness, purulence)
- Systemic signs following injection (urticaria, flushing, dyspnea — anaphylaxis cannot be excluded for any non-endogenous peptide in a sensitized individual)
- Significant sedation or cognitive slowing inconsistent with Selank's reported profile (raises concern about product identity or contamination)
- Any neurological symptoms not present at baseline
- If WADA prohibition is confirmed and the individual is subject to anti-doping rules: immediate cessation and NADO consultation

## 6. Concentration Audit, Grey-Market Commercialization, and Evidentiary Realism

### 6.1 The ~87–93% Single-Lineage Finding and Its Implications

The affiliation enumeration in Section 4.3 establishes that approximately 87–93% of the primary Selank evidence base traces to IMG RAS and/or the Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology — the co-developer institutions. This is not a coincidence or a measurement artifact; it is a structural feature of how Selank's development proceeded. Soviet-era and Russian Academy peptide pharmacology operated within a system where discovery, development, clinical testing, and regulatory approval were integrated within the same academic-government institutional complex. Myasoedov appears as an author in synthesis papers, mechanism reviews, clinical trials, and as a named inventor. Seredenin (director of the Zakusov Institute) appears in the clinical RCT alongside Myasoedov.

The practical consequence is that there is no body of independent Selank evidence to interrogate. When the originating inventor group reports a positive clinical finding, there is no independent group's contradictory or confirmatory data to triangulate against. The single published independent clinical source — Medvedev/RUDN — also reports favorable Selank findings but is a small Russian open-label study with its own limitations.

For consumers, this means the narrative confidence typically conveyed by "published in peer-reviewed journals" for Selank is substantially qualified by the single-lineage concentration of those publications. The journals are real; the studies are indexed; the peer review occurred. But the epistemic weight of findings from a single tightly author-continuous lineage — especially one where the developer, patent holder, and primary clinical researcher overlap — is categorically less than the same number of studies from independent groups in independent countries using independent patient populations.

### 6.2 The Analog Evidence Absence: A Category Below the Parent

For N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate, the evidentiary situation is a level below even the concentrated parent literature. There is no analog-specific primary literature from which a concentration can even be computed. The "evidence base" for this compound is 100% extrapolation from the parent Selank literature — which is itself ~87–93% concentrated in the developer institutional lineage. The compounding of extrapolation on top of single-lineage concentration on top of small-n open-label methodology creates an epistemic tower where confidence should attenuate at each level.

Multiple vendor and grey-market sources explicitly acknowledge this, and their admissions are among the most honest characterizations of the compound's status in any accessible source: "Studies on N-Acetyl Selank are lacking" [26, vendor_label]; "no human studies have been performed on NA-Selank" [27, anecdote_aggregate]; "data from Selank's research may be used as a reference" [27, anecdote_aggregate]. These are vendor-authored statements acknowledging that the product they sell has no direct supporting evidence. The honesty of the admission does not change the evidentiary fact it describes.

### 6.3 Grey-Market Commercialization

N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is commercially available from a proliferating set of US-based "research peptide" vendors, offered as lyophilized powder vials and as pre-formulated nasal sprays [26, vendor_label], [27, anecdote_aggregate]. Vendors uniformly claim ≥99% HPLC purity and third-party Certificates of Analysis, though the independence and analytical rigor of those certificates is unverifiable — no centrally accessible, independently validated purity database exists for these products.

Key commercialization dynamics:

**Identity conflation:** Marketing materials routinely present N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate as equivalent to or superior to Selank by virtue of improved stability, transferring the parent's Russian clinical data to the analog without evidentiary basis. Buyers sourcing the product for anxiety or cognitive purposes may not distinguish the analog from the parent compound. The Selank evidence base already requires substantial qualification to interpret honestly; that qualified evidence is then further diluted and over-attributed through the identity-conflation dynamic.

**Language barrier:** The foundational Selank clinical literature is in Russian, published in *Zhurnal Nevrologii i Psikhiatrii im. S.S. Korsakova*, and was conducted inside the Russian regulatory system [4, rct], [9, open_label], [20, rct]. Western buyers extrapolating to an unmodified analog are relying on a body of work they typically cannot read, produced by institutions they cannot independently audit, for a different molecule than the one they are using.

**No independent replication:** The Selank clinical programme — approximately 200–350 total patients across three published human trials — has never been independently replicated by a non-Russian, non-developer group in a Western regulatory context [27, anecdote_aggregate]. The analog adds a further extrapolation layer on top of this unverified foundation.

**FDA enforcement context:** The FDA has issued warning letters to US-based peptide vendors — US Chem Labs (February 2024) and Summit Research Peptides (December 2024) — citing unsubstantiated drug claims and dosing guidance that render these products unapproved new drugs and misbranded [28, vendor_label]. The grey-market peptide vendor ecosystem operates with regulatory risk for both vendors and consumers. This is not specific to Selank or its analog, but it is the commercial context in which these products are obtained.

## 7. Pharmacological Class: The Sibling Comparison and What It Means

Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) sits alongside Selank as the second flagship compound of the same Soviet-era IMG RAS research program [18, mechanism_review]. Its evidence base has the same fundamental structure as Selank's — primarily Russian, primarily single-lineage, primarily small open-label comparator trials — and it carries the same regulatory status abroad (not FDA/EMA approved, Russian/Ukrainian registered). The two compounds are sometimes discussed interchangeably in grey-market nootropic communities as if their evidence bases were interchangeable. They are not.

Semax operates via ACTH receptor pharmacology with downstream melanocortin receptor engagement; Selank operates via Tuftsin receptor/enkephalin system/GABAergic pathways. Their receptor profiles, metabolites, and reported effects are distinct. A published structural study of N-terminal acetylation of Semax [15, in_vitro] found that acetylating Semax's free N-terminus altered copper(II) coordination geometry and eliminated redox reactivity — structural perturbations that, while not directly translating to half-life or receptor affinity, illustrate that N-terminal acetylation is not pharmacologically neutral for this peptide class. Semax-specific findings must be attributed to Semax; they do not transfer to Selank or to either analog.

The "N-Acetyl-...-Amidate" naming convention is a grey-market commercial invention applied in parallel to both flagship compounds, positioning both as "enhanced stability" variants of their Russian parents. The convention has no scientific basis for inferring parallel evidence strength — the two analogs each have their own evidence gap, and the gap is not narrowed by the convention's consistency.

## 8. Honest Bottom Line

N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is a grey-market research chemical with three compounding epistemic problems:

**Problem 1 — No direct evidence at the compound level.** Zero peer-reviewed studies exist for this specific compound. The evidence tier for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate-specific claims is absent, not tier-2 or tier-3. Claims about its anxiolytic, nootropic, or immunomodulatory effects cannot be grounded in its own literature because that literature does not exist.

**Problem 2 — The extrapolated parent evidence is itself constrained.** Selank's human clinical evidence — the foundation being extrapolated — consists of three small Russian open-label comparator trials totaling under 200 patients, all conducted within the same institutional lineage that invented the compound, published in Russian, and never independently replicated outside Russia. The parent evidence is real but limited, and limited in a direction (single-lineage, small-n, limited-blinding, no Western replication) that requires proportionate confidence discounting.

**Problem 3 — The analog may disrupt the parent's mechanism.** N-terminal acetylation eliminates the free α-amine of the Thr residue identified as part of the Tuftsin receptor-binding epitope — the very structural feature that Selank inherits from Tuftsin as the mechanistic basis for its receptor engagement. The stability benefit and the pharmacological mechanism are in potential conflict. This is not a confirmed disruption (no analog-specific binding study has been conducted), but it is a plausible first-principle concern that has not been ruled out.

For any individual considering use: the theoretical pharmacological rationale is coherent, the parent compound's evidence suggests plausible anxiolytic activity within the studied population, and the modification logic is standard medicinal chemistry. But the compound being purchased is not the studied compound, its pharmacological equivalence is assumed not demonstrated, its receptor activity may be partially disrupted, no PK data characterizes what it does in the body, no safety data characterizes its risk profile directly, grey-market manufacturing provides no quality assurance, and the regulatory environment globally gives it no standing. These are not minor qualifications — they are the fundamental conditions of use.
