# N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate — the N-terminally acetylated, C-terminally amidated grey-market analog of Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro; TKPRPGP), a Russian-registered synthetic heptapeptide analog of Tuftsin developed at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG RAS) under Nikolai F. Myasoedov. Three languages surveyed: Russian (PRIMARY — Selank is a Russian compound; the bulk of the literature exists in Russian-language journals), Chinese, and German.

**Critical disambiguation applied throughout:** N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is a Western grey-market coinage. This compound is NOT a Russian research entity and does NOT appear by that name (or any near-equivalent) in Russian, Chinese, or German scientific literature. Search confirmed: zero publications exist in any language on the analog as a distinct chemical entity. All non-English primary literature located is Selank (parent compound) literature. This layer documents the parent's Russian-language record — the relevant scientific foundation — while explicitly noting that the analog is absent from it.

**Key admissibility distinction applied throughout:** A study authored by IMG RAS or Zakusov Institute researchers and published in an English-language international journal (e.g., *Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine* English edition via Springer, *Neurochemical Journal*, *Doklady Biochemistry and Biophysics*) is NOT counted as a genuinely non-English source — it belongs to the English layer. Only articles whose **primary publication language is Russian** (or another non-English language) are counted here as genuine non-English primaries.

**Translation provenance note for this compound:** Several key Russian-language Selank journals have no English-translation counterpart indexed in PubMed. *Eksp Klin Farmakol* (Experimental and Clinical Pharmacology) publishes in Russian with English abstracts via NCBI only — no published English-translation edition. *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S.S. Korsakova* (Journal of Neurology and Psychiatry named for S.S. Korsakov) is a leading Russian clinical neurology/psychiatry journal that does not issue a systematically translated English edition; NCBI carries English abstracts only. *Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat Im I P Pavlova* (Journal of Higher Nervous Activity named for I.P. Pavlov) publishes in Russian; its English content on NCBI is abstracts only. *Ross Fiziol Zh Im I M Sechenova* (Russian Journal of Physiology named for I.M. Sechenov) is Russian-language with no PubMed-indexed English translation edition. *Izv Akad Nauk Ser Biol* (Proceedings of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Biological Series) has a Springer English-translation counterpart (*Biology Bulletin*) but the indexed PubMed entries are the Russian originals.

**Result: 0 publications on N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate as a distinct chemical entity in any language — Russian, Chinese, German, or English.**

PubMed searches for "N-acetyl selank amidate" (title/abstract), "NA-selank" (title/abstract), and "acetyl selank" (title/abstract) returned zero results. Searches of CyberLeninka (cyberleninka.ru) for «ацетилселанк», «N-ацетил-селанк», and related Russian-character terms returned zero results. Wanfang Data and CNKI indirect searches for "acetyl selank" or "N-ацетил-селанк" returned zero results. No German-language source indexed in PubMed (german[la]) or in web-accessible German academic portals (Springermedizin.de, AWMF, ZB MED) carries any mention of N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate. Vendor marketing pages acknowledge the gap explicitly: "Studies on N-Acetyl Selank are lacking" and "no human studies have been performed on NA-Selank." This is not a search failure — the analog is a grey-market terminally modified derivative coined by Western research-chemical suppliers; it has never been the subject of any primary peer-reviewed study in any language.

This is stated explicitly here once and carried forward: **every citation in this layer is Selank (parent compound) literature.** The analog-specific evidence category is absent across all surveyed languages.

## Russian (PRIMARY SURVEY — IMG RAS + Zakusov Institute + independent Russian groups)

**Result: 10 genuinely Russian-language admissible primaries confirmed (7 PMID-bearing via PubMed eutils; 3 CyberLeninka stable-URL items). Zero analog-specific items.**

Selank is unique among compounds in this library in that the **Russian-language press is the primary scientific record** — IMG RAS and the Zakusov Institute published the foundational mechanistic and clinical work in Russian-language journals before and alongside any English output. PubMed language-filter search (term: selank AND russian[la]) returned 33 results; 7 were confirmed Selank primary studies with PMIDs selected for this survey (R1–R7). Three additional independently-authored Russian-language items lacking PMIDs were located via CyberLeninka (R8–R10). The non-English Russian layer here is not a supplement to the English layer — it IS the primary record of Selank's clinical and mechanistic evidence.

**Institutional authorship flags applied to all entries:** IMG RAS and Zakusov Institute are the co-developer institutions for Selank. Entries authored primarily by these institutions carry a higher within-lineage publication risk than entries from independent Russian groups.

### R1 — Medvedev VE, Tereshchenko ON, Kost NV, Ter-Israelyan AYu, Gushanskaya EV, Chobanu IK, Sokolov OYu, Myasoedov NF. 2015.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Оптимизация лечения тревожных расстройств селанком]

**English Translation of Title:** Optimization of the treatment of anxiety disorders with selank

**Journal:** Журнал неврологии и психиатрии им. С.С. Корсакова (*Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S S Korsakova* / Journal of Neurology and Psychiatry named for S.S. Korsakov)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2015; 115(6):33–40

**PMID:** 26356395

**Language:** Russian (PubMed LA field: rus; "[Article in Russian]"; *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* does not issue a systematically translated English-language edition in PubMed — English abstract only)

**Institutional authorship:** Mixed — Medvedev VE: RUDN University, Moscow (independent); Kost NV, Sokolov OYu, Myasoedov NF: affiliates of IMG RAS/RAMS network. Myasoedov NF is the Selank inventor.

**Study type:** Primary clinical study (human, open-label controlled comparison)

**Study population:** Patients with anxiety disorders (generalized anxiety disorder and adjustment disorder with anxious mood); treated with phenazepam alone vs. phenazepam + Selank intranasal. Human clinical.

**Key findings:** Combination of Selank + phenazepam produced faster anxiety reduction vs. phenazepam monotherapy, with fewer benzodiazepine side effects; Selank permitted earlier phenazepam dose reduction; quality-of-life measures improved more in the combination arm. The Medvedev/RUDN group represents the most clearly independent clinical voice in the Russian Selank literature — Myasoedov co-authorship is present but Medvedev's group is not IMG RAS.

**Tag:** [R1, open_label]

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language open-label clinical study; Selank-inventor co-authorship; no placebo arm; single Russian center; no blinding described)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract in English via NCBI; full article in Russian; *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* has no published English translation of full text.

### R3 — Sarkisova KIu, Kozlovskiĭ II, Kozlovskaia MM. 2008.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Влияние гептапептида селанка на генетически детерминированные и ситуационно-обусловленные симптомы депрессии у крыс WAG/Rij и мышей BALB/c]

**English Translation of Title:** Effects of heptapeptide selank on genetically-based and situation-provoked symptoms of depression in behavior in WAG/Rij and Wistar rats, and in BALB/c mice

**Journal:** Журнал высшей нервной деятельности им. И.П. Павлова (*Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat Im I P Pavlova* / Journal of Higher Nervous Activity named for I.P. Pavlov)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2008; 58(2):226–237

**PMID:** 18661785

**Language:** Russian (PubMed LA field: rus; "[Article in Russian]"; *Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat* is a Russian-language journal with no PubMed-indexed English translation edition)

**Institutional authorship:** Sarkisova KIu, Kozlovskaia MM: Institute of Higher Nervous Activity and Neurophysiology, RAS (not IMG RAS — a distinct RAS institute); Kozlovskiĭ II: Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology RAMS (Selank co-developer partner). ### R4 — Semenova TP, Kozlovskiĭ II, Zakharova NM, Kozlovskaia MM. 2009.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Сравнение эффектов селанка и тафтсина на метаболизм серотонина в мозге крыс с истощением запасов серотонина]

**English Translation of Title:** Comparison of the effects of selank and tuftsin on the metabolism of serotonin in the brain of rats pretreated with PCPA

**Journal:** Экспериментальная и клиническая фармакология (*Eksp Klin Farmakol* / Experimental and Clinical Pharmacology)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2009; 72(4):6–8

**PMID:** 19803361

**Language:** Russian (PubMed LA field: rus; "[Article in Russian]"; *Eksp Klin Farmakol* is a Russian-language pharmacology journal with no systematically published English translation edition in PubMed; NCBI provides English abstracts only)

**Institutional authorship:** Semenova TP: Institute of Higher Nervous Activity and Neurophysiology, RAS; Kozlovskiĭ II, Kozlovskaia MM: Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology RAMS (Selank co-developer); Zakharova NM: affiliation not specified in abstract. Partial developer network connection via Zakusov.

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vivo animal model, neurochemistry)

**Study population:** Rats pretreated with PCPA (para-chlorophenylalanine, a serotonin-synthesis inhibitor) to deplete serotonin stores. In vivo.

**Key findings:** Selank enhanced 5-HT (serotonin) metabolism in the brain stem 30 minutes after injection in PCPA-treated rats (i.e., even in serotonin-depleted animals); tuftsin parent peptide showed no comparable effect. This is the primary Russian-language mechanistic study establishing Selank's serotonin-pathway modulation as distinct from its Tuftsin parent — evidence that the Tuftsin extension (Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide) confers new pharmacological properties on the serotonergic side.

**Population note:** Animal (rats, PCPA-pretreated); in vivo neurochemistry; not human subjects.

**English Translation of Title:** Experimental optimization of learning and memory processes by selank

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2008; 71(5):8–12

**PMID:** 19093364

**Language:** Russian (PubMed LA field: rus; "[Article in Russian]"; *Eksp Klin Farmakol* — Russian-language journal, no English translation edition)

**Institutional authorship:** Narkevich VB, Kudrin VS, Klodt PM: NRC Institute of Biochemical Physics, RAS — independent RAS institute not IMG RAS; Pokrovskiĭ AA: Zakusov Institute RAMS (developer partner); Kozlovskaia MM, Maĭskiĭ AI, Raevskiĭ KS: Zakusov Institute. **Population note:** Animal (BALB/C and C57Bl/6 mice, inbred strains); in vivo neurochemistry; strain-specific.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**Russian (10 admissible — 7 PMID-bearing + 3 CyberLeninka-URL; all primary studies):** The Russian layer IS the primary scientific record for Selank and, by extension, the only indirect evidence base for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate. The compound was created, registered, and studied almost exclusively in Russian-language venues, primarily in *Eksp Klin Farmakol*, *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr*, and *Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat*. The non-English contributions are the foundation, not a supplement:

- **Principal clinical data (R1, R2):** The clinical studies of Selank in anxiety-asthenic patients are Russian-language (*Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* 2015 and 2008). These are the two largest human studies; their full texts exist only in Russian.
- **Monoamine neurochemistry (R3, R6):** The strain-comparative monoamine profiling establishing the neurochemical basis for Selank's anxiolytic selectivity (R6, *Eksp Klin Farmakol* 2008) and the antidepressant-range dose-response (R3, *Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat* 2008) are Russian-only.
- **Serotonin mechanism (R4, R5):** Both primary Russian-language studies establishing Selank's serotonin-metabolism modulation as distinct from Tuftsin parent are Russian-only (*Eksp Klin Farmakol* 2009, 2010); together they are the mechanistic foundation for Selank's proposed memory-consolidation nootropic effect.
- **Peripheral physiology — fully independent (R7):** The only study from a completely non-developer-network group (*Ross Fiziol Zh* 2005; MSU biology faculty) is Russian-only and documents peripheral physiological effects (gastric microcirculation, lymphatic contractility) with no CNS overlap.
- **Electrophysiology mechanistic synthesis (R8):** CyberLeninka 2016 — the only primary study using patch-clamp to directly demonstrate Selank's GABAergic hippocampal mechanism; Russian-language in *Nervnye Bolezni*; partially independent authorship (Scientific Center of Neurology).
- **Antidepressant spectrum, multi-model (R9):** CyberLeninka 2005 — Kozlovskaya/Sarkisova behavioral dose-response in WAG/Rij rats; establishes the dose-dependency of anxiolytic vs. antidepressant effects.
- **Social stress behavioral model, recent (R10):** CyberLeninka 2022 — Astrakhan State Medical University group; most recent Selank behavioral primary in this survey; confirms stress-protective effects under social stress.

**Chinese (0 admissible):** Confirmed absence — Selank has not entered the Chinese-language scientific press.

**German (0 admissible):** Confirmed absence — Selank has not entered the German-language scientific press; one false positive verified and dismissed.

**Analog-specific (0 in any language):** N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has zero primary evidence in Russian, Chinese, German, or English — confirmed by exhaustive search. The analog's entire putative evidence base is the Selank parent literature documented here.

**Net: the Russian non-English layer is the entire foundational scientific record for Selank. Chinese and German layers are genuinely absent. The analog has no separate literature in any language.**
