# N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate — Practitioner / Grey-Market Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is actually accessed, dosed, and (where applicable) monitored in the grey-market research-chemical context* — the dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions that circulate in vendor pages and dosing aggregators, plus the regulatory and compounding status that governs whether any legitimate clinical prescribing pathway exists. It is **not** an efficacy document. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (Tier 3), and `vendor_label` / `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4) sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) purity/reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is **not approved anywhere in the world** — not in Russia, not in the EU, not in the United States, not in any other jurisdiction. The **parent compound Selank** is registered as a pharmaceutical nasal spray in Russia (trade name Selanc, 0.15% intranasal drops) for generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia, but **Selank is not FDA-approved or EMA-approved**, and N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is not even Russia-approved — it is a grey-market synthetic analog invented by the US/EU research-chemical trade, not by the originating Russian Institute of Molecular Genetics (IMG RAS) research program. No Investigational New Drug (IND) application exists for this compound in the FDA's public database. It occupies a US regulatory grey zone: not a scheduled controlled substance, but not a legally marketed drug for human use. There is **no legitimate Western prescribing pathway** and **no compounding pathway**: Selank acetate (TP-7) was placed on FDA's 503A Category 2 (significant safety risk, barring compounding use) and was removed from Category 2 in September 2024 after the nominator withdrew the nomination — it was not cleared; it entered pending-evaluation status. It has not been listed on the 503A Category 1 (authorized for compounding) bulks list at any point. The unmodified Selank analog lacks a lawful compounding basis; the N-acetylated, C-amidated modification of that analog has even less regulatory standing. The "prescribing practice" described here is therefore a **research-chemical grey-market practice** — not an approved standard of care and not a lawfully compounded therapy.
>
> **No legitimate clinical-grade supply chain exists.** N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is accessible only from research-chemical vendors operating under "not for human use" disclaimers. No pharmaceutical company has filed a development program for this compound. No clinical-grade manufacturing standard applies to any current supply.

## Critical hazards — read before any section below

Four hazards are specific to N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate and must be disclosed upfront, not buried in the monitoring section:

**Hazard 1 — Selank-vs-analog conflation.** The grey-market trade treats N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate as pharmacologically equivalent to or superior to Selank. This assumption is **not validated**. The N-terminal acetylation eliminates the free α-amine of the Thr residue identified in the Tuftsin receptor-binding literature as a key recognition element for Tuftsin-family receptor engagement. This means the modification that improves stability *simultaneously* risks disrupting the structural feature that makes Tuftsin-family compounds pharmacologically active at their receptors. No published binding assay, functional assay, or in vivo pharmacological comparison between Selank and its N-acetylated form has been conducted. The entire marketed evidence base for this analog is Selank parent data extrapolated by an assumption that has not been tested and may be contradicted by first principles. Buyers relying on "same effects as Selank, better stability" are being sold an untested claim.

**Hazard 2 — Immunogenicity from subcutaneous injection.** The registered Selank formulation is intranasal. N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is frequently used by subcutaneous injection — a route that introduces a terminally modified non-endogenous peptide directly into subcutaneous tissue and the bloodstream. Repeated subcutaneous injection of a modified peptide not recognized by the body's self-tolerance mechanisms carries an immune-sensitization risk (injection-site reactions progressing to urticaria or systemic response) that is entirely absent from the registered intranasal route and entirely unstudied for this compound. This risk is not theoretical: it is the rationale behind the FDA's warning letters noting immunogenicity concerns for unapproved peptides.

**Hazard 3 — Population-direction reversal.** The psychostimulant/activating effect documented in the Selank parent clinical trials is THERAPEUTIC in the studied population (clinical anxiety patients; it counteracts benzodiazepine-induced asthenia). In healthy off-label users without a baseline anxiety disorder, the same activation can manifest as unwanted anxiety, agitation, or sleep disruption. The direction of the effect cannot be assumed to be beneficial in non-anxious users on the basis of evidence obtained in anxious patients. This is a population-direction reversal: the same pharmacological signal that is the sought benefit in one context may be adverse in another.

**Hazard 4 — WADA S0 applies; S2 is unconfirmed and categorically uncertain.** A 2026 MDPI review (PMC13164565) asserts Selank is WADA-prohibited, citing S2 classification; this claim could not be independently confirmed against the primary WADA 2026 Prohibited List document. However, WADA S0 ("Non-Approved Substances") **unambiguously applies** to N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate under its catchall clause: any pharmacological substance not approved by any governmental regulatory authority for human therapeutic use is prohibited in-competition at all times under S0. This analog has zero approved-drug status anywhere. S0 applies regardless of whether S2 does or does not. Athletes subject to anti-doping rules must verify with their National Anti-Doping Organization (NADO) or GlobalDRO.com; do not rely on this document or the MDPI review's S2 framing.

## Circulating grey-market dose/cycle/route conventions

There is **no human dose-finding trial for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate** and **no validated therapeutic dose for any route**. The figures below are conventions circulating in the grey-market research-chemical community, derived from Selank parent trial doses and scaled by unvalidated assumption. They are tagged accordingly. **No dose number from any vendor or anecdote source is cited here as a clinical reference; all are prescribing-practice conventions only.** The Selank parent's studied intranasal doses are the only citable anchor; they are tagged `rct` or `open_label` in the research report and cited here as the parent-compound context they represent.

### Intranasal spray convention (grey-market aggregator)

The dominant grey-market nasal spray convention, as circulated by peptides.org and the peptideinitiative.com aggregator, is:

- **Route:** intranasal nasal spray
- **Dose per pump:** 300 mcg per spray
- **Frequency/daily dose:** 2–3 sprays/day (600–900 mcg/day total)
- **Cycle:** up to 14 days on, ≥1 week off (some aggregators extend to 1–3 week washout; one source references 21-day cycle lengths used in Selank parent studies but applies them to the analog without evidence basis)

Source: peptides.org ("N-Acetyl Selank Amidate | Reviews, Clinical Studies, and Safety" and "N-Acetyl Selank Amidate Dosage Calculator and Chart"), retrieved June 2026. [8, anecdote_aggregate] Used here for route/dose convention ONLY; **grounds no efficacy claim.**

### Subcutaneous injection convention (grey-market aggregator)

A second convention, appearing in a subset of aggregator sources, describes subcutaneous injection:

- **Route:** subcutaneous injection
- **Dose range:** 150–300 mcg per injection daily; higher-dose convention adds 250–500 mcg/day
- **Frequency:** once daily
- **Cycle:** 4–6 weeks on, 1–2 weeks off (per peptideinitiative.com) or 14 days on / ≥1 week off (per peptides.org)

Source: peptideinitiative.com ("N-Acetyl Selank Protocol"), retrieved June 2026. Explicit caveat: "N-Acetyl Selank is an investigational research compound not approved by the FDA for human therapeutic use. This information is for educational purposes only." No named site operator. [9, anecdote_aggregate] Used here for route/dose convention ONLY; **grounds no efficacy claim.**

**The Selank parent's studied intranasal doses (cited for context — NOT the analog's doses):** The registered Russian Selank formulation is 0.15% (1.5 mg/mL) intranasal drops. The clinical trials used 2,700 mcg/day in the EEG subgroup study (Syunyakov et al., 2012; 20 adults, 14 days). Exact dose figures from the Zozulia 2008 (62-patient) and Medvedev 2014 (60-patient) trials are not specified in available English-language abstracts. These Selank parent doses are **not** the analog's doses; they are the parent's studied doses cited only to establish that the grey-market conventions for the analog are in the same general order of magnitude as parent study doses. The analog's doses are entirely convention-derived, not trial-derived.

**Convention divergence note:** The intranasal convention (600–900 mcg/day) and the subcutaneous injection convention (150–500 mcg/day) diverge 1.5–6×. This divergence reflects the absence of any PK anchor for the analog and the route-specific dose-equivalence differences that even the Selank parent literature documents (intranasal vs. IP dosing in rats produces distinct pharmacodynamic profiles). Neither convention is validated.

**Reconstitution technique (standard peptide practice):**
- Wipe stoppers with alcohol swab before drawing
- Inject bacteriostatic water slowly down the vial wall; swirl gently (do NOT shake — agitation can degrade peptide bonds)
- Allow to dissolve at room temperature; do not heat

**Storage:**
- Lyophilized powder: −20 °C (freezer); stable for extended periods when kept dry and frozen
- Reconstituted solution: 2–8 °C (refrigerator), use within 2–4 weeks; one aggregator specifies 7 days [8, anecdote_aggregate] — the shorter convention is more conservative and is preferred where uncertainty exists
- Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles of reconstituted solution

**Syringe (if SC injection):** U-100 insulin syringe, 27–31G, 0.5 mL; rotate injection sites (abdominal fat, lateral thigh, upper arm) between administrations

## Monitoring framework (experimental context only)

No validated clinical monitoring protocol exists for this compound. If a clinician were tracking an individual using N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate in an experimental or research context, the following framework is derived from: (a) the mechanistic targets documented for the Selank parent (GABAergic modulation, enkephalin enzyme inhibition, BDNF regulation), and (b) the specific hazards identified in Hazards 1–4 above. **This is a rational monitoring scaffold, not a validated clinical protocol.**

**Baseline (before starting):**

| Domain | Tests | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Immunological | CBC with differential; hs-CRP baseline | Immunogenicity risk from SC injection of modified non-endogenous peptide; establish pre-immune baseline |
| Hepatic/renal | ALT, AST, CMP (creatinine, BUN, eGFR) | No hepato/nephrotoxicity data exists; baseline establishes reference if symptoms emerge |
| Anxiety/mood | Validated self-report (e.g., GAD-7 or DASS-21) | Population-direction reversal risk: track whether activation is anxiolytic or anxiogenic in this individual |
| Autonomic | Resting HRV via consumer wearable | Autonomic adjunct consistent with GABAergic/anxiolytic mechanism; time-series baseline before dosing |
| HPA-axis adjacent | Morning cortisol (serum or salivary) | Not a validated Selank biomarker; mechanism-adjacent surveillance for HPA/stress-axis disruption |
| Blood pressure | Resting BP | Transient hypotensive signal observed in anaesthetized cat IV study of Selank parent; low-yield but low-cost |

**Follow-up (end of first cycle; days 14–21 for a 14-day run):**

- Repeat hs-CRP: rising trend without intercurrent infection is the earliest laboratory signal of immune sensitization to the peptide
- ALT, AST
- Injection-site physical inspection: redness, induration, nodule formation
- Repeat GAD-7/DASS-21 + symptom diary review
- Repeat HRV trajectory review
- Morning cortisol if baseline was outside normal range

**Stopping criteria (rational, not validated):**

- Progressive injection-site reactions: increasing induration, urticaria, or systemic symptoms with repeated dosing (immune sensitization flag — discontinue and do not rechallenge without medical evaluation)
- Any systemic reaction after injection (flushing, urticaria, dyspnea — anaphylaxis cannot be excluded for repeated non-endogenous peptide injection)
- Worsening anxiety, new-onset panic symptoms, or agitation (population-direction reversal; discontinue and reassess)
- Sleep disruption attributable to dosing timing (psychostimulant/activating effect; try AM-only dosing first; if persists, discontinue)
- Significant hs-CRP elevation (>3 mg/L above baseline, trend over serial measurements, absent other explanation)
- ALT or AST >3× ULN at any measurement
- WADA test risk confirmed: cessation and NADO consultation
