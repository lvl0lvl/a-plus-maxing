# AOD-9604 (Tyr-hGH 177-191) — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how AOD-9604 is dosed, accessed, and reconstituted in practice* — the gray-market and named-practitioner conventions, plus the trial-derived doses (for contrast) and the regulatory/access facts. It is **not** an efficacy document and **must not** be read as one. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol`, `vendor_label`, and `regulatory` sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution/access facts. **None** of these grounds an efficacy or adverse-event-rate claim, and none appears here for that purpose.
>
> **Read this first (defining fact).** AOD-9604 has **no approved clinical dose**. Its obesity program was terminated in 2007 after the lipolytic signal in animals/early human pharmacology **failed to translate into a statistically significant fat-loss effect** in the largest Phase-2b human trial. Everything below is therefore (a) a *gray-market convention*, (b) a *trial-derived* dose, or (c) a *regulatory/access* fact. The human-efficacy state is **FAILED** and lives in the research report (§2); nothing in this layer implies efficacy.

## Dose/route conventions

**Gray-market fat-loss convention.** The dominant convention across vendor and clinic-marketing pages is **~300 mcg subcutaneously once daily, in the morning, fasted** (typically 30–60 min before food), run in cycles of roughly **8–12 weeks** `[vendor_label / practitioner_protocol]`. The fasted-AM rationale offered (low circulating insulin removes an anti-lipolytic brake) is a *mechanistic rationale asserted by gray-market sources*, not a clinically validated instruction `[vendor_label]`. A common escalation pattern is to advance to **500 mcg/day**, sometimes split (250 mcg AM + 250 mcg pre-bed), "if results plateau after ~4 weeks" `[vendor_label]`. These figures are **convention, not a label** — there is no approved label to anchor them, and **human efficacy FAILED, so no efficacy is implied by any dose here**.

**One named-prescriber data point.** A dated, named clinician page exists: **Dr. Dan Wool, NMD** (Practical Vitality PLLC / "Natural Gastro," 20801 N Scottsdale Rd #205, Scottsdale, AZ 85255; article published 26 Feb 2026, medically reviewed Apr 2026) describes a clinical-use range of **250–500 mcg/day, morning, empty stomach**, explicitly under medical supervision with labs and monitoring `[practitioner_protocol]`. This is a single named source, consistent with the gray-market convention; it does **not** elevate any efficacy claim. Beyond this, most "clinic" pages (e.g., San Diego, Las Vegas, Beverly Hills med-spa entries surfaced in search) name a clinic but **no individual prescriber, venue-specific protocol, or date**, so they do **not** meet the name+venue+date bar for a `practitioner_protocol` and are recorded only as evidence that off-label clinic offering occurs `[practitioner_protocol — weak/venue-only]`.

**Trial-derived doses (for contrast, NOT a convention).** The human program used defined research doses that must be cited as *trial-derived*, never as a clinical regimen: the Phase-II obesity work evaluated **oral 1 mg/day** (within a broader dose range across the six trials / >900 subjects), with SC ranges used in earlier pharmacology `[regulatory — trial-derived]`. The frequent gray-market claim that "clinical trials used up to 1,000 mcg/day" is therefore *trial-derived* and **must not be repackaged as a recommended dose** `[vendor_label — misattribution risk]`. **Self-check:** the ~300 mcg/day figure is reported strictly as gray-market convention; the 1 mg / ~1000 mcg figures strictly as trial-derived; and because the pivotal human trial **FAILED**, no efficacy attaches to either.

## Compounding / access

**Compounding gate result: AOD-9604 is NOT lawfully compoundable in the U.S. under §503A.** The mechanical chain:

- AOD-9604 (acetate + free base) was placed in **Category 2** of the FDA interim §503A bulks list in **September 2023** — the "may present significant safety risks" bucket that effectively bars compounding use `[regulatory]`.
- FDA's cited concerns were **potential immune reactions, manufacturing/peptide-synthesis impurities, and a lack of human safety data** `[regulatory]`.
- It was **removed from Category 2 effective ~27 Sep 2024 — but only because the nominator withdrew the nomination**, not because it was cleared. Critically, AOD-9604 **was never on Category 1**, and only Category-1 substances may be lawfully compounded under §503A. Removal from Category 2 did **not** make it compoundable `[regulatory]`.
- Litigation (Evexias/Farmakeio, Texas APA challenge) named AOD-9604 among four peptides, and an April-2026 Federal Register notice set PCAC review dates — but **AOD-9604 is not on the July-2026 or February-2027 PCAC dockets**, so no near-term path to Category-1 is scheduled `[regulatory]`.

**Compounding data-sheet search result: NONE FOUND.** A targeted search for compounding-pharmacy data sheets (Empower, Tailor Made, Hallandale, Belmar, APS, Strive) returned **no AOD-9604 compounding data sheet**. This is the *expected* result given the regulatory gate — an unapproved, non-API fragment that is not lawfully compoundable should not be carried by reputable 503A/503B compounders. The practical access channel today is therefore **gray-market research-chemical vendors**, sold "for research use only," with vendor COA/purity but **no clinical labeling** `[vendor_label]`.

**Cost (qualitative).** Gray-market pricing is **cheap** — AOD-9604 is one of the lower-cost research peptides, sold as 5 mg vials by both Western and Russian-language vendors at commodity prices `[vendor_label]`. No clinical-grade pricing exists because there is no approved product.
