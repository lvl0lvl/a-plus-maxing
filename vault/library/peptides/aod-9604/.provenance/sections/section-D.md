## Section D — Safety & adverse effects

### Framing: failed on efficacy, not safety

AOD-9604 (Tyr-hGH 176–191, a synthetic 16-amino-acid C-terminal fragment of human growth hormone) is unusual among gray-market peptides in that it carries a comparatively substantial *human* safety dataset — six company-sponsored randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials run between 2001 and 2006 enrolling ~893 adults (all but one cohort clinically obese) [1]. The compound was ultimately abandoned not because of a safety signal but because it failed to separate convincingly from placebo on the weight-loss endpoint in its largest Phase IIb trial; clinical development was terminated in 2007 [1][2]. This is the central, honest framing for any safety discussion: the available trial evidence describes a peptide that was **well tolerated**, with an adverse-event profile **indistinguishable from placebo**, whose distinguishing pharmacological feature is the *absence* of the growth-hormone-axis effects that make full-length hGH problematic. The major caveat — developed below — is that none of this short-term tolerability data translates into validated *long-term* human safety, and essentially none of it was generated with the routes (subcutaneous injection) or the unregulated product identity now sold on the gray market.

### Trial-observed adverse events (well tolerated, placebo-comparable)

Across the six trials — two intravenous pilot/pharmacokinetic studies (METAOD001, n=15; METAOD002, n=23) and four oral studies including the two Phase IIb trials (METAOD005, n=300, 12 weeks; METAOD006, n=502, 24 weeks) — the pooled safety summary concluded that "AOD9604 displayed a very good safety and tolerability profile indistinguishable from placebo" [1] (mechanism_review / pooled-trial safety summary, Stier et al. 2013, ~893 humans).

Specific findings [1]:
- **Headache** was the most frequently reported adverse event in every study and the dominant complaint (e.g., 69.6% in the IV METAOD002 study; 42.6% in the active-treatment phase of METAOD005). Critically, headache frequency was **similar in placebo groups**, and "the distribution of the intensity of AEs was similar across all treatment groups."
- **Dose-related GI effects at the highest oral dose**: subjects receiving 54 mg AOD9604 (a supraphysiologic exploratory oral dose) experienced more headaches, diarrhea, and flatulence — the only clear dose-AE relationship reported.
- **Serious adverse events (SAEs)**: zero in the four smaller studies (METAOD001–004). In METAOD005, 5 SAEs occurred (3 skin cancers in the 20 mg group, 1 melanoma in the 10 mg group, 1 breast cancer in the 5 mg group), all judged by investigators as not related to study drug. In METAOD006, 18 SAEs occurred (3.6% of subjects) with a distribution "similar among all treatment groups." No SAE was attributed to AOD9604, and no treatment-related withdrawals occurred [1].
- **Injection-site reactions**: in the IV studies the compound was infused rather than self-injected subcutaneously, and the published record does not report a meaningful injection-site AE burden — this is relevant because the gray-market product is administered subcutaneously, a route for which *no published human AE data exist* (see Theoretical/unknown) [1][4].

### The GH-axis-sparing claim — VERIFIED

The defining safety differentiator of AOD-9604 is that, unlike full-length hGH, it reportedly retains lipolytic activity without activating the GH/IGF-1 axis or impairing carbohydrate metabolism. The human trial data support this:

- **No effect on IGF-1 (CONFIRMED)**: across the long-term oral trials, "there were no significant changes or notable trends" in serum IGF-1 in AOD9604 groups versus placebo [1]. The authors state this "confirms the hypothesis that AOD9604 does not act via IGF-1." The nutraceutical-safety review independently reports that "AOD9604 did not stimulate the production of IGF-1 in any of the systems studied," distinguishing it from full-length hGH [2] (mechanism_review, Moré et al.). This matters because IGF-1 elevation is the proximate mediator of hGH's growth/organomegaly and theoretical proliferative/oncogenic concerns; its absence is the core of the favorable differential profile.
- **No effect on glucose tolerance / insulin sensitivity (CONFIRMED)**: oral glucose tolerance testing across the trials showed "no significant changes or obvious trends in any mean values… at any time point" for AOD9604 versus placebo, supporting the conclusion that "AOD9604 has no negative effect on carbohydrate metabolism" — explicitly contrasted with the diabetogenic, insulin-resistance-inducing effect of full-length hGH [1]. The metabolism review concurs that "AOD9604 elicited the fat reducing effect without inducing insulin resistance" [2]. (One small early study even reported a glucose-tolerance *improvement* signal in a low-dose oral cohort, though this was not the primary safety conclusion and should not be overstated [3].)
- **No GH-like growth/organomegaly effects**: consistent with the absence of IGF-1 stimulation, no GH-type somatic growth, fluid retention, or organomegaly signal was reported in the human trials [1][2].

In short, the GH-axis-sparing claim — no IGF-1 rise, no adverse glucose/insulin effect — is **directly verified in the trial-reported human data** and is the single most defensible positive safety statement that can be made about this compound.

### Supporting nonclinical toxicology (genotoxicity, animal NOAEL)

The nutraceutical safety review summarizes a nonclinical package that is consistent with the human tolerability findings [2] (mechanism_review + animal toxicology):
- **Genotoxicity**: negative Ames test (no significant mutation-rate increase up to 2,000 µg/plate); non-clastogenic in the CHO chromosome-aberration assay; no dose-related micronucleus increase. Overall: "no evidence of genotoxicological or toxicological concerns" [2].
- **Animal toxicology**: 6-month oral rat study NOAEL ≥100 mg/kg/day; 9-month oral cynomolgus monkey study NOAEL 50 mg/kg/day, with no significant ophthalmic, cardiac, hematologic, biochemical, or histopathologic treatment-related findings [2] (animal: rat and cynomolgus monkey, chronic oral). A 4-week IV rat study (0.1–10 mg/kg/day) showed only reduced female body-mass gain and reduced male thymus mass at the top dose, with no organ pathology [2].
- **Immunogenicity (nonclinical + clinical)**: "no significant anti-AOD9604 antibodies detected in any animal experiments" [2]; in the human trials, "in none of the subjects at none of the timepoints were antibodies against the peptide detected" [1].

These data underpinned a conditional self-affirmed **GRAS determination** for use in foods/supplements (oral route) [2] — which is *not* the same as approval as a subcutaneous drug, and does not transfer to the injectable gray-market use case.

### Theoretical / unknown risks

The favorable short-term picture must be bounded honestly:
- **No long-term human safety data**: the longest human exposure was 24 weeks (METAOD006) [1]. There are no human data on chronic/multi-year use, and the program was halted, so no Phase III safety database exists. Any claim of long-term safety is unsupported.
- **Route mismatch**: the human evidence is overwhelmingly *oral* (and IV for PK); the FDA's December 2024 review specifically flagged that **no published human exposure data exist for the subcutaneous, transdermal, or intranasal routes** that compounding pharmacies and gray-market suppliers actually use [4] (regulatory, FDA PCAC briefing). The favorable oral/IV tolerability cannot be assumed to transfer to chronic subcutaneous injection.
- **Product identity / purity unverifiable**: gray-market "AOD-9604" is not the characterized clinical material. FDA noted that available certificates of analysis "did not consistently control for impurities, aggregates, or microbial limits," and that subcutaneous injection raises specific concern for **immunogenicity from peptide aggregation and impurities** [4]. So while the *characterized clinical peptide* was non-immunogenic, an *uncharacterized injectable gray-market product* carries unquantifiable aggregation/impurity/contamination risk that the trial data do not address.
- **Immunogenicity (residual)**: trial material was non-immunogenic [1][2], but immunogenicity is formulation- and impurity-dependent; the FDA concern above is route- and product-specific, not a refutation of the clinical finding [4].

### Contraindications (precautionary)

There are no established, evidence-based contraindications, because the compound is unapproved and was never labeled. Precautionary contraindications apply:
- **Pregnancy and lactation**: no human data; avoid (standard for any unapproved peptide) [4].
- **Unapproved-compound cautions**: AOD-9604 is not approved by FDA, EMA, or TGA; FDA declined to place it on the 503A bulks list for compounding in December 2024, citing inadequate physicochemical characterization, immunogenicity concerns, lack of clinical effectiveness data for any approved use, and absence of published human data for the proposed routes [4]. It is also **prohibited in sport by WADA** (S2 class). These are regulatory/practical contraindications rather than toxicological ones.
- It should be stated plainly that the short-term profile is favorable and GH-axis-sparing — the honest contraindication picture is "unapproved, uncharacterized injectable product with no long-term data," not "known to be harmful."

### Monitoring & stopping criteria

Because AOD-9604 does not activate the GH/IGF-1 axis [1][2], the usual hGH-monitoring markers (IGF-1, fasting glucose/HbA1c for diabetogenic effect) are expected to be uninformative as *activity* biomarkers — there is **no validated AOD-9604-specific biomarker**. Practical, conservative monitoring (general principles, not validated for this compound):
- Baseline and periodic fasting glucose/HbA1c — not because an effect is expected, but to document the absence of metabolic harm and per general unapproved-injectable caution.
- Injection-site assessment for local reactions/infection given the unverified product and subcutaneous route.
- General clinical review for any unexpected adverse event; given headache was the dominant trial AE, persistent or severe headache warrants attention.
- **Stopping criteria**: discontinue for any new IGF-1 elevation or impaired glucose tolerance (would signal an off-target or impurity effect inconsistent with the characterized peptide), any signs of injection-site infection/abscess, allergic/hypersensitivity reaction (possible immunogenicity from aggregates/impurities), or any serious adverse event. Because efficacy was not demonstrated even in well-powered trials, the absence of a measurable benefit is itself a reasonable reason to stop.
