## Section B — Efficacy: the failed human trials

**Bottom line up front:** AOD-9604 (Tyr-hGH(177–191), the C-terminal "lipolytic" fragment of human growth hormone, sometimes numbered hGH(176–191)) was developed as an oral anti-obesity drug by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals (Australia). Its pivotal human efficacy trial — a ~24-week, placebo-controlled Phase 2b study in several hundred obese adults — did **not** produce statistically significant weight loss versus placebo, and the company discontinued the obesity program in 2007. Preclinical rodent and adipocyte work showed lipolytic and weight-gain-reducing signals, and an earlier 12-week human study reported a modest weight difference, but there is **no proven human fat-loss efficacy**. The lipolysis story is a preclinical and short-term-signal story, not an established human drug effect.

### The headline: the failed Phase 2b obesity RCT

The defining trial was the Phase 2b "OPTIONS" study run by the sponsor, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd, across 16 clinical sites in Australia [1] (sponsor press disclosure, type: rct — design/operational details; outcome corroborated by peer-reviewed review [2]). Key design parameters from the sponsor's own disclosure [1]:

- **Enrollment:** 536 obese adults randomized, roughly equal numbers of men and women.
- **Design:** double-blind, placebo-controlled, four arms — placebo (0 mg) plus oral AOD-9604 at 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, and 1 mg once daily.
- **Duration:** 24 weeks of randomized treatment within a ~32-week total protocol (including follow-up).
- **Primary endpoint:** weight loss at 12 weeks of treatment, plus safety and tolerability.

The trial **failed to meet its primary weight-loss endpoint**: oral AOD-9604 did not produce clinically or statistically significant weight loss versus placebo at the doses tested [2] (mechanism_review / drug-development review by sponsor-affiliated authors). The peer-reviewed account states plainly that "effects of AOD9604 on weight loss were seen in initial trials but were not seen in the last study in which an intensive diet and exercise regime was incorporated" [2] — i.e., the larger, later, lifestyle-controlled Phase 2b did not reproduce the earlier signal. A widely reported (and biologically plausible) interpretation is that the intensive diet-and-exercise program imposed on all arms drove substantial weight loss in the placebo group, leaving no room for a modest peptide effect to separate; but the admissible, verifiable fact is the negative primary endpoint, not the mechanism of the null. Following this result, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals discontinued development of AOD-9604 as an obesity drug (reported in 2007) [2].

Notably, **AOD-9604 was never registered on ClinicalTrials.gov** — a direct query of the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API for "AOD9604" returns zero studies. The trials were conducted in Australia in 2004–2007, largely predating mandatory registration, so the public record rests on the sponsor's disclosures and on the peer-reviewed safety/development summaries [1][2][3] rather than on a trial registry entry. This is an integrity caveat: there is no independent registry record of the pivotal failed trial.

The safety side of the Phase 2b and the broader human program was, by contrast, reassuring. The sponsor's aggregate safety publication pooling **six randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled human trials** of AOD-9604 (intravenous 25–400 µg/kg and oral 0.25–54 mg daily, durations up to 24 weeks) concluded that AOD-9604 "displayed a very good safety and tolerability profile indistinguishable from placebo," with **no effect on serum IGF-1**, no negative effect on glucose tolerance (unlike full-length hGH), no anti-AOD9604 antibodies, and no treatment-related serious adverse events or withdrawals [3] (mechanism_review / pooled human safety analysis). Good safety, however, is not efficacy — the same human program that was clean on safety was negative on its primary weight-loss endpoint.

### The earlier human study: a modest 12-week signal that did not hold up

Before the Phase 2b, Metabolic ran a 12-week dose-ranging human study (the earlier Phase 2/2a efficacy trial) [1][4]. As reported in the sponsor disclosure and the conference publication, this study enrolled roughly 300 obese patients across multiple Australian sites, randomized to oral placebo or AOD-9604 at 1, 5, 10, 20, or 30 mg once daily for 12 weeks [4] (type: rct — conference abstract, Herd et al., Obesity Research 2005). The reported result was that the **1 mg group lost the most weight, ~2.6–2.8 kg over 12 weeks versus ~0.8 kg for placebo**, with an unusual flat/inverse dose-response (the lowest dose performed best), and small improvements in lipid profile and glucose tolerance [1][4].

Three integrity caveats attach to this earlier "positive" study:
1. It is published as a **conference abstract** (Herd C, Wittert G, Caterson L, Proietto J, et al., Obesity Research, 2005) and a later "Request PDF" full report; it is **not indexed in PubMed** and the full peer-reviewed dataset is not openly available. It is therefore lower-tier evidence than a published, peer-reviewed RCT.
2. The inverse dose-response (1 mg beating 5–30 mg) is biologically odd and is the kind of pattern that frequently does not replicate — and indeed it did not, in the larger Phase 2b.
3. Even taken at face value, a ~2 kg placebo-subtracted difference over 12 weeks is modest, and the definitive larger trial then **failed to confirm it** [2].

A still earlier human element of the program consisted of single- and multiple-dose pharmacokinetic/safety studies in healthy and obese volunteers (part of the six trials pooled in [3]); these characterized PK and confirmed the absence of IGF-1 elevation and glucose impairment but were not powered weight-loss efficacy trials [3].

### Preclinical lipolysis (animal and in vitro) — clearly NOT human fat loss

The biological rationale for AOD-9604 comes almost entirely from rodent and cell work, which must not be presented as human efficacy:

- **Ng et al., 2000 (Horm Res):** in **obese Zucker rats** (animal), oral AOD-9604 at 500 µg/kg/day for 19 days reduced body-weight gain by >50% (15.8 ± 0.6 vs 35.6 ± 0.8 g) and increased adipose lipolytic activity, without the insulin-sensitivity penalty of intact hGH [5] (animal — Zucker rats; n not stated in abstract).
- **Heffernan et al., 2001 (Int J Obes Relat Metab Disord):** in **obese (ob/ob) mice** (animal), 14 days of hGH or AOD-9604 reduced body-weight gain, increased fat oxidation and plasma glycerol, and — unlike hGH — avoided hyperglycemia; AOD-9604 did not compete for the hGH receptor or induce cell proliferation [6] (animal — ob/ob and lean C57BL/6J mice).
- **Heffernan et al., 2001 (Endocrinology):** in **wild-type obese mice and β3-adrenoceptor knockout mice** (animal/mechanism), chronic hGH and AOD-9604 reduced body weight and fat and raised β3-AR expression in wild-type mice, but produced **no such effect in β3-AR knockout mice**, implicating β3-adrenergic signaling in the chronic lipolytic action [7] (animal + mechanism).

These studies are the entire empirical basis for the "fat-loss peptide" marketing claim. They are short-duration rodent and in-vitro experiments. The one place this mechanism was put to a rigorous human test at scale — the Phase 2b RCT — it did not deliver weight loss [2].

### Enumeration of the human trials

The human program comprised the following (per the sponsor's pooled safety report [3] and disclosures [1][4]):

1. **Single-dose PK/safety study/studies** in healthy/obese volunteers (IV 25–400 µg/kg; oral up to 54 mg) — PK and safety, not efficacy [3].
2. **Multiple-dose PK/safety study/studies** — repeated oral dosing, safety/tolerability [3].
3. **12-week Phase 2/2a dose-ranging efficacy RCT**, ~300 obese adults, oral 1–30 mg/day — modest weight signal at 1 mg (~2 kg over placebo), conference-published, not PubMed-indexed [1][4].
4. **24-week Phase 2b "OPTIONS" efficacy RCT**, 536 obese adults, oral 0.25/0.5/1 mg/day vs placebo — **negative primary weight-loss endpoint; program discontinued** [1][2].

(The "six randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials" figure in the pooled safety paper [3] counts the single-dose and multiple-dose substudies separately from the two efficacy trials.)

### Verdict for the wiki

There is **no proven human fat-loss efficacy** for AOD-9604. The strongest human efficacy test — the 536-patient, 24-week Phase 2b RCT — failed its primary weight-loss endpoint, and the company abandoned the obesity indication. The only "positive" human data is a non-PubMed-indexed 12-week conference abstract showing a modest, dose-anomalous signal that the definitive trial did not confirm. All robust lipolysis evidence is preclinical (rodent/adipocyte). Human safety was good and IGF-1/glucose were unaffected, but safety is not efficacy. Any presentation of AOD-9604 as an established human fat-loss agent is unsupported by the clinical record.
