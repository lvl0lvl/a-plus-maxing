## Section E — Regulatory & sport status

AOD-9604 (hGH fragment 176-191; originally developed by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals Ltd., Australia) sits in an unusual regulatory limbo: it is an **unapproved drug** that its developer later tried — with limited and now-lapsed success — to reposition as a food/supplement ingredient, while every major anti-doping authority treats it as **prohibited in sport**. This section reports the actual status honestly: it is *not* an approved drug anywhere, its dietary/supplement status is contested and (in the US) regarded by anti-doping scientists and FDA-proximate regulatory analysts as *not lawful*, and it is on the WADA Prohibited List.

### 1. Drug-approval status: NONE (verified)

AOD-9604 **has never been approved as a drug by any government health authority** — not the FDA, TGA, or EMA. `[regulatory] [1][2]` It was developed by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals (Australia) in the 1990s–2000s as an anti-obesity agent. Its pivotal **Phase 2b trial — a 24-week multicentre study in ~536 obese subjects (2007) — failed to show a statistically significant or clinically meaningful weight-loss difference versus placebo.** `[mechanism_review] [3]` Following that failure, the parent company (Calzada) reported to shareholders that the compound showed no promise as a weight-loss drug, and pharmaceutical obesity development was **discontinued (~2007)**. `[mechanism_review] [3]` This matches WADA's own characterisation that "AOD-9604 is a substance still under pre-clinical and clinical development and has not been approved for therapeutic use by any government health authority in the world." `[regulatory] [4]` Confirmed: drug-approval status = **none**.

### 2. The food/supplement pursuit and FDA's position (reported accurately — NOT overstated)

After abandoning the drug program, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals pursued AOD-9604 as a **"functional food"/dietary-supplement ingredient**. The principal artifact cited for this is a **self-affirmed GRAS** determination: an industry expert panel self-affirmed AOD-9604 as Generally Recognized As Safe for limited food-ingredient use, based on the Phase 1/Phase 2 safety data. `[regulatory] [3]` It is essential **not to overstate this**: a self-affirmed GRAS conclusion is an *industry-side* safety determination for a narrow food-ingredient use; it is **not an FDA approval, not a drug approval, does not authorize therapeutic claims, and does not extend to injectable or compounded forms.** `[regulatory] [3]`

FDA's actual position is that AOD-9604 is **not a lawful dietary ingredient.** The Banned Substances Control Group (BSCG), an anti-doping certification/science body, states the rule plainly: "synthetic chemicals that were designed and developed as drugs can't be used as supplement ingredients under DSHEA, but many of them are still sold in capsules or powders as dietary supplements" — and identifies AOD-9604 as exactly such an unapproved drug being marketed unlawfully as a supplement. `[regulatory] [2]` AOD-9604 was a substance investigated as a new drug (an IND-stage clinical candidate) before any marketing as a supplement, which places it within the DSHEA **"drug-exclusion"** framing that bars such articles from lawful dietary-ingredient status. `[regulatory] [2]` In short: a *self-affirmed GRAS for a food-ingredient use* existed, but it does **not** make AOD-9604 a lawful US dietary ingredient, and FDA/anti-doping regulators treat products selling it as supplements as unapproved/unlawful. `[regulatory] [2][3]`

In its 2024 compounding review, FDA also flagged that AOD-9604 was nominated (for weight loss, osteoporosis, and osteoarthritis) with **inconsistent characterization data** — e.g., the nomination named AOD-9604 free base while the Certificate of Analysis was for AOD-9604 acetate, with mismatched CAS number/molecular formula — and cited **limited long-term human safety data, peptide impurities, and potential immunogenicity** as concerns. `[regulatory] [1][3]`

**Australia (TGA):** AOD-9604 is **not an approved/ARTG-registered therapeutic good** in Australia; it is captured as a **prescription-only (Schedule 4) substance** under the Poisons Standard via the scheduling of growth-hormone secretagogues / growth-hormone-releasing peptides / growth-hormone fragments as a class. `[regulatory] [5]` So even in its country of origin, its status is prescription-only-with-no-approved-product, not an approved drug and not a free-sale supplement.

### 3. FDA §503A peptide-bulks action (April 2026) — AOD-9604 status verified, not guessed

In September 2023 FDA placed several peptide bulk drug substances — including **AOD-9604** (acetate and free base), CJC-1295, ipamorelin acetate, thymosin alpha-1, and Selank acetate — into **Category 2** of the interim §503A bulks list ("may present significant safety risks"), effectively disallowing their use in compounding. `[regulatory] [1][6]` AOD-9604 was then discussed at the **December 4, 2024 PCAC meeting**, where FDA recommended *against* adding it to the §503A Bulks List, citing immunogenicity risk, inadequate characterization, and limited safety data. `[regulatory] [1]`

Crucially, regarding the **April 2026 peptide action**: FDA announced PCAC review of **12 peptides** (BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c [July 23, 2026]; emideltide/DSIP, Semax, Epitalon [July 24, 2026]; and GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, LL-37/cathelicidin, Dihexa acetate, PEG-MGF [before end of Feb 2027]). **AOD-9604 is NOT among these 12 peptides.** `[regulatory] [7]` Instead, AOD-9604 appears in the April-2026 coverage only as one of four peptides (with CJC-1295, ipamorelin acetate, thymosin alpha-1) whose Category-2 placement was **challenged in litigation** (the *Evexias/Farmakeio* APA suit). `[regulatory] [7]` So: AOD-9604 is **not** on the canonical removed-12 list; its compounding status remains that of a substance excluded from the §503A Category-1 list and previously held in Category 2 (now contested in court). `[regulatory] [7]`

### 4. WADA / sport — prohibited; the Essendon saga (verified + anchored)

AOD-9604 is **prohibited in sport.** WADA's determination evolved as follows:

- **As a non-approved substance (S0):** Because AOD-9604 had no regulatory approval for human therapeutic use anywhere, WADA held that it fell under the blanket **S0** prohibition ("any pharmacological substance not addressed by any other section of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use"). WADA publicly **clarified to ASADA on 22 April 2013** that AOD-9604 was prohibited. `[regulatory] [4]` WADA's statement: "AOD-9604 is a substance still under pre-clinical and clinical development and has not been approved for therapeutic use by any government health authority in the world." `[regulatory] [4]`
- **As a growth-hormone fragment (S2):** AOD-9604 is also covered by the **S2 — Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics** category, which expressly captures **growth hormone (GH) fragments** (AOD-9604 being hGH 176-191). Anti-doping scientific bodies list AOD-9604 under **S2**. `[regulatory] [2]` (S0 and S2 are not mutually exclusive — S0 was the catch-all basis in 2012–13; the GH-fragment language under S2 also applies.)

**The Essendon Football Club supplements saga (2012–2016)** is the anchoring real-world episode. Under a 2012 supplements program, Essendon AFL players were administered injectables including AOD-9604 and a thymosin variety. `[regulatory] [8]` Essendon captain Jobe Watson publicly acknowledged in June 2013 that he had been given "the anti-obesity drug AOD-9604." `[regulatory] [8]` ASADA, however, **declined to pursue AOD-9604 charges for use before 22 April 2013**, judging it too hard to obtain a guilty verdict for a substance not yet explicitly and publicly declared banned at the time of use. `[regulatory] [4]` The case that ultimately succeeded turned on **thymosin beta-4**, not AOD-9604: on **12 January 2016 the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS)**, on WADA's appeal, found 34 players guilty of using a prohibited substance (the banned thymosin beta-4 variety) and imposed 2016-season suspensions. `[regulatory] [8]` Net WADA status of AOD-9604: **PROHIBITED** (S0 catch-all basis confirmed by WADA in 2013; also within S2 GH-fragment scope), though its *enforceability for pre-April-2013 use* was the legally contested point that led ASADA not to charge it directly. `[regulatory] [4][8]`
