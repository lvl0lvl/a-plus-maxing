## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### Identity and origin

AOD-9604 is a synthetic peptide analogue corresponding to the C-terminal fragment of human growth hormone (hGH), spanning residues **176–191**, the segment historically designated the hGH "lipolytic domain." [1][2] More precisely, the molecule is built on the **hGH(177–191)** sequence with an **additional tyrosine residue added at the N-terminus** for stability, so it is most accurately written as **Tyr-hGH(177–191)** — a 16-residue ("hexadecapeptide") synthetic peptide. [1][6] The two cysteine residues native to this region are joined so that the peptide is **cyclized by an intramolecular disulphide bond**, reproducing the loop conformation that region adopts in intact hGH. [1] (mechanism_review [1]; mechanism_review [6]; regulatory/review [2])

The lipolytic activity of the hGH C-terminus was originally identified by **Professor Frank (F.M.) Ng and co-workers at Monash University (Clayton, Australia)**, and AOD-9604 was subsequently developed as a candidate anti-obesity drug by **Metabolic Pharmaceuticals Ltd (Australia)**. [1][3] The discovery lineage runs from early demonstrations that the synthetic hGH(177–191) fragment reproduces the fat-metabolizing actions of the parent hormone, through the optimized clinical candidate AOD-9604. [1][3] (mechanism_review [1]; in_vitro/animal lineage cited in [3])

A note on nomenclature: AOD-9604 is closely related to, but **not identical to**, the peptide sold by vendors as "HGH Fragment 176-191" — AOD-9604 specifically carries the stabilizing N-terminal Tyr and the disulphide cyclization described above. [1] This distinction is structural, not a numeric claim.

### Defining mechanistic claim: lipolysis WITHOUT the GH/IGF-1 axis (verified)

The central selling point of AOD-9604 — that it retains the fat-mobilizing activity of growth hormone but **does not engage the growth-hormone receptor (GHR) and does not raise IGF-1** — is supported by primary data rather than assumed.

1. **GH receptor binding.** Ligand-binding experiments reported in the Moré/Kenley 2014 safety-and-metabolism review state that **AOD-9604 "is not a high affinity antagonist of hGH binding to the GH receptor,"** and that, unlike intact hGH, it **does not stimulate proliferation of cells expressing the GH receptor.** [6] Consistent with this, the Heffernan et al. 2001 obesity study reports that **"AOD9604 does not compete for the hGH receptor and nor does it induce cell proliferation, unlike hGH."** [2] (mechanism_review [6]; animal + receptor data [2])

2. **IGF-1.** Across the systems studied, **AOD-9604 did not stimulate IGF-1 production.** [6] In the human safety program, **"there was no significant change in the levels of IGF-1 in any of the treatment groups,"** with no dose-dependent IGF-1 elevation across the trials. [5] (mechanism_review [6]; human safety/clinical [5])

3. **Carbohydrate metabolism / insulin sensitivity.** In obese Zucker rats, chronic AOD-9604 **"showed no adverse effect on insulin sensitivity"** by euglycemic-clamp testing, in direct contrast to chronic intact hGH. [3] In obese C57BL/6J mice, **"AOD9604 did not induce hyperglycaemia or reduce insulin secretion"** as hGH did. [4] In humans, oral glucose tolerance testing across the clinical program showed **"no significant changes or obvious trends"** in glucose handling. [5] (animal: Zucker rats [3]; animal: C57BL/6J mice [4]; human clinical [5])

Together these results substantiate the defining claim: the lipolytic effect is decoupled from the GHR/IGF-1/insulin-resistance liabilities of full-length GH. The claim is **VERIFIED** at the level of receptor-binding assays, IGF-1 measurements in animals and humans, and glucose/insulin endpoints. [2][3][4][5][6]

### Mechanism of fat metabolism

AOD-9604 is described as reproducing the **dual lipid action** attributed to the hGH C-terminal domain: **stimulation of lipolysis** (triglyceride breakdown / fat oxidation) **and inhibition of lipogenesis** (fat synthesis). [3][6]

- **Anti-lipogenic arm.** The hGH C-terminal region "appears to be a lipid mobilizing domain with **inhibitory action on the acetyl-CoA carboxylase activity in hepatocytes and adipocytes**," and early studies of the hGH(177–191) fragment showed it to **stimulate lipolysis and inhibit lipogenesis.** [6] Acetyl-CoA carboxylase is the committed enzyme of fatty-acid synthesis, so its inhibition is a plausible biochemical basis for reduced fat storage. (mechanism_review / in_vitro lineage [6])

- **Lipolytic / fat-oxidation arm in vivo.** In obese C57BL/6J mice, 14 days of AOD-9604 (mini-osmotic pump) **reduced body-weight gain, increased in vivo fat oxidation, and raised plasma glycerol** (a marker of triglyceride hydrolysis). [4] In obese Zucker rats, 19 days of oral AOD-9604 (500 µg/kg/day) cut body-weight gain by >50% and **increased lipolytic activity in adipose tissue.** [3] (animal: ob/ob C57BL/6J mice [4]; animal: obese Zucker rats [3])

**The β3-adrenergic-receptor question — nuance.** A widely repeated claim is that AOD-9604 works "via the β3-adrenergic receptor (β3-AR)." The primary mechanistic study (Heffernan et al., *Endocrinology* 2001) refines this materially: chronic hGH and AOD-9604 each **raised β3-AR mRNA expression** in obese-mouse fat (restoring it toward lean levels), and the **chronic** weight/lipolysis effect was **lost in β3-AR knockout mice** — yet in an **acute** experiment AOD-9604 still **increased energy expenditure and fat oxidation in the β3-AR knockouts.** The authors therefore conclude that the lipolytic actions **"are NOT mediated directly through the β3-AR,"** even though both compounds upregulate β3-AR expression, which may secondarily enhance lipolytic sensitivity. [1] So β3-AR is a **downstream/permissive contributor for the chronic effect, not the direct receptor target.** [1] (animal: obese, lean, WT and β3-AR knockout mice [1])

The Moré/Kenley 2014 review is candid that **"the mechanism of action of AOD9604 is not understood,"** noting the β3-AR-expression effect as a partial contributor rather than a settled pathway. [6] (mechanism_review [6])

### Pharmacokinetics and route

AOD-9604 was advanced specifically as an **orally usable** obesity agent — the Ng et al. rat study used an oral dose and explicitly framed the analogue as a candidate for an **"orally usable and safe therapeutic agent for obesity."** [3] The human clinical program likewise tested both **intravenous** (studies METAOD001–002) and **oral** (capsule/tablet, METAOD003–006) formulations; **subcutaneous dosing was not part of the registered clinical program** in that report. [5] (animal: oral, Zucker rats [3]; human: IV + oral [5])

The native peptide is **rapidly cleared**: the Moré/Kenley review reports a **serum half-life of ~4 min** (intact AOD-9604 undetectable ~56 min after plasma spiking) and an **IV half-life of ~3 min in pigs**, with oral AOD-9604 undergoing **rapid degradation by sequential N-terminal amino-acid removal**; some N-terminally truncated metabolites retain reduced anti-lipogenic activity in vitro. [6] This very short systemic exposure, set against persistent metabolic effects in animals, is part of why the precise mechanism remains unresolved. [6] (mechanism_review / PK [6])

### Contrast with GH and GH secretagogues

Unlike **full-length hGH**, AOD-9604 does not measurably bind/activate the GHR, does not drive cell proliferation, and does not raise IGF-1 or impair insulin sensitivity/glucose tolerance — while still producing GH-like fat-oxidation and weight effects in obese rodents. [2][3][4][5][6] It is therefore mechanistically distinct from both **GH replacement** and from **GH secretagogues** (e.g., GHRH analogues or ghrelin-receptor agonists), whose entire purpose is to **raise endogenous GH and IGF-1** through the somatotropic axis. AOD-9604 deliberately **bypasses that axis**, isolating the lipid-metabolic fragment from the growth/IGF-1 signaling of the parent hormone. [2][6] (animal + receptor [2]; mechanism_review [6])
