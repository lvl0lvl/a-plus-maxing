> **Read this first — the honest evidence state.** AOD-9604 is a synthetic **C-terminal fragment of human growth hormone (hGH 176-191 / Tyr-hGH 177-191)** marketed for fat loss on the premise of "lipolysis WITHOUT the GH side effects." ## TL;DR

AOD-9604 is the C-terminal "lipolytic" fragment of human growth hormone, engineered (with an N-terminal tyrosine and a disulphide loop) to keep GH's fat-mobilizing action while shedding the growth/IGF-1 signalling of the parent hormone. On the mechanistic side, the marketing premise is genuinely supported: in receptor-binding assays it does not engage the GH receptor, and across animals and ~893 pooled human subjects it did not raise IGF-1 or impair glucose tolerance [1][2][4][5]. The fat-loss premise, however, did not survive a rigorous human test. The strongest preclinical signal — obese rodents losing fat with increased oxidation and adipose lipolysis — was real but is **animal/in-vitro** [1][3][7]. When the mechanism was put to its one large, well-powered human test, the **24-week, 536-patient Phase-2b oral RCT failed its primary weight-loss endpoint**, and the company abandoned the obesity indication around 2007 [11][12]. The only "positive" human efficacy data is a non-PubMed-indexed 12-week conference abstract showing a modest, biologically-odd inverse-dose signal (1 mg beating higher doses) that the definitive trial did not confirm [12][22]. The peptide was clean on safety — well-tolerated, IGF-1/glucose-neutral, non-immunogenic *as the characterized clinical material* — but **safety is not efficacy** [4][5]. The osteoarthritis repositioning rests on a single rabbit study (whose main benefit needed hyaluronic-acid co-administration) and a sponsor patent, with **zero registered human OA trials**; the widely-circulated "Phase-II knee-OA" claim actually belongs to a **different molecule** (Paradigm's pentosan polysulfate) and must not be attributed to AOD-9604 [10][9][8]. The entire evidence base — obesity and OA — funnels through a **single Monash/Ng → Metabolic Pharmaceuticals lineage (~100% single-lineage concentration)** [8]. AOD-9604 is unapproved as a drug anywhere, is not a lawful US dietary ingredient despite a self-affirmed GRAS, is prescription-only (Schedule 4) even in its country of origin, and is prohibited in sport [11][14][15][18][16]. Net: **mechanistically interesting, human-efficacy-FAILED, clinically unproven** — best understood as a well-tolerated experimental peptide whose central fat-loss claim the clinical record does not support.

## 1. Identity, Structure & Mechanism

**Bottom line up front:** AOD-9604 is a synthetic 16-residue C-terminal fragment of hGH. Its defining mechanistic claim — lipolysis WITHOUT engaging the GH receptor or raising IGF-1 — is **VERIFIED** at the level of receptor-binding assays, IGF-1 measurements, and glucose/insulin endpoints in animals and humans [1][2][4][5]. The lipolytic mechanism itself, however, is established only in animals/in-vitro and remains incompletely understood [5].

### 1.1 Identity and origin

AOD-9604 is a synthetic peptide analogue corresponding to the **C-terminal fragment of human growth hormone (hGH), residues 176-191** — the segment historically designated the hGH "lipolytic domain" [1][14]. More precisely, the molecule is built on the **hGH(177-191)** sequence with an **additional tyrosine residue added at the N-terminus** for stability, so it is most accurately written as **Tyr-hGH(177-191)** — a 16-residue ("hexadecapeptide") synthetic peptide [1][5]. The two cysteine residues native to this region are joined so that the peptide is **cyclized by an intramolecular disulphide bond**, reproducing the loop conformation that region adopts in intact hGH [1] (mechanism_review [1]; mechanism_review [5]).

The lipolytic activity of the hGH C-terminus was originally identified by **Professor Frank (F.M.) Ng and co-workers at Monash University (Clayton, Australia)**, and AOD-9604 was subsequently developed as a candidate anti-obesity drug by **Metabolic Pharmaceuticals Ltd (Australia)** [1][3]. The discovery lineage runs from early demonstrations that the synthetic hGH(177-191) fragment reproduces the fat-metabolizing actions of the parent hormone, through to the optimized clinical candidate AOD-9604 [1][3].

A note on nomenclature: AOD-9604 is closely related to, but **not identical to**, the peptide sold by vendors as "HGH Fragment 176-191" — AOD-9604 specifically carries the stabilizing N-terminal Tyr and the disulphide cyclization described above [1]. This distinction is structural, not a numeric claim.

### 1.2 Defining mechanistic claim: lipolysis WITHOUT the GH/IGF-1 axis (VERIFIED)

The central selling point of AOD-9604 — that it retains the fat-mobilizing activity of growth hormone but **does not engage the growth-hormone receptor (GHR) and does not raise IGF-1** — is supported by primary data rather than assumed:

1. **GH receptor binding.** Ligand-binding experiments reported in the Moré/Kenley 2014 safety-and-metabolism review state that AOD-9604 "is not a high affinity antagonist of hGH binding to the GH receptor," and that, unlike intact hGH, it **does not stimulate proliferation of cells expressing the GH receptor** [5]. Consistent with this, the Heffernan et al. 2001 obesity study reports that "AOD9604 does not compete for the hGH receptor and nor does it induce cell proliferation, unlike hGH" [2] (mechanism_review [5]; animal + receptor data [2]).

2. **IGF-1.** Across the systems studied, AOD-9604 **did not stimulate IGF-1 production** [5]. In the human safety program, "there was no significant change in the levels of IGF-1 in any of the treatment groups," with no dose-dependent IGF-1 elevation across the trials [4] (mechanism_review [5]; human safety/clinical [4]).

3. **Carbohydrate metabolism / insulin sensitivity.** In obese Zucker rats, chronic AOD-9604 "showed no adverse effect on insulin sensitivity" by euglycemic-clamp testing, in direct contrast to chronic intact hGH [3]. In obese C57BL/6J mice, "AOD9604 did not induce hyperglycaemia or reduce insulin secretion" as hGH did [7]. In humans, oral glucose tolerance testing across the clinical program showed "no significant changes or obvious trends" in glucose handling [4] (animal: Zucker rats [3]; animal: C57BL/6J mice [7]; human clinical [4]).

Together these results substantiate the defining claim: the lipolytic effect is **decoupled** from the GHR/IGF-1/insulin-resistance liabilities of full-length GH. The claim is **VERIFIED** at the level of receptor-binding assays, IGF-1 measurements in animals and humans, and glucose/insulin endpoints [2][3][4][5][7]. (The honest boundary: "verified" here means the *absence of the GH-axis liabilities* is well-supported — it does **not** mean human fat loss was demonstrated; see §2.)

### 1.3 Mechanism of fat metabolism

AOD-9604 is described as reproducing the **dual lipid action** attributed to the hGH C-terminal domain: **stimulation of lipolysis** (triglyceride breakdown / fat oxidation) **and inhibition of lipogenesis** (fat synthesis) [3][5].

- **Anti-lipogenic arm.** The hGH C-terminal region "appears to be a lipid mobilizing domain with inhibitory action on the acetyl-CoA carboxylase activity in hepatocytes and adipocytes," and early studies of the hGH(177-191) fragment showed it to stimulate lipolysis and inhibit lipogenesis [5]. Acetyl-CoA carboxylase is the committed enzyme of fatty-acid synthesis, so its inhibition is a plausible biochemical basis for reduced fat storage (mechanism_review / in-vitro lineage [5]).

- **Lipolytic / fat-oxidation arm in vivo.** In obese C57BL/6J mice, 14 days of AOD-9604 (mini-osmotic pump) **reduced body-weight gain, increased in-vivo fat oxidation, and raised plasma glycerol** (a marker of triglyceride hydrolysis) [7]. In obese Zucker rats, 19 days of oral AOD-9604 (500 µg/kg/day) cut body-weight gain by >50% and **increased lipolytic activity in adipose tissue** [3] (animal: ob/ob C57BL/6J mice [7]; animal: obese Zucker rats [3]).

**The β3-adrenergic-receptor question — nuance.** A widely repeated claim is that AOD-9604 works "via the β3-adrenergic receptor (β3-AR)." The primary mechanistic study (Heffernan et al., *Endocrinology* 2001) refines this materially: chronic hGH and AOD-9604 each **raised β3-AR mRNA expression** in obese-mouse fat (restoring it toward lean levels), and the **chronic** weight/lipolysis effect was **lost in β3-AR knockout mice** — yet in an **acute** experiment AOD-9604 still **increased energy expenditure and fat oxidation in the β3-AR knockouts** [1]. The authors therefore conclude that the lipolytic actions "are NOT mediated directly through the β3-AR," even though both compounds upregulate β3-AR expression, which may secondarily enhance lipolytic sensitivity [1]. So β3-AR is a **downstream/permissive contributor for the chronic effect, not the direct receptor target** [1] (animal: obese, lean, WT and β3-AR knockout mice [1]).

The Moré/Kenley 2014 review is candid that "the mechanism of action of AOD9604 is not understood," noting the β3-AR-expression effect as a partial contributor rather than a settled pathway [5] (mechanism_review [5]).

### 1.4 Pharmacokinetics and route

AOD-9604 was advanced specifically as an **orally usable** obesity agent — the Ng et al. rat study used an oral dose and explicitly framed the analogue as a candidate for an "orally usable and safe therapeutic agent for obesity" [3]. The human clinical program likewise tested both **intravenous** (studies METAOD001-002) and **oral** (capsule/tablet, METAOD003-006) formulations; **subcutaneous dosing was not part of the registered clinical program** in that report [4] (animal: oral, Zucker rats [3]; human: IV + oral [4]). This is a critical caveat for the gray market, which administers AOD-9604 subcutaneously — a route with **no published human exposure data** (see §5, §6).

The native peptide is **rapidly cleared**: the Moré/Kenley review reports a **serum half-life of ~4 min** (intact AOD-9604 undetectable ~56 min after plasma spiking) and an **IV half-life of ~3 min in pigs**, with oral AOD-9604 undergoing rapid degradation by sequential N-terminal amino-acid removal; some N-terminally truncated metabolites retain reduced anti-lipogenic activity in vitro [5]. This very short systemic exposure, set against persistent metabolic effects in animals, is part of why the precise mechanism remains unresolved [5] (mechanism_review / PK [5]).

### 1.5 Contrast with GH and GH secretagogues

Unlike **full-length hGH**, AOD-9604 does not measurably bind/activate the GHR, does not drive cell proliferation, and does not raise IGF-1 or impair insulin sensitivity/glucose tolerance — while still producing GH-like fat-oxidation and weight effects in obese rodents [2][3][4][5][7]. It is therefore mechanistically distinct from both **GH replacement** and from **GH secretagogues** (e.g., GHRH analogues or ghrelin-receptor agonists), whose entire purpose is to **raise endogenous GH and IGF-1** through the somatotropic axis. AOD-9604 deliberately **bypasses that axis**, isolating the lipid-metabolic fragment from the growth/IGF-1 signalling of the parent hormone [2][5] (animal + receptor [2]; mechanism_review [5]).

### 1.6 Coverage gaps (Section 1)

- **Mechanism "not understood."** The sponsor-affiliated review itself states the mechanism of action is not understood; β3-AR is a permissive/downstream contributor, not the direct target [1][5].
- **All mechanism data are preclinical/biomarker.** GHR-binding, IGF-1-sparing, and lipolytic mechanisms derive from animal models, cell assays, and human biomarker panels — not from a demonstration of human fat loss (which failed; §2).
- **PK is non-human for the gray-market route.** Half-life figures are from plasma-spiking and pig IV; **no human subcutaneous PK** exists, and the clinical program never used the SC route now sold gray-market [4][5].
- **"Verified GH-axis-sparing" is an absence-of-liability claim, not an efficacy claim** — it must not be read as evidence of fat loss.

## 2. Efficacy: the FAILED human trials

**Bottom line up front:** There is **no proven human fat-loss efficacy** for AOD-9604. Its strongest human efficacy test — the 536-patient, 24-week Phase-2b oral RCT — **failed its primary weight-loss endpoint**, and Metabolic Pharmaceuticals abandoned the obesity indication (~2007) [11][12]. The only "positive" human data is a non-PubMed-indexed 12-week conference abstract showing a modest, dose-anomalous signal the definitive trial did not confirm [12][22]. All robust lipolysis evidence is **preclinical** (rodent/adipocyte) [1][3][7]. Human safety was good and IGF-1/glucose were unaffected — but **safety is not efficacy** [4].

### 2.1 The headline: the failed Phase-2b obesity RCT

The defining trial was the Phase-2b "OPTIONS" study run by the sponsor, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd, across 16 clinical sites in Australia [11] (sponsor press disclosure, type: rct — design/operational details; outcome corroborated by peer-reviewed review [12]). Key design parameters from the sponsor's own disclosure [11]:

- **Enrollment:** 536 obese adults randomized, roughly equal numbers of men and women.
- **Design:** double-blind, placebo-controlled, four arms — placebo (0 mg) plus oral AOD-9604 at 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, and 1 mg once daily.
- **Duration:** 24 weeks of randomized treatment within a ~32-week total protocol (including follow-up).
- **Primary endpoint:** weight loss at 12 weeks of treatment, plus safety and tolerability.

The trial **failed to meet its primary weight-loss endpoint**: oral AOD-9604 did not produce clinically or statistically significant weight loss versus placebo at the doses tested [12] (mechanism_review / drug-development review by sponsor-affiliated authors). The peer-reviewed account states plainly that "effects of AOD9604 on weight loss were seen in initial trials but were not seen in the last study in which an intensive diet and exercise regime was incorporated" [12] — i.e., the larger, later, lifestyle-controlled Phase-2b did not reproduce the earlier signal. A widely reported (and biologically plausible) interpretation is that the intensive diet-and-exercise program imposed on all arms drove substantial weight loss in the placebo group, leaving no room for a modest peptide effect to separate; but the admissible, verifiable fact is the **negative primary endpoint**, not the mechanism of the null. Following this result, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals discontinued development of AOD-9604 as an obesity drug (reported in 2007) [12].

Notably, **AOD-9604 was never registered on ClinicalTrials.gov** — a direct query of the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API for "AOD9604" returns **zero studies** [8]. The trials were conducted in Australia in 2004-2007, largely predating mandatory registration, so the public record rests on the sponsor's disclosures and on the peer-reviewed safety/development summaries [11][12][4] rather than on a trial-registry entry. This is an integrity caveat: there is **no independent registry record** of the pivotal failed trial.

The safety side of the Phase-2b and the broader human program was, by contrast, reassuring. The sponsor's aggregate safety publication pooling **six randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled human trials** of AOD-9604 (intravenous 25-400 µg/kg and oral 0.25-54 mg daily, durations up to 24 weeks) concluded that AOD-9604 "displayed a very good safety and tolerability profile indistinguishable from placebo," with **no effect on serum IGF-1**, no negative effect on glucose tolerance (unlike full-length hGH), no anti-AOD9604 antibodies, and no treatment-related serious adverse events or withdrawals [4] (mechanism_review / pooled human safety analysis). Good safety, however, is **not efficacy** — the same human program that was clean on safety was negative on its primary weight-loss endpoint.

### 2.2 The earlier human study: a modest 12-week signal that did not hold up

Before the Phase-2b, Metabolic ran a 12-week dose-ranging human study (the earlier Phase-2/2a efficacy trial) [11][22]. As reported in the sponsor disclosure and the conference publication, this study enrolled roughly 300 obese patients across multiple Australian sites, randomized to oral placebo or AOD-9604 at 1, 5, 10, 20, or 30 mg once daily for 12 weeks [22] (type: rct — conference abstract, Herd et al., *Obesity Research* 2005). The reported result was that the **1 mg group lost the most weight, ~2.6-2.8 kg over 12 weeks versus ~0.8 kg for placebo**, with an unusual flat/inverse dose-response (the lowest dose performed best), and small improvements in lipid profile and glucose tolerance [11][22].

Three integrity caveats attach to this earlier "positive" study:

1. It is published as a **conference abstract** (Herd C, Wittert G, Caterson L, Proietto J, et al., *Obesity Research*, 2005) and a later "Request PDF" full report; it is **not indexed in PubMed** and the full peer-reviewed dataset is not openly available. It is therefore lower-tier evidence than a published, peer-reviewed RCT.
2. The inverse dose-response (1 mg beating 5-30 mg) is **biologically odd** and is the kind of pattern that frequently does not replicate — and indeed it did not, in the larger Phase-2b.
3. Even taken at face value, a ~2 kg placebo-subtracted difference over 12 weeks is **modest**, and the definitive larger trial then **failed to confirm it** [12].

A still earlier human element of the program consisted of single- and multiple-dose pharmacokinetic/safety studies in healthy and obese volunteers (part of the six trials pooled in [4]); these characterized PK and confirmed the absence of IGF-1 elevation and glucose impairment but were **not powered weight-loss efficacy trials** [4].

### 2.3 Preclinical lipolysis (animal and in vitro) — clearly NOT human fat loss

The biological rationale for AOD-9604 comes almost entirely from rodent and cell work, which must **not** be presented as human efficacy:

- **Ng et al., 2000 (*Horm Res*):** in **obese Zucker rats** (animal), oral AOD-9604 at 500 µg/kg/day for 19 days reduced body-weight gain by >50% (15.8 ± 0.6 vs 35.6 ± 0.8 g) and increased adipose lipolytic activity, without the insulin-sensitivity penalty of intact hGH [3] (animal — Zucker rats; n not stated in abstract).
- **Heffernan et al., 2001 (*Int J Obes Relat Metab Disord*):** in **obese (ob/ob) mice** (animal), 14 days of hGH or AOD-9604 reduced body-weight gain, increased fat oxidation and plasma glycerol, and — unlike hGH — avoided hyperglycemia; AOD-9604 did not compete for the hGH receptor or induce cell proliferation [7] (animal — ob/ob and lean C57BL/6J mice).
- **Heffernan et al., 2001 (*Endocrinology*):** in **wild-type obese mice and β3-adrenoceptor knockout mice** (animal/mechanism), chronic hGH and AOD-9604 reduced body weight and fat and raised β3-AR expression in wild-type mice, but produced **no such effect in β3-AR knockout mice**, implicating β3-adrenergic signalling in the chronic lipolytic action [1] (animal + mechanism).

These studies are the **entire empirical basis** for the "fat-loss peptide" marketing claim. They are short-duration rodent and in-vitro experiments. The one place this mechanism was put to a rigorous human test at scale — the Phase-2b RCT — it did **not** deliver weight loss [12].

### 2.4 Enumeration of the human trials

The human program comprised the following (per the sponsor's pooled safety report [4] and disclosures [11][22]):

1. **Single-dose PK/safety study/studies** in healthy/obese volunteers (IV 25-400 µg/kg; oral up to 54 mg) — PK and safety, not efficacy [4].
2. **Multiple-dose PK/safety study/studies** — repeated oral dosing, safety/tolerability [4].
3. **12-week Phase-2/2a dose-ranging efficacy RCT**, ~300 obese adults, oral 1-30 mg/day — modest weight signal at 1 mg (~2 kg over placebo), conference-published, not PubMed-indexed [11][22].
4. **24-week Phase-2b "OPTIONS" efficacy RCT**, 536 obese adults, oral 0.25/0.5/1 mg/day vs placebo — **negative primary weight-loss endpoint; program discontinued** [11][12].

(The "six randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials" figure in the pooled safety paper [4] counts the single-dose and multiple-dose substudies separately from the two efficacy trials.)

### 2.5 Verdict for the wiki

There is **no proven human fat-loss efficacy** for AOD-9604. The strongest human efficacy test — the 536-patient, 24-week Phase-2b RCT — failed its primary weight-loss endpoint, and the company abandoned the obesity indication. The only "positive" human data is a non-PubMed-indexed 12-week conference abstract showing a modest, dose-anomalous signal that the definitive trial did not confirm. All robust lipolysis evidence is preclinical (rodent/adipocyte). Human safety was good and IGF-1/glucose were unaffected, but **safety is not efficacy**. Any presentation of AOD-9604 as an established human fat-loss agent is **unsupported by the clinical record**.

### 2.6 Coverage gaps (Section 2)

- **No registry record.** The pivotal failed trial has no ClinicalTrials.gov entry; the record rests on sponsor disclosure + sponsor-affiliated review [8][11][12].
- **The one "positive" human study is a non-PubMed-indexed conference abstract** with a biologically-odd inverse dose-response; full dataset not openly available [22].
- **Effect-magnitude precision** for the failed Phase-2b (exact deltas, p-values, per-arm means) is not in the corpus — only the qualitative "negative primary endpoint" is admissible [12].
- **Sponsor-affiliation.** The peer-reviewed account of the failure is by sponsor-affiliated authors [12]; it nonetheless reports the null, which cuts against sponsor interest.

## 3. Other claims: osteoarthritis / cartilage (PRECLINICAL-ONLY)

**Status banner (applies to every claim in this section):** AOD-9604 is **INVESTIGATIONAL** for all musculoskeletal indications. **There is no regulatory approval for any osteoarthritis (OA), cartilage, or bone indication anywhere.** A targeted search of ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, "AOD-9604") returned **zero registered interventional trials** for any indication, including OA [8]. The OA/cartilage "pivot" therefore rests on **preclinical (animal + in-vitro) data and patent disclosures only**. Vendor/clinic marketing that AOD-9604 "supports joint health / cartilage repair" in humans is **not grounded** in the verifiable literature and is excluded here per the no-vendor-grounding rule.

### 3.1 The post-obesity pivot to OA / cartilage

After AOD-9604's obesity program stalled (it met safety endpoints but failed to deliver clinically meaningful weight loss — §2), the developer **Metabolic Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd** repositioned the molecule toward **cartilage repair / osteoarthritis**, leaning on growth-hormone-axis biology (collagen/proteoglycan synthesis) while claiming AOD-9604 does **not** raise IGF-1 and lacks hGH's metabolic liabilities. This repositioning is documented primarily in the company's own **patent estate**, not in peer-reviewed clinical literature.

**Patent disclosure (regulatory/IP, not peer-reviewed):** US Patent **10,111,933 B2**, *"Use of growth hormone fragments,"* assignee **Metabolic Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd**, inventor David Kenley, priority 2011 / granted 2018, claims AOD-9604 for conditions that benefit from growth hormone — explicitly including **cartilage repair and osteoarthritis** — without typical hGH side effects, and discloses an **in-vitro chondrocyte experiment** in which AOD-9604 at **100 µg/mL increased proteoglycan and collagen content of cultured cartilage tissue at 3 weeks, without cytotoxicity** (DNA-content assay at 2 weeks) [9] (in_vitro; chondrocyte tissue culture; concentration disclosed in a patent, not a peer-reviewed paper). The same patent family additionally discloses **mesenchymal-stem-cell / bone differentiation** claims (AOD-9604 promoting differentiation of adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells toward bone) [9] (in_vitro/mechanism; patent disclosure). **Integrity note:** patent specifications are **self-reported by the sponsor and not peer-reviewed**; the in-vitro chondrocyte and bone data are therefore treated as **preliminary/uncorroborated**, not Tier-1 evidence.

### 3.2 Preclinical OA — the one verified animal study

The strongest verifiable OA evidence is a single controlled **animal** study:

- **Kwon DR, Park GY. *Effect of Intra-articular Injection of AOD9604 with or without Hyaluronic Acid in Rabbit Osteoarthritis Model.* Ann Clin Lab Sci. 2015;45(4):426-432. PMID 26275694** [10] (animal; New Zealand white rabbits, n = 32 male, 12 wk old).
  - **Model/population:** collagenase type-II-induced knee OA (chemically induced, **animal model — not human OA**); 4 arms of 8 animals each.
  - **Intervention:** ultrasound-guided **intra-articular** AOD-9604 **0.25 mg / 0.6 mL weekly**, alone or combined with hyaluronic acid (HA 6 mg); comparators saline and HA-alone.
  - **Arms:** (1) saline 0.6 mL; (2) HA 6 mg; (3) AOD-9604 0.25 mg; (4) AOD-9604 + HA.
  - **Result:** morphological and histopathological cartilage-damage scores were **significantly lower in the combination (AOD-9604 + HA) arm** than either agent alone; return to normal ambulation **11 ± 4 days (combination) vs 25 ± 2 days (control)**. The authors frame HA as chondroprotective and AOD-9604 as supporting cartilage regrowth; the headline benefit is **synergy with HA**, with AOD-9604 monotherapy showing weaker standalone effect.
  - **Sponsor linkage / provenance:** **AOD-9604 was supplied by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals (Melbourne, Australia)**; HA supplied by LG Life Science (Korea); no separate funding statement disclosed [10].

**Caveats:** single laboratory (Kwon DR / Park GY, Catholic University of Daegu-affiliated rehabilitation group, Korea), small per-arm n (8), chemically-induced rather than spontaneous/age-related OA, and the chief positive signal **requires HA co-administration**. This is **hypothesis-generating preclinical data**, not evidence of human efficacy.

**Unverifiable mention — OMITTED:** A 2020 human "short-term efficacy of intra-articular AOD9604 injection ... in osteoarthritis of the knee" attributed to "Joint Diseases and Related Surgery" surfaced **only inside an AI-summarized vendor result** and **could not be confirmed** to an exact PMID/author/volume on PubMed. Per the verify-or-omit rule it is **excluded** and must NOT be cited as a human trial until independently verified.

**Vendor/clinic conflation FLAGGED — do NOT propagate:** Multiple secondary sources conflated AOD-9604's OA story with **Paradigm Biopharmaceuticals' knee-OA program**. Paradigm's clinical OA asset is **injectable pentosan polysulfate sodium (iPPS / "Zilosul")** — a **different molecule** — not AOD-9604. AOD-9604 has **no** Phase-II human OA program; the Paradigm Phase-II/III data belong to pentosan polysulfate and are **out of scope and not attributable to AOD-9604** [8]. This is a recurring grounding error in the secondary literature and is **explicitly excluded** from this report.

### 3.3 Other explored indications (bone, combination products, metabolic)

- **Bone / cartilage combination products:** the patent (US 10,111,933 B2) frames AOD-9604 as a **tissue-regeneration adjunct**, pairing it with **hyaluronic acid** (echoed by the Kwon rabbit study's AOD+HA synergy) and, in disclosure, with cartilage-supportive agents and MSC-based approaches [9][10] (in_vitro/animal; combination concept; INVESTIGATIONAL, no product).
- **Bone / MSC differentiation:** in-vitro claims that AOD-9604 promotes adipose-derived MSC differentiation toward bone and does not stimulate IGF-1 appear in **patent disclosure only** [9] (mechanism; uncorroborated).
- **Metabolic / nutraceutical repositioning (lineage continuity, for audit context):** separate from OA, AOD-9604 was also repositioned as a **nutraceutical / "metabolic health" ingredient**; that line traces to the same molecule and the same originating science group (Ng/Monash) — relevant only as concentration-audit context, not an OA claim [5].

No approved AOD-9604 product exists for **any** of these indications. All are **INVESTIGATIONAL/preliminary**.

### 3.4 Coverage gaps (Section 3)

## 5. Safety & Adverse Effects

**Headline:** AOD-9604 is unusual among gray-market peptides in carrying a substantial *human* safety dataset — six company-sponsored randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (2001-2006) enrolling **~893 adults** (mostly obese) [4]. It was abandoned **not** for a safety signal but because it **failed to separate from placebo on weight loss** in its largest Phase-2b trial; development was terminated in 2007 [4][12]. The trial peptide was **well tolerated**, with an adverse-event profile **indistinguishable from placebo**, and its distinguishing feature is the *absence* of the GH-axis effects that make full-length hGH problematic. The major caveat: none of this short-term tolerability translates into validated **long-term** human safety, and essentially none of it was generated with the **subcutaneous route** or the **unregulated product identity** now sold on the gray market [13].

### 5.1 Trial-observed adverse events (well tolerated, placebo-comparable)

Across the six trials — two intravenous pilot/PK studies (METAOD001, n=15; METAOD002, n=23) and four oral studies including the two Phase-2b trials (METAOD005, n=300, 12 weeks; METAOD006, n=502, 24 weeks) — the pooled safety summary concluded that AOD-9604 "displayed a very good safety and tolerability profile indistinguishable from placebo" [4] (mechanism_review / pooled-trial safety summary, Stier et al. 2013, ~893 humans).

Specific findings [4]:
- **Headache** was the most frequently reported adverse event in every study (e.g., 69.6% in the IV METAOD002 study; 42.6% in the active-treatment phase of METAOD005). Critically, headache frequency was **similar in placebo groups**, and "the distribution of the intensity of AEs was similar across all treatment groups."
- **Dose-related GI effects at the highest oral dose:** subjects receiving 54 mg AOD-9604 (a supraphysiologic exploratory oral dose) experienced more headaches, diarrhea, and flatulence — the only clear dose-AE relationship reported.
- **Serious adverse events (SAEs):** zero in the four smaller studies (METAOD001-004). In METAOD005, 5 SAEs occurred (3 skin cancers in the 20 mg group, 1 melanoma in the 10 mg group, 1 breast cancer in the 5 mg group), **all judged by investigators as not related to study drug.** In METAOD006, 18 SAEs (3.6% of subjects) with a distribution "similar among all treatment groups." No SAE was attributed to AOD-9604, and no treatment-related withdrawals occurred [4].
- **Injection-site reactions:** in the IV studies the compound was infused rather than self-injected subcutaneously, and the published record does not report a meaningful injection-site AE burden — relevant because the gray-market product is administered **subcutaneously**, a route for which **no published human AE data exist** [4][13].

### 5.2 The GH-axis-sparing claim — VERIFIED

The defining safety differentiator is that, unlike full-length hGH, AOD-9604 retains lipolytic activity without activating the GH/IGF-1 axis or impairing carbohydrate metabolism. The human trial data support this:

- **No effect on IGF-1 (CONFIRMED):** across the long-term oral trials, "there were no significant changes or notable trends" in serum IGF-1 in AOD-9604 groups versus placebo [4]. The authors state this "confirms the hypothesis that AOD9604 does not act via IGF-1." The metabolism review independently reports that "AOD9604 did not stimulate the production of IGF-1 in any of the systems studied," distinguishing it from full-length hGH [5]. This matters because IGF-1 elevation is the proximate mediator of hGH's growth/organomegaly and theoretical proliferative/oncogenic concerns; its absence is the core of the favorable differential profile.
- **No effect on glucose tolerance / insulin sensitivity (CONFIRMED):** oral glucose tolerance testing across the trials showed "no significant changes or obvious trends in any mean values… at any time point" for AOD-9604 versus placebo, supporting the conclusion that "AOD9604 has no negative effect on carbohydrate metabolism" — explicitly contrasted with the diabetogenic, insulin-resistance-inducing effect of full-length hGH [4]. The metabolism review concurs that "AOD9604 elicited the fat reducing effect without inducing insulin resistance" [5]. (One small early study even reported a glucose-tolerance *improvement* signal in a low-dose oral cohort, though this was not the primary safety conclusion and should not be overstated [22].)
- **No GH-like growth/organomegaly effects:** consistent with the absence of IGF-1 stimulation, no GH-type somatic growth, fluid retention, or organomegaly signal was reported in the human trials [4][5].

In short, the GH-axis-sparing claim — no IGF-1 rise, no adverse glucose/insulin effect — is **directly verified in the trial-reported human data** and is the single most defensible positive safety statement about this compound.

### 5.3 Supporting nonclinical toxicology (genotoxicity, animal NOAEL)

The metabolism/safety review summarizes a nonclinical package consistent with the human tolerability findings [5] (mechanism_review + animal toxicology):
- **Genotoxicity:** negative Ames test (no significant mutation-rate increase up to 2,000 µg/plate); non-clastogenic in the CHO chromosome-aberration assay; no dose-related micronucleus increase. Overall: "no evidence of genotoxicological or toxicological concerns" [5].
- **Animal toxicology:** 6-month oral rat study NOAEL ≥100 mg/kg/day; 9-month oral cynomolgus monkey study NOAEL 50 mg/kg/day, with no significant ophthalmic, cardiac, hematologic, biochemical, or histopathologic treatment-related findings [5] (animal: rat and cynomolgus monkey, chronic oral). A 4-week IV rat study (0.1-10 mg/kg/day) showed only reduced female body-mass gain and reduced male thymus mass at the top dose, with no organ pathology [5].
- **Immunogenicity (nonclinical + clinical):** "no significant anti-AOD9604 antibodies detected in any animal experiments" [5]; in the human trials, "in none of the subjects at none of the timepoints were antibodies against the peptide detected" [4].

These data underpinned a conditional **self-affirmed GRAS** determination for use in foods/supplements (oral route) [5] — which is *not* the same as approval as a subcutaneous drug, and does not transfer to the injectable gray-market use case (see §6).

### 5.4 Theoretical / unknown risks

The favorable short-term picture must be bounded honestly:
- **No long-term human safety data:** the longest human exposure was 24 weeks (METAOD006) [4]. There are no human data on chronic/multi-year use, and the program was halted, so **no Phase-III safety database exists.** Any claim of long-term safety is unsupported.
- **Route mismatch:** the human evidence is overwhelmingly *oral* (and IV for PK); the FDA's December 2024 review specifically flagged that **no published human exposure data exist for the subcutaneous, transdermal, or intranasal routes** that compounding pharmacies and gray-market suppliers actually use [13] (regulatory, FDA PCAC review). The favorable oral/IV tolerability cannot be assumed to transfer to chronic subcutaneous injection.
- **Product identity / purity unverifiable:** gray-market "AOD-9604" is not the characterized clinical material. FDA noted that available certificates of analysis "did not consistently control for impurities, aggregates, or microbial limits," and that subcutaneous injection raises specific concern for **immunogenicity from peptide aggregation and impurities** [13]. So while the *characterized clinical peptide* was non-immunogenic, an *uncharacterized injectable gray-market product* carries unquantifiable aggregation/impurity/contamination risk that the trial data do not address.
- **Immunogenicity (residual):** trial material was non-immunogenic [4][5], but immunogenicity is formulation- and impurity-dependent; the FDA concern above is route- and product-specific, not a refutation of the clinical finding [13].

### 5.5 Contraindications (precautionary)

There are no established, evidence-based contraindications, because the compound is unapproved and was never labeled. Precautionary contraindications apply:
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** no human data; avoid (standard for any unapproved peptide) [13].
- **Unapproved-compound cautions:** AOD-9604 is not approved by FDA, EMA, or TGA; FDA recommended *against* placing it on the §503A bulks list in December 2024, citing inadequate physicochemical characterization, immunogenicity concerns, lack of clinical effectiveness data for any approved use, and absence of published human data for the proposed routes [13]. It is also **prohibited in sport by WADA** (S0/S2) [16][20]. These are regulatory/practical contraindications rather than toxicological ones.
- It should be stated plainly that the short-term profile is favorable and GH-axis-sparing — the honest contraindication picture is **"unapproved, uncharacterized injectable product with no long-term data," not "known to be harmful."**

### 5.6 Monitoring & stopping criteria

Because AOD-9604 does not activate the GH/IGF-1 axis [4][5], the usual hGH-monitoring markers (IGF-1, fasting glucose/HbA1c for diabetogenic effect) are expected to be **uninformative as activity biomarkers** — there is **no validated AOD-9604-specific biomarker.** Practical, conservative monitoring (general principles, not validated for this compound):
- **Baseline and periodic fasting glucose / HbA1c** — not because an effect is expected, but to document the absence of metabolic harm and per general unapproved-injectable caution.
- **IGF-1** — baseline/periodic to document the expected *absence* of elevation (any rise would signal off-target or impurity effect inconsistent with the characterized peptide).
- **Injection-site assessment** for local reactions/infection given the unverified product and subcutaneous route.
- **General clinical review** for any unexpected adverse event; given headache was the dominant trial AE, persistent or severe headache warrants attention.
- **Stopping criteria:** discontinue for any new IGF-1 elevation or impaired glucose tolerance, any signs of injection-site infection/abscess, allergic/hypersensitivity reaction (possible immunogenicity from aggregates/impurities), or any serious adverse event. Because efficacy was not demonstrated even in well-powered trials, **the absence of a measurable benefit is itself a reasonable reason to stop.**

### 5.7 Coverage gaps (Section 5)

- **No long-term human safety** — max human exposure 24 weeks; no Phase-III safety database [4].
- **No human SC-route data** — the gray-market route was never in the clinical program [4][13].
- **Product-identity/purity risk** — gray-market material is uncharacterized; FDA flagged aggregation/impurity/immunogenicity for the injectable [13].
- **No validated activity biomarker** — IGF-1/glucose are expected to be uninformative as *activity* markers (they confirm the GH-axis-sparing *absence* of effect, not a benefit) [4][5].
- **All safety data are sponsor-run/sponsor-published** (see §4).

## 6. Regulatory & Sport Status

**Bottom line:** AOD-9604 sits in regulatory limbo — an **unapproved drug** that its developer later tried (with now-lapsed success) to reposition as a food/supplement ingredient, while every major anti-doping authority treats it as **prohibited in sport.** It is *not* an approved drug anywhere; its US dietary/supplement status is regarded by FDA-proximate analysts as **not lawful**; and it is on the WADA Prohibited List [11][12][14][15][16].

### 6.1 Drug-approval status: NONE (verified)

AOD-9604 **has never been approved as a drug by any government health authority** — not the FDA, TGA, or EMA [13][14]. It was developed by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals in the 1990s-2000s as an anti-obesity agent. Its pivotal **Phase-2b trial — a 24-week multicentre study in ~536 obese subjects (2007) — failed to show a statistically significant or clinically meaningful weight-loss difference versus placebo** [12]. Following that failure, the parent company (Calzada) reported to shareholders that the compound showed no promise as a weight-loss drug, and pharmaceutical obesity development was **discontinued (~2007)** [15]. This matches WADA's own characterisation that "AOD-9604 is a substance still under pre-clinical and clinical development and has not been approved for therapeutic use by any government health authority in the world" [16]. Confirmed: drug-approval status = **none.**

### 6.2 The food/supplement pursuit and FDA's position (reported accurately — NOT overstated)

After abandoning the drug program, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals pursued AOD-9604 as a **"functional food" / dietary-supplement ingredient.** The principal artifact cited is a **self-affirmed GRAS** determination: an industry expert panel self-affirmed AOD-9604 as Generally Recognized As Safe for limited food-ingredient use, based on the Phase-1/Phase-2 safety data [15]. It is essential **not to overstate this**: a self-affirmed GRAS conclusion is an *industry-side* safety determination for a narrow food-ingredient use; it is **NOT an FDA approval, not a drug approval, does not authorize therapeutic claims, and does not extend to injectable or compounded forms** [15].

FDA's actual position is that AOD-9604 is **not a lawful dietary ingredient.** The Banned Substances Control Group (BSCG) states the rule plainly: "synthetic chemicals that were designed and developed as drugs can't be used as supplement ingredients under DSHEA, but many of them are still sold in capsules or powders as dietary supplements" — and identifies AOD-9604 as exactly such an unapproved drug being marketed unlawfully as a supplement [14]. AOD-9604 was investigated as a new drug (an IND-stage clinical candidate) **before** any marketing as a supplement, which places it within the DSHEA **"drug-exclusion"** framing that bars such articles from lawful dietary-ingredient status [14]. In short: a *self-affirmed GRAS for a food-ingredient use* existed, but it does **not** make AOD-9604 a lawful US dietary ingredient, and FDA/anti-doping regulators treat products selling it as supplements as unapproved/unlawful [14][15].

In its 2024 compounding review, FDA also flagged that AOD-9604 was nominated (for weight loss, osteoporosis, and osteoarthritis) with **inconsistent characterization data** — e.g., the nomination named AOD-9604 free base while the Certificate of Analysis was for AOD-9604 acetate, with mismatched CAS number/molecular formula — and cited **limited long-term human safety data, peptide impurities, and potential immunogenicity** as concerns [13][15].

**Australia (TGA):** AOD-9604 is **not an approved / ARTG-registered therapeutic good** in Australia; it is captured as a **prescription-only (Schedule 4) substance** under the Poisons Standard via the scheduling of growth-hormone secretagogues / growth-hormone-releasing peptides / growth-hormone fragments as a class [18]. So even in its country of origin, its status is **prescription-only with no approved product** — not an approved drug and not a free-sale supplement.

### 6.3 FDA §503A peptide-bulks action — AOD-9604 status verified, not guessed

In September 2023 FDA placed several peptide bulk drug substances — including **AOD-9604** (acetate and free base), CJC-1295, ipamorelin acetate, thymosin alpha-1, and Selank acetate — into **Category 2** of the interim §503A bulks list ("may present significant safety risks"), effectively disallowing their use in compounding [13][24]. AOD-9604 was then discussed at the **December 4, 2024 PCAC meeting**, where FDA recommended *against* adding it to the §503A Bulks List, citing immunogenicity risk, inadequate characterization, and limited safety data [13].

Crucially, regarding the **April 2026 peptide action**: FDA announced PCAC review of **12 peptides** (BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c [July 23, 2026]; emideltide/DSIP, Semax, Epitalon [July 24, 2026]; and GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, LL-37/cathelicidin, Dihexa acetate, PEG-MGF [before end of Feb 2027]). **AOD-9604 is NOT among these 12 peptides** [17]. Instead, AOD-9604 appears in the April-2026 coverage only as one of four peptides (with CJC-1295, ipamorelin acetate, thymosin alpha-1) whose **Category-2 placement was challenged in litigation** (the *Evexias/Farmakeio* APA suit) [17]. So: AOD-9604 is **not** on the canonical removed-12 list; its compounding status is that of a substance **excluded from the §503A Category-1 list**: it was placed on the interim Category-2 list (Sept 2023), then **removed from Category 2 (~27 Sep 2024) when the nominator withdrew the nomination** (see §8), while the broader Category-2 listing process was also challenged in the *Evexias/Farmakeio* APA litigation [17]. **It is not lawfully compoundable** — only Category-1 substances may be compounded under §503A, and AOD-9604 was never in Category 1 [13][17].

### 6.4 WADA / sport — PROHIBITED; the Essendon saga (verified + anchored)

AOD-9604 is **prohibited in sport.** WADA's determination evolved as follows:

- **As a non-approved substance (S0):** because AOD-9604 had no regulatory approval for human therapeutic use anywhere, WADA held that it fell under the blanket **S0** prohibition ("any pharmacological substance not addressed by any other section of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use"). WADA publicly **clarified to ASADA on 22 April 2013** that AOD-9604 was prohibited [16]. WADA's statement: "AOD-9604 is a substance still under pre-clinical and clinical development and has not been approved for therapeutic use by any government health authority in the world" [16].
- **As a growth-hormone fragment (S2):** AOD-9604 is also covered by the **S2 — Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics** category, which expressly captures **growth-hormone (GH) fragments** (AOD-9604 being hGH 176-191). Anti-doping scientific bodies list AOD-9604 under **S2** [14][20]. (S0 and S2 are not mutually exclusive — S0 was the catch-all basis in 2012-13; the GH-fragment language under S2 also applies.)

**The Essendon Football Club supplements saga (2012-2016)** is the anchoring real-world episode. Under a 2012 supplements program, Essendon AFL players were administered injectables including AOD-9604 and a thymosin variety [17b]. Essendon captain Jobe Watson publicly acknowledged in June 2013 that he had been given "the anti-obesity drug AOD-9604" [17b]. ASADA, however, **declined to pursue AOD-9604 charges for use before 22 April 2013**, judging it too hard to obtain a guilty verdict for a substance not yet explicitly and publicly declared banned at the time of use [16]. The case that ultimately succeeded turned on **thymosin beta-4**, not AOD-9604: on **12 January 2016 the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS)**, on WADA's appeal, found 34 players guilty of using a prohibited substance (the banned thymosin beta-4 variety) and imposed 2016-season suspensions [17b]. **Net WADA status of AOD-9604: PROHIBITED** (S0 catch-all basis confirmed by WADA in 2013; also within S2 GH-fragment scope), though its *enforceability for pre-April-2013 use* was the legally contested point that led ASADA not to charge it directly [16][17b].

### 6.5 Coverage gaps (Section 6)

- **Drug-approval status = none, verified across multiple sources** [13][14][15][16].
- **Self-affirmed GRAS ≠ FDA approval and ≠ lawful US dietary ingredient** (DSHEA drug-exclusion) — stated without overstatement [14][15].
- **§503A:** AOD-9604 is **NOT** in the April-2026 removed-12; it sits in prior Category-2 / litigation; never Category-1, so not lawfully compoundable [13][17].
- **WADA:** S0 confirmed 2013, S2 GH-fragment scope; Essendon/CAS context anchored — but the CAS verdict turned on thymosin beta-4, and AOD-9604 was never directly charged for pre-April-2013 use [16][17b].
- **EU/EMA status** is "no approval found," not affirmatively characterized.

## 7. Why evidence_tier C / risk_tier experimental

### 7.1 Why evidence_tier C

1. **The pivotal human efficacy FAILED.** The one large, well-powered human test — the 536-patient, 24-week Phase-2b oral RCT — did not beat placebo on weight loss, and the program was abandoned (~2007) [11][12]. A higher tier is impossible for a compound whose definitive human trial was negative.
2. **The remaining efficacy evidence is preclinical/in-vitro.** All robust lipolysis data are rodent + adipocyte [1][3][7]; the OA/cartilage case is one rabbit study + a sponsor patent [10][9]. Mechanism→human is extrapolation, and the one human extrapolation that was tested failed.
3. **~100% single-lineage concentration.** Every primary funnels through the Monash/Ng → Metabolic Pharmaceuticals lineage; no sponsor-independent replication using independently sourced material exists [8] (§4).
4. **The "positive" human signal is a non-PubMed-indexed conference abstract** with a biologically-odd inverse dose-response that the definitive trial did not confirm [22][12].

What keeps AOD-9604 at **C rather than lower** is a genuinely *verified* mechanistic differentiator (GHR-independent, IGF-1-sparing, glucose-neutral, confirmed in animals and ~893 humans) [1][2][4][5] and a substantial, clean human *safety* dataset — more human data than most gray-market peptides have. What prevents anything higher is the **failed human efficacy**, the **preclinical-only** lipolysis/OA evidence, and the **single-lineage concentration**. **C = mechanistically verified, human-efficacy-FAILED, clinically unproven.**

### 7.2 Why risk_tier experimental

1. **Never approved as a drug anywhere** [11][12][14].
2. **Gray-market injectable with no published human SC-route data** — the clinical program used oral/IV only; the route now sold has zero human exposure data [4][13].
3. **No long-term human safety** — max exposure 24 weeks; no Phase-III safety database [4].
4. **FDA-flagged immunogenicity/aggregation/impurity** for uncharacterized injectable product [13].
5. **Not lawfully compoundable; WADA-prohibited; prescription-only even in Australia** [13][16][18].

The honest nuance that keeps this from reading as "dangerous": the *characterized clinical peptide* was well-tolerated, GH-axis-sparing, and non-immunogenic short-term [4][5]. The experimental-tier designation reflects **unapproved status + uncharacterized injectable + no long-term/SC data**, not a known toxicity signal.

## 8. Prescribing-practice & Non-English Summary

> **Critical framing:** AOD-9604 has **no approved clinical dose.** Its obesity program was terminated in 2007 after the lipolytic signal failed to translate into significant human fat loss [12][21]. Everything below is either (a) a *gray-market convention*, (b) a *trial-derived* dose, or (c) a *regulatory/access* fact. **No practitioner or vendor source grounds any efficacy or adverse-event-rate claim** — those are Tier-1 only and live in §2 (efficacy FAILED) and §5 (safety). The full prescribing-practice and non-English detail lives in the sibling layer files; this is the brief.

### 8.1 Dosing conventions (gray-market vs trial-derived)

- **Gray-market fat-loss convention:** the dominant convention across vendor/clinic pages is **~300 mcg subcutaneously once daily, in the morning, fasted** (typically 30-60 min before food), in cycles of ~8-12 weeks [25][26]. The fasted-AM rationale (low insulin removes an anti-lipolytic brake) is a *mechanistic rationale asserted by gray-market sources*, not a clinically validated instruction (vendor_label / practitioner_protocol) [25][26]. A common escalation is to **500 mcg/day**, sometimes split (250 mcg AM + 250 mcg pre-bed), "if results plateau after ~4 weeks" (vendor_label) [26]. These are **convention, not a label** — there is no approved label to anchor them.
- **One named-prescriber data point:** **Dr. Dan Wool, NMD** (Practical Vitality PLLC, Scottsdale AZ; article 26 Feb 2026, medically reviewed Apr 2026) describes a clinical-use range of **250-500 mcg/day, morning, empty stomach**, under medical supervision with labs/monitoring (practitioner_protocol) [27]. Consistent with the gray-market convention; it does **not** elevate any efficacy claim. Most "clinic" pages name a clinic but no individual prescriber/protocol/date and do not meet the practitioner bar [25].
- **Trial-derived doses (for contrast, NOT a convention):** the Phase-II obesity work evaluated **oral 1 mg/day** (within a broader dose range across the 6 trials / >900 subjects), with SC ranges used in earlier pharmacology [21][23]. The frequent gray-market claim that "clinical trials used up to 1,000 mcg/day" is therefore *trial-derived* and must not be repackaged as a recommended dose (vendor_label — misattribution risk) [26]. **Self-check:** the ~300 mcg/day figure is reported strictly as gray-market convention; the 1 mg / ~1000 mcg figures strictly as trial-derived.

### 8.2 Compounding / access

**AOD-9604 is NOT lawfully compoundable in the U.S. under §503A.** The mechanical chain:
- Placed in **Category 2** of the FDA interim 503A bulks list (Sept 2023) — the "may present significant safety risks" bucket that effectively bars compounding use (regulatory) [13][19].
- FDA's cited concerns: potential immune reactions, manufacturing/peptide-synthesis impurities, lack of human safety data (regulatory) [13].
- **Removed from Category 2 (~27 Sep 2024) only because the nominator withdrew the nomination** — not because it was cleared. AOD-9604 **was never on Category 1**, and only Category-1 substances may be lawfully compounded under §503A. So removal from Category 2 did **not** make it compoundable (regulatory) [13][17]. (One inference: removal-by-withdrawal is an administrative/commercial act, not an FDA safety/efficacy clearance — neutral-to-negative for the evidence base, not a vindication.)
- Litigation (Evexias/Farmakeio, Texas APA challenge) named AOD-9604 among four peptides, and an April-2026 Federal Register notice set PCAC review dates — but **AOD-9604 is not on the July-2026 or February-2027 PCAC dockets**, so no near-term path to Category-1 is scheduled (regulatory) [17].

**Compounding data-sheet search: NONE FOUND.** A targeted search for compounding-pharmacy data sheets (Empower, Tailor Made, Hallandale, Belmar, APS, Strive) returned **no AOD-9604 compounding data sheet** — the *expected* result given the regulatory gate. The practical access channel today is **gray-market research-chemical vendors**, sold "for research use only," with COA/purity but no clinical labeling (vendor_label) [28].

**Reconstitution (math only, vendor_label):** a common 5 mg vial reconstituted with bacteriostatic water: e.g., **2.0 mL → 2.5 mg/mL**, or **3.0 mL → ~1.667 mg/mL**; on a U-100 insulin syringe at the 3.0 mL fill, 1 unit (0.01 mL) ≈ 16.7 mcg, so a 300 mcg dose ≈ **18 units (0.18 mL)** [29][30]. Vendor pages claim reconstituted stability ~30-45 days refrigerated [30]. Arithmetic/handling figures only; no efficacy weight.

**Cost (qualitative):** gray-market pricing is **cheap** — AOD-9604 is one of the lower-cost research peptides, sold as 5 mg vials by Western and Russian-language vendors at commodity prices (vendor_label) [28][31]. No clinical-grade pricing exists because there is no approved product.
