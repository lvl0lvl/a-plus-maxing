# Section A — Pharmacology & Mechanism: BPC-157

> Citation-integrity re-run. Every claim below traces to a source that was actually fetched (URLs in Bibliography). Type-tags follow the project enum. No PMIDs, DOIs, authors, or findings were generated from memory; claims without a locatable real source were dropped (see "Dropped claims").

## A.1 Molecular identity — what BPC-157 actually is

BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide (a "pentadecapeptide") with the sequence Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val and a molecular weight of ~1419.55 Da [1, mechanism_review]. The abbreviation "BPC" stands for "Body Protection (Protective) Compound," and the peptide is conventionally described as having been "isolated from human gastric juice" [1, mechanism_review][6, mechanism_review].

The widely marketed phrasing "derived from a protein found in gastric juice" requires a precise correction. The honest state is: the **15-residue sequence does not occur as such in nature** — it is a designed/synthesized fragment that is the portion claimed to be "essential and fully responsible for biological activities" of the parent BPC material [1, mechanism_review]. A mechanistic review describes BPC-157 as a "15 amino acid peptide BPC fragment" of a larger gastric protein [6, mechanism_review], and an independent review states it shows "no sequence homology with known intestinal peptides" [1, mechanism_review]. An independent clinical review likewise classifies it plainly as a "synthetic pentadecapeptide originally isolated from human gastric juice," originally reported by Sikiric in 1993 [2, mechanism_review]. So the accurate framing is: a synthetic peptide whose sequence is asserted to correspond to an active fragment of a gastric-juice protein, not a peptide that circulates naturally in that form.

## A.2 Physicochemical / stability claims

BPC-157 is repeatedly characterized as unusually stable in human gastric juice. The originating (Sikiric/Zagreb) literature states it is "essentially stable and native in human gastric juice" and "resistant to degradation in human gastric juice [for] more than 24 hours" [6, mechanism_review]. A structural rationale is offered by an independent group: the N-terminal glycine "can serve as a stabilizer that can regulate the compound's protease degradation," and the multiple proline residues "strongly prevent … non-specific proteolysis"; the peptide is described as "stable in water and gastric juice" [1, mechanism_review]. Note these are stability characterizations, not a measured plasma half-life — a numerical circulating half-life could not be confirmed against a primary fetched source and is therefore omitted (see "Dropped claims").

## A.3 Receptor pharmacology — the honest state

There is **no well-characterized specific receptor** for BPC-157. The independent reviews surveyed do not identify a dedicated receptor or binding target; mechanisms are described as multi-pathway (VEGFR2, nitric oxide, Src–caveolin-1, ERK1/2) rather than via a defined BPC-157 receptor [1, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review]. The originating group frames the absence of a required carrier or co-agent as a feature — "BPC 157 always given alone, acts alone" and needs no special delivery system [6, mechanism_review] — but this is not evidence of a characterized receptor. For wiki purposes: **receptor target unknown / not established.**

## A.4 Proposed mechanisms — demonstrated (in vitro / animal) vs extrapolated

The mechanisms below are demonstrated only in cell and animal models. No completed human clinical trial supports any mechanistic claim, and BPC-157 is an unapproved drug (see A.5) [7, regulatory].

**VEGFR2 / angiogenesis (animal + in vitro).** In a rat hind-limb ischemia model plus human endothelial cell culture, BPC-157 accelerated blood-flow recovery, increased vessel density and vascular VEGFR2 expression, up-regulated VEGFR2 (mRNA and protein) but not VEGF-A, promoted VEGFR2 internalization (blocked by the endocytosis inhibitor dynasore), and time-dependently activated the VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS pathway [3, animal]. Independent reviews summarize the same VEGFR2-mediated proangiogenic mechanism [1, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review].

**Nitric-oxide system / Src–caveolin-1–eNOS (animal + in vitro).** In isolated male Sprague-Dawley rat aorta (plus HUVEC and rat vascular smooth-muscle cells), BPC-157 produced endothelium-dependent, NO-mediated vasorelaxation (markedly attenuated in endothelium-denuded rings and by L-NAME or hemoglobin) and reduced eNOS/caveolin-1 binding (to ~50% of control), activating the Src–Cav-1–eNOS cascade [4, animal]. The originating group has long asserted that BPC-157's effects "always" involve interaction with the NO system [6, mechanism_review].

**FAK–paxillin pathway / tendon-fibroblast effects (in vitro).** In cultured tendon fibroblasts from rat Achilles tendon, BPC-157 dose-dependently increased phosphorylation of FAK and paxillin (without changing total protein), accelerated tendon-explant outgrowth and fibroblast migration/spreading, and improved cell survival under H2O2 oxidative stress; cell proliferation per se was not directly affected [5, in_vitro].

**Growth-factor / growth-hormone-receptor axis (in vitro).** In cultured rat Achilles tendon fibroblasts, BPC-157 at 0.5 μg/mL induced growth hormone receptor (GHR) expression time-dependently (days 1–3), with up to ~sevenfold increase, offering a proposed route by which it could potentiate growth-hormone/IGF tendon signaling [8, in_vitro].

**Dopamine / serotonin system interactions (animal).** In rat models, the originating group reports BPC-157 counteracts haloperidol- and L-NAME-induced catalepsy, counteracts dopaminergic disturbances (receptor blockade, supersensitivity, nigrostriatal damage, vesicle depletion), and is active against serotonin syndrome with region-specific serotonin release [9, animal][6, mechanism_review]. These are mechanistic/behavioral animal findings, not receptor-binding data, and they originate from a single lab (see Concentration note).

**Marketed / extrapolated beyond the above.** Broader "systemic regeneration," "gut–brain axis repair," human tendon/ligament cure claims, and oral-dosing efficacy in humans are **extrapolations** from the animal/in-vitro data above and from preclinical oral-stability observations; they are not established in humans and should be presented as marketed/hypothesized, not demonstrated [2, mechanism_review][7, regulatory].

## A.5 Regulatory / status anchor

BPC-157 is "not a dietary ingredient … [it] is an unapproved drug and cannot be legally prescribed or sold over the counter"; it does not appear in the FDA Approved Drugs database, the FDA has cautioned against compounded products containing it, and it is on WADA's Prohibited List (class S0, Non-Approved Substances). "There is little to no reliable scientific evidence to support the safety or effectiveness of BPC-157 in humans." [7, regulatory]

## Concentration note

Distinct primary/originating labs behind the cited mechanism evidence: **2** (Sikiric / University of Zagreb; and the Pang/Chang group at Chang Gung University, Taiwan), plus 2 independent review groups (Gdansk-Warsaw [1]; Utah [2]) and 1 government regulatory source [7]. Of the **6 primary-evidence/originating-literature citations [3,4,5,6,8,9]: the Chang Gung (Taiwan) group accounts for 4/6 (~67%) and the Sikiric/Zagreb group 2/6 (~33%).** Important nuance for the single-lab-dominance audit: the *cellular/angiogenesis/tendon mechanism* evidence cited here is actually dominated by the **independent Chang Gung group**, which mitigates (but does not eliminate) the well-known concern that BPC-157's broader claimed effects rest disproportionately on the originating Sikiric/Zagreb lab. The dopamine/serotonin and gastric-origin/stability claims, by contrast, trace to the Sikiric/Zagreb group [6,9] and should be read with single-lab caution.
