# Section C: Animal & Preclinical Efficacy — BPC-157

> **Citation-integrity note.** Every primary below was fetched from a real source (PubMed / publisher / PMC). Each animal citation carries **species + n** (where the source reports n; where n was not stated in the accessible abstract/text this is marked "n not stated"). Lab/group affiliation is recorded for every primary because the central credibility issue for BPC-157 is single-lab dominance (see **Concentration audit**). Routes/doses are reported as stated in each study; no route-extrapolation is performed.

BPC-157 is a synthetic pentadecapeptide (sequence GEPPPGKPADDAGLV) presented by its originators as a partial sequence of a "body protection compound" from gastric juice. Its preclinical efficacy literature spans musculoskeletal, gastrointestinal, vascular, and neural injury models. The defining feature of this literature is that the large majority of in-vivo efficacy work originates from a single group (Sikiric and collaborators, University of Zagreb School of Medicine, historically linked to the Croatian firm Pliva, which trialed the peptide as PL-14736 / PLD-116). A minority of musculoskeletal/angiogenesis findings have been reproduced by an independent Taiwanese group (Chang Gung University / Taipei Medical University), and those are flagged explicitly.

## Tendon / ligament / Achilles

- Transected rat Achilles tendon detached from the calcaneus heals faster functionally and biomechanically with BPC-157, which also opposes corticosteroid-induced aggravation of healing [2, animal]. Independent of the originating group, BPC-157 accelerated outgrowth of tendon explants, increased tendocyte survival under H₂O₂ stress, and dose-dependently increased fibroblast migration via the FAK–paxillin pathway, with a confirmatory in-vivo transected-Achilles arm [1, animal] — this is one of the few in-vivo musculoskeletal results from a lab outside Zagreb.
- Early functional recovery of the Achilles tendon-to-bone unit after transection is improved by BPC-157 through an anti-inflammatory effect plus increased new-vessel formation, whereas methylprednisolone suppressed inflammation but failed to support early recovery (n = 72 rats) [3, animal].
- Medial collateral ligament transection healing is improved across functional, biomechanical, macroscopic, and histological endpoints over a 90-day course, with efficacy by IP, per-oral (drinking water), and topical routes [4, animal].
- Mechanistic in-vitro support from the independent Taiwanese group: BPC-157 dose- and time-dependently up-regulates the growth-hormone receptor in rat Achilles tendon fibroblasts, potentiating GH-driven proliferation [5, in_vitro].

## Muscle

- Gastrocnemius crush injury (delivered force 0.727 Ns/cm²) heals better with BPC-157 given IP or as a local cream — less hematoma/edema, no post-injury leg contracture, improved microscopy and function, and normalized serum enzyme markers (CK, LDH, AST, ALT) [6, animal].

## Bone

- Segmental bone defect in **rabbits**: intramuscular BPC-157 (10 µg/kg) produced callus and bony union comparable to bone-marrow grafting and superior to controls, in a head-to-head against autologous cortical bone and bone marrow implantation [7, animal]. (Note: one of the few non-rodent in-vivo efficacy models in the BPC-157 corpus.)

## Gastrointestinal

- **Foundational gastric/duodenal ulcer work:** in three rat ulcer models — 48-h restraint stress, subcutaneous cysteamine (duodenal), and intragastric 96% ethanol — BPC-157 (IP and intragastric) was consistently protective across all three, where several reference standards were not [8, animal]. This 1994 paper is the cited origin of the "stable gastric pentadecapeptide" anti-ulcer claim.
- **Intestinal anastomosis:** ileoileal anastomosis healing is improved on all measured wound-healing parameters by the two higher IP doses (10 µg/kg, 10 ng/kg) [9, animal].
- **Fistula:** persistent colocutaneous fistula (5 mm colonic + 5 mm skin defect) closes faster with BPC-157 given IP or per-oral, with the effect tied to the nitric-oxide system [10, animal].
- **Esophagitis / sphincter failure:** BPC-157 (10 µg/kg IP daily, or 0.16 µg/mL in drinking water) recovers esophageal mucosa and restores both lower-esophageal and pyloric sphincter pressures in a chronic sphincter-failure model; ranitidine was ineffective in the same protocol [11, animal].

## Vascular / "hit-and-run" angiogenesis

- **Major-vessel occlusion:** permanent superior-mesenteric-artery ligation in rats — BPC-157 (10 µg/kg or 10 ng/kg IP) rapidly activates collateral pathways (e.g., inferior anterior pancreaticoduodenal and inferior mesenteric arteries) that bypass the occlusion, reversing multiple-organ dysfunction including portal/caval hypertension, brain swelling, and thrombosis (n = 6 rats/group/interval) [12, animal]. This is the in-vivo basis for the originators' "hit-and-run" collateral-recruitment framing.
- **Hindlimb ischemia (independent Taiwanese group):** BPC-157 accelerated laser-Doppler blood-flow recovery and increased vessel density in ischemic rat hindlimb muscle, with up-regulation and internalization of VEGFR2 and activation of the VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS pathway (confirmed in human endothelial cells in vitro) [13, animal] — independent in-vivo replication of the pro-angiogenic claim.

## Neuroprotection

- **Spinal cord injury:** after laminectomy (L2–L3) and 60-s compression of the dural sac, a single IP dose (200 or 2 µg/kg) given 10 min post-injury produced clinical improvement, better tail motor function, absence of autotomy, and resolved spasticity by day 15 (≥6 rats/group) [14, animal].

## Concentration audit

**Denominator note:** the bibliography has **14 numbered entries [1]–[14]**, each a distinct primary (no duplicates), so **distinct primaries = 14**. Of these, **13 are in-vivo efficacy primaries**; the one exception is **[5] (in-vitro only)**. Unless stated otherwise, the single-lab-share denominator below is the **13 in-vivo primaries**.

**Distinct labs/groups among the 14 cited primaries: 2 (plus this is conservative — within "Zagreb" the senior authors Sikiric and Seiwerth recur on nearly every entry).**

| Group | Affiliation | Citations | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sikiric / Zagreb | Univ. of Zagreb School of Medicine (Dept. Pharmacology + Pathology; Univ. Hospital Center / Traumatology Zagreb); historically tied to Pliva (PL-14736) | [2],[3],[4],[6],[7],[8],[9],[10],[11],[12],[14] | **11** |
| Chang Gung / Taipei (Taiwan) | Chang Gung University / Chang Gung Memorial Hospital / Taipei Medical University | [1],[5],[13] | 3 |

> Note: [1] and [13] are counted as both — they are independent in-vivo works but [5] is in-vitro only. Distinct-primary total = **14**; **in-vivo** primaries = **13** (one Taiwanese paper [5] is in-vitro, the other two [1],[13] include in-vivo arms).

**Single-largest-lab share (by in-vivo primary count): 11 / 13 ≈ 85%** of in-vivo efficacy primaries originate from the Sikiric/Zagreb group — the Zagreb group authored every in-vivo study here except the two Taiwanese ones ([1] tendon, [13] hindlimb ischemia). (Across all **14** distinct primaries, including the in-vitro [5], the share is **11 / 14 ≈ 79%**; the in-vivo-restricted **11 / 13 ≈ 85%** figure is the headline because [5] is mechanism-only, not an efficacy study.) By **domain**, Zagreb is the *sole* source for: bone, GI (ulcer/anastomosis/fistula/esophagitis), neuro/SCI, and major-vessel-occlusion collateral recruitment.

**Sikiric-only (NOT independently replicated) findings:**
- Bone defect healing (rabbit) [7]
- All GI efficacy: gastric/duodenal ulcer [8], ileoileal anastomosis [9], colocutaneous fistula [10], esophagitis/sphincter [11]
- Spinal cord injury functional recovery [14]
- Major-vessel-occlusion "hit-and-run" collateral recruitment [12]
- Achilles tendon-to-bone and MCL ligament biomechanics [2],[3],[4] (the *in-vivo biomechanical* claims; see partial replication below)

**Independently replicated (Chang Gung / Taipei, Taiwan):**
- Tendon healing / tendocyte biology: explant outgrowth, fibroblast survival and migration, with an in-vivo transected-Achilles arm [1]; plus GH-receptor mechanism [5]. This corroborates the *direction* (BPC-157 promotes tendon-cell repair) but not the specific Zagreb biomechanical magnitudes.
- Pro-angiogenic effect: hindlimb-ischemia blood-flow recovery + VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS pathway [13]. This is the strongest cross-lab corroboration of BPC-157's core mechanistic claim (angiogenesis), and it partially underpins the Zagreb "vascular recruitment" GI/vascular narrative — though the specific *collateral-rerouting* phenomenon [12] is Zagreb-only.

**Bottom line for the wiki:** BPC-157's preclinical efficacy is real in the literature but **heavily concentrated in one group (11 of 13 in-vivo primaries, ≈85%, from Sikiric/Zagreb).** Only two themes — tendon-cell repair and VEGFR2-mediated angiogenesis — have any independent-lab in-vivo support, and that support is mechanistic/directional rather than a replication of the specific functional/biomechanical magnitudes. All GI, bone, neuro, and collateral-vessel "hit-and-run" claims rest on the originating group alone.
