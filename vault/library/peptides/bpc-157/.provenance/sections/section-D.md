# Section D — Safety, Adverse Events & Contraindications (BPC-157)

> **Evidence-floor statement (read first).** Human safety data for BPC-157 is essentially absent. There is **no completed, published randomized controlled trial**; the only human exposure on record is three small pilot/case studies (combined n ≈ 30) plus one Phase I trial (NCT02637284, 42 healthy volunteers) whose results were **never submitted and the submission cancelled in 2016** [2, mechanism_review]. Consequently, the great majority of the "safety" picture below rests on (a) **animal toxicology**, (b) **theoretical mechanism** (the pro-angiogenic concern), and (c) **qualitative anecdotal/clinic-aggregate patterns**. No numerical human adverse-event rate can be admissibly derived from current data. Vendor labels and anecdote aggregates are NOT used anywhere below to ground a number or dose.

---

## D.1 Animal toxicology / absence of a lethal or toxic dose

Across the published preclinical record, **no minimum toxic dose and no lethal dose (LD50) have been identified** for BPC-157 [1, animal][2, mechanism_review].

- **Single-dose, rat (Sprague-Dawley):** a single intramuscular dose of **20 mg/kg** produced **no deaths and no obvious abnormalities** in body weight, food intake, or behavior [2, mechanism_review][1, animal] (species: rat).
- **Single-dose, dog (Beagle):** **10 mg/kg i.m.** produced **no distinct adverse effects** [2, mechanism_review] (species: dog).
- **Repeated-dose, 28-day:** doses up to **4 mg/kg/day (rat)** and **2 mg/kg/day (dog)** produced **no apparent changes vs. saline controls**; the one signal noted was a **decrease in creatinine at 2 mg/kg in dogs, not at lower doses** [1, animal][2, mechanism_review] (species: rat and dog).
- **Genotoxicity:** the preclinical battery reported **no genetic (genotoxic) effect** [1, animal].
- **Embryo-fetal / reproductive:** reported as showing **no embryo-fetal toxicity** [1, animal].
- **Local tolerance:** irritation was characterized as **mild** [1, animal].

The Xu et al. 2020 evaluation spanned **mice, rats, rabbits, and dogs** [1, animal]. The widely repeated "LD50 ≈ 2 g/kg" figure circulates in secondary/practitioner sources but is **not cleanly attributable to a primary toxicology endpoint**; the defensible primary-literature statement is that a toxic/lethal dose was *not reached* at the doses tested (up to 20 mg/kg single i.m.) [1, animal][2, mechanism_review]. The "2 g/kg" number is therefore **omitted as a sourced figure** (see Dropped claims).

> Per-group n values are not stated in the openly accessible abstract/review text obtained; the full Xu 2020 article (Elsevier) was paywalled. n-per-group is therefore reported as **not verifiable from accessible text** rather than invented.

## D.2 The theoretical oncologic concern — what the actual basis is

The cancer concern is **mechanistic/theoretical, not empirical**, and the empirical signals that exist point the *opposite* direction. The honest framing:

**The basis of the worry.** BPC-157 is **pro-angiogenic**. Hsieh et al. 2017 showed that in a rat hind-limb ischemia model BPC-157 accelerated blood-flow recovery and increased vessel number and vascular **VEGFR2** expression; in human vascular endothelial cells it increased VEGFR2 mRNA/protein, promoted **VEGFR2 internalization** (blocked by the endocytosis inhibitor dynasore), and activated the **VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS** signaling pathway [4, animal] (in vivo species: rat; plus in vitro human endothelial cells). Because tumor growth and metastasis depend on neovascularization, a pro-angiogenic, VEGFR2-engaging agent **could in principle support tumor vascularization** — this is the literal mechanistic basis for the contraindication-in-cancer concern [2, mechanism_review].

**The countervailing empirical evidence.**
- *In vitro, human melanoma:* Radeljak, Seiwerth & Sikirić (2004), *Melanoma Research* abstract, reported that BPC-157 **inhibited** human melanoma cell growth and VEGF signaling via the MAPK/ERK pathway, reducing the S-phase fraction by ~20% (lower conc.) up to ~55% (higher conc.) and decreasing ERK phosphorylation [5, in_vitro].
- *In vivo, colon adenocarcinoma cachexia (C26 model, mice):* BPC-157 improved total body weight; tumor volume was lower but **without statistical significance** between treated and untreated xenograft mice [2, mechanism_review] (species: mouse).

**Net.** The pro-angiogenic mechanism is real and is the legitimate theoretical basis for caution; the *direct* tumor data that exist (one in-vitro melanoma abstract, one underpowered C26 xenograft) show **inhibition or no significant effect**, not promotion. A frequently cited claim that BPC-157 "reduced B16 lung metastases in mice" traces to a **personal communication / unpublished observation** and is **omitted** (see Dropped claims). The defensible statement: *tumor promotion in humans is unproven and untested; the angiogenesis mechanism makes active malignancy a precautionary contraindication despite contrary preclinical signals.*

## D.3 Immunogenicity & peptide-impurity concerns (the FDA Category-2 rationale)

The FDA placed BPC-157 in **Category 2** ("bulk drug substances that may present significant safety risks") for compounding under section 503A, effective **September 29, 2023** [3, regulatory]. The FDA's stated rationale, in its own language, is that BPC-157:

> "may pose risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration and may have complexities with regard to peptide-related impurities and active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) characterization," and that the FDA "identified no, or only limited, safety-related information for the proposed routes of administration." [3, regulatory]

The general FDA immunogenicity framework (PCAC, Dec 2024 deliberations) holds that **parenteral routes (SC, IV, IM, intradermal, inhalation, intravitreal) carry greater immunogenic risk** than oral, and that **aggregated peptide or peptide on scaffolding can elicit immune responses**; product-related impurities are assessed via LC-MS, MS-MS, peptide mapping, and in-vitro MHC-binding/T-cell-activation assays [8, regulatory]. This is the mechanistic content behind the immunogenicity flag.

**Regulatory-status caveat (dynamic).** The documented, authoritative status is that BPC-157 **remains in FDA 503A Category 2** (placed **2023-09-29**) as of mid-2026, with a Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) review scheduled for **23 July 2026** to consider it (alongside KPV, TB-500, MOTs-C) for the 503A bulks list; any move out of Category 2 would require notice-and-comment rulemaking and is neither final nor imminent (FDA Law Blog, Hyman Phelps & McNamara, Apr 2026) [3, regulatory]. A purported **HHS removal of BPC-157 from Category 2 on 2026-04-15 is reported only by a secondary low-trust source ([6], agemd.com) and is NOT confirmed against an FDA/HHS primary** as of 2026-06-18 — that source in fact traces the claim to an RFK Jr. podcast statement and concedes "the FDA has not yet published its formal updated list," so **treat the removal as unverified**. New developments add **no new safety data** regardless; the immunogenicity/impurity *concerns themselves remain unrebutted by human evidence*.

## D.4 Drug-interaction theory

No human drug-interaction studies exist. Theoretical interactions derive entirely from the **nitric-oxide (NO) system**, which BPC-157 modulates [2, mechanism_review]:

- **NO-pathway drugs (e.g., aspirin, which inhibits eNOS):** the Jozwiak review explicitly recommends "deep analysis of [BPC-157's] interaction with potent drugs affecting NO pathways" [2, mechanism_review].
- **Antiplatelets / anticoagulants:** elevated NO is a "potent inhibitor of platelet aggregation," prompting caution in bleeding-risk and cardiovascular patients [2, mechanism_review]; practitioner references list bleeding disorders / anticoagulant co-use as relative contraindications [7, practitioner_protocol].
- **CYP450-metabolized drugs:** high NO can inhibit cytochrome P450 and heme insertion, a theoretical basis for altered drug metabolism [2, mechanism_review].

All of the above are **theoretical/mechanistic**, not demonstrated in humans.

## D.6 Adverse-event pattern (qualitative only)

In the three human pilot/case studies (combined n ≈ 30; intra-articular knee, intravesical/cystitis, IV), **no adverse events were reported** [2, mechanism_review][9, open_label]. The IV pilot (Lee & Burgess, 2 healthy adults, up to 20 mg IV) reported **no adverse events and no clinically meaningful changes in vital signs, ECG, or cardiac/hepatic/renal/thyroid/metabolic labs** [9, open_label] (n = 2 — far too small for any rate). The most commonly *described* AE in clinical/practitioner reporting is **mild local injection-site reaction** [7, practitioner_protocol] — **qualitative only; no admissible incidence rate exists.**

> Anecdote-aggregate / clinic patterns (fatigue, GI upset, dizziness) circulate but are **not** cited here to ground any number, per integrity rules.

## D.7 Honest long-term statement

**Long-term human safety of BPC-157 is uncharacterized.** No completed/published RCT exists; the longest human follow-up on record is ~12 months in a small knee-pain series, and there is **no multi-year safety data** [2, mechanism_review]. The one registered Phase I trial (42 volunteers) **never reported results** [2, mechanism_review]. The reassuring animal toxicology (no LD50 reached, negative genotox/embryo-fetal) and the small zero-AE pilots are **encouraging but underpowered**; the pro-angiogenic mechanism remains a real theoretical oncologic liability that **has not been excluded in humans**. Cautious-practitioner posture (cancer/pregnancy/bleeding contraindications, malignancy + lab monitoring, conservative cycling) is **convention, not evidence**.
