# BPC-157 (Pentadecapeptide "Body Protection Compound")

## Metadata
- class: peptide
- evidence_tier: C — animal/preclinical-dominant; ~85% of in-vivo efficacy primaries from a single lab; no completed human RCT with published results
- risk_tier: experimental — unapproved drug, no controlled human safety dataset; placed in FDA 503A Category 2 (2023-09-29), removed from interim Category 2 ~April 2026 (FDA's 503A Category 2 action [13]) but NOT approved or lawfully compoundable; separately listed among the 7 peptides under PCAC review on 23 July 2026 (Federal Register meeting notice 2026-07361 [35])
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-18

> **Read this first — the honest evidence state.** BPC-157 is one of the most aggressively marketed "healing peptides," but the evidence underneath the marketing is thin in a specific, documentable way. As of this writing there is **no completed human randomized controlled trial of BPC-157 with published, peer-reviewed results.** The efficacy literature is **overwhelmingly animal/preclinical**, and roughly **85% of the in-vivo efficacy studies come from a single research group** (Predrag Sikiric and collaborators at the University of Zagreb). BPC-157 is **not approved as a drug anywhere** that this review could locate; the FDA placed it in **503A Category 2** ("significant safety concerns") on 29 September 2023, then **removed it from interim Category 2 ~April 2026 (FDA's own 503A Category 2 action [13], corroborated by regulatory-law analysis [14])** — but that removal confers **no FDA approval and no lawful compounding** (the July 2026 PCAC vote on a possible Category-1 move had not occurred), so it still cannot be lawfully compounded or sold as a therapeutic; Australia's TGA scheduled it as prescription-only; and it is **prohibited at all times in sport under WADA category S0** (Non-Approved Substances), with no Therapeutic Use Exemption available. A widely circulated "~15-minute half-life" is **real data but is rat-IV, never human.** Every one of those statements is sourced below, and the single-lab concentration concern is carried as a first-class section (§4), not buried. Operator-specific fields (Walter's status, baseline biomarkers, stack) are intentionally left blank — this is a goal-agnostic library entry.

## TL;DR

BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide marketed for tendon, ligament, muscle, gut, and "systemic" healing. The mechanistic story (pro-angiogenesis via VEGFR2, nitric-oxide modulation, tendon-fibroblast signaling) is biologically coherent and has *some* independent-lab support, but **clinical efficacy in humans is essentially unevidenced**: no completed published human RCT exists, the animal literature is concentrated in one originating lab, and the compound is unapproved and a banned substance in sport (WADA S0). It was placed in FDA 503A Category 2 (2023-09-29) and then removed from interim Category 2 ~April 2026 (FDA's 503A Category 2 action [13]), but that removal confers **no FDA approval and no lawful compounding** — the net status remains unapproved + WADA-prohibited. It is best understood as an **experimental compound with a marketing footprint far larger than its evidence base.**

## 1. Identity & Molecular Mechanism

### 1.1 What BPC-157 actually is

BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide (a "pentadecapeptide") with the sequence **Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val** (GEPPPGKPADDAGLV) and a molecular weight of approximately **1419.55 Da** [1, mechanism_review][29, animal]. The abbreviation "BPC" stands for "Body Protection (Protective) Compound," and the peptide is conventionally described as having been "isolated from human gastric juice" [1, mechanism_review][6, mechanism_review].

The marketing phrasing "derived from a protein found in gastric juice" needs a precise correction, because it is the single most common source of confusion about this compound. The honest state is: **the 15-residue sequence does not occur as such in nature.** It is a designed/synthesized fragment — the portion claimed by its originators to be "essential and fully responsible for biological activities" of a larger parent BPC material associated with gastric juice [1, mechanism_review]. A mechanistic review describes BPC-157 as a "15 amino acid peptide BPC fragment" of a larger gastric protein [6, mechanism_review][20, animal], and an independent review states it shows "no sequence homology with known intestinal peptides" [1, mechanism_review]. An independent clinical review classifies it plainly as a "synthetic pentadecapeptide originally isolated from human gastric juice," originally reported by Sikiric in 1993 [2, mechanism_review]. So the accurate framing is: **a synthetic peptide whose sequence is asserted to correspond to an active fragment of a gastric-juice protein — not a peptide that circulates naturally in that form.** When a vendor or practitioner says BPC-157 is "a natural body-protective compound," that is a marketing simplification of this designed-fragment reality.

### 1.2 Physicochemical and stability claims

BPC-157 is repeatedly characterized as unusually stable in human gastric juice — a property that underwrites the entire "you can take it orally" marketing claim. The originating (Sikiric/Zagreb) literature states it is "essentially stable and native in human gastric juice" and "resistant to degradation in human gastric juice [for] more than 24 hours" [6, mechanism_review][34, mechanism_review]. A structural rationale is offered by an independent group: the N-terminal glycine "can serve as a stabilizer that can regulate the compound's protease degradation," and the multiple proline residues "strongly prevent … non-specific proteolysis"; the peptide is described as "stable in water and gastric juice" [1, mechanism_review]. A formal ADME study likewise states BPC-157 "is resistant to hydrolysis, enzyme digestion, and even gastric juice" [29, animal].

Two cautions belong with these claims. First, **the specific ">24 hours in gastric juice" figure is the originating lab's assertion, repeated in their own reviews, and was not independently re-measured** in any source located here [34, mechanism_review]. It is qualitatively supported (multiple groups agree the peptide is unusually stable) but the precise number is author-sourced. Second, **a "gastric-juice stability" characterization is not the same as a measured plasma half-life or a measured oral bioavailability.** Stability in a test tube of gastric fluid says nothing definitive about how much intact peptide reaches systemic circulation after an oral dose in a human — and as §5 documents, **no human oral bioavailability has ever been measured.** The leap from "stable in gastric juice" to "therefore orally bioavailable and effective in humans" is a marketing extrapolation, not a demonstrated fact.

### 1.3 Receptor pharmacology — the honest state

**There is no well-characterized specific receptor for BPC-157.** The independent reviews surveyed do not identify a dedicated receptor or binding target; the proposed mechanisms are described as multi-pathway (VEGFR2, nitric oxide, Src–caveolin-1, ERK1/2) rather than acting via a defined "BPC-157 receptor" [1, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review]. The originating group frames the absence of a required carrier or co-agent as a feature — "BPC 157 always given alone, acts alone" and needs no special delivery system [6, mechanism_review] — but this is a pharmacological talking point, not evidence of a characterized receptor. For library purposes the accurate statement is: **receptor target unknown / not established.** This matters because a compound with no identified receptor and effects spread across many unrelated organ systems is exactly the profile that should raise the question of how much of the claimed "pleiotropy" is real biology versus a single lab reporting wide-ranging endpoints.

### 1.4 Proposed mechanisms — demonstrated (in vitro / animal) vs extrapolated

The mechanisms below are demonstrated **only in cell and animal models.** No completed human clinical trial supports any mechanistic claim, and BPC-157 is an unapproved drug (see §3) [7, regulatory].

**VEGFR2 / angiogenesis (animal + in vitro).** In a rat hind-limb ischemia model plus human endothelial cell culture, BPC-157 accelerated blood-flow recovery, increased vessel density and vascular VEGFR2 expression, up-regulated VEGFR2 (mRNA and protein) but not VEGF-A, promoted VEGFR2 internalization (blocked by the endocytosis inhibitor dynasore), and time-dependently activated the **VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS** pathway [3, animal]. Independent reviews summarize the same VEGFR2-mediated proangiogenic mechanism [1, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review]. This is the most important mechanism for BPC-157 because (a) it is the proposed engine behind the broad "healing/regeneration" claims, (b) it has independent-lab in-vivo support (the work above is from the Chang Gung group in Taiwan, not Zagreb), and (c) it is simultaneously the basis for the chief safety worry — pro-angiogenesis and cancer (see §6.2).

**Nitric-oxide system / Src–caveolin-1–eNOS (animal + in vitro).** In isolated male Sprague-Dawley rat aorta (plus HUVEC and rat vascular smooth-muscle cells), BPC-157 produced endothelium-dependent, NO-mediated vasorelaxation (markedly attenuated in endothelium-denuded rings and by L-NAME or hemoglobin) and reduced eNOS/caveolin-1 binding (to ~50% of control), activating the **Src–Cav-1–eNOS** cascade [4, animal]. The originating group has long asserted that BPC-157's effects "always" involve interaction with the NO system [6, mechanism_review]. The NO link is also the basis for several theoretical drug interactions (§6.4).

**FAK–paxillin pathway / tendon-fibroblast effects (in vitro).** In cultured tendon fibroblasts from rat Achilles tendon, BPC-157 dose-dependently increased phosphorylation of FAK and paxillin (without changing total protein), accelerated tendon-explant outgrowth and fibroblast migration/spreading, and improved cell survival under H₂O₂ oxidative stress; cell proliferation per se was not directly affected [5, animal]. This is the cellular correlate of the tendon-healing claims and, notably, comes from the independent Taiwanese group.

**Growth-factor / growth-hormone-receptor axis (in vitro).** In cultured rat Achilles tendon fibroblasts, BPC-157 at 0.5 μg/mL induced growth hormone receptor (GHR) expression time-dependently (days 1–3), with up to ~sevenfold increase, offering a proposed route by which it could potentiate growth-hormone/IGF tendon signaling [8, in_vitro].

**Dopamine / serotonin system interactions (animal).** In rat models, the originating group reports BPC-157 counteracts haloperidol- and L-NAME-induced catalepsy, counteracts dopaminergic disturbances (receptor blockade, supersensitivity, nigrostriatal damage, vesicle depletion), and is active against serotonin syndrome with region-specific serotonin release [9, animal][6, mechanism_review]. These are mechanistic/behavioral animal findings, not receptor-binding data, and they originate from a single lab (see §4) — they should be read with single-lab caution.

**Marketed / extrapolated beyond the above.** Broader "systemic regeneration," "gut–brain axis repair," human tendon/ligament cure claims, and oral-dosing efficacy in humans are **extrapolations** from the animal/in-vitro data above and from preclinical oral-stability observations. They are **not established in humans** and should be presented as marketed/hypothesized, not demonstrated [2, mechanism_review][7, regulatory].

## 2. Human Clinical Evidence

This is the section that most directly contradicts the marketing. **There is no admissible human efficacy evidence for BPC-157.**

### 2.1 The complete inventory of human trials

A search of ClinicalTrials.gov for the intervention "BPC-157" returns **exactly two records, and neither has posted results** [10, rct][11, rct]. There is **no completed human randomized controlled trial of BPC-157 with published, peer-reviewed results** that this search located. That is the headline, and it bears repeating because it is so at odds with how the compound is sold.

**(1) NCT02637284 — "PCO-02 Safety and Pharmacokinetics Trial."** A Phase 1, randomized, placebo-controlled pilot in **42** healthy volunteers, sponsored by PharmaCotherapia d.o.o., testing an oral BPC-157 formulation (labeled Bepecin / PCO-02) versus placebo (PCO-03) for safety and pharmacokinetics. Listed start October 2015, estimated completion March 2016. Its overall status on the registry is **"UNKNOWN"** (verbatim API value) and **hasResults = No** [10, rct]. Independent reporting indicates results were briefly submitted to ClinicalTrials.gov (around May 2016) and then **withdrawn before quality-control / outside review**, so no vetted results ever entered the record; a 2025 review by the originating group asserts the trial showed safety but does so **without sharing the underlying data or a citation** [12, mechanism_review]. The "42" enrollment figure is grounded in the registry record [10, rct]; no efficacy outcome can be grounded because none was published.

**(2) NCT07437547 — "BPC 157 for Acute Hamstring Muscle Strain Repair."** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled **Phase 2** trial, sponsor Hudson Biotech, of subcutaneous pentadecapeptide BPC-157 once daily for 14 days versus matching placebo in MRI-confirmed acute Grade II hamstring strain. Estimated enrollment **120**; registry start 2 February 2026; estimated primary completion 14 February 2027. Overall status is **"RECRUITING"** with **hasResults = No** [11, rct]. This trial is **registered and ongoing — not completed, with no results** — and therefore provides **no efficacy or safety evidence yet.** It is, however, the first properly registered, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 of BPC-157 to actually start, and is worth tracking; results are not due before 2027.

**Older PLIVA / ulcerative-colitis-era work.** Reporting describes clinical work in the 2000s (BPC-157 enemas for ulcerative colitis, associated with the PLIVA company and the Sikiric group, under codes such as PL-14736 / PLD-116), but the resulting papers **"do not appear to be indexed in major global databases like PubMed"** and could not be located or verified here [12, mechanism_review]. Because they cannot be retrieved and adjudicated, **no claim is grounded on them**; they are noted only as unverifiable. This is itself a finding: the most-cited "human trials" in BPC-157 marketing are not retrievable in the indexed literature.

### 2.2 Provenance of the evidence base

Essentially all BPC-157 efficacy research is preclinical (animal models), and the great majority originates from a single Croatian research group led by Predrag Sikiric; human data are characterized as "very little," involving small numbers of subjects without rigorous designs [12, mechanism_review]. This provenance concern is the subject of §4 (the concentration audit) and is treated as a first-class issue, not a footnote.

### 2.3 Human safety data

There is **no controlled human safety dataset** for BPC-157. The one Phase 1 human PK/safety trial (NCT02637284) never published vetted results [10, rct][12, mechanism_review], and the ongoing Phase 2 trial has no results yet [11, rct]. Regulatory bodies explicitly frame the human safety picture as **unknown** — no established safe dose, no characterized adverse-event profile in a controlled setting [15, regulatory]. The only human exposures on record are a handful of tiny pilot/case reports (combined n ≈ 30, all zero-AE, all far too small to estimate a rate; see §6.6). **No human adverse-event rate can be admissibly derived** from current data, and none is asserted here.

## 3. Regulatory Status

### 3.1 United States — FDA (compounding)

On **29 September 2023**, FDA placed BPC-157 into **Category 2** of the 503A bulk drug substances evaluation — the category for substances that "raise significant safety concerns" and are **not eligible** for the interim policy that protects compounding of Category 1 substances [13, regulatory][14, regulatory]. The FDA's stated concerns, in its own language, are that BPC-157 "may pose risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration and may have complexities with regard to peptide-related impurities and active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) characterization," and that the FDA "identified no, or only limited, safety-related information for the proposed routes of administration" [13, regulatory]. Practically, Category-2 placement means BPC-157 **cannot be lawfully compounded under 503A** while in that category.

**The 2026 removal from Category 2.** As of mid-2026 the picture has changed: BPC-157 was **removed from interim 503A Category 2 ~April 2026**, as part of a parallel FDA 503A interim-bulks action removing **twelve** peptides from Category 2 (the canonical removed-12: BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, DSIP/Emideltide, Semax, Epitalon, GHK-Cu, Melanotan-II, Dihexa, PEG-MGF, and LL-37). This removal is reflected on **FDA's own 503A Category 2 page** [13, regulatory] and corroborated by independent regulatory-law analysis [14, regulatory]. **Separately**, a **Federal Register notice (document 2026-07361, published 2026-04-16; Docket FDA-2025-N-6895)** [35, regulatory] establishes a PCAC public docket and announces a **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) meeting on 23–24 July 2026**; on its face that notice enumerates only the **seven** peptides scheduled for review — BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, and MOTS-c on July 23, and Emideltide/DSIP, Semax, and Epitalon on July 24 — so BPC-157 appears in it as one of the seven *under review*, not as a "removed-from-Category-2" tally. (Note the precise mechanism: 2026-07361 is the *meeting / public-docket-establishment* instrument and does not itself print the twelve-peptide removal; the Category-2 removal is the parallel FDA bulks-list action documented on FDA's Category 2 page [13]. The July PCAC vote is the next step on a possible Category-1 move.)

**Crucially, removal from Category 2 does NOT confer FDA approval or lawful compounding.** It changes the legal channel, not the evidence or the net status. As of this writing BPC-157 is still **not an FDA-approved drug for any indication**, is **not** established as lawfully compoundable under 503A (the PCAC vote on a possible Category-1 move had not occurred), and there is no legal basis to sell it as a drug, food, or dietary supplement [15, regulatory]. The reclassification adds **no new safety data** — the immunogenicity and impurity concerns that drove the original Category-2 placement remain unrebutted by human evidence, and the compound remains WADA-prohibited at all times (S0; §3.4).

### 3.2 Australia — TGA

Following an interim decision (November 2023), the TGA amended the Poisons Standard to place BPC-157 in **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** and add it to **Appendix D**, with the change reported to take effect **1 June 2024**; the action was prompted in part by importation referrals (reported as 48 since 1 July 2022) and reflects the lack of established human safety/efficacy data [16, regulatory]. *(Caveat: the TGA primary pages/PDF repeatedly timed out on fetch during the source-gathering phase; the Schedule-4 / Appendix-D / 1-June-2024 specifics are corroborated across the TGA notice listing and secondary summaries but the full primary decision text could not be rendered.)*

### 3.3 Europe — EMA

No EMA marketing authorization for BPC-157 was located; BPC-157 is **not an EMA-approved medicine.** No specific EMA decision document was retrieved, so no EMA action is asserted beyond the absence of authorization.

### 3.4 Sport — WADA / USADA

BPC-157 is **prohibited at all times (in and out of competition)** under the WADA Prohibited List **category S0 (Non-Approved / Unapproved Substances)**, because it is not approved by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use; it is **not eligible for a Therapeutic Use Exemption** [15, regulatory]. USADA states plainly that "because BPC-157 has not been extensively studied in humans, no one knows if there is a safe dose, or if there is any way to use this compound safely," and that its use "is not supported by medical literature or by any medical associations" [15, regulatory]. For any drug-tested athlete, BPC-157 is a straightforward anti-doping violation.

### 3.5 What "503A Category 2" actually means in practice

Because the FDA Category-2 status is the most consequential and most misunderstood regulatory fact about BPC-157, it is worth unpacking what it does and does not mean. Section 503A of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act governs traditional pharmacy compounding — the practice of a pharmacist preparing a customized medication for an individual patient. For a bulk drug substance (the raw active ingredient) to be lawfully used in 503A compounding when it is not a component of an FDA-approved drug and lacks a USP/NF monograph, it generally must appear on FDA's positive "503A bulks list," a list FDA builds by evaluating nominated substances and sorting them into categories. **Category 1** substances may be used under FDA's interim enforcement policy while evaluation continues; **Category 2** is the explicit "significant safety risk" bucket and is **excluded** from that interim policy. BPC-157's placement in Category 2 on 29 September 2023 meant, in plain terms, that **a compounding pharmacy could not lawfully compound BPC-157 for a patient** under 503A, and that FDA had affirmatively judged the safety questions serious enough to keep it off the permissive list [13, regulatory][14, regulatory]. The April 2026 HHS-directed removal from Category 2 (§3.1) changes this status, but **removal from Category 2 is not the same as placement on the permissive Category-1 list, and confers no FDA drug approval** — so BPC-157 still has no established lawful compounding channel pending the July 2026 PCAC vote.

This has two practical implications that the marketing elides. First, the "research peptide" and "for research use only" framing under which BPC-157 is widely sold is a way of routing around the fact that it is **neither an approved drug nor a compoundable substance nor a dietary ingredient** — it has no lawful consumer channel as a therapeutic. Second, the very basis for the Category-2 designation (immunogenicity for parenteral routes, peptide-related impurities, incomplete API characterization; §6.3) is a statement about the *uncertainty and risk* of the manufactured product, which directly implicates the gray-market supply most users actually obtain. The regulatory status is thus not a bureaucratic footnote — it is a substantive, safety-grounded judgment that bears on real-world risk.

### 3.6 Net regulatory picture

BPC-157 is **unapproved as a drug anywhere located**; in the U.S. compounding system it was **removed from interim 503A Category 2 ~April 2026 (FDA's 503A Category 2 action [13], corroborated by [14])** but **not** approved or placed on the permissive Category-1 list — so it has no established lawful compounding channel pending the **23–24 July 2026 PCAC** vote (scheduled by Federal Register meeting notice 2026-07361 [35], which lists BPC-157 among the seven peptides under review); it is a **prescription-only medicine** in Australia, has **no EMA authorization**, and is **banned at all times in sport** (WADA S0, no TUE). There is no jurisdiction located in this review in which it is a legal dietary supplement or an approved therapeutic. The trajectory to watch is the July 2026 PCAC vote — but as §3.1 notes, the Category-2 removal changes the legal channel, not the underlying evidence: it adds no new human safety or efficacy data, and the net status remains unapproved + WADA-prohibited (S0).

## 4. Concentration Audit — The Single-Lab Problem (FIRST-CLASS CAVEAT)

This section is deliberately placed before the detailed efficacy data, not after it, because **the credibility of nearly every animal efficacy claim in §5 depends on understanding who produced it.** This is the central methodological caveat for BPC-157 and the whole reason the evidence should be read skeptically despite its apparent breadth.

### 4.1 The headline number

Across the animal/preclinical efficacy literature compiled here (14 distinct primary studies spanning tendon, ligament, muscle, bone, GI, vascular, and neural models), **distinct labs/groups = 2 — and exactly two: the originating Sikiric/Zagreb lab plus a single corroborating lab.** Of the **13 in-vivo efficacy primaries** (one of the 14 is in-vitro only), **11 originate from the Sikiric / University of Zagreb group — approximately 85%** (11/13). The remaining two in-vivo studies come from **one** Taiwanese group (Chang Gung University / Chang Gung Memorial Hospital), and — as the bibliography shows — refs [3], [5], and [8] (the entire non-Zagreb base, including the in-vitro paper) **all share author J.S. Pang.** So "2 distinct labs" means **originating lab + exactly one corroborating lab**, not broad multi-lab replication: no *third* independent group has reproduced any in-vivo BPC-157 finding. Across all 14 distinct primaries (including the in-vitro study), the Zagreb share is 11/14 ≈ 79%; the headline **11/13 ≈ 85%** uses the in-vivo-efficacy denominator because the single in-vitro paper is mechanism-only, not an efficacy study.

### 4.2 What is Sikiric-only (NOT independently replicated)

By domain, the Zagreb group is the **sole source** for:

- **Bone:** segmental bone-defect healing in rabbits [21].
- **All gastrointestinal efficacy:** gastric/duodenal ulcer [22], ileoileal anastomosis [23], colocutaneous fistula [24], esophagitis / sphincter failure [25].
- **Neurological:** spinal-cord-injury functional recovery [27]; the dopamine/serotonin CNS findings [9].
- **Major-vessel-occlusion "hit-and-run" collateral recruitment** [26] — the dramatic "reroutes blood around an occluded artery" claim that features heavily in marketing.
- **The specific in-vivo biomechanical magnitudes** for Achilles-tendon-to-bone and MCL ligament healing [17][18][19].

**Every one of these claims rests on the originating group alone.** None has been replicated by an independent lab in vivo.

### 4.3 What HAS independent-lab support

Only **two themes** have any independent in-vivo corroboration, and both come from the **same single corroborating lab** — the Chang Gung (Taiwan) group, with **J.S. Pang on every one** of refs [3], [5], [8]. This is "one additional lab," not multi-lab replication:

- **Tendon-cell repair:** explant outgrowth, fibroblast survival under oxidative stress, and migration via FAK–paxillin, with a confirmatory in-vivo transected-Achilles arm [5]; plus the GH-receptor mechanism [8].
- **Pro-angiogenesis:** hindlimb-ischemia blood-flow recovery and the VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS pathway [3]. This is the strongest cross-lab corroboration of BPC-157's core mechanistic claim.

But note two important limits. First, **this independent support is mechanistic/directional, not a replication of the specific functional or biomechanical magnitudes** reported by Zagreb. The Taiwanese work confirms *that* BPC-157 promotes tendon-cell repair and angiogenesis in a direction consistent with the Zagreb narrative — it does not confirm the dramatic effect sizes or the breadth of organ-system claims. Second, **the corroboration is a single second lab (Pang/Chang Gung), not several** — so the honest description of the whole corpus is "originating lab + exactly one corroborating lab," and **no third group has reproduced any in-vivo finding.**

### 4.4 Why this matters

A literature in which ~85% of in-vivo efficacy comes from one group, with recurring senior authors (Sikiric, Seiwerth) on nearly every Zagreb paper — and whose *entire* remaining corroboration is a **single** second lab (Pang/Chang Gung), with no third group reproducing any in-vivo result — is **structurally fragile.** It is not evidence of fraud, but it is the textbook setup for the well-documented phenomenon where a compound looks broadly effective in the originating lab's hands and that breadth never fully reproduces elsewhere. The honest framing for any reader: **BPC-157's preclinical efficacy is real in the literature but heavily concentrated; treat the GI, bone, neuro, and collateral-vessel claims as single-source until independently replicated, and treat even the (better-supported) tendon and angiogenesis claims as preclinical-only with no human efficacy demonstration behind them.**

## 4b. Why this lands at evidence_tier C (grading rationale)

The library's evidence tiers run S (multiple human RCTs / meta-analyses) → A (a single well-designed human RCT or strong cohort) → B (mixed human, mostly observational) → C (animal/preclinical only, or weak human data) → D (anecdotal, n=few, speculative). BPC-157 maps cleanly to **C**, and it is worth being explicit about why it is not higher or lower:

- **Not S, A, or B:** these tiers all require human efficacy data of some quality. BPC-157 has **none** — no completed human RCT with published results, no cohort, no observational efficacy dataset. The two registry entries are a withdrawn-results Phase 1 and a recruiting Phase 2 (§2.1). The handful of human exposures are tiny zero-AE safety pilots (§6.6), which are safety anecdotes, not efficacy evidence. So every human-evidence tier is ruled out on the simple ground that the human efficacy denominator is zero.
- **Not D:** tier D is for the genuinely anecdotal or speculative. BPC-157 has a substantial, internally coherent, published *animal* efficacy literature with reproducible-looking effects across many models, plus in-vivo confirmation of its core angiogenesis/tendon mechanisms from a single independent lab (Pang/Chang Gung). That is materially stronger than "speculative" — it is real preclinical science. It just isn't human science.
- **Therefore C:** "animal/preclinical only" is the precise description. The single-lab concentration (§4) is a *within-tier* weakness — it makes this a comparatively *fragile* C rather than a robust one — but it does not change the tier, because tier is set by the species and study type of the strongest efficacy evidence, and that evidence is animal.

The risk tier is set separately to **experimental**, reflecting the unapproved status, the absent controlled human safety dataset, the FDA-flagged immunogenicity/impurity concerns, and the unexcluded oncologic mechanism (§6). A compound can be a defensible C on efficacy-evidence type while still being high-risk on safety/regulatory grounds; BPC-157 is exactly that combination.

## 5. Animal & Preclinical Efficacy by Domain

With §4 as the lens, here is the actual efficacy literature. Every entry carries species and (where the source reports it) sample size; routes and doses are reported as stated; no route extrapolation is performed.

### 5.1 Tendon / ligament / Achilles

- Transected rat Achilles tendon detached from the calcaneus heals faster functionally and biomechanically with BPC-157, which also opposes corticosteroid-induced aggravation of healing (Wistar rat; doses 10 µg, 10 ng, 10 pg/kg IP) [17, animal].
- **Independent of the originating group:** BPC-157 accelerated outgrowth of tendon explants, increased tendocyte survival under H₂O₂ stress, and dose-dependently increased fibroblast migration via the FAK–paxillin pathway, with a confirmatory in-vivo transected-Achilles arm (Sprague-Dawley rat) [5, animal] — one of the few in-vivo musculoskeletal results from a lab outside Zagreb.
- Early functional recovery of the Achilles tendon-to-bone unit after transection is improved by BPC-157 through an anti-inflammatory effect plus increased new-vessel formation, whereas methylprednisolone suppressed inflammation but failed to support early recovery (**n = 72** Wistar rats; 10 µg/kg IP) [18, animal].
- Medial collateral ligament transection healing is improved across functional, biomechanical, macroscopic, and histological endpoints over a 90-day course, with efficacy by IP, per-oral (drinking water), and topical routes (Wistar rat; 10 µg or 10 ng/kg IP, 1.0 µg topical, 0.16 µg/mL drinking water) [19, animal].
- Mechanistic in-vitro support from the independent Taiwanese group: BPC-157 dose- and time-dependently up-regulates the growth-hormone receptor in rat Achilles tendon fibroblasts, potentiating GH-driven proliferation [8, in_vitro].

### 5.2 Muscle

- Gastrocnemius crush injury (delivered force 0.727 Ns/cm²) heals better with BPC-157 given IP or as a local cream — less hematoma/edema, no post-injury leg contracture, improved microscopy and function, and normalized serum enzyme markers (CK, LDH, AST, ALT) (Wistar rat) [20, animal].

### 5.3 Bone

- Segmental bone defect in **rabbits**: intramuscular BPC-157 (10 µg/kg) produced callus and bony union comparable to bone-marrow grafting and superior to controls, in a head-to-head against autologous cortical bone and bone-marrow implantation [21, animal]. This is one of the few **non-rodent** in-vivo efficacy models in the BPC-157 corpus — but it is Zagreb-only and unreplicated.

### 5.4 Gastrointestinal (entirely Zagreb-sourced)

- **Foundational gastric/duodenal ulcer work:** in three rat ulcer models — 48-h restraint stress, subcutaneous cysteamine (duodenal), and intragastric 96% ethanol — BPC-157 (IP and intragastric) was consistently protective across all three, where several reference standards were not [22, animal]. This 1994 paper is the cited origin of the "stable gastric pentadecapeptide" anti-ulcer claim.
- **Intestinal anastomosis:** ileoileal anastomosis healing is improved on all measured wound-healing parameters by the two higher IP doses (10 µg/kg, 10 ng/kg) (Wistar rat) [23, animal].
- **Fistula:** persistent colocutaneous fistula (5 mm colonic + 5 mm skin defect) closes faster with BPC-157 given IP or per-oral, with the effect tied to the nitric-oxide system (10 µg/kg, 10 ng/kg) [24, animal].
- **Esophagitis / sphincter failure:** BPC-157 (10 µg/kg IP daily, or 0.16 µg/mL in drinking water) recovers esophageal mucosa and restores both lower-esophageal and pyloric sphincter pressures in a chronic sphincter-failure model; ranitidine was ineffective in the same protocol [25, animal].

Note: the entire GI efficacy story — the one most aggressively marketed for "gut healing" and "leaky gut" — rests on this single group (see §4.2).

### 5.5 Vascular / "hit-and-run" angiogenesis

- **Major-vessel occlusion:** permanent superior-mesenteric-artery ligation in rats — BPC-157 (10 µg/kg or 10 ng/kg IP) rapidly activates collateral pathways (e.g., inferior anterior pancreaticoduodenal and inferior mesenteric arteries) that bypass the occlusion, reversing multiple-organ dysfunction including portal/caval hypertension, brain swelling, and thrombosis (Wistar rat, **n = 6/group/interval**) [26, animal]. This is the in-vivo basis for the originators' "hit-and-run" collateral-recruitment framing — and it is Zagreb-only.
- **Hindlimb ischemia (independent Taiwanese group):** BPC-157 accelerated laser-Doppler blood-flow recovery and increased vessel density in ischemic rat hindlimb muscle, with up-regulation and internalization of VEGFR2 and activation of the VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS pathway (confirmed in human endothelial cells in vitro) [3, animal] — the strongest independent in-vivo replication of the pro-angiogenic claim.

### 5.6 Neuroprotection

- **Spinal cord injury:** after laminectomy (L2–L3) and 60-s compression of the dural sac, a single IP dose (200 or 2 µg/kg) given 10 min post-injury produced clinical improvement, better tail motor function, absence of autotomy, and resolved spasticity by day 15 (Wistar rat, **≥6/group**) [27, animal].

### 5.7 Summary of the efficacy state

The preclinical signal is genuinely broad and internally consistent — BPC-157 "works" across many injury models in the literature. But that breadth is exactly what §4 should make you discount: it is overwhelmingly one group's output, the doses are micro-to-nanogram/kg in rodents (not obviously translatable to human practitioner doses; see §5/§7 route flags), and **none of it has been confirmed in a completed human trial.** Tendon repair and angiogenesis are the two claims with any independent legs; everything else is single-source.

### 5.8 A note on dose ranges, routes, and the internal coherence of the rodent data

One feature of the Zagreb efficacy corpus deserves explicit attention because it cuts both ways on credibility. Across nearly every model — tendon [17], ligament [19], anastomosis [23], fistula [24], ulcer [22], esophagitis [25], occlusion [26], spinal cord [27] — the doses cluster on a recurring **10 µg/kg and 10 ng/kg (and sometimes 10 pg/kg) IP** ladder, with the peptide frequently reported as effective across several orders of magnitude of dose and via multiple routes (IP, per-oral in drinking water, topical). On one reading this is a strength: a flat, wide dose-response that tolerates oral and topical delivery is exactly what you would want from a robust cytoprotective agent, and it is internally consistent across the group's many papers. On a more skeptical reading, an agent that appears similarly effective at nanogram and microgram doses, across unrelated organ systems, by nearly any route, and almost always in one lab's hands, is precisely the profile that should prompt caution about how much is a true pharmacological dose-response versus a recurring experimental signature. The honest synthesis does not adjudicate between these readings — it flags that **the very uniformity that makes the Zagreb data look impressive is also what makes independent replication so important and so conspicuously absent** for most domains.

A second coherence point: the rodent efficacy doses (µg/kg, IP) and the rodent PK doses (µg/kg, IV/IM; §7) are in the same micro-dose neighborhood, which is internally consistent — but both are far below, and via different routes than, the milligram-range oral and subcutaneous doses used in the two human trials and the practitioner-convention space. There is no validated bridge from "10 µg/kg IP heals a rat anastomosis" to "250–500 µg/day SC does anything in a human." That bridge is exactly what a completed human trial would have to build, and none exists.

### 5.9 What the preclinical literature does NOT show

It is as important to state the negative space as the positive findings, because marketing tends to fill the negative space with implication:

- **No human efficacy** for any indication (this is the §2 finding, restated here because it is the single most load-bearing absence).
- **No demonstrated oral efficacy in humans** — gastric-juice stability (§1.2, §7.3) is a test-tube/animal property, not a demonstrated human clinical effect after oral dosing.
- **No replicated, independent confirmation** of the GI, bone, neurological, or collateral-vessel ("hit-and-run") claims (§4.2).
- **No head-to-head human comparison** against standard-of-care for any of the conditions it is marketed to treat (tendinopathy, IBD, post-surgical healing, TBI).
- **No demonstrated disease-modifying effect** as opposed to model-specific acute healing acceleration in rodents.

A reader who internalizes both the §5 positives and these §5.9 negatives has the accurate picture: a broad, coherent, but concentrated and unreplicated *animal* efficacy literature, with a human efficacy record that is, as of mid-2026, blank.

## 6. Safety, Adverse Events & Contraindications

> **Evidence-floor statement.** Human safety data for BPC-157 is essentially absent. There is no completed, published RCT; the only human exposure on record is a handful of small pilot/case studies (combined n ≈ 30 — a *secondary-review aggregate* per [2], of which only the n=2 IV pilot [33b] is individually citable; see §6.6) plus the one Phase 1 trial (NCT02637284, 42 volunteers) whose results were never submitted (submission cancelled in 2016) [12, mechanism_review]. The "safety" picture therefore rests on (a) animal toxicology, (b) a theoretical mechanism (the pro-angiogenic/cancer concern), and (c) qualitative anecdotal/clinic patterns. **No numerical human adverse-event rate can be admissibly derived.** Vendor labels and anecdote aggregates are not used anywhere below to ground a number or dose.

### 6.1 Animal toxicology — no lethal or toxic dose identified

Across the published preclinical record, **no minimum toxic dose and no lethal dose (LD50) have been identified** for BPC-157 [28, animal][2, mechanism_review].

- **Single-dose, rat (Sprague-Dawley):** a single intramuscular dose of **20 mg/kg** produced no deaths and no obvious abnormalities in body weight, food intake, or behavior [2, mechanism_review][28, animal].
- **Single-dose, dog (Beagle):** **10 mg/kg i.m.** produced no distinct adverse effects [2, mechanism_review].
- **Repeated-dose, 28-day:** doses up to **4 mg/kg/day (rat)** and **2 mg/kg/day (dog)** produced no apparent changes vs saline controls; the one signal noted was a **decrease in creatinine at 2 mg/kg in dogs**, not at lower doses [28, animal][2, mechanism_review].
- **Genotoxicity:** the preclinical battery reported no genetic (genotoxic) effect [28, animal].
- **Embryo-fetal / reproductive:** reported as showing no embryo-fetal toxicity [28, animal].
- **Local tolerance:** irritation characterized as mild [28, animal].

The evaluation spanned mice, rats, rabbits, and dogs [28, animal]. The widely repeated **"LD50 ≈ 2 g/kg" figure circulates in secondary/practitioner sources but is not cleanly attributable to a primary toxicology endpoint** and is therefore omitted as a sourced number; the defensible statement is that a toxic/lethal dose was *not reached* at the doses tested (up to 20 mg/kg single i.m.) [28, animal][2, mechanism_review]. Per-group n values were not stated in the accessible text and are not invented.

The animal toxicology is reassuring as far as it goes — but two bounds apply. First, the per-group n values were **not stated** in the accessible text of [28] (and that study's lab-independence is unconfirmed), so a *negative* finding ("no LD50, negative genotox/embryo-fetal") of unknown statistical power is **bounded by study power, not a positive demonstration of safety** — absence of a detected toxic dose at unknown n is weaker than a powered safety demonstration. Second, "no LD50 reached in animals" is not the same as "safe in humans over months or years," and the relevant human-exposure long-term dataset simply does not exist.

### 6.2 The theoretical oncologic concern

The cancer concern is **mechanistic/theoretical, not empirical**, and the limited empirical signals point the *opposite* direction.

**The basis of the worry.** BPC-157 is **pro-angiogenic.** In a rat hindlimb-ischemia model it increased vessel number and vascular VEGFR2; in human vascular endothelial cells it increased VEGFR2 mRNA/protein, promoted VEGFR2 internalization (dynasore-blocked), and activated VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS [3, animal]. Because tumor growth and metastasis depend on neovascularization, a pro-angiogenic, VEGFR2-engaging agent **could in principle support tumor vascularization** — this is the literal mechanistic basis for the contraindication-in-cancer concern [2, mechanism_review].

**The countervailing empirical evidence.**
- *In vitro, human melanoma:* an abstract reported that BPC-157 **inhibited** human melanoma cell growth and VEGF signaling via the MAPK/ERK pathway, reducing the S-phase fraction by ~20% (lower conc.) up to ~55% (higher conc.) and decreasing ERK phosphorylation [31, in_vitro]. **Single-lab caveat:** this reassuring "opposite-direction" tumor signal is itself a **Sikiric/Zagreb-group abstract** (Radeljak, Seiwerth, Sikiric), so it carries the *same* single-lab provenance flagged for the efficacy literature in §4 — the safety-reassuring counter-evidence is not independent of the originating group.
- *In vivo, colon-adenocarcinoma cachexia (C26 model, mice):* BPC-157 improved total body weight; tumor volume was lower but **without statistical significance** between treated and untreated xenograft mice [2, mechanism_review].

**Net.** The pro-angiogenic mechanism is real and is the legitimate theoretical basis for caution; the *direct* tumor data that exist (one in-vitro melanoma abstract, one underpowered C26 xenograft) show inhibition or no significant effect, not promotion. A frequently cited claim that BPC-157 "reduced B16 lung metastases in mice" traces to a **personal communication / unpublished observation** and is omitted. The defensible statement: **tumor promotion in humans is unproven and untested; the angiogenesis mechanism makes active malignancy a precautionary contraindication despite contrary preclinical signals.**

### 6.3 Immunogenicity & peptide-impurity concerns (the FDA Category-2 rationale)

The FDA's Category-2 placement (§3.1) rests on immunogenicity and impurity concerns [13, regulatory]. The general FDA framework (PCAC deliberations) holds that **parenteral routes (SC, IV, IM, intradermal, inhalation, intravitreal) carry greater immunogenic risk than oral**, and that aggregated peptide or peptide on scaffolding can elicit immune responses; product-related impurities are assessed via LC-MS, MS-MS, peptide mapping, and in-vitro MHC-binding / T-cell-activation assays [32, regulatory]. This is the mechanistic content behind the immunogenicity flag — and it is route-dependent, which matters because the most common practitioner route (subcutaneous injection) is in the higher-risk category. These concerns remain **unrebutted by human evidence.**

### 6.4 Drug-interaction theory

No human drug-interaction studies exist. Theoretical interactions derive entirely from the **nitric-oxide (NO) system**, which BPC-157 modulates [2, mechanism_review]:

- **NO-pathway drugs (e.g., aspirin, which inhibits eNOS):** the literature explicitly recommends "deep analysis of [BPC-157's] interaction with potent drugs affecting NO pathways" [2, mechanism_review].
- **Antiplatelets / anticoagulants:** elevated NO is a potent inhibitor of platelet aggregation, prompting caution in bleeding-risk and cardiovascular patients [2, mechanism_review]; practitioner references list bleeding disorders / anticoagulant co-use as relative contraindications [33, practitioner_protocol].
- **CYP450-metabolized drugs:** high NO can inhibit cytochrome P450 and heme insertion, a theoretical basis for altered drug metabolism [2, mechanism_review].

All of the above are **theoretical/mechanistic**, not demonstrated in humans.

### 6.5 Contraindication candidates

| Candidate | Basis | Sourcing tier |
|---|---|---|
| Active malignancy / significant cancer risk | Pro-angiogenic, VEGFR2-engaging mechanism (theoretical tumor-vascularization) | Mechanism [2,3] + practitioner-convention [33] |
| Pregnancy & lactation | No human reproductive safety data; unknown breast-milk presence | Practitioner-convention [33] (animal embryo-fetal was negative [28], but human data absent) |
| Anticoagulant/antiplatelet use, bleeding disorders | NO-mediated platelet inhibition (theory) | Mechanism [2] + practitioner-convention [33] |
| Cardiovascular disease | NO-driven vasodilation / platelet effects (theory) | Mechanism [2] |
| Severe hepatic/renal impairment | Relative; theoretical CYP/heme effects + creatinine signal in dogs | Practitioner-convention [33]; animal signal [28] |

### 6.6 Adverse-event pattern (qualitative only)

In the three human pilot/case studies (combined n ≈ 30; intra-articular knee, intravesical/cystitis, IV), **no adverse events were reported** [2, mechanism_review][33, practitioner_protocol]. **Citation caveat:** the **n ≈ 30 is a secondary-review aggregate** drawn from review [2] (plus practitioner ref [33]); of the three pilots, **only the IV pilot (n = 2) is individually citable** in this bibliography ([33b]) — the intra-articular-knee and intravesical/cystitis pilots are not separately sourced here, so the specific count of ~30 should be read as a soft secondary-review figure, not a directly verified enrollment. The IV pilot (Lee & Burgess, **2 healthy adults**, up to 20 mg IV) reported **no adverse events and no clinically meaningful changes in vital signs, ECG, or cardiac/hepatic/renal/thyroid/metabolic labs** [33b, open_label] (n = 2 — far too small for any rate). The most commonly *described* AE in clinical/practitioner reporting is **mild local injection-site reaction** [33, practitioner_protocol] — qualitative only; no admissible incidence rate exists. Anecdote-aggregate / clinic patterns (fatigue, GI upset, dizziness) circulate but are not cited to ground any number.

### 6.7 Long-term safety

**Long-term human safety of BPC-157 is uncharacterized.** No completed/published RCT exists; the longest human follow-up on record is ~12 months in a small knee-pain series, and there is **no multi-year safety data** [2, mechanism_review]. The reassuring animal toxicology (no LD50 reached, negative genotox/embryo-fetal) and the small zero-AE pilots are encouraging but underpowered; the pro-angiogenic mechanism remains a real theoretical oncologic liability **that has not been excluded in humans.** A cautious-practitioner posture (cancer/pregnancy/bleeding contraindications, malignancy + lab monitoring, conservative cycling) is **convention, not evidence** — there is no validated, human-evidence-based stopping rule for BPC-157.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics, Dose, Route & Formulation

> **Integrity note.** The widely circulated "~15-minute half-life for BPC-157" is **real data but is rat-IV (n=6)**, not human. **No measured human PK parameter (half-life, Cmax, AUC, bioavailability) has ever been published.** Every numeric PK value below is animal, tagged with species + route. No human PK number is asserted.

### 7.1 Human PK: essentially none exists

There is **no published measured human pharmacokinetic profile** for BPC-157. The one human trial designed to produce one — the Phase 1 oral study NCT02637284 — listed Cmax, Tmax, AUC and T½ as *secondary outcomes*, but **no results are posted** and the registry overall status is **"UNKNOWN"** [10, rct]. The trial used **oral tablets, 1 mg per tablet**, single doses of 1/3/6 tablets and a 3-tablets-every-8-h ×14-day arm (9 mg/day), ~42 volunteers [10, rct]. Because no PK results are public, **no human half-life, Cmax, AUC, or oral bioavailability value can be stated.** The active Phase 2 trial (NCT07437547) is an efficacy study, not a PK study, and **does not disclose its subcutaneous dose** [11, rct]. **`human_pk_exists = false`.**

### 7.2 Measured animal PK (single ADME source: rat + dog)

All quantitative figures below come from one formal ADME study in Sprague-Dawley rats and beagle dogs [34b, animal] (MW 1419 Da).

**Half-life (elimination t½) — animal only**
- **Rat, IV, 20 µg/kg, n=6: 15.2 min** — *this is the figure mis-circulated as "BPC-157's half-life"; it is rat-IV.*
- Rat, IM single dose, n=6/dose: ~7.87–29.7 min across 20/100/500 µg/kg.
- Rat, repeated IM 100 µg/kg ×7 d, n=6: 18.5 min.
- **Beagle dog, IV, 6 µg/kg, n=6: 5.27 ± 2.25 min.**
- Beagle dog, IM single dose, n=6/dose: ~20.0–29.3 min.
- Authors' summary across both species/routes: **"less than 30 min."** [34b, animal]

**Absolute bioavailability (IM vs IV) — animal only**
- **Rat IM, n=6/dose: ~14.5–19.4%** (18.82% at 20 µg/kg; 14.49% at 100 µg/kg; 19.35% at 500 µg/kg).
- **Beagle dog IM, n=6/dose: ~45–51%** (45.27% / 47.64% / 50.56% at 6/30/150 µg/kg).
- **No oral bioavailability was measured.** [34b, animal]

**Exposure parameters (rat, IM single dose, n=6/dose)** [34b, animal]

| Dose (IM) | Tmax | Cmax | AUC₀–t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 µg/kg | 3.00 min | 12.3 ng/ml | 75.1 ng·min/ml |
| 100 µg/kg | 3.00 min | 48.9 ng/ml | 289 ng·min/ml |
| 500 µg/kg | 3.00 min | 141 ng/ml | 1930 ng·min/ml |

Rat IV (20 µg/kg, n=6): AUC₀–∞ 400 ng·min/ml; Vss 36.4 ml/kg; CL 50.1 ml/min/kg; MRT 0.727 min. Dog IV (6 µg/kg, n=6): AUC₀–t 76.4 ng·min/ml; Vss 243 ml/kg; CL 90.8 ml/min/kg; MRT 2.49 min. PK was **linear** across tested doses in both species [34b, animal].

**Metabolism, distribution, excretion (rat, n=6)** [34b, animal]
- **Metabolism:** rapid peptidase degradation; intact BPC-157 was the main plasma component at ~3 min, then degraded to small fragments and ultimately amino acids (at 1 h, [³H]proline = 86.65% of plasma radioactivity).
- **Distribution:** highest in kidney; elevated in liver, stomach wall, spleen, thymus; lower than plasma in brain, myocardium, skeletal muscle, fat.
- **Excretion:** mainly urine (~16–18%) and bile (~9% in bile-duct-cannulated rats).

**Route-extrapolation flag.** These are rat/dog **IV and IM** parameters at **µg/kg** doses. They cannot be converted to human oral or human subcutaneous exposure. Any inference from dog IM 45–51% %F to human SC, or from rat IV 15.2 min to human t½, is unsupported.

### 7.3 Oral vs injectable stability

The claim that BPC-157 is "stable / resistant to degradation in human gastric juice for more than 24 hours" originates with the discovering group and is repeated in their reviews [34, mechanism_review]; the ADME paper similarly states it "is resistant to hydrolysis, enzyme digestion, and even gastric juice" [34b, animal]. **Caveat:** this is the originating lab's assertion, not an independent re-measurement; treat the ">24 h" figure as author-sourced, qualitatively supported but not independently replicated. **No quantitative human oral stability/degradation kinetics were located.**

## 9. Bottom Line — What's Actually Supported vs Marketed

**What is marketed:** BPC-157 is sold as a near-universal "healing peptide" — tendon, ligament, muscle, joint, gut ("leaky gut"), brain, and "systemic regeneration" — frequently with the claims that it is "natural" (from gastric juice), orally bioavailable, has a well-understood mechanism, and is safe with a benign side-effect profile.

**What is actually supported:**

1. **No human efficacy evidence exists.** There is no completed, published, peer-reviewed human RCT of BPC-157 for any indication. The entire clinical registry footprint is two studies: one Phase 1 (status "UNKNOWN," results withdrawn before review) and one Phase 2 that is merely recruiting as of 2026 with no results due before 2027. The most-cited "human trials" (PLIVA-era ulcerative colitis) are not retrievable in indexed databases.

2. **The efficacy base is animal/preclinical and single-lab-concentrated.** ~85% of in-vivo efficacy primaries come from one group (Sikiric/Zagreb). Only tendon-cell repair and VEGFR2-mediated angiogenesis have any independent in-vivo support, and that support is from a **single** corroborating lab (Pang/Chang Gung) and is directional/mechanistic, not a replication of the dramatic effect sizes — i.e., originating lab + exactly one corroborating lab, not multi-lab replication. All GI, bone, neuro, and "hit-and-run" collateral-vessel claims rest on the originating lab alone.

3. **The mechanism is plausible but the receptor is unidentified.** Pro-angiogenesis (VEGFR2), NO-system modulation, and tendon-fibroblast signaling are real preclinical findings — but there is no characterized BPC-157 receptor, and the same pro-angiogenic mechanism that drives the "healing" story is also the basis for the (theoretical, unexcluded-in-humans) cancer concern.

4. **"Natural / orally bioavailable" is a marketing simplification.** The sequence does not occur naturally; it is a synthetic fragment. Gastric-juice stability is real but is the originating lab's claim and does not equal demonstrated human oral bioavailability — which has never been measured. The famous "~15-min half-life" is rat-IV.

5. **It is unapproved and regulated as a risk.** Not approved as a drug anywhere. Placed in FDA 503A **Category 2** on 2023-09-29 (significant safety concerns: immunogenicity, impurities, API characterization), then **removed from interim Category 2 ~April 2026 as part of FDA's twelve-peptide 503A Category 2 action (FDA's Category 2 page [13], corroborated by [14])**; separately scheduled for PCAC review on 23–24 July 2026 by Federal Register meeting notice 2026-07361 (Docket FDA-2025-N-6895) [35], which lists BPC-157 among the seven peptides under review — but removal **does not** confer FDA approval or lawful compounding, so the net status is unchanged: unapproved drug, no legal supplement/therapeutic channel, Australia prescription-only, no EMA authorization, and **WADA-prohibited at all times (S0)** with no TUE.

6. **Human safety is unknown, not "proven safe."** Animal toxicology is reassuring (no LD50 reached, negative genotox/embryo-fetal) and the tiny human pilots reported zero AEs — but combined human exposure is ~30 people in underpowered studies, there is no multi-year human data, and the oncologic mechanism has not been excluded in humans.

**Net:** BPC-157 is an **experimental, unapproved compound (evidence_tier C, risk_tier experimental)** whose marketing breadth vastly outstrips its evidence. The preclinical story is real but concentrated and unreplicated for most claims; the human evidence is, as of mid-2026, effectively a blank page. Anyone considering it should treat every efficacy claim as preclinical-only, weigh the unapproved/banned regulatory status and the supply-quality risk, treat active malignancy, pregnancy, and bleeding-risk as contraindications, and recognize that "studies show it heals X" is — for humans — not yet a supportable statement.
