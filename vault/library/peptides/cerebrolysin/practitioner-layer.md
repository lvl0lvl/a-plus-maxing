# Cerebrolysin — Practitioner / Clinical-Use + Grey-Market Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how Cerebrolysin is actually prescribed, accessed, dosed, and monitored in practice* — the approved-in-~50-countries national-registration pathway (labeled IV/IM infusion for stroke, TBI, and vascular dementia), the manufactured-biologic reality (no compounding pathway; no 503A candidate), the grey-market import channel, and the honest clinical picture: a real approved therapy in neurology wards across Eastern Europe and Asia that has failed its pivotal independent RCT, carries a non-fatal SAE signal in the Cochrane synthesis, and whose preclinical AD-mechanism evidence base has been substantially compromised by research misconduct. Source-tagging rule: `regulatory` (Tier 1) grounds regulatory status, labeled posology, and official contraindications. `rct` (Tier 2) and `meta_analysis` (Tier 2) ground safety signals and efficacy characterization. `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7) and `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4) are admissible **only** for dose/route/administration conventions actually used in clinical or grey-market practice — **never** for efficacy or adverse-event-rate claims.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** Cerebrolysin (EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH, Unterach, Austria; production facility EVER Pharma Jena GmbH, Germany) is a **manufactured proprietary biologic** — an enzymatically hydrolyzed porcine brain peptide extract, concentration 215.2 mg/mL, approved under national regulatory procedures in approximately 50 countries (Russia, China, South Korea, Austria, and much of Eastern Europe) for ischemic stroke, TBI, and dementia. **It is NOT FDA-approved and NOT EMA-centrally-authorized.** Individual EU member states (Austria, some Eastern European countries) hold national marketing authorizations; there is no EU-wide centralized EMA approval. In the US, UK, Australia, and Canada, there is no legal human-use pathway. **Three things are simultaneously true:** (1) Cerebrolysin is a real, clinician-prescribed infusion therapy in approved jurisdictions — it is given in neurology wards as labeled posology; (2) independent evidence does not support the efficacy claims manufacturers advance — the pivotal CASTA trial was null on its primary endpoint [1, rct] and the 2023 Cochrane synthesis found no mortality benefit and a statistically significant increase in non-fatal SAEs [2, meta_analysis]; and (3) a grey-market nootropic ecosystem circulates this injectable biologic without any prescription, clinical oversight, cold-chain guarantee, or GMP verification.

## Approved Prescribing: National Registrations (~50 Countries)

### Product and Manufacturer

Cerebrolysin is manufactured exclusively by EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH (Austria) and EVER Pharma Jena GmbH (Germany). It is a finished manufactured biologic — **not a compoundable preparation.** The active fraction consists of enzymatically derived low-molecular-weight peptides (<10 kDa) and free amino acids from porcine brain tissue; approximately 20% neuropeptides and 80% free amino acids by mass. Standard concentration: 215.2 mg/mL solution for injection. [3, regulatory]

**Approved jurisdictions (non-exhaustive):** Russia, China, South Korea, Austria, Romania, Bulgaria, Hungary, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Georgia, Vietnam, and numerous other Eastern European and Central/East Asian countries. Available in some EU member states under national procedures. NOT centrally authorized by EMA; NOT FDA-approved in the US; NOT approved in UK, Australia, Canada. [3, regulatory; 4, regulatory]

### Approved Indications (National SmPC Framework)

Based on the EVER Neuro Pharma product information and the clinical prescribing practice in approving jurisdictions [3, regulatory; 4, regulatory]:

- **Ischemic stroke** (acute and rehabilitation phases)
- **Traumatic brain injury (TBI)** — commotio and contusio cerebri
- **Vascular dementia** (multi-infarct dementia)
- **Alzheimer's disease** (senile dementia of the Alzheimer type; approved in several jurisdictions)

**What the approval is, precisely:** A national registration based on clinical data packages (predominantly manufacturer-sponsored trials and Eastern European RCTs). These approvals are country-level decisions; there is no multinational regulatory body that has centrally evaluated and approved Cerebrolysin. The EMA has not issued a positive CHMP opinion for Cerebrolysin; individual member-state approvals predate EMA centralized procedures or represent Article 10 mutual recognition between specific countries.

**What the approval is not:** The national approval status does NOT mean the independent evidence synthesis endorses clinical benefit. The 2023 Cochrane review — 7 RCTs, 1,773 participants — found moderate-certainty evidence of no mortality benefit and probable increase in non-fatal SAEs [2, meta_analysis]. Practitioners in approving countries prescribe from a licensed formulary; the independent evidence base for that decision is contested.

### Adverse Events (Clinical Trial Data)

The labeled adverse event profile from clinical trial experience in stroke and dementia populations [7, meta_analysis; 8, meta_analysis]:

| Adverse Event | Frequency Category | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Injection site reactions (pain, swelling) | Common | Intramuscular route most affected |
| Headache, dizziness, sensation of warmth | Uncommon | Reported in RCTs; generally mild and transient |
| Tremors, mild fever | Uncommon | Trial data; generally mild |
| Severe allergic reaction / anaphylaxis | Very rare (<1/10,000 per label) | Life-threatening case documented [6, open_label]; emergency management required |
| Non-fatal serious adverse events (class) | Signal in Cochrane (RR 2.39) | Specific event types not uniformly reported across trials; higher signal at labeled 30 mL stroke dose |

**What the Cochrane SAE signal does and does not mean:** The Cochrane finding of RR 2.39 for non-fatal SAEs is moderate-certainty evidence from 3 trials (n=1,335). The specific types of non-fatal SAEs are not uniformly disaggregated across contributing trials. The signal does not mean Cerebrolysin causes a specific identified adverse event with known frequency — it means more participants in the Cerebrolysin arm experienced a non-fatal serious adverse event vs. placebo. At the labeled 30 mL/10-day stroke regimen, the point estimate rises (RR 2.87). The 2021 meta-analysis [7, meta_analysis] did not replicate this signal, creating a genuine unresolved discrepancy that neither source alone resolves.

## Compounding Status / Data-Sheet Gate Finding

**Gate finding: NO compounding data sheet — Cerebrolysin is a finished manufactured biologic.**

Cerebrolysin cannot be and is not compounded by any 503A or 503B pharmacy. The reasons are structural:

1. **It is a complex biologic hydrolysate, not a defined chemical entity.** The active content consists of hundreds of peptide fragments and amino acids derived from enzymatic hydrolysis of porcine brain tissue. There is no defined single API that can be synthesized or compounded. A pharmacy cannot replicate the EVER Pharma product.
2. **It is not a compoundable substance under 503A doctrine.** 503A compounding applies to drug products for patient-specific prescriptions. The "essentially a copy" framework presupposes a defined chemical entity that could be reproduced with a change in route or concentration. A complex multi-component biologic hydrolysate does not fit this framework.
3. **No US 503A or 503B pharmacy is known to offer compounded Cerebrolysin.** Searches of publicly accessible compounding pharmacy databases and product pages locate no US-licensed compounder offering Cerebrolysin. This is the expected outcome given the structural analysis above.
4. **Cerebrolysin has no FDA-approved form for any indication** — so there is also no "essentially a copy" analysis needed. There is no approved US product to copy, and no US-approved manufacturing standard against which a compounder could certify equivalence.

**What is available instead:** The EVER Neuro Pharma SmPC / product information serves as the functional regulatory data sheet for this manufactured biologic [3, regulatory]. The SmPC and national package insert are the primary clinical reference for posology, contraindications, storage, and labeled safety in approving jurisdictions. These are cited as `regulatory/SmPC` throughout this document.

**Grey-market supply:** Several import vendors (RUPharma.com, CosmicNootropic.com, others) sell what they represent as EVER Neuro Pharma product, shipping from Russia, Austria, or Dubai-based distribution. See Grey-Market section. These are tagged `anecdote_aggregate`; they are not licensed US suppliers and are explicitly off-whitelist.

## The Independent-Evidence Reality: What the Trials Actually Show

### The CASTA Trial — Null on Primary Endpoint

**CASTA (Cerebrolysin Acute Stroke Treatment in Asia):** Heiss WD et al. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 14-country Asian trial (n=1,070; 529 Cerebrolysin, 541 placebo). Intervention: 30 mL Cerebrolysin IV daily × 10 days within 72 hours of stroke onset, plus aspirin 100 mg/day. Primary endpoint: combined global directional test (mRS + Barthel Index + NIHSS). **Result: No significant difference between Cerebrolysin and placebo on the primary combined endpoint.** A post-hoc subgroup showing NIHSS improvement at Day 90 in patients with NIHSS >12 at baseline was a hypothesis-generating finding, not a confirmatory result. [1, rct] — PMID 22282884

This is the largest single independent RCT of Cerebrolysin in stroke. Its null result on the pre-specified primary endpoint is the pivotal evidence point.

### The 2023 Cochrane Review — No Benefit, Non-Fatal SAE Signal

**Ziganshina LE, Abakumova T, Nurkhametova D, Ivanchenko K. Cerebrolysin for acute ischaemic stroke.** Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2023 Oct 11. PMID 37818733. DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD007026.pub7. 7 RCTs; 1,773 participants. [2, meta_analysis]

Key findings:
- **All-cause mortality:** RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.65–1.41); moderate-certainty evidence. No demonstrated mortality benefit.
- **Non-fatal serious adverse events:** RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23); 3 trials, n=1,335; moderate-certainty evidence. **Statistically significant increase in non-fatal SAEs in Cerebrolysin group.**
- At the labeled 30 mL/10-day dose: RR 2.87 (95% CI 1.24–6.69) — more pronounced signal.
- **Conclusion:** "Cerebrolysin or Cortexin probably result in little to no difference in all-cause death… [and] increases the number of people with serious, non-fatal unwanted effects."
- Overall evidence certainty: low to very low.

### The Manufacturer-Sponsored Meta-Analyses

**CARS trials (Guekht A et al., 2017):** Meta-analysis of two manufacturer-sponsored RCTs (CARS-1 and CARS-2; combined n=442; intervention 30 mL/day × 3 weeks within 72h of stroke onset). EVER Neuro Pharma employees (Edith Doppler, Stefan Winter) were co-authors. Primary outcome (ARAT day 90): Mann-Whitney effect size 0.62 (p<0.0001) favoring Cerebrolysin; early NIHSS improvement significant. [5, rct] — PMID 28707130. **This is manufacturer-funded; the Cochrane review flags manufacturer sponsorship as a source of bias risk across the Cerebrolysin RCT literature.**

The contrast between the manufacturer-affiliated trials (positive signals on pre-specified endpoints) and the CASTA trial (null on primary endpoint) and the Cochrane synthesis (no mortality benefit, SAE signal) is the central interpretive challenge in the Cerebrolysin literature.

**Industry sponsorship prevalence:** The Cochrane review and independent meta-analyses consistently note that a substantial proportion of Cerebrolysin RCTs are EVER Neuro Pharma-sponsored or involve authors with EVER Pharma financial relationships. An estimated 60–70% of the pivotal clinical RCT evidence base involves manufacturer-sponsorship or co-authorship ties. [2, meta_analysis; 8, meta_analysis]

## Research Integrity: The Two Integrity Axes

### Axis 1: Masliah / NIH Misconduct (Preclinical AD Mechanism Evidence)

**Background:** Eliezer Masliah served as director of the Division of Neuroscience at the US National Institute on Aging (NIA) from 2016 until September 2024. On September 15, 2024, NIH announced findings of research misconduct against Masliah involving "falsification and/or fabrication involving re-use and relabel of figure panels" across his publication record. A September 2024 Science magazine dossier identified approximately 132 papers with apparently manipulated images. [9, regulatory]

**Cerebrolysin-specific scope:** 8 of the papers in the Science dossier concern Cerebrolysin — predominantly preclinical studies in transgenic mouse models investigating Cerebrolysin's mechanism of action in Alzheimer's disease and synucleinopathy. Herbert Mössler, former EVER Pharma general manager, co-authored 57 Cerebrolysin papers and co-founded Neuropore Therapies with Masliah in 2008. [9, regulatory]

**Confirmed Retractions (Cerebrolysin-involved, Rockenstein as lead):**

1. **Rockenstein E, Ubhi K, Mante M, et al. "Neuroprotective effects of Cerebrolysin in triple repeat Tau transgenic model of Pick's disease and fronto-temporal tauopathies." BMC Neurosci. 2015;16:85.** — PMID 26611895. **[RETRACTED March 2025; retraction PMID 40065222]** Reason: Panel images representing tissues from animals undergoing different treatments found to overlap when horizontally flipped; editor no longer has confidence in the scientific integrity of the article. Masliah and Mössler co-authors. Edward Rockenstein (lead author) has since died; remaining authors did not respond to publisher correspondence. [10, animal]

2. **Rockenstein E, Ubhi K, Trejo M, et al. "Cerebrolysin™ efficacy in a transgenic model of tauopathy: role in regulation of mitochondrial structure." BMC Neurosci. 2014;15:90.** — PMID 25047000. **[RETRACTED December 2025; retraction DOI 10.1186/s12868-025-00985-1]** Reason: p-Tau band overlaps, edited actin band background, overlapping panels from different experimental conditions, overlapping p-Drp1 bands. [11, animal]

**What these retractions mean for the Cerebrolysin evidence base:** Both retracted papers contributed to the mechanistic narrative for Cerebrolysin's proposed neuroprotective action in tau pathology and mitochondrial regulation — the biological plausibility story for its use in AD. The clinical RCT evidence (stroke, dementia) is structurally separate from these preclinical papers, but the retractions eliminate two anchor citations for the proposed AD mechanism. The AD mechanism evidence base for Cerebrolysin must now be considered seriously compromised.

### Axis 2: Sharma / High-Volume Industry-Linked Publications

A secondary integrity concern identified in the Cerebrolysin literature involves Hari Shanker Sharma and Aruna Sharma (Sweden), who published 39 Cerebrolysin papers; 25 have been flagged on PubPeer for image concerns, with 5 retractions. An entire Springer-edited volume they authored was retracted for image duplication. Dafin Muresanu published 60 Cerebrolysin papers since 2008 while serving simultaneously as a journal editor. These patterns compound the pre-existing concern about industry sponsorship bias in the Cerebrolysin literature. [9, regulatory]

## Critical Hazards for the Grey-Market / Off-Label / Healthy-Adult Context

### 1. Healthy-Adult Population Mismatch

**ALL efficacy and safety data for Cerebrolysin comes from disease populations** — elderly patients with acute ischemic stroke, TBI, or dementia. The pivotal RCTs enrolled patients with documented acute neurological events; the CASTA trial required acute ischemic stroke within 72 hours [1, rct]. A pilot study of oral Cerebrolysin in healthy elderly individuals exists (an uncontrolled finding of qEEG alpha activity change and self-reported memory improvement) but it was uncontrolled, could reflect placebo effect, and used an oral route (oral bioavailability of Cerebrolysin is not established). **No controlled RCT in healthy adults documents safety or efficacy.** The entire nootropic use case — cognitive enhancement in healthy individuals — extrapolates from disease-population data across a fundamental population boundary, with no controlled data in the target population. [1, rct; 2, meta_analysis]

### 2. The Independent-Null Reality vs. Marketed Claims

Grey-market vendors and nootropic communities market Cerebrolysin as a neurotrophic/neuroregeneration agent with documented cognitive enhancement. The honest independent synthesis [2, meta_analysis] finds:
- No mortality benefit in stroke
- No demonstrated functional independence benefit in the independent synthesis
- A non-fatal SAE signal (RR 2.39) at the labeled stroke dose
- Preclinical mechanism papers partly retracted
- 60–70% industry sponsorship in the clinical evidence base

Buyers are effectively sold "neuroregenerative" claims that the independent evidence does not support. The gap between industry-associated meta-analytic signals and the Cochrane independent synthesis is the key information deficit in grey-market purchasing decisions.

### 3. Injectable Biologic Hazards

| Hazard | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hypersensitivity / anaphylaxis | Documented (Grade III, life-threatening, PMID 39055722 [6, open_label]); the label states "very rare" but the case report emphasizes the reaction can occur at any point in a treatment course, including the first infusion. No prescription, no clinical oversight, no emergency reversal capacity = an unmanaged risk in the grey-market context. |

Grey-market shipments routed through Dubai, Russia, or EU-to-USA routes involving variable temperatures, prolonged transit, and no chain-of-custody documentation do not guarantee temperature compliance. Degraded Cerebrolysin is a biologic with unknown composition. |

### 4. WADA Status

Cerebrolysin is **not explicitly named** on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List. No search of publicly available WADA List summaries identifies Cerebrolysin by name. This is a reasoned inference based on available information — the 2026 List was published with effect January 1, 2026; the substance categories that could theoretically cover Cerebrolysin (S0 — non-approved substances; S2 — peptide hormones and related) were not confirmed to name Cerebrolysin in available summaries. **This is not a clearance for athlete use.** Athletes must consult GlobalDRO.com (search by ingredient) and their national anti-doping organization (NADO) before any use. An unverified import of a non-FDA-approved injectable biologic in a competition context carries regulatory risk independent of the prohibited list status. [12, anecdote_aggregate]

### 5. US / UK / Australia Legal Status

**United States:** Cerebrolysin is not FDA-approved and is not legally marketed for any human indication in the US. FDA personal importation policy allows some case-by-case importation of unapproved drugs for serious/life-threatening conditions with no US equivalent; Cerebrolysin imported for nootropic/cognitive enhancement does not meet this bar (the condition is not serious/life-threatening; the importer is self-treating a wellness goal). Grey-market vendors ship to US addresses, but the import violates FDA regulations and CBP may seize packages. There is no legal US human-use pathway. [13, regulatory]

**United Kingdom:** Not authorized by MHRA. Personal importation for personal medical use of up to 3 months' supply from outside the UK is a grey area under MHRA enforcement discretion, but Cerebrolysin is not a licensed UK product and self-administration without prescriber involvement carries no licensed-product protections.

**Australia:** Not TGA-registered. Similar personal importation constraints as UK/US.

### Stopping Criteria (Label-Derived + Evidence-Based)

- Anaphylaxis or significant hypersensitivity reaction: discontinue permanently; emergency management
- Epileptic seizure development: discontinue immediately; evaluate for resumption only after seizure management
- Severe renal failure develops: discontinue
- No clinical improvement after one full 10–20 day course: reassess indication; independent evidence does not support continuing if no response

### Honest Expectation-Setting

1. **The independent efficacy evidence is weak:** The CASTA null, Cochrane no-mortality-benefit finding, and industry-sponsorship concerns in positive meta-analyses create genuine uncertainty about whether Cerebrolysin provides net clinical benefit beyond standard care in its labeled indications. This uncertainty is distinct from the approval status.
2. **The non-fatal SAE signal is real:** RR 2.39 (moderate-certainty, Cochrane) means more serious adverse events in the treated group, not fewer. The signal is unresolved but not dismissible.
3. **The healthy-adult use case has no evidentiary floor:** There are no controlled safety or efficacy data in healthy adults. Claims that Cerebrolysin is safe for nootropic use in healthy individuals are marketing, not evidence.
4. **The research misconduct is load-bearing:** Two Rockenstein-Masliah-EVER Pharma papers underpinning the AD/tau mechanism are retracted. The preclinical evidence for why Cerebrolysin should work mechanistically in neurodegeneration is substantially compromised.
