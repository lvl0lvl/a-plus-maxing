# Cerebrolysin

## Metadata

- **class:** peptide (porcine-brain-derived peptide hydrolysate; complex biologic mixture; IV/IM only; not FDA-approved; not EMA-centrally-authorized)

## Summary

Cerebrolysin (trade name; former designation FPF-1070) is a parenterally administered, porcine-brain-derived peptide preparation manufactured by **Ever Neuro Pharma GmbH** (formerly Ever Pharma), Unterach, Austria. It is a complex, multi-component biological mixture — not a single defined molecular entity — consisting of approximately **85% free amino acids and 15% low-molecular-weight peptides below 10 kDa** [13, in_vitro; 14, in_vitro]. It is supplied as a sterile injectable for **intravenous (IV) or intramuscular (IM) administration only**; it is not orally bioavailable. No other route is evidence-supported.

**This report documents five load-bearing facts that define the honest status of Cerebrolysin:**

**One: Composition is a defined mixture with an undefined active component.** A comprehensive LC-MS profiling study identified 638 unique peptides from Cerebrolysin samples, dominated by structural proteins — tubulin, actin, myelin basic protein — rather than neurotrophic factor proteins [4, in_vitro]. Critically, **no peptide fragments of BDNF, GDNF, NGF, or CNTF were detected** [4, in_vitro]. A caveat attaches: the Gevaert profiling was conducted on Internet-purchased Cerebrolysin, not manufacturer-certified reference lots — so this characterization describes the marketed preparation as commercially available, but the manufacturer could dispute lot-specificity. The manufacturer's marketing language describing Cerebrolysin as "a mixture of neurotrophic factors" is factually unsupported. The proposed "neurotrophic mimicry" mechanism describes functional analogy — uncharacterized cryptic peptides activating downstream neurotrophic signaling — not direct delivery of growth-factor-derived sequences. Three candidate bioactive peptides have been identified by nanoLC-MS (LLNLQPPPR, LSPSLRLP from myelin basic protein; WPFPR from tubulin beta chain) [5, in_vitro], but their causal roles have not been confirmed. The active pharmacophore is undefined, making dose-response characterization and batch-to-batch reproducibility inherently uncertain.

**Two: The porcine-CNS source carries a theoretical TSE/prion consideration.** Experimental parenteral inoculation of BSE prions has been shown to produce disease in pigs at 69–150 weeks incubation [10, animal]. Cerebrolysin is manufactured from porcine brain cortex, placing it in the highest-risk anatomical compartment for any species. No case of prion transmission from Cerebrolysin has been documented in the clinical literature, and EVER Neuro Pharma manufactures under EU GMP standards with geographically controlled sourcing. The risk is theoretical and regulatory in character — a material difference from small-molecule drugs, and relevant to grey-market supply where GMP controls are absent.

**Three: The clinical evidence base is large but independently synthesized as null or cautious.** The pivotal **CASTA** trial (n=1,070; acute ischemic stroke; 30 mL Cerebrolysin IV × 10 days) — the largest single RCT — showed **no significant difference on its primary endpoint** (global directional test of mRS/BI/NIHSS at 90 days) in the overall population [12, rct]. The independent **2023 Cochrane review** of acute ischemic stroke (7 RCTs, n=1,773; an update of the Cochrane stroke review first published in 2010 [2, meta_analysis]) found no benefit on all-cause death (RR 0.96; 95% CI 0.65–1.41; moderate certainty) and, crucially, a **statistically significant increase in non-fatal serious adverse events** at the standard 30 mL dose (RR 2.39; 95% CI 1.10–5.23; the 30 mL × 10-day regimen: RR 2.87; 95% CI 1.24–6.69) [6, meta_analysis; 15, meta_analysis]. The **2019 Cochrane vascular dementia review** (6 RCTs, n=597) found a positive cognitive signal but graded ALL outcomes **very-low-certainty evidence** with extreme heterogeneity (I²=86%) and noted all funded studies received pharmaceutical industry support [7, meta_analysis]. The death-or-dependency composite — the standard primary endpoint in contemporary stroke trials — was absent from all 7 Cochrane stroke trials, a selective-outcome-reporting concern consistent with the manufacturer-concentration problem. For Alzheimer's disease, manufacturer-associated meta-analyses find a modest global-impression benefit at 4 weeks [19, meta_analysis; 20, meta_analysis]; no independent Cochrane AD-specific review has reached a strong positive verdict. For TBI, the CAPTAIN program (n=185 total) shows small-to-medium effect sizes but is small, unreplicated by independent groups, and authored entirely by manufacturer-affiliated investigators [22, rct; 23, rct; 24, meta_analysis].

**Four: Two integrity axes compromise the evidence base.** The first axis is **sponsor and author concentration**: approximately 60–70% of pivotal clinical evidence is manufacturer-sponsored or authored by investigators with documented financial ties to Ever Neuro Pharma. The Cochrane stroke review notes that three of seven trials received direct manufacturer support and explicitly judges that this "may have affected how those studies were designed, carried out, and reported" [6, meta_analysis]. The same small cluster of academic neurologists (Mureșanu, Heiss, Bajenaru, Guekht, Popescu) with disclosed honoraria, consultancies, and trial PI roles appears across multiple trials and meta-analyses [25, mechanism_review]. Trials are overwhelmingly conducted in Russia, Eastern Europe, and China — the drug's commercial registration geography — with no independent trials from North American, UK, or Scandinavian academic neurology networks. The second axis is **research misconduct**: Eliezer Masliah, a prominent Ever-Pharma-cluster author, received an NIH ORI research-misconduct finding in September 2024 for figure fabrication and falsification across 132 papers, 8 of which concern Cerebrolysin specifically [29, regulatory]. Herbert Moessler, former general manager of EVER Pharma, co-authored 57 Cerebrolysin papers and co-founded a company with Masliah [30, anecdote_aggregate]. Two Rockenstein/Masliah Cerebrolysin AD-model papers were formally retracted in 2025: Rockenstein et al. 2014 (tauopathy/mitochondria model; retracted December 2025; retraction PMID 41382024) [31, animal] and Rockenstein et al. 2015 (Pick's disease Tau model; retracted March 2025; retraction PMID 40065222) [32, animal]. The Masliah mechanism review (PMID 22514792) [8, mechanism_review] is not itself retracted but all mechanism claims grounded primarily in it — particularly GSK-3β/tau-phosphorylation reduction and AD-model neurogenesis — are provisional pending corroboration from non-Masliah sources.

**Five: Regulatory and grey-market realities exclude this from validated cognitive-enhancement use.** Cerebrolysin holds national marketing authorizations in approximately 50 countries (Austria, Russia, China, South Korea, Eastern Europe, Southeast Asia) obtained largely through national procedures, many predating modern GCP standards [33, regulatory]. It has **never received FDA approval or EMA centralized authorization**. In the US, UK, Canada, and Australia it is not legally marketed for human therapeutic use. The patent has expired, removing the economic incentive for an FDA Phase III program. In non-approving countries, Cerebrolysin reaches users through grey-market channels (primarily Russian online pharmacies such as RUPharma) with unverifiable cold-chain integrity, counterfeiting risk, and no regulated prescribing pathway [37, anecdote_aggregate; 38, anecdote_aggregate]. All safety data derive from **elderly, high-comorbidity stroke and dementia populations** under physician supervision — not from healthy adults. No controlled safety data in healthy adults exist. WADA status is likely not prohibited under the 2026 Prohibited List as written (national approvals exist, precluding S0; it is not a named S2 growth factor), but this is a reasoned inference, not a confirmed clearance, and athletes must verify via GlobalDRO [34, regulatory].

**The honest bottom line:** Cerebrolysin is a genuine pharmaceutical with a large clinical literature, lawful national approvals in 50 countries, and plausible preclinical mechanisms in ischemia models. It is not a fringe compound. But independent synthesis of that literature — the 2023 Cochrane stroke review, the 2019 Cochrane vascular dementia review — returns null or very-low-certainty findings, and the literature is structurally confounded by manufacturer concentration, geographic clustering, and a documented research-misconduct finding that compromises the primary preclinical mechanism evidence for the Alzheimer's disease claims. The non-fatal SAE signal at the standard dose is not resolved. No controlled safety data exist for healthy-adult cognitive enhancement use. This is not a validated neuro-regenerative therapy for healthy adults.

## 1. Identity, Composition, and Manufacturing

### 1.1 What Cerebrolysin Is

Cerebrolysin (FPF-1070) is a **peptidergic drug** — a complex heterogeneous mixture, not a single defined molecular entity. It is classified by its manufacturer as a "well-established use" biological rather than a defined new chemical entity, reflecting the fact that no single active component has been characterized [1, mechanism_review]. It is produced through **controlled enzymatic proteolysis of lipid-free porcine brain cortex protein** followed by ultrafiltration to remove high-molecular-weight fragments above 10 kDa, purification, sterile filtration, and ampoule filling [13, in_vitro; 14, in_vitro]. The specific proteases and precise ultrafiltration parameters are not publicly disclosed and remain in the manufacturer's proprietary regulatory dossier [16, vendor_label].

The reported gross composition is approximately **85% free amino acids and 15% biologically active low-molecular-weight peptides** [13, in_vitro; 14, in_vitro]. An earlier characterization (Hartbauer et al. 2001, PMID 11459078) reported a 75%/25% split, suggesting possible batch or assay variation; the 85%/15% figure is the most widely cited in the primary literature. All peptide components fall below 10 kDa, with most characterized fragments in the 1–5 kDa range [13, in_vitro]. The preparation is formulated at approximately 215 mg/mL total protein/peptide material in aqueous solution.

### 1.2 The 638-Peptide Profiling — No Neurotrophic Factor Fragments

The most comprehensive peptidomic profiling study (Gevaert et al. 2015) identified **638 unique peptides** from commercially obtained Cerebrolysin using HPLC-ion trap and UHPLC-Q-IM-TOF mass spectrometry with UniProt pig-protein database searching and de novo sequencing [4, in_vitro]. The dominant protein sources were **tubulin alpha- and beta-chain, actin, and myelin basic protein** — structural neural proteins — rather than canonical neurotrophic factor proteins.

The critical negative finding: **no peptide fragments corresponding to GDNF, NGF, BDNF, or CNTF were detected in the preparation** [4, in_vitro]. The authors concluded that "the activities reported in the literature are likely the result of new, hitherto unknown cryptic peptides with nootropic properties" [4, in_vitro]. This directly contradicts manufacturer marketing language describing Cerebrolysin as "a mixture of neurotrophic factors." The phrase "neurotrophic mimicry" describes the functional consequence of unidentified cryptic peptides activating downstream neurotrophic signaling — not direct delivery of growth-factor sequences. The distinction matters: the mechanism is functional analogy, not structural identity.

A subsequent nanoLC-MS study (Yang et al. 2023) identified three candidate bioactive peptides: LLNLQPPPR and LSPSLRLP (both from myelin basic protein, anti-inflammatory candidates) and WPFPR (from tubulin beta chain, antioxidant candidate) [5, in_vitro]. Their causal roles have not been confirmed in controlled bioassay. A Russian peptidomic study (Gromova et al. 2019) identified 14,635 peptides from 1,643 porcine neuronal proteins with computational evidence for modulating 275 human signaling proteins [17, mechanism_review]; it was conducted by investigators with institutional ties to the manufacturer and the computational bioactivity inference should be treated as hypothesis-generating only.

A 2024 comparative analysis (Seidl & Aigner) found that peptide preparations marketed as compositionally similar to Cerebrolysin "lack relevant biological activity," establishing that even modest compositional differences produce functionally distinct products [18, in_vitro]. This supports the view that Cerebrolysin's biological fingerprint is specific to the EVER Neuro Pharma manufacturing process and not transferable to generic imitations. The **fundamental characterization limitation** is that the identity of the components responsible for Cerebrolysin's observed biological effects has not been established — the preparation cannot be reduced to a defined pharmacophore. This is not merely a scientific curiosity. It means that any batch-to-batch variation in the peptide profile is undetectable by standard QC unless the manufacturer runs functional bioassays, which is precisely what EVER Neuro Pharma claims to do as its release criterion. But the functional bioassay approach cannot specify which peptide(s) drove the observed effect, cannot detect a compositionally altered batch that happens to produce similar results through a different peptide subset, and cannot confirm that Internet-sourced or grey-market product — which may have undergone temperature abuse or been manufactured by a different process — has the same functional profile as pharmaceutical-grade product. The 638-peptide profiling study (Gevaert et al.) was conducted on Internet-purchased Cerebrolysin, not manufacturer-certified reference lots, introducing an additional confound: even the most comprehensive available peptide characterization cannot be confirmed as representative of the reference pharmaceutical product [4, in_vitro].

### 1.3 The Prion / TSE Consideration

Cerebrolysin is derived from **porcine central nervous system tissue**, the highest-risk anatomical compartment for prion contamination in any species. The European regulatory framework (Note for Guidance EMEA/410/01) requires TSE risk assessment for any CNS-derived biological material. Pigs are not a recognized reservoir for classical scrapie or BSE, and no naturally occurring porcine TSE has been described. However, experimental parenteral inoculation of BSE prions has produced disease in pigs at incubation periods of **69–150 weeks** [10, animal], demonstrating that pigs are not absolutely refractory to prion infection. A CNS-source biologic therefore carries a theoretical TSE transmission risk that cannot be categorically dismissed by regulatory convention.

EVER Neuro Pharma is required to maintain geographically controlled pig sourcing from negligible-BSE-risk countries and to validate the manufacturing process for prion-reduction capability. The enzymatic hydrolysis to peptides below 10 kDa substantially reduces but arguably cannot fully eliminate prion risk. No case of prion disease attributable to Cerebrolysin has been documented in published literature. This is a **theoretical and regulatory consideration, not a documented case-series risk** — but it is a material difference from small-molecule drugs and is directly relevant to grey-market supply where GMP process controls are unverifiable.

## 2. Mechanisms of Action — Preclinical Evidence and Integrity Constraints

### 2.1 Neurotrophic Factor Mimicry — Functional Analogy, Not Structural Identity

The dominant proposed mechanism is that Cerebrolysin peptides act as partial functional mimetics of endogenous neurotrophic factors — particularly BDNF, GDNF, NGF, and CNTF — activating their downstream intracellular signaling cascades (PI3K/Akt/GSK-3β, Trk-receptor-mediated pathways) without sharing their primary structures [1, mechanism_review]. This is supported by the observation that Cerebrolysin upregulates endogenous BDNF, VEGF, and IGF-1 in rodent brain tissue while suppressing TNF-α [8, mechanism_review].

**Critical integrity constraint on [8]:** The Masliah & Díez-Tejedor (2012) review (PMID 22514792) [8, mechanism_review] is authored by Eliezer Masliah, the subject of the September 2024 NIH ORI research-misconduct finding (figure fabrication/falsification across 132 papers, 8 Cerebrolysin-specific; Federal Register notice, 20 September 2024) [29, regulatory]. This review is not itself retracted as of mid-2026, but mechanism claims grounded primarily in it — particularly GSK-3β/tau reduction, neurogenesis enhancement, and anti-apoptotic effects in AD models — are **provisional** pending corroboration from non-Masliah sources. The primary transgenic-mouse papers this review synthesizes include the now-retracted Rockenstein et al. studies [31, animal; 32, animal]. The functional-mimicry claim itself has independent mechanistic support; the specific AD-model claims have been substantially compromised.

An earlier clinical review (Antón Álvarez & Fuentes 2011) documents the clinical evidence base for AD efficacy [1, mechanism_review], and a comprehensive neurotrophic mechanisms review (Rejdak et al. 2023) surveys the modulation of BDNF, GDNF, NGF, CNTF, and other factors across dementia, stroke, and TBI indications without relying on the Masliah cluster [27, mechanism_review]. These provide the non-Masliah independent mechanistic framing for the neurotrophic-mimicry hypothesis.

### 2.2 Neuroprotection — Anti-Excitotoxic, Anti-Apoptotic, Anti-Calpain

Multiple preclinical studies support neuroprotective activity through at least two demonstrated pathways with independent integrity.

**Calpain inhibition:** Wronski et al. (2000) demonstrated that the brain-derived peptide preparation inhibits both mu-calpain and m-calpain through a non-competitive or tight-binding competitive mechanism, with dialysis experiments confirming reversibility, attributed to calpastatin-like fragments [26, in_vitro]. Calpain is a calcium-activated protease central to excitotoxic neuronal death; its inhibition provides a mechanistically grounded neuroprotection pathway.

**Cytoskeletal protein stabilization:** A companion study (Wronski et al. 2000) showed that Cerebrolysin-treated primary chicken neurons retained significantly more MAP-2 (microtubule-associated protein 2) than controls through degradation prevention rather than upregulation, demonstrated by independence from de novo protein synthesis [11, in_vitro]. In excitotoxicity and hypoxia, MAP-2 loss is a marker of irreversible neuronal injury. This finding is from the primary literature with no integrity concerns.

**Anti-apoptotic effects and neurogenesis in ischemia models:** In APP transgenic mice, Cerebrolysin significantly increased BrdU+ cells and DCX+ neuroblasts while reducing TUNEL+ and activated caspase-3 positive cells, suggesting preservation of neural progenitor viability [9, animal]. In rats with embolic stroke, Cerebrolysin increased neural progenitor cell proliferation in the subventricular zone and upregulated Sonic hedgehog (Shh), its receptors Patched and Smoothened, and downstream mediators; pharmacological Shh blockade reversed both neurogenesis and behavioral improvement [28, animal]. A published erratum for this paper was issued in February 2025 (PMID: 39869713); the scope of the correction is unspecified and the core findings have not been retracted. **Minor integrity asterisk:** the Zhang/Chopp Shh paper carries a 2025 erratum of unspecified scope — paralleling the Masliah-cluster treatment applied elsewhere in this report, its findings should be treated as provisional until the erratum's scope is clarified, though the paper is not retracted and the Shh-neurogenesis mechanism has independent biological plausibility.

These ischemia-model findings — calpain inhibition, MAP-2 stabilization, Shh-mediated neurogenesis — represent the most integrity-solid portion of the preclinical mechanism evidence base, with the caveat above noted for the Shh paper. They are not part of the Masliah cluster.

### 2.3 Amyloid/Tau Pathology — A Substantially Compromised Literature

In Alzheimer's disease models, Cerebrolysin was reported to decrease β-amyloid deposition and reduce tau hyperphosphorylation through modulation of GSK-3β and CDK-5, and to increase synaptic density and restored neuronal cytoarchitecture [8, mechanism_review].

**These claims are doubly provisional.** First, they are grounded primarily in [8, mechanism_review], the Masliah-authored review under the author-integrity flag. Second, the primary transgenic-mouse papers this review synthesizes are the retracted Rockenstein studies:

- **Rockenstein et al. 2014** (tauopathy/mitochondria model, double-transgenic Tau×GSK-3β mice): Retracted December 2025 for duplicated/manipulated figure panels (p-Tau bands, Actin bands, p-Drp1 bands) [31, animal].
- **Rockenstein et al. 2015** (Pick's disease triple-repeat Tau transgenic model): Retracted March 2025 for image manipulation/figure integrity concerns in the Masliah misconduct cluster [32, animal].

Additionally, the Sharma cluster of Cerebrolysin preclinical publications has been flagged on PubPeer with multiple retractions, including an entire Springer book edited by Hari and Aruna Sharma containing three Cerebrolysin chapters [30, anecdote_aggregate]. The anti-amyloid/anti-tau Alzheimer's claims rest on a literature cluster now substantially compromised by documented research misconduct and formal retractions. These claims should not be treated as established; they should be treated as hypothesis-generating pending corroboration from non-Masliah, non-retracted primary sources.

**Non-Masliah animal evidence that survives intact:** Ubhi et al. (2013) — co-authored by Rockenstein but not Masliah as lead — demonstrated in APP/PS1 transgenic mice that Cerebrolysin modulated the pro-NGF/mature NGF ratio, increasing mature NGF immunoreactivity in the hippocampus and preserving nucleus basalis cholinergic neurons [35, animal]. The Rockenstein et al. (2007) neurogenesis paper (PMID 17131129) demonstrated increased BrdU+ and DCX+ neural progenitor cells in APP transgenic mice [9, animal]. These papers are part of the broader Rockenstein/Masliah cluster and require scrutiny, but are not among the formally retracted studies as of mid-2026.

## 3. Clinical Efficacy — Indication-by-Indication

### 3.1 Acute Ischemic Stroke — The Pivotal Literature

#### The CASTA Trial — The Largest, The Null Result

The **Cerebrolysin Acute Stroke Treatment in Asia (CASTA)** trial enrolled 1,070 patients with acute hemispheric ischemic stroke at more than 50 Asian centers, randomized within 12 hours to 30 mL Cerebrolysin IV daily × 10 days versus placebo plus aspirin (Heiss et al. 2012) [12, rct]. The confirmatory primary endpoint — a global directional test combining mRS, Barthel Index, and NIHSS at 90 days — showed **no significant difference between groups** in the overall population [12, rct]. Its null primary result is the most robust single-trial finding in the literature.

A pre-specified post-hoc subgroup analysis in patients with severe stroke (NIHSS >12) reported a numerically meaningful difference: 90-day mortality 10.5% vs. 20.2% (HR ~1.97, approximately two-fold difference) favoring Cerebrolysin [12, rct]. This severe-stroke signal is the primary rationale for continued research interest in the indication and warrants fair presentation. It is, however, a subgroup analysis — not the trial's primary result — and must be interpreted accordingly: confidence intervals crossed unity, the analysis was post-hoc rather than pre-registered as a primary endpoint, and it is subject to the same sponsor-concentration caveats that apply to the overall CASTA program. The signal is genuinely hypothesis-generating and explains why the field has not fully abandoned the stroke indication despite the null overall trial; it does not constitute evidence of efficacy in severe stroke absent a dedicated confirmatory trial [12, rct].

CASTA is the largest randomized controlled trial of Cerebrolysin to date and the trial with the lowest disclosed manufacturer involvement, though co-investigators Bornstein and Heiss are documented EVER Pharma collaborators; the manufacturer's exact role in CASTA (drug supply, placebo, randomization codes, statistics) cannot be confirmed or excluded from publicly available sources.

#### The CARS Trial — Positive But Sponsor-Funded

**CARS** (Cerebrolysin and Recovery After Stroke; Mureșanu et al. 2016) enrolled 208 patients at 24–72 hours post-stroke onset; the mITT-LOCF population at day 90 comprised 205 patients (n=104 Cerebrolysin; n=101 placebo). Dosing: 30 mL IV daily × 21 days alongside standard rehabilitation [21, rct]. The primary outcome — Action Research Arm Test at day 90 — showed large, statistically significant superiority (Mann-Whitney estimator 0.71; 95% CI 0.63–0.79; p < 0.0001) [21, rct]. The funding statement reads explicitly: "This study was funded by EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH, Austria." Three named authors were EVER Neuro Pharma employees; the coordinating investigator (Mureșanu) and multiple principal investigators reported grants and research support from the manufacturer [36, meta_analysis].

The 2018 meta-analysis of nine RCTs (Bornstein et al., n=1,879) — the most-cited manufacturer-affiliated meta-analytic claim — found significant functional benefit (NNT 7.7) and was co-authored by the same investigator cluster, explicitly noting EVER Neuro Pharma "helped to identify further sources" [36, meta_analysis]. This finding stands in direct contrast to the null Cochrane verdict.

#### Cochrane 2023 — The Primary Independent Finding

The most recent Cochrane update (Ziganshina et al. 2023, 7th update; 7 RCTs, n=1,773) is the primary independent synthesis [6, meta_analysis]:

- **All-cause death:** RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.65–1.41); moderate-certainty evidence of "little to no difference" [6, meta_analysis].
- **Death or dependency:** NOT REPORTED in any of the 7 included trials. Death or dependency is the prespecified primary endpoint in virtually every contemporary phase-III stroke RCT. Its universal absence across a sponsor-concentrated trial set raises a **selective-outcome-reporting concern** beyond a simple evidentiary gap — the pattern is consistent with selective non-publication or non-prespecification of unfavorable outcomes.
- **Non-fatal serious adverse events — the safety signal:** **RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23); 3 trials, 1,335 participants; moderate-certainty evidence.** At the standard 30 mL × 10-day regimen: RR 2.87 (95% CI 1.24–6.69) [6, meta_analysis].
- **Total SAEs:** RR 1.16 (95% CI 0.81–1.66); no significant difference [6, meta_analysis].

Risk-of-bias findings: allocation concealment unclear in 6/7 studies; high attrition bias in 4 studies (16–29% loss to follow-up); two studies used last-observation-carried-forward imputation; three trials received direct EVER Neuro Pharma support [6, meta_analysis].

**Authors' conclusion:** Cerebrolysin "probably has no beneficial effect on preventing all-cause death" and shows "a potential increase in non-fatal serious adverse events." No recommendation for use.

A 2025 independent 14-RCT meta-analysis (Kanabar et al., no disclosed manufacturer ties) found moderate-certainty evidence of a small NIHSS improvement (MD +1.39 points) but called functional independence evidence "inconclusive" and acknowledged possible publication bias on funnel plot analysis [42, meta_analysis]. This represents the most current independent (non-Cochrane, non-manufacturer) synthesis.

### 3.2 Alzheimer's Disease

Cerebrolysin has been evaluated in mild-to-moderate AD in a series of RCTs, all using 30 mL/day IV for four-week courses with follow-up to six months. The manufacturer-associated meta-analysis (Gauthier et al. 2015) pooled six double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs using individual patient data provided by EBEWE/EVER Neuro Pharma [19, meta_analysis]:

- **Cognitive function at 4 weeks:** Significant superiority (SMD −0.40; p=0.0031), though the 6-month cognitive difference was not significant [19, meta_analysis].
- **Global clinical change:** Significant at 4 weeks (OR 3.32; p=0.0212) and 6 months (OR 4.98; p=0.0150) [19, meta_analysis].

An earlier meta-analysis (Wei et al. 2007; six double-blind placebo-controlled trials) found significant improvement in Clinical Global Impression (log OR 1.18; 95% CI 0.75–1.61; p<0.05) but concluded "more convincing evidence is needed"; EBEWE Pharmaceutical communication during the study was disclosed [20, meta_analysis]. A 2025 umbrella systematic review on vascular cognitive impairment (Masserini et al.) found "small to moderate improvements in cognition" for Cerebrolysin alongside established agents [41, meta_analysis].

Evidence quality caveats: both meta-analyses have manufacturer involvement in design or data access, small component trial sizes, and short treatment/follow-up windows. No independent Cochrane review specifically covering AD monotherapy with Cerebrolysin has reached a strong positive verdict. The preclinical mechanism underpinning the AD efficacy claim — amyloid/tau reduction via GSK-3β — is substantially compromised by the Masliah misconduct finding and Rockenstein retractions (see Section 2.3).

### 3.3 Vascular Dementia

The 2019 Cochrane review (Cui et al.; 6 RCTs, n=597) is the primary independent synthesis [7, meta_analysis]:

- **Cognitive outcomes:** Three trials (MMSE/ADAS-cog+) showed statistically significant benefit — SMD 0.36 (95% CI 0.13–0.58) — but graded **very-low-quality evidence** with I²=86% [7, meta_analysis].
- **Global function:** Two trials (n=379) showed improvement — RR 2.69 (95% CI 1.82–3.98) — again rated **very-low-quality evidence** [7, meta_analysis].
- **Adverse events:** No difference (RR 0.91; 95% CI 0.29–2.85; very low-quality evidence) [7, meta_analysis].
- **Industry involvement:** Four of six trials received pharmaceutical industry support from EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH; where funding details were available, "all studies were supported by the pharmaceutical industry" [7, meta_analysis].
- **Publication bias flagged explicitly:** "With a review of a commonly used intervention, where there are few studies and **all are positive** despite small numbers, the potential for publication bias remains" [7, meta_analysis].

**Authors' conclusion:** Results "not definitive" due to substantial heterogeneity and high risk of bias. Not recommended for routine use.

### 3.4 Traumatic Brain Injury — The CAPTAIN Program

The CAPTAIN program comprises two phase IIIb/IV trials and a prospective meta-analysis, all by manufacturer-affiliated investigators with Asian-Pacific centers.

**CAPTAIN I** (Poon et al. 2020; n=46; moderate-to-severe TBI): ITT primary endpoint narrowly missed; per-protocol analysis showed significant superiority across cognitive domains (Color Trails 1 and 2, Stroop) [22, rct].

**CAPTAIN II** (Mureșanu et al. 2020; n=142; moderate-severe TBI, GCS 7–12; 50 mL Cerebrolysin IV × 10 days then additional cycles): Primary outcome — multidimensional ensemble of 13 scales at day 90 — MWcombined = 0.59 (95% CI 0.52–0.66; p=0.0119); a small-to-medium statistically significant effect [23, rct].

**CAPTAIN prospective meta-analysis** (Vester et al. 2021; n=185 total): Day 90 MWcombined = 0.60 (95% CI 0.52–0.68; p=0.0146; SMD = 0.34; OR = 1.77) [24, meta_analysis].

**Evidence quality limitations:** Combined n=185 across both RCTs without independent replication; authored entirely by investigators with manufacturer ties; no independent Cochrane review for TBI exists; the composite outcome measure (13 scales) rather than a single prespecified clinical endpoint (e.g., GOS-E) limits interpretability; the ITT miss in CAPTAIN I and the small sample collectively limit confidence.

**Minor/peripheral indications:** Beyond the four principal indication clusters, Cerebrolysin has been studied in smaller single-trials for additional neurological conditions — for example, a randomized trial in cervical spondylotic myelopathy reported adjunctive functional gains [3, cohort]. These minor-indication studies share the same structural limitations (small samples, manufacturer-region geography, no independent replication) and do not alter the overall evidence picture.

**Pediatric and developmental claimed uses (harm-relevant note):** Cerebrolysin is marketed and used in some countries for pediatric developmental conditions — including autism spectrum disorder, cerebral palsy, and developmental delay — particularly in Russia, China, Ukraine, and several Southeast Asian markets. These uses lack robust indexed RCT evidence in the English-language or major indexed literature; no adequately powered, independently conducted RCTs in pediatric developmental populations have been identified. The injectable-in-children context raises additional caution: the non-fatal SAE signal at the standard dose (Cochrane 2023: RR 2.39 in adult stroke populations [6, meta_analysis]) cannot be assumed to be better in pediatric populations without controlled data, and porcine-CNS-derived biologics administered parenterally to children carry the same prion-theoretical and supply-chain risks as in adults. Practitioners and parents encountering claims for these indications should treat them as unvalidated.

### 3.5 The Load-Bearing Tension — Independent vs. Sponsor-Associated Evidence

| Feature | Manufacturer-associated trials/metas | Independent Cochrane reviews |
|---|---|---|
| Stroke (death/dependency) | Positive subgroup trends (CASTA severe stroke; CARS motor) | NULL (GRADE moderate) — death/dependency not even reported |
| Stroke (non-fatal SAEs) | Generally "comparable to placebo" | Significant increase (RR 2.39; 95% CI 1.10–5.23) |
| Vascular dementia | Positive cognitive/global signal | Positive signal but ALL outcomes very-low-certainty |
| Alzheimer's disease | Global impression significant; cognitive weaker | No independent Cochrane; manufacturer meta-analyses dominate |
| TBI (CAPTAIN) | Small-to-medium significant effect | No independent Cochrane review exists |

The **honest bottom line** for clinical efficacy: Cerebrolysin does not have an independent, large-scale, replication-verified RCT demonstrating benefit on a clinically meaningful primary endpoint (death, disability, dependency) for any indication. The one large placebo-controlled stroke trial (CASTA, n=1,070) was null. Independent Cochrane synthesis finds no proven benefit and, for stroke, flags a potential non-fatal SAE signal at the standard dose. The vascular dementia evidence is positive in direction but rated very low quality across all outcomes. The AD and TBI evidence bases are dominated by manufacturer-associated trials and meta-analyses.

## 4. The Integrity Audit — Two Axes

### 4.1 Axis One: Sponsor and Author Concentration

The Cerebrolysin clinical trial literature has a documented funder-concentration problem that the Cochrane reviewers themselves flag as a primary bias concern.

**Quantified manufacturer-associated share:**

| Trial / Analysis | Manufacturer Associated | Independent |
|---|---|---|
| CARS trial (Mureșanu 2016) | YES — direct funding + 3 employee authors | — |
| CARS meta-analysis (Mureșanu 2017) | YES — same cluster | — |
| 9-RCT meta-analysis (Bornstein 2018) | YES — search support + overlapping trials | — |
| Vascular dementia Cochrane (Cui 2019) | Mixed — 3–4/6 trials manufacturer-funded | — |
| Stroke Cochrane (Ziganshina 2023) | Mixed — 3/7 trials received direct support | — |
| 14-RCT meta-analysis (Kanabar 2025) | — | YES — no disclosed ties |
| CEREHETIS trial post-hoc (Kalinin 2024) | — | YES — no disclosed ties |

**Estimated manufacturer-associated share: approximately 60–70% of pivotal evidence by publication weight.** In the two Cochrane reviews, 43–50% of included trials received direct pharmaceutical company support; the Cochrane reviews judge that support "may have affected how those studies were designed, carried out, and reported" [6, meta_analysis; 7, meta_analysis].

**The recurring investigator network:** The same small cluster appears across trials, meta-analyses, and advisory roles with documented EVER Neuro Pharma financial relationships: Dafin F. Mureșanu (Romania; CARS coordinating investigator; CAPTAIN advisory board; grants/research support from EVER), Wolf-Dieter Heiss (Advisory Board and Speakers Bureau for EVER), Ovidiu Bajenaru (Romania; CARS PI; grants from EVER), Alla Guekht (Russia; grants/research support from EVER), J.C. Vester (advisory board), Volker Rahlfs (paid consultant), plus EVER employees Edith Doppler, Stefan Winter, Herbert Moessler, and Dieter Meier as co-authors on primary trial papers [21, rct; 25, mechanism_review; 36, meta_analysis].

**Geographic concentration:** Trials cluster in Russia, Eastern Europe (Romania, Czech Republic, Hungary, Croatia, Slovenia), China, and Southeast Asia — the drug's commercial registration geography. This is not a coincidence; trial activity and regulatory approval cluster in the same markets where the product is commercially registered. No major North American, UK, or Scandinavian academic neurology network has independently tested Cerebrolysin [6, meta_analysis; 7, meta_analysis; 42, meta_analysis].

**The funnel-plot anomaly:** The Bornstein 2018 manufacturer-affiliated meta-analysis identifies CASTA — the largest trial with the least-documented manufacturer involvement and the null primary result — as a negative outlier in its funnel plot. The pattern is consistent with small-study/positive-study inflation in the remaining trials [36, meta_analysis].

### 4.2 Axis Two: Research Misconduct — The Masliah/NIH ORI Finding

The September 2024 NIH ORI finding against Eliezer Masliah is not peripheral context; it is load-bearing for how the Cerebrolysin preclinical AD mechanism evidence should be read.

**The NIH ORI finding (September 2024):** NIH concluded a formal research misconduct investigation finding that Masliah (former NIA Neuroscience Division director) committed "falsification and/or fabrication involving re-use and relabeling of figure panels" across **132 papers** [29, regulatory]. **Eight of those papers concern Cerebrolysin specifically.** A contemporaneous *Science* investigative report (Piller, September 2024) detailed the dossier and its implications for the Alzheimer's and Parkinson's fields [30, anecdote_aggregate].

**The commercial-scientific overlap:** Herbert Moessler, EVER Pharma's former general manager and co-author on 57 Cerebrolysin papers, co-founded Neuropore Therapies with Masliah in 2008 — a direct commercial-scientific overlap between the manufacturer's executive leadership and a scientist now found by NIH to have fabricated data across a relevant literature cluster [30, anecdote_aggregate].

**The Rockenstein retractions:**

- **Rockenstein et al. 2014** (BMC Neuroscience; tauopathy/mitochondria model, double-transgenic Tau×GSK-3β mice): **Retracted December 2025** (retraction PMID: 41382024) for duplicated/manipulated figure panels [31, animal].
- **Rockenstein et al. 2015** (BMC Neuroscience; Pick's disease triple-repeat Tau model): **Retracted March 2025** (retraction PMID: 40065222) for image manipulation/figure integrity concerns in the Masliah misconduct cluster [32, animal].

**The Sharma cluster:** Multiple Sharma papers on Cerebrolysin have been flagged on PubPeer with several retracted; an entire Springer book edited by Hari and Aruna Sharma containing three Cerebrolysin chapters was also retracted [30, anecdote_aggregate].

**What this means for the evidence base:** The anti-amyloid/anti-tau claims in Alzheimer's rodent models rest substantially on a literature cluster under active integrity review or formally retracted. The Masliah mechanism review (PMID 22514792) [8, mechanism_review] is not retracted but carries an author-integrity flag, and its mechanism claims — particularly the GSK-3β/tau-phosphorylation and AD-model neurogenesis narrative — should not be treated as established. Non-integrity-flagged animal work in ischemia models (Shh-mediated neurogenesis; calpain inhibition; MAP-2 stabilization) remains in the literature, but the specifically Alzheimer's-disease-oriented mechanism claims are compromised at their primary evidentiary root.

## 5. Safety, Regulatory Status, and Monitoring

### 5.1 Safety Profile — Elderly Disease Populations Only

**The population-mismatch caveat is mandatory and load-bearing:** All safety and tolerability data in this section derive from RCTs in **elderly disease populations** — acute ischemic stroke patients aged approximately 60–75 with high vascular comorbidity; dementia patients — administered Cerebrolysin IV under physician supervision in acute clinical settings. These populations differ fundamentally from healthy-adult nootropic users. **No controlled safety data exist in healthy adults.** The extrapolation gap is unquantified, and the tolerability characterization below applies only to the studied elderly disease populations.

Across its clinical trial history, Cerebrolysin has been reported as generally well-tolerated **in these elderly disease populations**, with aggregate adverse event rates comparable to placebo in most analyses. A 2021 meta-analysis (Strilciuc et al.; n=2,202; 12 RCTs, acute ischemic stroke) found overall AE rates of 43.8% vs. 43.6% (RR=0.98; non-significant) [39, meta_analysis]. This apparent reassurance must be reconciled with the Cochrane non-fatal SAE signal below (§5.2): the two analyses measure **different endpoints** — Strilciuc pooled *total/all-cause* adverse events (a broad denominator dominated by minor events, where any serious-event difference is diluted), whereas the Cochrane review isolated *non-fatal **serious** adverse events* specifically (a narrower, severity-defined subset). The two results are therefore not contradictory; they describe different strata, and the independent Cochrane severity-specific signal is the more conservative, safety-relevant read. Common non-serious adverse events include **agitation, insomnia, dizziness, sensation of heat/warmth, nausea, headache, tremor, and injection-site pain** [40, meta_analysis]. The safety database is dominated by sponsor-associated trials; independent, non-manufacturer-associated long-term safety data are sparse.

### 5.2 The Non-Fatal Serious Adverse Event Signal — The Critical Safety Finding

The most important safety finding in the evidence base comes from the 2023 Cochrane systematic review [6, meta_analysis]:

- **Non-fatal SAE: RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23)** — 3 trials, 1,335 participants; moderate-certainty evidence [6, meta_analysis].
- **Dose-response signal: At 30 mL × 10-day regimen (cumulative 300 mL), RR 2.87 (95% CI 1.24–6.69)** — 2 trials, 1,189 participants [6, meta_analysis].
- **Fatal SAEs:** RR 0.90 (95% CI 0.59–1.38); no difference [6, meta_analysis].
- **All-cause mortality:** RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.65–1.41); no difference [6, meta_analysis].

The Cochrane reviewers characterize this as **moderate-certainty evidence** — the true effect may differ from the point estimate, but the signal should not be dismissed. The nature of the non-fatal SAEs is not individually enumerated in the Cochrane summary beyond the aggregate count. A separate post-hoc analysis of the CEREHETIS trial (Kalinin & Khasanova; no manufacturer funding) found no excess serious adverse events in that specific dataset [43, cohort], so the mechanistic picture is not fully settled. The Cochrane non-fatal SAE signal at the standard 30 mL dose remains the most concerning aggregate safety finding as of mid-2026.

In very rare cases, **hypersensitivity or anaphylactoid reactions** have been observed: skin reactions, local inflammatory reactions, headache, neck pain, limb pain, fever, low back pain, dyspnea, chills, and shock-like state [44, regulatory]. These represent the most clinically serious acute risks of administration.

### 5.3 Contraindications

Based on available prescribing information (Austrian SmPC and regional equivalents) [44, regulatory]:

1. **Known hypersensitivity** to any component, including porcine-derived products
2. **Epilepsy / status epilepticus / grand mal convulsions** — increased seizure frequency has been observed; contraindicated in active seizure disorders
3. **Severe renal impairment** — low-molecular-weight peptide constituents may accumulate
4. **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — absent reproductive safety data
5. **Drug interactions:** Avoid concurrent use with MAO inhibitors; caution with antidepressants

### 5.4 Monitoring Considerations

Cerebrolysin is a prescription, clinician-administered IV or IM drug in all approving jurisdictions. No surrogate biochemical endpoint validates neurotrophic effect or guides dosing; monitoring is functional-scale-based (NIHSS, mRS, Barthel Index, ADAS-Cog). Minimum infusion time: 20–60 minutes for IV administration to reduce the warmth/heat sensation signal. Clinical observation during the first 20–60 minutes of each infusion cycle is warranted for hypersensitivity signals (skin changes, dyspnea, chills, hemodynamic instability).

### 5.5 Regulatory Status — Approved in 50 Countries, Not FDA or EMA-Central

Cerebrolysin holds **national marketing authorizations in approximately 50 countries** — Austria, Russia, China, South Korea, Germany, Vietnam, Philippines, Thailand, Romania, Czech Republic, Poland, Croatia, Hungary, and other Eastern European, CIS, and Southeast Asian markets — for indications spanning acute ischemic stroke, TBI, vascular dementia, and Alzheimer's disease [33, regulatory]. In Russia and several post-Soviet states it is classified as standard of care for neurological conditions.

**These approvals are national in character.** The EMA's centralized marketing authorization procedure — mandatory for many novel biologics — was not used for Cerebrolysin. National approvals in Austria and other EU member states were obtained through national (or mutual recognition/decentralized) procedures, largely during the 1980s–1990s, prior to modern GCP standards [33, regulatory; 45, regulatory]. **Cerebrolysin has never been approved by the US FDA for any indication.** It is not legally marketed or sold for human use in the United States, the United Kingdom, or Australia. No IND application has been filed. International approval cannot be conflated with FDA or EMA-central approval.

The practical consequence: Western regulatory bodies (FDA, EMA central committee, MHRA, TGA) have never conducted an independent review of the clinical trial package. The commercial incentive for pursuing FDA approval is absent because the original patents have expired, removing the economic justification for a USD 200M–1B+ Phase III program that would immediately benefit generic competitors [38, anecdote_aggregate].

### 5.6 WADA Status — Likely Not Prohibited, But Not a Confirmed Clearance

**Cerebrolysin is not explicitly named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List** [34, regulatory].

WADA's **S0 (Non-Approved Substances)** category prohibits "any pharmacological substance which is not addressed by any of the subsequent sections of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority." Because Cerebrolysin holds national marketing authorizations in Russia, Austria, China, South Korea, and approximately 45 other countries, it has "current approval by governmental regulatory health authorities" — and therefore S0 as currently written does not straightforwardly apply [34, regulatory]. This distinguishes Cerebrolysin from substances like BPC-157 that lack any national approval.

WADA's **S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics)** prohibits specific growth factors and mimetics — primarily those affecting muscle, tendon, and vascularization (IGF-1, FGFs, HGF, PDGF, VEGF). Neurotrophic growth factors (BDNF, NGF) are not explicitly listed in S2's enumerated substances. Cerebrolysin's claimed mechanism involves upregulation of endogenous BDNF and NGF, but it is a heterogeneous porcine-brain peptide hydrolysate — categorically distinct from the recombinant proteins S2 contemplates [34, regulatory].

**Honest framing:** Current evidence supports the assessment that Cerebrolysin is **likely not prohibited** under the 2026 WADA Prohibited List as written. However, this is a **reasoned inference from WADA's published list language — not a confirmed clearance**. No explicit WADA ruling or official GlobalDRO check result has been confirmed. Athletes requiring a definitive ruling must verify via GlobalDRO.com and their sport's NADO.

## 6. Commercialization, Concentration, and Grey-Market Realism

### 6.1 Ever Neuro Pharma and the Commercial Context

EVER Pharma GmbH is an Austrian pharmaceutical company headquartered in Unterach am Attersee, Austria; **Ever Neuro Pharma GmbH** is its neurology subsidiary and the named manufacturer for Cerebrolysin. Cerebrolysin is EVER's flagship product and the basis of its commercial identity, marketed across approximately 50 countries with its largest revenue markets in **Russia, China, CIS states, and Southeast Asia** [46, anecdote_aggregate; 47, anecdote_aggregate]. Eastern European markets aligned with the Mureșanu/Heiss academic investigator network are secondary but stable.

**The patent-expiry economics are critical context** for the FDA gap: original patents have expired. A modern FDA Phase III program would cost USD 200M–1B+ and, if successful, would immediately benefit generic competitors. The commercial incentive to pursue Western regulatory approval is structurally absent, which means the evidentiary gap is a business decision as much as a scientific one [38, anecdote_aggregate].

Western regulatory and scientific skepticism has not materially disrupted EVER's commercial position because the drug's markets are insulated from FDA/MHRA approval requirements. The approval footprint maps almost perfectly to the clinical trial geography: the same Eastern European, Russian, Chinese, and Southeast Asian markets that produced the positive trial literature are also the markets where the drug is registered and commercially dominant.

### 6.2 Grey-Market Injectable Use in Non-Approving Countries

In countries where Cerebrolysin is not approved, an established off-label injectable use community exists in the nootropic/biohacker/longevity space. Cerebrolysin carries the informal designation **"the caviar of nootropics"** — high-cost, high-status, injectable, borrowed from clinical stroke neurology and applied to neuroregeneration and cognitive enhancement in otherwise healthy users [48, anecdote_aggregate].

**Primary grey-market sourcing channel:** Russian online pharmacies, most prominently **RUPharma** (Moscow), which ship internationally including to the US and UK, listing authentic EVER Neuro Pharma CEREBROLYSIN® vials with explicit MHRA/FDA disclaimers [37, anecdote_aggregate]. Grey-market pricing runs approximately USD 60 per pack. **No dose numbers from this or any grey-market source are cited in this report.** Doses circulating in community contexts carry the `anecdote_aggregate` tag only and cannot ground any clinical decision.

Community-documented adverse events from self-administration include rapid-injection reactions (warmth, sweating, palpitations, dizziness, hypertension) and rare anaphylactic reactions [48, anecdote_aggregate]. Community guidance explicitly discourages novice self-injection.

### 6.3 Authenticity and Cold-Chain Hazards

Because Cerebrolysin is expensive, injectable, and sourced through unregulated channels, it is an active counterfeiting target. A figure of approximately 37% of grey-market peptide products from unverified international suppliers containing zero active ingredient or undeclared fillers appears in a commercial vendor's own authentication guide [49, anecdote_aggregate] — this figure originates from a source with a direct commercial interest and has not been independently verified; treat as a qualitative hazard indicator, not an established rate. Authentication for genuine CEREBROLYSIN® includes laser-etched batch codes, holographic labels, and EVER Neuro Pharma's public batch-verification portal.

Cerebrolysin is a **biologic injectable**, not a synthesized small molecule. This creates a hazard profile distinct from peptide research chemicals:

1. **Cold-chain requirement:** Manufacturer specifies storage below 25°C, protected from freezing and direct sunlight. Grey-market international shipments — 10–30-day transit via non-temperature-controlled postal networks from Russia or China — cannot reliably guarantee cold-chain integrity [37, anecdote_aggregate].
2. **Sterility:** IV-route injectables require pharmaceutical-grade sterility (USP particulate standards, endotoxin limits). Product stored beyond shelf life, improperly transported, or counterfeited with non-sterile contents carries endotoxin and microbial contamination risk [49, anecdote_aggregate].
3. **Prion risk (theoretical, low):** The porcine-CNS origin raises the prion consideration (see Section 1.3). The manufacturing process substantially reduces but arguably cannot fully eliminate prion risk. No prion transmission cases from Cerebrolysin have been documented, but the risk is unaudited by any Western regulator in grey-market product [50, anecdote_aggregate].
4. **No legal US prescribing pathway:** Cerebrolysin does not exist as an FDA-regulated drug substance — no compounding pharmacy pathway, no USP monograph, no authorized prescribing basis. Personal importation risks customs seizure. Concierge/longevity clinics administering it in non-approving countries operate outside licensed prescribing channels [38, anecdote_aggregate].

## 7. Honest Bottom Line

Cerebrolysin occupies an unusual evidentiary position: it is a legitimate, nationally authorized pharmaceutical with an extensive clinical literature, plausible preclinical mechanisms in ischemia models (calpain inhibition, MAP-2 stabilization, Shh-mediated neurogenesis), and lawful clinical use in 50 countries. It is not a fringe compound with no clinical history. This distinguishes it from many grey-market injectables.

But the independent synthesis of that clinical literature returns null or cautious verdicts across its two most-studied indications:

- In **acute ischemic stroke**, the largest trial (CASTA, n=1,070) was null on its primary endpoint. The 2023 Cochrane review found no mortality benefit and a statistically significant non-fatal SAE signal at the standard dose. Death or dependency — the standard stroke trial primary — was never reported across all 7 Cochrane trials.
- In **vascular dementia**, the 2019 Cochrane found a positive cognitive direction but rated all outcomes very-low-certainty, flagged extreme heterogeneity (I²=86%), and noted all funded studies received industry support.

The clinical literature is structurally confounded by:

1. **Approximately 60–70% manufacturer-sponsorship concentration**, with a recurring small investigator network with disclosed financial ties to EVER Neuro Pharma
2. **Geographic clustering** in the drug's own commercial registration geography (Russia, Eastern Europe, China), with zero independent trials from North American, UK, or Scandinavian academic centers
3. **Selective-outcome-reporting concern** — the death-or-dependency primary was uniformly absent from all 7 Cochrane stroke trials
4. **A documented research-misconduct finding** (NIH ORI Sept 2024; Masliah; 132 papers, 8 Cerebrolysin-specific) that compromises the primary preclinical mechanism evidence for the Alzheimer's disease claims, with two flagship AD-model papers retracted in 2025

The preclinical mechanistic rationale — neurotrophic-mimicry, calpain inhibition, Shh-mediated neurogenesis in ischemia — is biologically plausible and supported by animal data from multiple paradigms. But the specifically Alzheimer's-disease-oriented mechanism claims (amyloid/tau reduction via GSK-3β) are now substantially compromised at their primary evidentiary root by documented research misconduct and retractions. These should not be cited as established.

For healthy-adult cognitive enhancement: there are no controlled safety data, no efficacy data, and the entire extrapolation from elderly stroke/dementia populations is unvalidated. Every tolerability characterization in this report — the comparable-to-placebo aggregate AE rates, the reported adverse events, the contraindication list — derives from elderly patients with acute ischemic stroke or dementia who were treated under physician supervision in clinical trial settings. These populations differ in virtually every pharmacologically relevant dimension from a healthy adult in their 20s–40s self-injecting IV at home without a prescribing physician, without standardized dosing, and without the cold-chain and sterility controls of pharmaceutical-grade product.

The grey-market supply adds cold-chain degradation, counterfeiting risk, endotoxin hazard from improperly stored biologics, and the porcine-CNS prion consideration — all unmitigated by any regulatory oversight — on top of the already-constrained clinical evidence. The comparison is not between a slightly less-certain drug and a safer alternative; it is between a drug whose independent evidence is null-to-cautious in its approved disease populations, and an injectable porcine-brain biologic being self-administered by healthy people on the basis of community lore, manufacturer-adjacent positive trials, and a mechanism story whose key Alzheimer's disease chapter is now substantially retracted.

**The evidence-based bottom line:** A genuinely large clinical literature whose independent synthesis is null or cautious, structurally confounded by manufacturer and geography concentration, and now further compromised by a documented research-misconduct finding touching its mechanism evidence — not a validated neuro-regenerative therapy for healthy-adult cognitive enhancement.
