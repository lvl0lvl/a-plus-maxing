# Cerebrolysin — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for Cerebrolysin (FPF-1070; trade name; manufactured by EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH, Unterach, Austria). Three languages surveyed: **Russian** (one of the two largest prescription markets; primary real-world effectiveness literature), **Chinese** (major registration market; Chinese-language domestic primaries), and **German** (manufacturer's home-region language; Austrian/German regulatory and early-era foundational literature).

**Critical admissibility distinction applied throughout:** This layer covers only genuinely non-English-language publications — articles whose primary publication language is Russian, Chinese, or German. Papers authored by Russian or Chinese investigators but published in English-language international journals (e.g., *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* entries that carry English-language titles in PubMed because they include English abstracts, but whose full text is Russian) are included here when the primary publication language confirmed via PubMed LA field = `rus` or `chi`. Papers published entirely in English by authors from these countries belong to the English layer, not here.

**Concentration-caveat notice (applies uniformly across all three language layers):** Cerebrolysin's clinical literature is subject to a documented sponsor + geography concentration concern that is NOT resolved by surveying the non-English press. The non-English literature is, if anything, MORE concentrated than the English corpus: Russian-language primaries are predominantly conducted in Russia, the drug's largest prescription market, by investigators who frequently hold institutional ties to the Russian distributor network or to the European manufacturer (EVER Neuro Pharma). Chinese-language primaries are conducted in China, where Cerebrolysin holds national marketing authorization. The same conflict-of-interest structure that applies to the English-language trials applies here. Surveying this literature reinforces the quantity of publication but does not provide an independent evidentiary layer from outside the commercial-registration geography. An honest reading is: the non-English literature is voluminous, but it reinforces — not resolves — the concentration concern.

**Masliah-misconduct caveat (forward-referenced from the main research report):** The primary preclinical mechanism cluster for Cerebrolysin includes papers by Eliezer Masliah (NIH ORI research-misconduct finding, September 2024, 8 Cerebrolysin-specific papers) and the Sharma group (25 Cerebrolysin papers flagged). This caveat applies to mechanism claims in the English layer; it does not directly compromise the Russian or German clinical literature surveyed here, but it contextualizes the overall integrity environment of the Cerebrolysin evidence base.

## Russian (PRIMARY SURVEY — Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr + Eksp Klin Farmakol + CEREHETIS Russian-language outputs)

**Result: 154 total PubMed-indexed Russian-language publications with "cerebrolysin" in title/abstract (LA=rus confirmed). 7 confirmed primary clinical or experimental studies selected for this survey (R1–R7). The Russian literature is the largest single non-English evidence corpus for Cerebrolysin. Zero analog-specific items (expected — Cerebrolysin is the compound, not an analog).**

**Database search:** PubMed eutils (term: cerebrolysin[tiab] AND russian[la]) returned **154 results** — a substantially larger corpus than Selank's 33. This reflects Cerebrolysin's status as a major Russian-prescription neuroprotective agent with decades of use. The primary Russian-language venue is *Zhurnal nevrologii i psikhiatrii imeni S.S. Korsakova* (*Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S S Korsakova*), which carries the substantial majority of Russian-language Cerebrolysin primaries. eLibrary.ru is the primary Russian repository but was access-blocked from this session; Google site-search and PubMed LA-filter together covered the major indexed publications.

**Institutional authorship landscape:** Unlike Selank, where the developer-network concentration was through IMG RAS and the Zakusov Institute, the Russian Cerebrolysin literature is spread across independent Russian academic medical centers — Kazan State Medical University (CEREHETIS program), Mental Health Research Center Moscow (Gavrilova group), Pirogov Russian National Research Medical University, RUDN University, Volgograd State Medical University, and others. However, EVER Neuro Pharma is the European manufacturer with Russia as a major market, and Russian neurological consensus documents (e.g., R4 below) function as expert opinion supporting continued use — the conflict structure is market-economic rather than institutional co-developer.

**Translation provenance note:** *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S S Korsakova* (Journal of Neurology and Psychiatry named for S.S. Korsakov) is a leading Russian-language clinical neurology/psychiatry journal. PubMed indexes English abstracts for recent articles, but full text is in Russian with no systematically published English translation edition. Articles are identified as Russian-language by LA=rus in MEDLINE.

### R1 — Khasanova DR, Kalinin MN. 2023.

**Russian Title (PubMed):** [Эффекты одновременного применения Церебролизина и альтеплазы на геморрагическую трансформацию мозгового инфаркта и функциональные исходы у пациентов с инсультом: CEREHETIS, рандомизированное многоцентровое пилотное исследование]

**English Translation of Title (as given in PubMed):** Effects of simultaneous use of Cerebrolysin and alteplase on hemorrhagic transformation of brain infarction and functional outcome in stroke patients: CEREHETIS, a randomized, multicenter pilot trial

**Journal:** Журнал неврологии и психиатрии им. С.С. Корсакова (*Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S S Korsakova*)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2023; 123(8):60–69

**PMID:** 37682097

**Language:** Russian (PubMed LA field: rus; full text in Russian; English abstract on PubMed only)

**Institutional authorship:** Khasanova DR, Kalinin MN — Kazan State Medical University; Interregional Clinical Diagnostic Center, Kazan, Russia. **Independent Russian academic medical center;** no direct EVER Neuro Pharma co-authorship confirmed in this primary PMID. CEREHETIS is a Russian-initiated pilot trial.

**Study type:** Primary clinical study (randomized multicenter pilot RCT)

**Study population:** Acute ischemic stroke patients in Russia; Cerebrolysin + alteplase (n=126) vs. alteplase alone (n=215); Russian clinical centers. Human clinical.

**Key findings:** Simultaneous Cerebrolysin + alteplase (thrombolysis) "resulted in a significant reduction of the symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation (HT) rate" vs. alteplase alone; no serious adverse events with Cerebrolysin; early neurological improvement observed at day 14; functional outcome trend at day 90 did not reach statistical significance. This is the primary Russian-language publication of the CEREHETIS pilot program — a Russian-center-led trial distinct from the EVER-Pharma-sponsored international trials.

**Population note:** Human, acute ischemic stroke patients, Russian centers.

**Tag:** [R1, rct]

**Tier:** 3 (Russian-language RCT; single country; pilot design; small n for the combined endpoint; thrombolysis context; Russian academic authorship without confirmed manufacturer co-authorship is a relative strength, but the drug is commercially registered in Russia which creates a market-incentive concentration)

**Translation provenance:** English abstract on PubMed; full article in Russian; *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* has no published English translation edition.

### Russian landscape summary

**Databases searched:** PubMed eutils (term: cerebrolysin[tiab] AND russian[la]): **154 results total** (first and second pages retrieved = 40 PMIDs; a sample spanning 2018–2026); eLibrary.ru (access blocked from session; Google site-search supplemented); CyberLeninka (search for «Церебролизин» — page returned no results in the accessible format; PubMed LA filter provided equivalent coverage for PMID-bearing items).

**Search terms (Russian-character):** «Церебролизин», «Церебролизин инсульт рандомизированное», «Церебролизин деменция», «Церебролизин CEREHETIS»; PubMed: "cerebrolysin"[tiab] AND russian[la]; "Khasanova"[au]; "Kalinin MN"[au]; "Gavrilova SI"[au].

**Key landscape finding:** The Russian-language corpus is large (154 PubMed-indexed items alone, likely many more in eLibrary not accessible), concentrated in *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S S Korsakova*, and spans primary RCTs (CEREHETIS program, R1–R3), prospective clinical studies (R5, MCI-to-dementia conversion), animal model pharmacology (R6), systematic reviews (R7), and expert consensus documents (R4). The CEREHETIS program (Khasanova/Kalinin, Kazan) is the most productive independent-Russian primary trial line. The Gavrilova/Selezneva group at the Mental Health Research Center represents the Alzheimer's disease prevention literature. Notably, this literature is almost entirely OUTSIDE the Cochrane-included English corpus — the 2023 Cochrane stroke review's 7 included RCTs are primarily international (non-Russian-language) trials. The Russian-language primaries are neither contradicted nor corroborated by Cochrane because they are largely not the same dataset.

**Concentration and concentration-caveat note:** Russia is Cerebrolysin's largest (or co-largest with China) commercial market. Russian neurological consensus strongly endorses Cerebrolysin use (R4), and the *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* journal is the institutional home of Russian neurology's Cerebrolysin literature. The CEREHETIS program shows what appears to be an independent Russian academic initiative (Kazan University), but the commercial incentive structure — Cerebrolysin is a major prescription drug in Russia, with the distributor and clinical community both invested in continued use — constitutes a market-derived concentration that parallels the manufacturer-concentration concern in the English literature. The Russian literature is voluminous and reinforces the existence of a clinical effect in stroke and dementia contexts, but does not independently resolve the concentration concern. A "many Russian primaries exist BUT they reinforce rather than resolve the concentration concern" finding is the honest result.

Additional search for the Chinese drug name 施普善 (Cerebrolysin brand name in China) and 脑蛋白水解物 (cerebroprotein hydrolysate) with chinese[la] filter returned the PubMed-indexed Chinese-language corpus. **Important landscape note on Chinese domestic literature:** Cerebrolysin (marketed as 施普善, Shìpǔshàn) holds national marketing authorization in China (CFDA/NMPA-registered). A Chinese domestic generic equivalent — 脑蛋白水解物注射液 (cerebroprotein hydrolysate injection) — has been marketed since 1997 and is produced by Chinese manufacturers. Chinese-language RCTs exist in CNKI for both the Austrian original and domestic Chinese products. A 2005 systematic review in *循证医学* (Journal of Evidence-Based Medicine) evaluated 6 RCTs (n=802) of Cerebrolysin for Alzheimer's disease from Chinese and international sources (jebm.cn; identified via Google Scholar). Direct CNKI access was blocked from this session, preventing full bibliographic verification of the CNKI corpus. The Chinese-language literature exists, is non-trivial, and overlaps with the concentration concern: Chinese trials are conducted in a market where the drug is authorized and used, meaning the commercial incentive structure mirrors the Russian context.

**Chinese Title:** Analysis of the content of ten kinds of metal elements in cerebrolysin by atomic absorption spectrophotometry (Chinese-language original)

**Journal:** 光谱学与光谱分析 (*Guang Pu Xue Yu Guang Pu Fen Xi* / Spectroscopy and Spectral Analysis)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2001; 21(3):397–399

**PMID:** 12947679

**Language:** Chinese (PubMed LA field: chi; confirmed via efetch; "[Article in Chinese]")

**Institutional authorship:** Liu W, Leng H, Zhu Z, Chen G — Jilin Military Medical College; Fourth Military Medical University, Jilin, China. **Military medical university authorship — independent of EVER Neuro Pharma; no manufacturer co-authorship.**

**Study type:** Analytical chemistry / pharmaceutical quality study (not a clinical RCT; flame atomic absorption spectrophotometry analysis of metal element content in 8 Cerebrolysin formulations)

**Study population:** Not applicable (in vitro pharmaceutical analysis of product samples).

**Key findings:** "Significant difference" in trace metal element composition (Mg, K, Ca, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn) between seven Chinese-manufactured cerebroprotein hydrolysate products and the Austrian-manufactured Cerebrolysin original. **Tier:** 3 (Chinese-language pharmaceutical analysis study; independent military medical university authorship; in vitro analytical method; not a clinical efficacy study; PMID-bearing and directly verified)

**Translation provenance:** Abstract on PubMed (English summary available via NCBI); full article in Chinese; *Guang Pu Xue Yu Guang Pu Fen Xi* is a Chinese-language spectroscopy journal with no English translation edition.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**Russian (7 admissible primaries selected: R1–R7; out of 154 PubMed-indexed items total):** The Russian layer is large, active (entries through 2026), and covers both the CEREHETIS RCT program (R1–R3; pilot-scale Russian-led randomized trial; stroke outcome) and longer-term prospective clinical studies (R5; 3-year MCI-to-dementia conversion). It provides:
- The largest acute-stroke RCT program conducted in Russian-language format (CEREHETIS; Khasanova/Kalinin, Kazan; R1–R3)
- A 3-year MCI-to-dementia conversion study from the Gavrilova Mental Health Research Center group (R5)
- A three-way comparative antioxidant pharmacology study in rats (R6; Russian competitive landscape: Cerebrolysin vs. Cortexin vs. Actovegin — a uniquely Russian-market context)
- The dominant Russian expert consensus position (R4; *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* 2023)
- The most recent Russian-language systematic review of post-stroke rehabilitation evidence (R7; 2026)

This literature is substantially OUTSIDE the Cochrane-included English corpus and adds a Russian-center trial program (CEREHETIS) not included in the 2023 Cochrane review. However, the Russian literature operates under the same concentration concern: Russia is the drug's major market; Russian neurological consensus strongly endorses it; commercial incentives align with positive publication. The Russian literature does not resolve the independent-evidence gap — it expands the quantity of evidence while deepening the geographic and market-incentive concentration.

**Chinese (2 verified + larger CNKI corpus inferred):** The PubMed-verified Chinese literature is small (2 PMID-bearing items; C1 and C2). C1 is a significant pharmaceutical quality finding: Chinese domestic generic products have measurably different elemental profiles from the Austrian original — a result not available in the English literature and directly relevant to the generalizability concern. C2 documents Cerebrolysin as standard neurotrophic background care in Chinese ICUs. The CNKI corpus (not directly verified) appears to contain Chinese-language RCTs and systematic reviews confirming the existence of a Chinese-domestic clinical research base. This literature exists and is not absent; it carries the same geographic concentration concern (Chinese authorization market) and adds the additional variable of Chinese domestic generic products with compositional differences.

**German (5 selected primaries G1–G5; 13 total PubMed-indexed):** The German/Austrian literature is the oldest and is the manufacturer-origin literature. G1–G2 (Windisch 1985, *Arzneimittelforschung*) are the foundational animal pharmacology studies from an EVER Pharma-affiliated researcher. G3 (Kofler/Harrer 1990) is the earliest clinical RCT from the inventor's institution. G4 (Koppi/Barolin 1996) is an Austrian non-randomized comparative study in stroke. G5 (Biesenbach et al. 1997) is the only genuinely independent Austrian controlled trial (small n=20, neuropathy). The Austrian national Fachinformation (SmPC) is the primary German-language regulatory document and authorizes Cerebrolysin for three indications in Austria. No current German-language clinical literature exists (last PubMed-indexed German entry is 1997); German-language neurology has effectively moved to English-language publication.

**Net honest assessment:** The three non-English layers together are voluminous (154+ Russian items, non-trivial Chinese CNKI corpus, 13 German items) but they reinforce rather than resolve the independent-evidence gap. They confirm: (a) a large, active Russian-language clinical literature concentrated in a major commercial market; (b) a Chinese domestic market with domestic generic products that differ compositionally from the Austrian original; (c) a German/Austrian early-era founder-institution literature that is the manufacturer's original evidence base. None of these layers provides evidence from a fully independent research community outside the drug's commercial geography and institutional sponsorship network.
