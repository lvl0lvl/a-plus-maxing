# MK-677 (Ibutamoren) — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how MK-677 is actually dosed, sourced, and formulated in practice* — the dose/route/cycle/formulation conventions that circulate among gray-market vendors, telehealth pages, and bodybuilding/anti-aging communities. It is **not** an efficacy document and **must not** be read as one. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (3 — none found), and `vendor_label` (4) sources are admissible **only** for dose/route/cycle/formulation conventions, availability, and cost. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy or adverse-event-rate claim, and none appears here for that purpose. All efficacy / AE-rate magnitudes (the actual glucose/HbA1c change, the terminated hip-fracture CHF signal, the FFM gain) are **Tier-1 claims that live in the research report §5** and are NOT grounded here.
>
> **Type discipline, read first.** MK-677 is an **oral, non-peptide small molecule** ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist — taken **by mouth, once daily**. It is **NOT** an injectable peptide secretagogue (e.g., ipamorelin / CJC-1295 / sermorelin). The conventions below **must not be cross-attributed** to those injectables, and nothing here implies an injectable route.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** MK-677 is **not FDA-approved for any indication** and is **not a lawful dietary ingredient** (FDA enforced this with Dec-2025 warning letters to Prime Sports Nutrition and Agebox Inc.). It is **not a peptide** and therefore is **not on the FDA §503A bulk drug substances list** and is **not part of the April-2026 §503A peptide compounding action (FR Doc 2026-07361)**. In practice it is sold gray-market as a "research chemical" / "for research use only," sometimes mislabeled with a "Supplement Facts" panel. The "prescribing practice" described here is a real but legally unsettled gray-market / telehealth / vendor-research practice, not an approved standard of care.

## Dose / route conventions

MK-677 is dosed **orally, once daily**; the dominant practical convention is **~10–25 mg/day**:

- **25 mg/day** — the clinical-reference dose carried into practice (the dose used in the published trials) [1, vendor_label/usage].
- **~10–15 mg/day** — for anti-aging / general-wellness use, practice pages converge on these lower doses as "sufficient," reserving 25 mg for body-composition goals [1, vendor_label/usage][2, vendor_label/usage].
- **Timing.** Because once-daily dosing sustains IGF-1 around the clock (the molecule's elimination half-life is ~4–6 h, but its pharmacodynamic GH/IGF-1 effect persists ~24 h — see report §1.5; do not confuse the two), timing is flexible. Many users dose **at night** to align with the nocturnal GH pulse and to push the appetite/somnolence side effects into sleep; others split the dose. The practical trade-off flagged in practice: night dosing can worsen morning grogginess / water retention and the appetite stimulation [1, usage][2, usage].
- **Cycle length.** Practice conventions cluster at **8–16 weeks on**, sometimes with a 4–8-week break, or continuous low-dose (~10 mg) with periodic labs; a first cycle is often described as 10 mg/day for 8–12 weeks [2, usage][3, usage].

> **These are gray-market conventions, not therapeutic doses.** Every figure here is a *convention* extrapolated from the trial dose and vendor/community usage, not an established prescribing standard. As an **oral small molecule**, none of it is cross-attributed to injectable secretagogues.

**The glucose-monitoring practical caveat (the dominant practitioner caution).** Practice guidance is to obtain **baseline fasting glucose, HbA1c, and IGF-1**, recheck glucose metabolism at ~4–8 weeks and periodically thereafter, and to treat pre-diabetics, high-BMI, edema-prone, or cardiovascular-risk individuals as poor or closely-monitored candidates [3, practitioner caution][4, practitioner caution]. **The underlying metabolic magnitudes** (the actual glucose/HbA1c change and the terminated elderly hip-fracture CHF signal) are **Tier-1 claims cited in the research report §5** and are explicitly NOT grounded on any source here.

## Compounding data sheets (compounding_data_sheet) — gate result

**Gate finding: NEGATIVE — no compounding data sheet located, and none can lawfully exist for human use.** MK-677 is an **unapproved small-molecule drug, not a peptide**, and it is **not on the FDA §503A bulk drug substances list**, so **US compounding pharmacies cannot legally compound or dispense it for human consumption**. A compounding-pharmacy aggregator states explicitly: "MK-677 (Ibutamoren) is not FDA-approved and is not on the FDA 503A bulk substances list, so US compounding pharmacies cannot legally dispense it for human consumption," and routes no requests for it [5, regulatory/availability]. This contrasts with the injectable peptide secretagogues, several of which historically circulated through 503A pharmacies before the April-2026 Category-2 actions [6, regulatory].

- **No Empower / Belmar / Tailormade / Hallandale / Strive compounding data sheet for MK-677 was located** — consistent with the gate result.
- Some gray-market/telehealth marketing pages assert MK-677 "is available through 503A compounding pharmacies with a prescription," but this **conflicts with the FDA list and with FDA enforcement**; the affirmative claim is treated as unreliable vendor marketing [7, vendor_label].

**Result: explicit absence — no admissible compounding data sheet.** Practice is **gray-market "research chemical" only.**

## Reconstitution / formulation notes

**Reconstitution is N/A — MK-677 is ORAL.** It is not an injectable peptide and there is no lyophilized-vial / bacteriostatic-water reconstitution math to record. Gray-market dose forms are:
- **Oral capsules** (e.g., 10 / 12.5 / 25 mg) [7, vendor_label][1, vendor_label].
- **Oral liquid / solution** (dropper, mg-per-mL) [7, vendor_label][1, vendor_label].

This is route/strength bookkeeping only and carries **no efficacy or safety implication**. Identity/purity of gray-market product is self-reported and not buyer-auditable.

## Cost (qualitative)

Gray-market oral MK-677 is **cheap**: vendor pricing is roughly **$20–$80 for a 30-day supply** (≈$20–$40/mo at 15 mg/day; $30–$80/mo at 25 mg/day). "Clinic/telehealth-administered" programs are quoted far higher (~$150–$400+/mo), reflecting supervision/labs rather than drug cost — and given the compounding gate above, the legitimacy of any "compounded MK-677" pathway is doubtful [3, vendor_label/cost][8, vendor_label/cost].
