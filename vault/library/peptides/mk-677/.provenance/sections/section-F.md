## Section F — Practitioner-practice + non-English

**Compound:** MK-677 (Ibutamoren / ibutamoren mesylate), an **oral, non-peptide small molecule** ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist. Note the type discipline up front: MK-677 is taken **by mouth, once daily** and is NOT an injectable peptide secretagogue (e.g., ipamorelin/CJC-1295/sermorelin). Practitioner conventions below must not be cross-attributed to those injectables. All efficacy / AE-rate magnitudes in this section are deferred to Section D (Tier-1 clinical literature); practitioner, vendor, and anecdote sources here are used ONLY for usage conventions, availability, and cost — never to ground efficacy or adverse-event rates.

### Prescribing-practice layer

**Compounding gate result — NEGATIVE (no compounding data sheets located).** MK-677 is an unapproved small-molecule drug, not a peptide, and it is **not on the FDA 503A bulk drug substances list**, so US compounding pharmacies cannot legally compound or dispense it for human use. A compounding-pharmacy aggregator explicitly states: "MK-677 (Ibutamoren) is not FDA-approved and is not on the FDA 503A bulk substances list, so US compounding pharmacies cannot legally dispense it for human consumption" and routes no requests for it [1] (`regulatory`/availability). This contrasts with the injectable peptide secretagogues, several of which historically circulated through 503A pharmacies before the April 2026 Category-2 actions [2] (`regulatory`). I located **no Empower / Belmar / Tailormade / Hallandale / Strive compounding data sheet for MK-677** — consistent with the gate result. Some gray-market/telehealth marketing pages assert MK-677 "is available through 503A compounding pharmacies with a prescription," but this conflicts with the FDA list and with FDA enforcement (see below); I treat the affirmative claim as unreliable vendor marketing [3] (`vendor_label`).

**Regulatory status (US government sources).** The DoD Operation Supplement Safety (OPSS) program states MK-677 is "not approved for human use, which makes it an unapproved drug," is "not legal for use as an ingredient in dietary supplements or any other consumer or commercial products," is **prohibited for military members**, and is **on the WADA prohibited list** (banned in sport) [4] (`regulatory`). FDA enforcement is active: on 19 Dec 2025 the FDA issued a warning letter to **Agebox Inc.** for marketing a children's growth supplement containing undeclared ibutamoren mesylate, reiterating that ibutamoren "has never been approved by FDA for any use" and is excluded from the legal definition of a dietary supplement [5] (`regulatory`). In practice MK-677 is sold gray-market as a "research chemical" / "for research use only," sometimes mislabeled with a "Supplement Facts" or "Research Facts" panel [5] (`regulatory`/`vendor_label`).

**Dosing conventions (bodybuilding / anti-aging gray-market practice — not a named clinical protocol).** The dominant practical convention is **~10–25 mg orally, once daily**:
- Clinical-reference dose carried into practice is **25 mg/day** (the dose used in the published trials) [6] (`vendor_label`/usage).
- For anti-aging / general-wellness use, practice pages converge on **lower doses (~10–15 mg/day)** as "sufficient," reserving 25 mg for body-composition goals [6][7] (`vendor_label`/usage).
- **Timing:** because once-daily dosing sustains IGF-1 around the clock (the molecule's elimination half-life is ~4–6 h, but its pharmacodynamic GH/IGF-1 effect persists ~24 h — see §A; do not confuse the two), timing is flexible; many users dose **at night** to align with the nocturnal GH pulse and to push the appetite/somnolence side effects into sleep; others split the dose. Note the practical trade-off flagged in practice — night dosing can worsen morning grogginess/water retention and the appetite stimulation [6][7] (usage).
- **Cycle length:** practice conventions cluster at **8–16 weeks on**, sometimes with a 4–8 week break, or continuous low-dose (~10 mg) with periodic labs; a first cycle is often described as 10 mg/day for 8–12 weeks [7][8] (usage).
- These are gray-market conventions. **No named-prescriber protocol with name + venue + date was located** for MK-677 (e.g., no Seeds Scientific / A4M / IFM named-clinician MK-677 protocol surfaced). Telehealth pages describe a generic clinician workflow — baseline labs, prescription, monitoring — but attach **no attributable prescriber name/venue/date**, so they do not qualify as `practitioner_protocol` cites; they are reported as `vendor_label`/usage only [8].

**The glucose-monitoring practical caveat.** The dominant practitioner caution is **glucose/insulin monitoring**: MK-677 raises fasting glucose and HbA1c and reduces insulin sensitivity, so practice guidance is to obtain **baseline fasting glucose, HbA1c, and IGF-1**, recheck glucose metabolism at ~4–8 weeks and periodically thereafter, and to treat pre-diabetics, high-BMI, edema-prone, or cardiovascular-risk individuals as poor or closely-monitored candidates [8][9] (practitioner caution). The underlying metabolic magnitude (the actual glucose/HbA1c change and the terminated elderly hip-fracture CHF signal) is a **Tier-1 claim cited in Section D** and is NOT grounded here.

**Dose forms & reconstitution.** Reconstitution is **N/A — MK-677 is oral.** Gray-market dose forms are **oral capsules** (e.g., 10/12.5/25 mg) and **oral liquid/solution** (dropper, mg-per-mL) [3][6] (`vendor_label`).

**Cost (qualitative).** Gray-market oral MK-677 is **cheap**: vendor pricing is roughly **$20–$80 for a 30-day supply** (≈$20–$40/mo at 15 mg/day; $30–$80/mo at 25 mg/day). "Clinic/telehealth-administered" programs are quoted far higher (~$150–$400+/mo), reflecting supervision/labs rather than drug cost — and given the compounding gate above, the legitimacy of the "compounded MK-677" pathway is doubtful [8][10] (`vendor_label`/cost).

### Non-English literature

The honest result: **most non-English MK-677 material is gray-market/encyclopedic (bodybuilding wikis, vendor shops, translated SARM blogs), not independent non-English-language primary research.** I distinguish below.

**Japanese.** The most substantive Japanese-indexed item is a **Japanese-language patent, JP5336349B2**, "成長ホルモン分泌促進物質による筋肉減少症の処置方法" ("Method for treating sarcopenia with a growth hormone secretagogue"), assignee **Michael O. Thorner**, granted 2013-11-06. It claims oral GH-secretagogue (MK-677) administration in healthy elderly subjects for sarcopenia/lean-mass maintenance [11] (`regulatory`/patent — primary document, Japanese-language). A Japanese PubMed-abstract aggregator (bibgraph) also surfaces the Murphy et al. obese-subjects lipoprotein study in Japanese translation — this is an **English-published primary indexed/translated in Japanese**, NOT a Japanese-language primary [12] (`secondary`, translation). Otherwise Japanese results are vendor/note-blog tier (e.g., note.com, SARM shops) and do not ground claims.

**Chinese.** No CNKI/Wanfang independent clinical primary located. Chinese-language results are reagent-catalog and encyclopedic: MedChemExpress / chemical databases list ibutamoren mesylate (伊布莫仑甲磺酸盐, CAS 159752-10-0) as a "research-use-only" GHSR agonist with the standard oral GH/IGF-1 mechanism, and a Zhihu (知乎) explainer ("SARMS系列（一）—MK-677") repeats the convention that it raises GH/IGF-1 without raising cortisol [13] (`vendor_label`/reagent). These corroborate the mechanism/type framing but are not independent efficacy evidence.

**Russian.** No eLibrary/CyberLeninka independent clinical primary located. Russian results are bodybuilding-encyclopedia and shop tier — SportWiki ("Ибутаморен"), 2gym, kulturist1, and product listings for "Frog Tech Platinum Ibutamoren MK-677 25 mg" capsules [14] (`vendor_label`). These restate the same gray-market convention (15–30 mg/day, 10–12 week courses, oral capsules) but **must not ground efficacy/AE numbers**; note SportWiki is a wiki and is treated as non-grounding (no-Wikipedia spirit).

**Non-English bottom line:** **No independent non-English-LANGUAGE clinical primary located** (Russian/Chinese/Japanese). The only non-English-language primary *document* is the Japanese patent JP5336349B2; everything else non-English is either an English-published primary translated/indexed abroad, a reagent catalog, or a bodybuilding wiki/vendor shop. This is reported honestly as "none located" for non-English primary research — not fabricated.
