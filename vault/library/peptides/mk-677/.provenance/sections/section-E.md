## Section E — Regulatory & sport status

**Compound:** MK-677 (ibutamoren; Merck development code MK-0677; also L-163,191). An **orally active, non-peptide small molecule** that acts as a ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist / growth-hormone secretagogue. This small-molecule identity is the load-bearing distinction for this section: MK-677 is governed by drug law as an unapproved drug, but it is **not** a peptide and therefore does **not** sit inside the §503A peptide-compounding framework that captures BPC-157, TB-500, and the other peptides (see §503A subsection below). [type-tag: `regulatory`]

### FDA / EMA approval status — never approved anywhere

MK-677 has **never been approved by the FDA (or EMA) for any indication**, in the United States or elsewhere. It was developed by Merck Research Laboratories (as MK-0677) and advanced through Phase II clinical investigation for indications including growth-hormone deficiency, frailty/sarcopenia in the elderly, and Alzheimer's-associated indications, but Merck **discontinued clinical development** and the compound was never submitted for or granted marketing approval. [1][2] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

The candidate has since changed hands and indication: a successor program licensed the molecule and renamed it (LUM-201) for pediatric growth-hormone deficiency, but this remains an **unapproved investigational drug** — no approved finished drug product containing ibutamoren mesylate exists. [1] [type-tag: `regulatory`] The U.S. Department of Defense's Operation Supplement Safety (OPSS, published by the Uniformed Services University CHAMP) states plainly that MK-677 "is not approved for human use, making it an unapproved drug." [2] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

**Bottom line:** Unapproved everywhere. There is no FDA- or EMA-approved MK-677 product. [type-tag: `regulatory`]

### Dietary-supplement / sports-nutrition status — NOT a lawful dietary ingredient

MK-677 is **not a lawful dietary ingredient** and may not legally be sold as (or in) a dietary supplement. Two independent legal exclusions apply:

1. **The drug-exclusion (IND-preclusion) clause.** Because ibutamoren was authorized for investigation as a new drug and was the subject of substantial clinical investigations that were made public *before* it was marketed as a food/supplement, it is excluded from the statutory definition of a "dietary ingredient/dietary supplement" under FD&C Act §201(ff)(3)(B). A product marketed as a supplement that contains it is therefore **adulterated**. [2][3] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

2. **Unapproved-new-drug status.** Independently, because the products make drug-like (GH/IGF-1-raising) claims and contain an unapproved active, FDA treats them as **unapproved new drugs**. [3][4] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

FDA has **actually enforced** this position. In December 2025 the agency issued warning letters to firms selling MK-677:

- **Prime Sports Nutrition (719433, dated 2025-12-12):** FDA cited products marketed as "MK-677" / "Ibutamoren" as **unapproved new drugs** under FD&C Act §505(a); introducing them into interstate commerce violates §§301(d) and 505(a). [3] [type-tag: `regulatory`]
- **Agebox Inc. (718252, dated 2025-12-19):** FDA found two "iKids-Growth IGF-1 Support" children's products contained **undeclared ibutamoren mesylate** by laboratory analysis; the agency deemed them unapproved new drugs in violation of §§505(a) and 301(d). [4] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

OPSS/DoD corroborates that MK-677 "is also not legal for use as an ingredient in dietary supplements or any other consumer or commercial products," and lists it on the **DoD Prohibited Dietary Supplement Ingredients List**, noting vendors deceptively label it with "Supplement Facts" panels or as "Research Use Only" chemicals. [2] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

**Gray-market reality:** despite this, MK-677 is widely sold online as a "research chemical" / "not for human consumption" liquid or capsule, often alongside SARMs. This is gray-market commerce that does not confer any legal supplement or drug status. [2][3] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

### §503A peptide-bulks list / April-2026 Federal Register action — DOES NOT apply to MK-677

This must be stated precisely to avoid conflation. In 2026 FDA opened a §503A bulk-drug-substances compounding process specifically for a set of **peptides**, and on **April 16, 2026** published the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) meeting notice — **FR Doc 2026-07361** (91 FR 20465; docket FDA-2025-N-6895) — listing the bulk substances the committee would consider. [5] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

The substances on that action are peptides only. Enumerated list (the "N" tally) of bulk substances in FR Doc 2026-07361, all **peptides**:

1. BPC-157 (free base / acetate)
2. KPV (free base / acetate)
3. TB-500 (free base / acetate)
4. MOTS-c (free base / acetate)
5. Emideltide / DSIP (free base / acetate)
6. Semax (free base / acetate)
7. Epitalon (free base / acetate)

(A further peptide cohort — e.g., GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, LL-37/cathelicidin, dihexa, PEG-MGF — is slated for a later PCAC session, and 12 peptides were removed from Category 2; these are likewise peptides.) [5][6] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

**Ibutamoren / MK-677 does NOT appear anywhere in FR Doc 2026-07361, and it is not a peptide.** [5] [type-tag: `regulatory`] The §503A *peptide*-bulks framework and the April-2026 peptide action therefore **do not apply to MK-677 the way they apply to BPC-157, TB-500, etc.** MK-677 is not in scope of that compounding pathway. This does **not** make MK-677 more permissible — it remains an unapproved drug and a non-lawful dietary ingredient regardless of the peptide proceeding. The two issues are simply distinct regulatory tracks: peptide compounding (§503A bulks) versus an unapproved small-molecule drug being sold illegally as a supplement/research chemical. [3][4][5] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

### WADA — prohibited at all times (Class S2, growth-hormone secretagogues)

Ibutamoren / MK-677 is **prohibited at all times (in- and out-of-competition)** under the World Anti-Doping Code Prohibited List, within **Section S2 — Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics**, specifically the **growth-hormone secretagogue (GHS) / ghrelin-mimetic** subcategory (S2.2 — GH and its releasing factors; the GHS group). [7][8] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

Anti-doping authorities name ibutamoren explicitly in this class. The independent national anti-doping organization Sport Integrity Australia (an Australian Government body) classifies "Ibutamoren (MK-677)" under **S2 Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics — Prohibited at all times**, citing the WADA Prohibited List. [7] [type-tag: `regulatory`] OPSS/DoD likewise states MK-677 "is on … the World Anti-Doping Agency Prohibited List." [2] [type-tag: `regulatory`] Other GHS/ghrelin-mimetic examples named alongside it in S2 include anamorelin, capromorelin, ipamorelin, lenomorelin, macimorelin, and tabimorelin. [7][8] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

> **Primary-source flag (self-check #1):** The exact wada-ama.org primary text could not be machine-fetched (WADA's site returned blocked/empty bodies and the PDF would not render through the retrieval tool). The WADA-S2 naming above is therefore anchored to a **government anti-doping primary that quotes the WADA List** (Sport Integrity Australia, a national anti-doping organization) plus a **U.S. DoD primary** (OPSS). This is a load-bearing fact resting on official anti-doping primaries rather than a successfully-opened wada-ama.org page; a reader should confirm the precise S2 subsection number against the live wada-ama.org 2026 Prohibited List. The substantive status (prohibited at all times, S2 GHS) is consistent across all opened sources. [2][7] [type-tag: `regulatory`]

### EU status

There is no EMA marketing authorization for MK-677, and it is not an authorized medicine or a permitted novel-food/supplement ingredient in the EU; as in the U.S., EU-facing sale is gray-market "research chemical" commerce rather than any approved status. (Asserted by analogy from the global never-approved status [1][2]; no EU-primary regulatory document was opened for this specific point, so it is presented as the lower-confidence inference and not as a verified EU-primary fact.) [type-tag: `regulatory`]

### Summary of verified status

- **Approval (FDA/EMA, anywhere):** Never approved. Merck (MK-0677) discontinued; successor (LUM-201) still unapproved/investigational. [1][2]
- **Dietary supplement / sports nutrition:** Not a lawful dietary ingredient; excluded under FD&C §201(ff)(3)(B) and treated as an unapproved new drug (FD&C §505(a)); FDA enforced via Dec-2025 warning letters (Prime Sports Nutrition; Agebox). [2][3][4]
- **§503A peptide bulks / FR Doc 2026-07361 (Apr-2026):** Not applicable — MK-677 is a small molecule and does not appear on the peptide action. [5]
- **WADA:** Prohibited at all times, S2 growth-hormone secretagogue / ghrelin-mimetic; ibutamoren named. [2][7][8]
