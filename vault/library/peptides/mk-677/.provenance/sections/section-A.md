## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### Defining fact

MK-677 (ibutamoren; Merck development codes **MK-0677** and **L-163,191**) is a **non-peptidic, orally-active small molecule** that acts as a potent, long-acting agonist of the **ghrelin receptor / growth-hormone-secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHS-R1a)** `[mechanism_review]` `[1][2]`. It is **not a peptide** and is **not a GHRH analogue** — a distinction that matters mechanically (it signals through a different receptor and pathway than GHRH-class drugs) and pharmacokinetically (it is the only orally-once-daily member of the secretagogue family, because the injectable peptide secretagogues are not orally bioavailable) `[mechanism_review]` `[1][5]`.

### Chemical identity and structural class

MK-677 is a synthetic small molecule of the **spiroindane / spiropiperidine** structural class — built on a spiro[indoline-3,4'-piperidine] core (a **spiroindanyl-piperidine**) bearing an N-methanesulfonamide, with the piperidine nitrogen acylated by an **O-benzyl-D-serine** residue capped with a 2-amino-2-methylpropanamide (α-methylalanine / aminoisobutyryl) group `[mechanism_review]` `[1]`. The canonical record (PubChem CID 178024, free base) gives `[regulatory]` `[7]`:

- IUPAC name: 2-amino-2-methyl-N-[(2R)-1-(1-methylsulfonylspiro[2H-indole-3,4'-piperidine]-1'-yl)-1-oxo-3-phenylmethoxypropan-2-yl]propanamide
- Molecular formula: C27H36N4O5S
- Molecular weight: 528.7 g/mol
- Connectivity SMILES: CC(C)(C(=O)NC(COCC1=CC=CC=C1)C(=O)N2CCC3(CC2)CN(C4=CC=CC=C34)S(=O)(=O)C)N

The compound is most commonly handled and dosed as the **mesylate (methanesulfonate) salt** `[regulatory]` `[7]`.

**Discovery and design lineage.** L-163,191/MK-0677 was reported by **A. A. Patchett, R. P. Nargund, and colleagues at Merck** in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* in 1995 `[mechanism_review]` `[1]`. The Merck program — building on the earlier growth-hormone-releasing-peptide (GHRP) work of **C. Y. Bowers** and **R. G. Smith** — deliberately set out to convert the peptidic GH-releasing peptides (e.g., GHRP-6) into a **non-peptide, metabolically stable, orally active** secretagogue. The spiroindane scaffold was engineered to retain high-affinity engagement of the secretagogue target while resisting the proteolytic degradation that destroys peptide secretagogues in the gut `[mechanism_review]` `[1]`. In the foundational characterization, L-163,191 released GH from rat anterior-pituitary cells in culture with an **EC50 = 1.3 ± 0.09 nM**, and elevated GH in **dogs after oral doses as low as 0.125 mg/kg**, while being **specific** for GH release — no significant change in plasma aldosterone, luteinizing hormone, thyroxine, or prolactin after oral 1 mg/kg, with only modest cortisol increases `[animal: rat pituitary cells, in vitro EC50; dog oral GH response]` `[1]`.

### Mechanism of action

**Receptor target.** MK-677 mimics the action of the endogenous peptide hormone **ghrelin**, which was later identified as the natural ligand for the same receptor `[mechanism_review]` `[6]`. The receptor itself — a class-A, seven-transmembrane G-protein-coupled receptor (GHS-R1a) — was **cloned and identified as the target of the growth-hormone secretagogues** in the landmark 1996 *Science* report by **A. D. Howard and colleagues** `[mechanism_review]` `[2]`. That work established that the GHS-R is a distinct GPCR that is the molecular target of the GHSs and binds the radiolabeled secretagogue (`[35S]`-MK-0677) with high affinity — i.e., MK-0677 acts through its **own receptor**, not the GHRH receptor `[mechanism_review]` `[2]`.

**Signal transduction.** GHS-R1a is most commonly coupled to **Gαq/11**. Agonist binding activates **phospholipase C (PLC)**, which hydrolyzes PIP2 to generate **inositol-1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3)** and diacylglycerol; IP3 mobilizes **intracellular Ca²⁺ stores**, raising cytosolic Ca²⁺, with downstream activation of CREB-type transcriptional signaling `[mechanism_review]` `[6]`. In pituitary **somatotrophs**, this Ca²⁺ rise drives exocytotic **GH release** `[mechanism_review]` `[6]`. This is **mechanistically distinct from GHRH-class drugs**: GHRH analogues act on the **GHRH receptor**, a separate GPCR that signals through the **Gs → adenylate-cyclase → cyclic-AMP → protein-kinase-A** pathway `[mechanism_review]` `[5]`. Because the two receptors and pathways are different, ghrelin-receptor agonists and GHRH analogues are **synergistic** when GH is the endpoint — a key reason injectable practice often pairs a GHRP with a GHRH analogue (see Section on protocols), whereas MK-677 supplies the ghrelin-receptor arm orally `[mechanism_review]` `[5]`.

**Pattern of GH/IGF-1 elevation.** MK-677 amplifies the **physiological pulsatile pattern** of GH secretion rather than imposing a flat supraphysiological surge. In healthy elderly subjects, 25 mg once daily increased mean 24-hour GH concentration by **97 ± 23%**, an effect attributed to **enhancement of pre-existing pulsatile GH secretion** — both **GH pulse height and interpulse (trough/nadir) concentrations rose significantly without a significant change in the number of pulses** `[rct: n=32, healthy elderly 64–81 y]` `[3]`. The downstream consequence is a sustained rise in hepatic **IGF-1**; in the same elderly trial, 25 mg/day **restored serum IGF-1 concentrations into the young-adult range** `[rct]` `[3]`. In normal young men, a 7-day course similarly raised plasma IGF-1 dose-dependently and increased the number of (predominantly low-amplitude) GH pulses, again without disturbing cortisol profiles `[rct: n small, healthy young men]` `[4]`.

### Oral pharmacokinetics — the defining route

The decisive differentiator is **oral activity with once-daily dosing**. MK-677 shows **oral bioavailability >60%** (commonly cited ~60–70%) `[mechanism_review]` `[5]`. The reviewed pharmacology summary tabulates an **elimination (plasma) half-life of ≈4.7 hours** for the molecule itself `[mechanism_review]` `[5]`; preclinical work in dogs likewise places the molecular t½ in the **~4–6 hour** range, consistent with the original spiroindane class being designed for oral absorption rather than for an intrinsically long circulating half-life `[mechanism_review]` `[1][5]`.

An important reconciliation: many secondary/aggregator sources quote a "~24-hour half-life" for MK-677. This figure is **not the elimination half-life of the molecule** (~4.7 h); it is better understood as the **pharmacodynamic duration of effect** — the rise in GH and especially **IGF-1 is sustained across the full 24-hour dosing interval** because IGF-1 turns over slowly and GH pulsatility is amplified throughout the day `[mechanism_review]` `[5]`. Clinically, this is what permits **once-daily oral dosing** despite a short molecular half-life: in the elderly RCT, **once-daily** 25 mg oral MK-677 produced sustained 24-hour GH elevation and young-adult-range IGF-1 over 2–4 weeks `[rct]` `[3]`. (Per type-tag discipline, the >60% bioavailability and 4.7-h half-life figures here are sourced to a peer-reviewed pharmacology review, not to vendor labels or anecdote.)

### Contrast with the injectable peptide secretagogues

The functional point of MK-677 is that it delivers ghrelin-receptor agonism **by mouth, once daily**, whereas the comparable secretagogues are **peptides requiring subcutaneous injection** and acting on one of two distinct receptor systems `[mechanism_review]` `[5][6]`:

1. **GHRPs / ghrelin-receptor agonists (same receptor as MK-677, but peptidic + injectable):** GHRP-6, GHRP-2, hexarelin, and the selective agonist **ipamorelin** all activate **GHS-R1a** via the same Gq/PLC/Ca²⁺ cascade, but as peptides they are not orally bioavailable and are dosed by injection `[mechanism_review]` `[5][6]`.
2. **GHRH analogues (different receptor, also injectable):** **sermorelin**, **CJC-1295**, and **tesamorelin** act on the **GHRH receptor** (Gs/cAMP/PKA), not the ghrelin receptor, and are administered by injection `[mechanism_review]` `[5]`.

Thus MK-677 occupies the ghrelin-receptor arm (like ipamorelin/GHRP) while being the **only orally-once-daily** option, because its non-peptide spiroindane structure confers the oral bioavailability and metabolic stability that the peptide secretagogues lack `[mechanism_review]` `[1][5]`. This combination — oral route, once-daily convenience, amplification of both **pulse amplitude and basal/trough GH**, and sustained 24-hour IGF-1 elevation — is the core identity-level differentiator that distinguishes MK-677 from every injectable peptide secretagogue `[rct; mechanism_review]` `[3][5]`.
