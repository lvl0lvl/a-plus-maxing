## Section D — Safety & adverse effects

**Scope note.** MK-677 (ibutamoren) is an orally active, non-peptide small-molecule ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist — a growth-hormone secretagogue, **not** a peptide. It has never received marketing approval from any regulator, so there is no official drug label, prescribing information, or post-marketing pharmacovigilance dataset. However, unlike most "research" compounds it carries an unusually rich randomized-controlled-trial (RCT) safety record from Merck's 1990s–2000s development program (healthy elderly, obese, Alzheimer's disease, and hip-fracture populations). The adverse-effect profile below is grounded in those trials. The single most important and best-replicated harm is **metabolic: MK-677 raises fasting glucose, lowers insulin sensitivity, and raises HbA1c**, a direct and predictable consequence of sustained GH/IGF-1 elevation. A distinct **congestive-heart-failure (CHF) safety signal** terminated the elderly hip-fracture program and is reported honestly below.

### Metabolic effects — the dominant safety concern

Sustained activation of the GH/IGF-1 axis is intrinsically diabetogenic (GH is a counter-regulatory, insulin-antagonizing hormone), and every controlled MK-677 trial that measured glucose handling has detected this signal.

- In the pivotal 2-year, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 65 healthy adults aged 60–81 (25 mg/day), **fasting blood glucose rose by an average of 0.3 mmol/L (5 mg/dL) in the MK-677 group (P = 0.015) and insulin sensitivity decreased**; the authors explicitly flagged this as a metabolic cost of treatment. *[1] (rct)*
- In the original dose-ranging trial in 32 healthy elderly subjects (64–81 y) given 2/10/25 mg/day, **MK-677 produced a significant rise in fasting glucose, from 5.4 ± 0.3 to 6.8 ± 0.4 mmol/L at 4 weeks (≈97 → 122 mg/dL; P < 0.01 vs. baseline)** — a substantially larger shift than in the long-term trial, indicating dose- and individual-dependent severity. *[2] (rct)*
- In an 8-week trial in 24 obese men aged 18–50 (25 mg/day), **fasting glucose and fasting insulin were unchanged, but an oral glucose tolerance test showed impaired glucose homeostasis at both 2 and 8 weeks** — i.e., the defect surfaces on glucose challenge even when fasting values look normal. *[3] (rct)*
- Narrative and review syntheses of the long-term healthy-older-adult data describe small but statistically significant increases in **HbA1c** accompanying the fasting-glucose rise, consistent with the diabetogenic action of chronic GH excess. *[5] (mechanism_review)*

**Implication for diabetics / pre-diabetics.** Because the drug reliably worsens fasting glucose, insulin sensitivity, and HbA1c, anyone with established type 2 diabetes, impaired fasting glucose, impaired glucose tolerance, or metabolic syndrome is at meaningfully elevated risk of glycemic deterioration. The 4-week elderly data (fasting glucose rising into the frankly diabetic range in some subjects) show this is not merely theoretical. This is a trial-observed effect, not a class extrapolation.

### Other trial-observed adverse effects

- **Increased appetite** — a direct on-target consequence of ghrelin-receptor agonism (ghrelin is the principal orexigenic hunger signal). In the 2-year healthy-elderly trial this was the single most frequent side effect, though it "subsided in a few months." *[1] (rct)*
- **Fluid retention / peripheral edema** — "transient, mild lower-extremity edema" was among the most frequent adverse events in the 2-year trial; GH-mediated sodium and fluid retention is the mechanism and is the plausible link to the cardiac signal below. *[1] (rct)*
- **Arthralgia / myalgia** — "muscle pain" was a frequent adverse event in the 2-year trial; joint and muscle pain are classic GH-excess (acromegaly-like) effects. *[1] (rct)*
- **Cortisol** — in the 2-year healthy-elderly trial cortisol rose modestly (+47 nmol/L, P = 0.020), but short-term trials (the 4-week dose-ranging and the 8-week obese-male trials) found **no significant change in serum or urinary cortisol**, and the authors judged the small long-term cortisol rise too small to explain the glucose increase. The cortisol effect is therefore minor and inconsistent across trials. *[1][2][3] (rct)*
- **Lethargy / fatigue / somnolence** — commonly reported as a class effect of GH secretagogues and noted in aggregated user reports; the controlled trials did not isolate it as a prominent quantified adverse event, so it should be regarded as a probable but not rigorously quantified effect. *[5] (mechanism_review)*

### Congestive heart failure / cardiac signal — reported honestly

A genuine cardiac safety signal emerged in the frail-elderly program. In a **multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase IIb trial in 123 patients ≥60 years recovering from hip fracture (62 MK-0677 25 mg/day vs. 61 placebo), the trial was terminated early because of an excess of congestive heart failure: 4 CHF events (≈6.5%) in the MK-677 arm versus 1 (≈1.7%) in placebo.** The investigators concluded that the IGF-1 rise did not translate into functional benefit and that **MK-0677 has "an unfavorable safety profile in this patient population."** *[4] (rct)*

The mechanistically coherent explanation is GH/IGF-1-driven **sodium and fluid retention** (the same effect that produces the peripheral edema above) precipitating overt failure in elderly patients with reduced cardiac reserve or subclinical cardiac dysfunction. The large 563-patient Alzheimer's-disease RCT (25 mg/day for 12 months) — another elderly, comorbid population — confirmed robust target engagement (serum IGF-1 +60% at 6 weeks, +72.9% at 12 months) but no cognitive benefit; it is the program's other large elderly dataset and is the appropriate companion when judging cardiac/edema risk in older or comorbid users. *[6] (rct)*

**Honest characterization of the CHF signal:** it is a real, trial-derived event imbalance that was serious enough to stop a trial — but it was observed specifically in **frail, acutely-ill elderly hip-fracture patients**, a population predisposed to fluid-overload heart failure. It should not be silently extrapolated as a quantified CHF rate for healthy young users; rather it establishes that MK-677's fluid-retaining, GH-driven physiology can decompensate a vulnerable heart, which is a legitimate contraindication trigger and monitoring rationale.

### Theoretical / class risks (distinguished from trial-observed AEs)

- **Malignancy (IGF-1):** chronically elevated IGF-1 is epidemiologically associated with certain cancers (breast, prostate, colorectal). This is a **theoretical class concern from IGF-1 biology and observational epidemiology, not an adverse event observed in the MK-677 RCTs**, which were too short and small to detect cancer endpoints. *[5] (mechanism_review)*
- **Acromegaly-like soft-tissue/skeletal effects** with very long-term supraphysiologic IGF-1 are a theoretical extrapolation from GH-excess states, not a documented MK-677 trial finding.

### Contraindications, monitoring, and stopping criteria

**Contraindications (relative-to-absolute):**
- **Diabetes mellitus, impaired fasting glucose, or impaired glucose tolerance** — direct, trial-replicated worsening of glycemia. *[1][2][3]*
- **Congestive heart failure or significant cardiac risk / reduced cardiac reserve** — fluid-retention CHF signal in elderly trial. *[4]*
- **Active or recent malignancy** — IGF-1 elevation is a theoretical proliferation risk. *[5]*
- **Pregnancy and lactation** — no human data; should not be used. *[5]*

**Monitoring (primary → secondary):**
- **Fasting glucose + HbA1c** are the primary surveillance labs (baseline and on-treatment); add an OGTT if pre-diabetic, since the obese-male trial showed defects appearing only on glucose challenge. *[1][2][3]*
- **IGF-1** to confirm/limit target engagement and bound the malignancy-relevant exposure. *[2][6]*
- **Clinical surveillance for edema and cardiac decompensation** (weight gain, dyspnea, peripheral edema), particularly in older or cardiac-risk individuals. *[1][4]*

**Stopping criteria:** discontinue for new or worsening edema with weight gain or dyspnea (possible CHF), for a clinically meaningful rise in fasting glucose/HbA1c into or toward the diabetic range, or for any sign of cardiac decompensation. The hip-fracture trial's early termination is the precedent: a CHF signal in a comorbid population warrants immediate cessation. *[1][4]*
