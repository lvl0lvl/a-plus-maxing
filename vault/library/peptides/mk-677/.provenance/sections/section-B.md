## Section B — Long-term human RCT efficacy

**Compound class note:** MK-677 (ibutamoren) is an orally active, non-peptide **small molecule** — a spiropiperidine ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist — not a peptide. It is, by a wide margin, the **most extensively studied growth-hormone (GH) secretagogue in long-term human randomized controlled trials**, with a Merck-led development program spanning healthy elderly, obese, catabolic, osteoporotic, and Alzheimer-disease populations. The central, recurring finding across this entire RCT corpus is a sharp dissociation: MK-677 **durably and reliably raises GH, IGF-1, and fat-free (lean) mass**, but across the longest and best-powered trials this biomarker and body-composition response **failed to translate into improvements in muscle strength, physical function, or any disease-specific clinical endpoint.** The "biomarker/lean-mass increase ≠ functional benefit" distinction is the spine of this section.

### The pivotal long-term trial: Nass 2008 (Annals of Internal Medicine)

The single most important efficacy trial is Nass et al., a **2-year, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, modified-crossover trial** in **65 healthy older adults aged 60–81** (men, women on hormone-replacement therapy, and women off HRT), dosed with **MK-677 25 mg orally once daily** vs placebo `[1]` `(rct; population: healthy community-dwelling elderly, not GH-deficient).`

Biomarker and body-composition results were unambiguously positive: MK-677 increased **IGF-1 into the young-adult range** and increased **fat-free mass by 1.1 kg (95% CI 0.7–1.5 kg)** versus a *decrease* in the placebo group (P < 0.001) `[1]` `(rct).` Limb fat also increased more with MK-677 (1.1 kg vs 0.24 kg; P = 0.001), while there were no significant differences in visceral or total fat `[1]` `(rct).` Crucially, however, the authors state plainly that the **increased fat-free mass did not result in changes in strength or function**, and neither MK-677 nor placebo affected thigh muscle area `[1]` `(rct).` Adverse signals included a small but sustained rise in **fasting glucose (≈0.3 mmol/L) and a decrease in insulin sensitivity**, plus increased appetite, transient mild lower-extremity edema, and muscle pain `[1]` `(rct).`

This is the defining result for the compound: **two years of durable GH/IGF-1 elevation and a real lean-mass gain produced no measurable functional benefit in healthy elderly people**, while measurably worsening glucose handling. The 2-year duration and the modified-crossover design (effects appeared on drug and reversed on withdrawal) make this the strongest single piece of efficacy evidence in either direction.

### Body-composition / biomarker-restoration trials (shorter-term)

**Chapman 1996 (JCEM)** — the foundational dose-finding trial — randomized **32 healthy elderly subjects aged 64–81** (double-blind, placebo-controlled) to placebo or **2, 10, or 25 mg/day** for 14- and 28-day periods `[2]` `(rct; population: healthy elderly).` MK-677 produced a **dose-dependent rise in pulsatile GH** (mean 24-h GH +97 ± 23% at 25 mg) and raised **IGF-1 from 141 ± 21 to 265 ± 29 µg/L at 4 weeks at 25 mg, restoring IGF-1 to the young-adult range** `[2]` `(rct).` Fasting glucose rose (5.4 → 6.8 mmol/L at 4 weeks) and prolactin increased ~23% (remaining within normal range); cortisol was unchanged `[2]` `(rct).` This trial established the 25 mg dose and the IGF-1-restoration claim but was far too short to assess function; it is a **biomarker/mechanism result, not a functional-benefit result.**

**Svensson 1998 (JCEM)** — a **2-month (8-week) double-blind, placebo-controlled trial** in **24 healthy obese men aged ~19–49** (BMI > 30, waist/hip > 0.95), MK-677 25 mg/day vs placebo (n = 12 each) `[3]` `(rct; population: healthy obese young/middle-aged men).` MK-677 produced a **sustained increase in GH, IGF-1, and IGFBP-3**, a **significant increase in fat-free mass** (by DXA, P < 0.01; by four-compartment model, P < 0.05), and a **transient** increase in basal metabolic rate (significant at 2 weeks, not at 8 weeks) `[3]` `(rct).` Again the endpoints are biomarker- and body-composition-level; **no strength or physical-function outcome was demonstrated.**

**Murphy 1998 (JCEM)** — a short-term **catabolism-reversal** crossover study in **8 healthy young volunteers aged 24–39** undergoing caloric restriction (18 kcal/kg/day), receiving **MK-677 25 mg/day or placebo for 7 days** during each diet period `[4]` `(rct; population: healthy young adults, diet-induced catabolism model).` MK-677 **reversed diet-induced nitrogen wasting**: mean nitrogen balance +0.31 ± 0.21 g/day on drug vs −1.48 ± 0.21 g/day on placebo (P < 0.01), with IGF-1 264 ± 31 vs 188 ± 19 ng/mL (P < 0.01) `[4]` `(rct).` This is a **proof-of-concept anabolic-signal result in a metabolic-ward model**, explicitly framed by the authors as hypothesis-generating for catabolic disease — not a demonstrated clinical benefit.

### Bone-turnover and bone-mineral-density trials

**Murphy 1999 (J Bone Miner Res)** — pooled analysis of **three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies (187 elderly adults ≥65)** lasting 2–9 weeks, across healthy elderly and functionally impaired elderly populations, with MK-677 doses of 5–50 mg/day `[5]` `(rct; population: healthy + functionally impaired elderly).` MK-677 raised IGF-1 and **increased biochemical markers of both bone formation and bone resorption** `[5]` `(rct).` This is a **bone-turnover-marker (surrogate) result only**; the short duration precluded any BMD or fracture endpoint.

**Murphy 2001 (JCEM)** — the long-term bone trial: a **multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 18-month study** in **292 postmenopausal osteoporotic women aged 64–85** with low femoral-neck BMD, testing MK-677 25 mg/day, alendronate 10 mg/day, the combination, and placebo (markers over 12 months, BMD over 18 months) `[6]` `(rct; population: postmenopausal osteoporotic women).` MK-677 raised IGF-1 by 39–45% `[6]` `(rct).` The combination increased **femoral-neck BMD (4.2% vs 2.5% for alendronate alone)**, but **no similar additive benefit appeared at the total hip or lumbar spine**, and the authors judged the limited, site-specific gain a concern when weighed against MK-677's metabolic side effects `[6]` `(rct).` Net: even over 18 months, **MK-677 did not deliver a convincing, generalized BMD benefit**, consistent with the biomarker-vs-outcome dissociation.

### Sleep-quality trial

**Copinschi 1997 (Neuroendocrinology)** — a double-blind, placebo-controlled study in **8 young adults (18–30)** and **6 older adults (65–71)** across multiple dosing periods (2–25 mg, 7–14 days) `[7]` `(rct; population: healthy young + older adults).` MK-677 increased **stage IV (slow-wave) sleep by ~50%** and **REM sleep by >20% in young and ~50% in older subjects**, and reduced sleep deviations (42% → 8% on high dose, young group) `[7]` `(rct).` This is a polysomnographic-outcome result; it is genuinely a functional-domain signal but in a **very small, short-term sample**, and it has not been replicated in a large long-term trial.

### The decisive disease-endpoint trial: Sevigny 2008 (Neurology)

The most informative "biomarker-vs-clinical-benefit" trial outside the elderly body-composition setting is Sevigny et al.: a **12-month, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial** in **563 patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer disease** (416 completers), MK-677 25 mg/day vs placebo `[8]` `(rct; population: mild-to-moderate Alzheimer patients).` The drug achieved clear **target engagement — IGF-1 rose 60.1% at 6 weeks and 72.9% at 12 months** — yet there were **no significant differences on ADAS-Cog, CIBIC-plus, ADCS-ADL, or CDR-sob**, and the authors concluded MK-677 was **ineffective at slowing Alzheimer-disease progression** `[8]` `(rct).` This trial is the cleanest demonstration that **MK-677's reliable IGF-1 elevation does not predict clinical efficacy.**

### Synthesis: durable biomarker/lean-mass response, absent functional payoff

Across this enumerated RCT corpus, the pattern is consistent and reproducible:

1. **What MK-677 reliably does** — raises pulsatile GH, restores IGF-1 toward (or to) the young-adult range, durably over up to 24 months, and produces a modest but real increase in **fat-free/lean mass** (≈1.1 kg) plus increases in bone-turnover markers `[1][2][3][5][6][8].`
2. **What MK-677 has not been shown to do** — improve **muscle strength, thigh muscle area, or physical function** (Nass 2008, the longest trial) `[1]`; deliver a generalized **BMD** benefit (Murphy 2001) `[6]`; or alter a **disease endpoint** despite full target engagement (Sevigny 2008, Alzheimer disease) `[8].`
3. **Consistent harm signal** — sustained reductions in **insulin sensitivity** and increases in **fasting glucose** appear across trials `[1][2]`, which must be weighed against any biomarker or body-composition "benefit."

The honest long-term-efficacy verdict is therefore that MK-677 is a **pharmacologically effective GH/IGF-1 secretagogue and a weak lean-mass anabolic, but not a demonstrated functional or clinical therapeutic** in any studied population. The increase in lean mass and biomarkers must not be read as evidence of strength, function, bone-fracture, or disease benefit — the trials that were long and powerful enough to test those outcomes were negative.
