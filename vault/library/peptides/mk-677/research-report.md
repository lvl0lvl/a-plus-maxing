## TL;DR

MK-677 (ibutamoren; Merck MK-0677 / L-163,191) is an **oral, non-peptide small-molecule** ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist — a growth-hormone secretagogue that mimics ghrelin to amplify the body's own pulsatile GH secretion and, downstream, raise IGF-1. Its identity-level differentiator is the **oral, once-daily route**: every comparable secretagogue (ipamorelin/GHRP on the same receptor; sermorelin/CJC-1295/tesamorelin on the separate GHRH receptor) is an **injectable peptide**, whereas MK-677's spiroindane structure confers oral bioavailability and metabolic stability. The pharmacology is genuine and reproducible: it restores IGF-1 toward the young-adult range and produces a modest, real increase in **fat-free (lean) mass (≈+1.1 kg over 2 years)**. But the central finding across the entire long-term RCT corpus is a sharp dissociation — **this biomarker and body-composition response does not translate into strength, physical function, bone-fracture, or any disease-specific clinical benefit.** The longest trial (Nass 2008, 2 years) showed lean mass without strength or function; the two large disease programs — **Alzheimer's (Sevigny 2008, n=563) and hip-fracture recovery (Adunsky 2011)** — both **FAILED** despite full IGF-1 target engagement, and the hip-fracture trial was **stopped early for a congestive-heart-failure signal**. The dominant, best-replicated harm is **metabolic** (raised fasting glucose, reduced insulin sensitivity, raised HbA1c), an intrinsic consequence of chronic GH excess. About **75%** of the human primaries carry a Merck footprint, and no Merck-independent disease-endpoint trial exists. MK-677 is **unapproved everywhere** (Merck discontinued it), is **not a lawful dietary ingredient** (FDA enforced this with Dec-2025 warning letters), sits **outside** the §503A peptide-compounding framework because it is a small molecule (not a peptide), and is **WADA-prohibited at all times**. Net: a pharmacologically effective GH/IGF-1 secretagogue and a weak lean-mass anabolic, but **not a demonstrated functional or clinical therapeutic** — and the only large, well-powered trials that tested hard outcomes were negative or harmful. Treat all human-use framing as off-label experimentation and discuss with a clinician.

## 1. Identity, Structure & Mechanism

### 1.1 The defining fact

MK-677 (ibutamoren; Merck development codes **MK-0677** and **L-163,191**) is a **non-peptidic, orally-active small molecule** that acts as a potent, long-acting agonist of the **ghrelin receptor / growth-hormone-secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHS-R1a)** [1][2] (`mechanism_review`). It is **not a peptide** and is **not a GHRH analogue** — a distinction that matters both **mechanically** (it signals through a different receptor and pathway than GHRH-class drugs) and **pharmacokinetically** (it is the only orally-once-daily member of the secretagogue family, because the injectable peptide secretagogues are not orally bioavailable) [1][5] (`mechanism_review`). This small-molecule identity is also load-bearing for §6 (regulation): MK-677 is governed as an unapproved *drug*, but it is **not** a peptide and therefore does not sit inside the §503A peptide-compounding framework.

### 1.2 Chemical identity and structural class

MK-677 is a synthetic small molecule of the **spiroindane / spiropiperidine** structural class — built on a spiro[indoline-3,4'-piperidine] core (a **spiroindanyl-piperidine**) bearing an N-methanesulfonamide, with the piperidine nitrogen acylated by an **O-benzyl-D-serine** residue capped with a 2-amino-2-methylpropanamide (α-methylalanine / aminoisobutyryl) group [1] (`mechanism_review`). The canonical record (PubChem CID 178024, free base) gives [7] (`regulatory`):

- **IUPAC name:** 2-amino-2-methyl-N-[(2R)-1-(1-methylsulfonylspiro[2H-indole-3,4'-piperidine]-1'-yl)-1-oxo-3-phenylmethoxypropan-2-yl]propanamide
- **Molecular formula:** C27H36N4O5S
- **Molecular weight:** 528.7 g/mol
- **Connectivity SMILES:** CC(C)(C(=O)NC(COCC1=CC=CC=C1)C(=O)N2CCC3(CC2)CN(C4=CC=CC=C34)S(=O)(=O)C)N

The compound is most commonly handled and dosed as the **mesylate (methanesulfonate) salt** (CAS 159752-10-0) [7][35] (`regulatory`).

### 1.3 Discovery and design lineage

L-163,191/MK-0677 was reported by **A. A. Patchett, R. P. Nargund, and colleagues at Merck** in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* in 1995 [1] (`mechanism_review`). Building on the earlier growth-hormone-releasing-peptide (GHRP) work of **C. Y. Bowers** and **R. G. Smith**, the Merck program deliberately set out to convert the peptidic GH-releasing peptides (e.g., GHRP-6) into a **non-peptide, metabolically stable, orally active** secretagogue; the spiroindane scaffold was engineered to retain high-affinity engagement of the secretagogue target while resisting the proteolytic degradation that destroys peptide secretagogues in the gut [1] (`mechanism_review`). In the foundational characterization, L-163,191 released GH from rat anterior-pituitary cells in culture with an **EC50 = 1.3 ± 0.09 nM**, elevated GH in **dogs after oral doses as low as 0.125 mg/kg**, and was **specific** for GH release — no significant change in plasma aldosterone, luteinizing hormone, thyroxine, or prolactin after oral 1 mg/kg, with only modest cortisol increases [1] (`animal`: rat pituitary cells in-vitro EC50; dog oral GH response).

### 1.4 Mechanism of action

**Receptor target.** MK-677 mimics the action of the endogenous peptide hormone **ghrelin**, later identified as the natural ligand for the same receptor [6] (`mechanism_review`). The receptor — a class-A, seven-transmembrane GPCR (GHS-R1a) — was **cloned and identified as the target of the growth-hormone secretagogues** in the landmark 1996 *Science* report by **A. D. Howard and colleagues** [2] (`mechanism_review`), which established that the GHS-R is a distinct GPCR that binds the radiolabeled secretagogue ([35S]-MK-0677) with high affinity — i.e., MK-0677 acts through its **own receptor**, not the GHRH receptor [2] (`mechanism_review`).

**Signal transduction.** GHS-R1a is most commonly coupled to **Gαq/11**. Agonist binding activates **phospholipase C (PLC)**, which hydrolyzes PIP2 to **inositol-1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3)** and diacylglycerol; IP3 mobilizes **intracellular Ca²⁺ stores**, raising cytosolic Ca²⁺ with downstream CREB-type transcriptional signaling [6] (`mechanism_review`). In pituitary **somatotrophs** this Ca²⁺ rise drives exocytotic **GH release** [6]. This is **mechanistically distinct from GHRH-class drugs**, which act on the **GHRH receptor** — a separate GPCR signaling through **Gs → adenylate cyclase → cyclic-AMP → protein-kinase-A** [5] (`mechanism_review`). Because the two receptors and pathways are different, ghrelin-receptor agonists and GHRH analogues are **synergistic** on GH, which is why injectable practice often pairs a GHRP with a GHRH analogue — whereas MK-677 supplies the ghrelin-receptor arm orally [5].

**Pattern of GH/IGF-1 elevation.** MK-677 amplifies the **physiological pulsatile pattern** of GH secretion rather than imposing a flat supraphysiological surge. In healthy elderly subjects, 25 mg once daily increased mean 24-hour GH by **97 ± 23%**, attributed to enhancement of pre-existing pulsatile GH secretion — both **GH pulse height and interpulse (trough/nadir) concentrations rose significantly without a change in the number of pulses** [3] (`rct`, n=32, healthy elderly 64–81 y). The downstream consequence is a sustained rise in hepatic **IGF-1**: in that same elderly trial, 25 mg/day **restored serum IGF-1 into the young-adult range** [3]. In normal young men, a 7-day course raised plasma IGF-1 dose-dependently and increased the number of (predominantly low-amplitude) GH pulses without disturbing cortisol profiles [4] (`rct`, healthy young men).

### 1.5 Oral pharmacokinetics — the defining route, and the half-life that gets misquoted

The decisive differentiator is **oral activity with once-daily dosing**. MK-677 shows **oral bioavailability >60%** (commonly cited ~60–70%) [5] (`mechanism_review`). The reviewed pharmacology summary tabulates an **elimination (plasma) half-life of ≈4.7 hours** for the molecule itself [5]; preclinical dog work likewise places the molecular t½ in the **~4–6 hour** range, consistent with the spiroindane class being designed for oral absorption rather than for an intrinsically long circulating half-life [1][5].

**An important reconciliation that this report carries explicitly:** many secondary/aggregator sources quote a "~24-hour half-life" for MK-677. That figure is **NOT the elimination half-life of the molecule** (~4.7 h); it is the **pharmacodynamic duration of effect** — the rise in GH and especially **IGF-1 is sustained across the full 24-hour dosing interval** because IGF-1 turns over slowly and GH pulsatility is amplified throughout the day [5]. This is what permits **once-daily oral dosing** despite a short molecular half-life: in the elderly RCT, once-daily 25 mg oral MK-677 produced sustained 24-hour GH elevation and young-adult-range IGF-1 over 2–4 weeks [3]. (Per type-tag discipline, the >60% bioavailability and 4.7-h half-life figures are sourced to a peer-reviewed pharmacology review, not to vendor labels or anecdote.)

### 1.6 Contrast with the injectable peptide secretagogues

The functional point of MK-677 is that it delivers ghrelin-receptor agonism **by mouth, once daily**, whereas the comparable secretagogues are **peptides requiring subcutaneous injection** acting on one of two distinct receptor systems [5][6] (`mechanism_review`):

1. **GHRPs / ghrelin-receptor agonists (same receptor as MK-677, but peptidic + injectable):** GHRP-6, GHRP-2, hexarelin, and the selective agonist **ipamorelin** all activate **GHS-R1a** via the same Gq/PLC/Ca²⁺ cascade, but as peptides they are not orally bioavailable and are dosed by injection [5][6].
2. **GHRH analogues (different receptor, also injectable):** **sermorelin**, **CJC-1295**, and **tesamorelin** act on the **GHRH receptor** (Gs/cAMP/PKA), not the ghrelin receptor, and are administered by injection [5].

Thus MK-677 occupies the ghrelin-receptor arm (like ipamorelin/GHRP) while being the **only orally-once-daily** option, because its non-peptide spiroindane structure confers the oral bioavailability and metabolic stability the peptide secretagogues lack [1][5]. This combination — oral route, once-daily convenience, amplification of both **pulse amplitude and basal/trough GH**, and sustained 24-hour IGF-1 elevation — is the core identity-level differentiator distinguishing MK-677 from every injectable peptide secretagogue [3][5].

### 1.7 Coverage gaps (Section 1)

- **Human absorption parameters are review-tabulated, not primary-PK-derived here** — the >60% bioavailability and ~4.7-h t½ come from a peer-reviewed pharmacology review; no full human concentration–time/Cmax/AUC primary was carried into the corpus.
- **Molecular vs PD half-life is the recurring misquote trap** — sources that state "~24 h" almost always mean PD duration, not elimination t½; do not propagate the conflation.
- **No de-novo receptor-binding constant for MK-677-in-humans** — receptor pharmacology rests on the foundational in-vitro/animal and cloning reports.

## 2. Long-term Human RCT Efficacy

**Compound-class note:** MK-677 is an oral, non-peptide **small molecule** — a spiropiperidine GHS-R1a agonist — not a peptide. It is, by a wide margin, the **most extensively studied GH secretagogue in long-term human RCTs**, with a Merck-led program spanning healthy elderly, obese, catabolic, osteoporotic, and Alzheimer-disease populations. The central, recurring finding across this RCT corpus is a sharp dissociation: MK-677 **durably and reliably raises GH, IGF-1, and fat-free (lean) mass**, but across the longest and best-powered trials this biomarker/body-composition response **failed to translate into improvements in muscle strength, physical function, or any disease-specific clinical endpoint.** The **"biomarker/lean-mass increase ≠ functional benefit"** distinction is the spine of this section.

### 2.1 The pivotal long-term trial — Nass 2008 (*Annals of Internal Medicine*)

The single most important efficacy trial is Nass et al.: a **2-year, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, modified-crossover trial** in **65 healthy older adults aged 60–81** (men, women on HRT, and women off HRT), dosed with **MK-677 25 mg orally once daily** vs placebo [8] (`rct`; population: healthy community-dwelling elderly, not GH-deficient).

Biomarker and body-composition results were unambiguously positive: MK-677 increased **IGF-1 into the young-adult range** and increased **fat-free mass by 1.1 kg (95% CI 0.7–1.5 kg)** versus a *decrease* in the placebo group (P < 0.001) [8]. Limb fat also increased more with MK-677 (1.1 kg vs 0.24 kg; P = 0.001), while there were no significant differences in visceral or total fat [8]. Crucially, however, the authors state plainly that the **increased fat-free mass did not result in changes in strength or function**, and neither MK-677 nor placebo affected thigh muscle area [8]. Adverse signals included a small but sustained rise in **fasting glucose (≈0.3 mmol/L) and a decrease in insulin sensitivity**, plus increased appetite, transient mild lower-extremity edema, and muscle pain [8].

This is the defining result for the compound: **two years of durable GH/IGF-1 elevation and a real lean-mass gain produced no measurable functional benefit in healthy elderly people**, while measurably worsening glucose handling. The 2-year duration and the modified-crossover design (effects appeared on drug and reversed on withdrawal) make this the strongest single piece of efficacy evidence in either direction.

### 2.2 Body-composition / biomarker-restoration trials (shorter-term)

**Chapman 1996 (*JCEM*)** — the foundational dose-finding trial — randomized **32 healthy elderly subjects aged 64–81** (double-blind, placebo-controlled) to placebo or **2, 10, or 25 mg/day** for 14- and 28-day periods [3] (`rct`; population: healthy elderly). MK-677 produced a **dose-dependent rise in pulsatile GH** (mean 24-h GH +97 ± 23% at 25 mg) and raised **IGF-1 from 141 ± 21 to 265 ± 29 µg/L at 4 weeks at 25 mg, restoring IGF-1 to the young-adult range** [3]. Fasting glucose rose (5.4 → 6.8 mmol/L at 4 weeks) and prolactin increased ~23% (remaining within normal range); cortisol was unchanged [3]. This established the 25 mg dose and the IGF-1-restoration claim but was far too short to assess function — a **biomarker/mechanism result, not a functional-benefit result.**

**Svensson 1998 (*JCEM*)** — a **2-month (8-week) double-blind, placebo-controlled trial** in **24 healthy obese men aged ~19–49** (BMI > 30, waist/hip > 0.95), MK-677 25 mg/day vs placebo (n = 12 each) [9] (`rct`; population: healthy obese young/middle-aged men). MK-677 produced a **sustained increase in GH, IGF-1, and IGFBP-3**, a **significant increase in fat-free mass** (by DXA, P < 0.01; by four-compartment model, P < 0.05), and a **transient** increase in basal metabolic rate (significant at 2 weeks, not at 8 weeks) [9]. Again the endpoints are biomarker- and body-composition-level; **no strength or physical-function outcome was demonstrated.**

**Murphy 1998 (*JCEM*)** — a short-term **catabolism-reversal** crossover study in **8 healthy young volunteers aged 24–39** undergoing caloric restriction (18 kcal/kg/day), receiving **MK-677 25 mg/day or placebo for 7 days** during each diet period [10] (`rct`; population: healthy young adults, diet-induced catabolism model). MK-677 **reversed diet-induced nitrogen wasting**: mean nitrogen balance +0.31 ± 0.21 g/day on drug vs −1.48 ± 0.21 g/day on placebo (P < 0.01), with IGF-1 264 ± 31 vs 188 ± 19 ng/mL (P < 0.01) [10]. This is a **proof-of-concept anabolic-signal result in a metabolic-ward model**, explicitly framed by the authors as hypothesis-generating for catabolic disease — not a demonstrated clinical benefit.

### 2.3 Bone-turnover and bone-mineral-density trials

**Murphy 1999 (*J Bone Miner Res*)** — pooled analysis of **three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies (187 elderly adults ≥65)** lasting 2–9 weeks, across healthy and functionally impaired elderly populations, MK-677 5–50 mg/day [11] (`rct`; population: healthy + functionally impaired elderly). MK-677 raised IGF-1 and **increased biochemical markers of both bone formation and bone resorption** [11]. This is a **bone-turnover-marker (surrogate) result only**; the short duration precluded any BMD or fracture endpoint.

**Murphy 2001 (*JCEM*)** — the long-term bone trial: a **multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 18-month study** in **292 postmenopausal osteoporotic women aged 64–85** with low femoral-neck BMD, testing MK-677 25 mg/day, alendronate 10 mg/day, the combination, and placebo (markers over 12 months, BMD over 18 months) [12] (`rct`; population: postmenopausal osteoporotic women). MK-677 raised IGF-1 by 39–45% [12]. The combination increased **femoral-neck BMD (4.2% vs 2.5% for alendronate alone)**, but **no similar additive benefit appeared at the total hip or lumbar spine**, and the authors judged the limited, site-specific gain a concern when weighed against MK-677's metabolic side effects [12]. Net: even over 18 months, **MK-677 did not deliver a convincing, generalized BMD benefit** — consistent with the biomarker-vs-outcome dissociation.

### 2.4 Sleep-quality trial

**Copinschi 1997 (*Neuroendocrinology*)** — a double-blind, placebo-controlled study in **8 young adults (18–30)** and **6 older adults (65–71)** across multiple dosing periods (2–25 mg, 7–14 days) [13] (`rct`; population: healthy young + older adults). MK-677 increased **stage IV (slow-wave) sleep by ~50%** and **REM sleep by >20% in young and ~50% in older subjects**, and reduced sleep deviations (42% → 8% on high dose, young group) [13]. This is a polysomnographic-outcome result — a genuine functional-domain signal — but in a **very small, short-term sample**, and it has not been replicated in a large long-term trial.

### 2.5 The decisive disease-endpoint trial — Sevigny 2008 (*Neurology*)

The cleanest "biomarker-vs-clinical-benefit" trial outside the body-composition setting is Sevigny et al.: a **12-month, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial** in **563 patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer disease** (416 completers), MK-677 25 mg/day vs placebo [14] (`rct`; population: mild-to-moderate Alzheimer patients). The drug achieved clear **target engagement — IGF-1 rose 60.1% at 6 weeks and 72.9% at 12 months** — yet there were **no significant differences on ADAS-Cog, CIBIC-plus, ADCS-ADL, or CDR-sob**, and the authors concluded MK-677 was **ineffective at slowing Alzheimer-disease progression** [14]. This trial is the canonical demonstration that **MK-677's reliable IGF-1 elevation does not predict clinical efficacy.** (Its FAILED-program status and the safety implications are developed in §3 and §5.)

### 2.6 Synthesis — durable biomarker/lean-mass response, absent functional payoff

Across this enumerated RCT corpus the pattern is consistent and reproducible:

1. **What MK-677 reliably does** — raises pulsatile GH, restores IGF-1 toward (or to) the young-adult range, durably over up to 24 months, and produces a modest but real increase in **fat-free/lean mass** (≈1.1 kg) plus increases in bone-turnover markers [3][8][9][11][12][14].
2. **What MK-677 has NOT been shown to do** — improve **muscle strength, thigh muscle area, or physical function** (Nass 2008, the longest trial) [8]; deliver a generalized **BMD** benefit (Murphy 2001) [12]; or alter a **disease endpoint** despite full target engagement (Sevigny 2008, Alzheimer disease) [14].
3. **Consistent harm signal** — sustained reductions in **insulin sensitivity** and increases in **fasting glucose** appear across trials [3][8], which must be weighed against any biomarker or body-composition "benefit."

The honest long-term-efficacy verdict: MK-677 is a **pharmacologically effective GH/IGF-1 secretagogue and a weak lean-mass anabolic, but not a demonstrated functional or clinical therapeutic** in any studied population. Lean-mass and biomarker increases must not be read as evidence of strength, function, bone-fracture, or disease benefit — the trials long and powerful enough to test those outcomes were negative.

### 2.7 Coverage gaps (Section 2)

- **No trial demonstrating strength/function gain** — the longest, best-powered functional readout (Nass) was effectively negative on strength and function.
- **Magnitudes are largely sponsor-affiliated and surrogate-level** — see the concentration audit (§4).
- **The sleep signal is small-n and unreplicated** at scale.

## 3. Failed Programs + Other Indications

**Compound-class note:** MK-677 (ibutamoren mesylate; MK-0677; L-163,191) is an oral, **non-peptide small-molecule** GHS-R1a agonist — NOT a peptide. Throughout this section, a rise in serum IGF-1 ("target engagement") is treated as a **biomarker, not a clinical benefit** — the two largest disease programs below demonstrate exactly this dissociation.

**Demarcation legend:** EVIDENCED (positive RCT/controlled human data on a clinical endpoint) · INVESTIGATIONAL (mechanistic / biomarker / small-n signal, no confirmatory endpoint) · **FAILED** (adequately-powered program that missed its primary clinical endpoint and/or was halted for safety) · ABSENT (no admissible human controlled evidence located).

### 3.1 Alzheimer's disease — **FAILED** (primary cognitive endpoint missed despite biomarker engagement)

**Sevigny et al. 2008** [14] (`rct`). Double-blind, multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled (MK-677 Protocol 30 Study Group). Population: **mild-to-moderate Alzheimer disease** (n = 563 randomized; 416 completed 12-month assessments). Intervention: MK-677 25 mg/day vs placebo for **12 months**. Target engagement: serum IGF-1 rose +60.1% at 6 weeks and +72.9% at 12 months. Primary outcome: **NO** significant between-group difference on the co-primary CIBIC-plus or on ADAS-Cog, and none on ADCS-ADL or CDR-sob. Verbatim conclusion: *"Despite evidence of target engagement as indicated by an increase in serum insulin-like growth factor-1, the human growth hormone secretagogue MK-677 25 mg was ineffective at slowing the rate of progression of Alzheimer disease."* The authors further argued that age-related somatotropic decline is **not** in the causal pathway of AD. This is the canonical "biomarker↑ ≠ benefit" failure for MK-677: a large, well-powered Phase 2b/3-scale trial that hit its IGF-1 pharmacodynamic target and still missed every cognitive and functional endpoint. **Status: FAILED.**

### 3.2 Hip-fracture functional recovery — **FAILED** (endpoint missed + safety signal → early termination)

**Adunsky et al. 2011** [15] (`rct`). Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled **Phase IIb** trial. Population: **elderly patients recovering from hip fracture** (n = 123; MK-0677 n = 62, placebo n = 61). Intervention: MK-0677 25 mg/day for up to 24 weeks. Target engagement: plasma IGF-1 rose +51.4 ng/mL vs placebo (95% CI 34.42–68.44; p < 0.001). Primary functional endpoint: **NOT met** — although gait-speed score favored treatment (p = 0.011) and stair-climbing power was numerically higher but non-significant (+12.5 W; 95% CI −10.95 to 35.88; p = 0.292), the prespecified rank analysis showed **no improvement across most functional performance measures**. A secondary falls signal favored treatment (fewer falls; p = 0.096, NS). **Safety:** the trial was **terminated early** for a **congestive-heart-failure safety signal** (reported as ~4/62 [6.5%] on MK-0677 vs ~1/61 [1.7%] placebo — mechanistically consistent with GH-mediated fluid retention unmasking subclinical cardiac dysfunction in a frail geriatric population). Verbatim conclusion: *"…the increase in plasma IGF-1 levels was not paralleled by improvement in most functional performance measures. MK-0677 has an unfavorable safety profile in this patient population."* **Status: FAILED (efficacy) + safety-halted.** Together with the AD result, this program drove Merck's discontinuation of MK-677 as a therapeutic.

### 3.3 Other evidence (each labeled)

**Catabolic states (diet-induced nitrogen wasting) — INVESTIGATIONAL.** **Murphy et al. 1998** [10] (`rct`, crossover). n = 8 healthy volunteers (24–39 y), calorically restricted (18 kcal/kg/day) over two 14-day periods, MK-677 25 mg vs placebo during the final 7 days. Nitrogen balance shifted from catabolic to near-neutral: +0.31 g N/day on MK-677 vs −1.48 g N/day on placebo (p < 0.01); IGF-1 264 ± 31 vs 188 ± 19 ng/mL (p < 0.01). A real reversal of nitrogen wasting, but a **metabolic surrogate in healthy dieters**, not a clinical outcome in a diseased catabolic population — the authors frame it conditionally. **Status: INVESTIGATIONAL.**

**Obesity — INVESTIGATIONAL.** **Svensson et al. 1998** [9] (`rct`). n = 24 obese males, MK-677 25 mg (n = 12) vs placebo (n = 12) for 8 weeks. IGF-1 rose ~40%; fat-free mass increased (sustained) with a transient rise in basal metabolic rate. No fat-loss or hard clinical-outcome endpoint; FFM gain ≠ demonstrated functional or cardiometabolic benefit. **Status: INVESTIGATIONAL.**

**Frailty / sarcopenia of aging — INVESTIGATIONAL (function NOT improved).** **Nass et al. 2008** [8] (`rct`, 2-year modified-crossover). n = 65 healthy older adults (60–81 y), MK-677 25 mg/day. Fat-free mass increased (+1.1 kg vs −0.5 kg placebo; p < 0.001) and GH/IGF-1 were restored toward young-adult levels. **Critically, the increased fat-free mass did NOT translate into improved muscle strength or physical function** — a second clean dissociation between an MK-677 surrogate (lean mass, IGF-1) and the outcome that matters (function). **Status: INVESTIGATIONAL** (anti-frailty *efficacy* unproven; the functional endpoint was effectively negative).

**GH-deficiency diagnostics / GH-axis stimulation — EVIDENCED (as a pharmacologic provocation tool, not as therapy).** **Chapman et al. 1996** [3] (`rct`): n = 32 healthy elderly (64–81 y), MK-677 2/10/25 mg over 14- and 28-day periods; 25 mg/day enhanced pulsatile GH release and restored IGF-1 to young-adult levels (~+97% 24-h mean GH). **Chapman et al. 1997** [16] (`open_label/mechanism`): n = 9 selected GH-deficient adults; oral MK-677 stimulated the GH/IGF-1 axis (IGF-1 +52% at 10 mg, +79% at 50 mg). These establish reliable, dose-dependent GH-axis stimulation (the basis for MK-677's later orphan-drug interest in GH deficiency and its use as an oral GH-provocation/diagnostic agent) — but they evidence **pharmacology, not a treated clinical endpoint.** **Status: EVIDENCED (pharmacodynamic/diagnostic); therapeutic GHD efficacy INVESTIGATIONAL.**

**Slow-wave / REM sleep — INVESTIGATIONAL (small-n polysomnography signal).** **Copinschi et al. 1997** [13] (`rct`, crossover). Young (n = 8, 18–30 y) and older (n = 6, 65–71 y) subjects, 7–14-day periods. High-dose MK-677 produced ~50% increase in stage IV slow-wave sleep and >20% increase in REM in young subjects, and ~50% increase in REM with reduced REM latency in older subjects; sleep abnormalities fell. Real but **very small-n** and a sleep-architecture surrogate, not a validated insomnia/clinical sleep endpoint. **Status: INVESTIGATIONAL.**

**Bodybuilding / athletic-performance efficacy — ABSENT.** No admissible randomized or controlled human trial demonstrating MK-677 improves athletic performance, strength, hypertrophy-for-performance, or recomposition in healthy/trained populations was located. The closest controlled data (Nass [8], Svensson [9]) measured body composition in older/obese non-athletes and showed **lean-mass gain WITHOUT strength or functional gain.** Performance/physique claims rest on vendor copy and anecdote, which are excluded as grounding. **Status: ABSENT.**

### 3.4 Coverage gaps (Section 3)

- **Every hard disease endpoint tested was negative or harmful** — AD and hip-fracture both FAILED; the one large independent functional readout (Nass) showed lean mass without function.
- **No EVIDENCED therapeutic indication** — the only EVIDENCED category is pharmacologic GH-axis stimulation (a diagnostic/provocation property, not a treated clinical outcome).
- **Performance/physique evidence is entirely ABSENT** at the controlled-trial level.

## 5. Safety & Adverse Effects

**Scope note.** MK-677 has **never received marketing approval** from any regulator, so there is no official drug label, prescribing information, or post-marketing pharmacovigilance dataset. But unlike most "research" compounds it carries an unusually rich RCT safety record from Merck's 1990s–2000s program (healthy elderly, obese, Alzheimer's, hip-fracture). The adverse-effect profile below is grounded in those trials. The single most important and best-replicated harm is **metabolic: MK-677 raises fasting glucose, lowers insulin sensitivity, and raises HbA1c**, a direct and predictable consequence of sustained GH/IGF-1 elevation. A distinct **congestive-heart-failure (CHF) signal** terminated the elderly hip-fracture program and is reported honestly below.

### 5.1 Metabolic effects — the dominant safety concern

Sustained activation of the GH/IGF-1 axis is intrinsically diabetogenic (GH is a counter-regulatory, insulin-antagonizing hormone), and every controlled MK-677 trial that measured glucose handling has detected this signal.

- In the **pivotal 2-year, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial** in 65 healthy adults aged 60–81 (25 mg/day), **fasting blood glucose rose by an average of 0.3 mmol/L (5 mg/dL) in the MK-677 group (P = 0.015) and insulin sensitivity decreased**; the authors explicitly flagged this as a metabolic cost of treatment [8] (`rct`).
- In the **original dose-ranging trial** in 32 healthy elderly subjects (64–81 y) given 2/10/25 mg/day, **MK-677 produced a significant rise in fasting glucose, from 5.4 ± 0.3 to 6.8 ± 0.4 mmol/L at 4 weeks (≈97 → 122 mg/dL; P < 0.01 vs baseline)** — a substantially larger shift than in the long-term trial, indicating dose- and individual-dependent severity [3] (`rct`).
- In an **8-week trial** in 24 obese men aged 18–50 (25 mg/day), **fasting glucose and fasting insulin were unchanged, but an oral glucose tolerance test showed impaired glucose homeostasis at both 2 and 8 weeks** — i.e., the defect surfaces on glucose challenge even when fasting values look normal [9] (`rct`).
- Narrative/review syntheses of the long-term healthy-older-adult data describe small but statistically significant increases in **HbA1c** accompanying the fasting-glucose rise, consistent with the diabetogenic action of chronic GH excess [17] (`mechanism_review`).

**Implication for diabetics / pre-diabetics.** Because the drug reliably worsens fasting glucose, insulin sensitivity, and HbA1c, anyone with established type 2 diabetes, impaired fasting glucose, impaired glucose tolerance, or metabolic syndrome is at meaningfully elevated risk of glycemic deterioration. The 4-week elderly data (fasting glucose rising into the frankly diabetic range in some subjects) show this is not merely theoretical — it is a trial-observed effect, not a class extrapolation.

### 5.2 Other trial-observed adverse effects

- **Increased appetite** — a direct on-target consequence of ghrelin-receptor agonism (ghrelin is the principal orexigenic hunger signal). In the 2-year healthy-elderly trial this was the single most frequent side effect, though it "subsided in a few months" [8] (`rct`).
- **Fluid retention / peripheral edema** — "transient, mild lower-extremity edema" was among the most frequent adverse events in the 2-year trial; GH-mediated sodium and fluid retention is the mechanism and the plausible link to the cardiac signal below [8] (`rct`).
- **Arthralgia / myalgia** — "muscle pain" was a frequent adverse event in the 2-year trial; joint and muscle pain are classic GH-excess (acromegaly-like) effects [8] (`rct`).
- **Cortisol** — in the 2-year trial cortisol rose modestly (+47 nmol/L, P = 0.020), but the short-term trials (4-week dose-ranging, 8-week obese-male) found **no significant change** in serum or urinary cortisol, and the authors judged the small long-term rise too small to explain the glucose increase. The cortisol effect is therefore minor and inconsistent across trials [8][3][9] (`rct`).
- **Lethargy / fatigue / somnolence** — commonly reported as a class effect of GH secretagogues and noted in aggregated user reports; the controlled trials did not isolate it as a prominent quantified adverse event, so treat it as a probable but not rigorously quantified effect [17] (`mechanism_review`).

### 5.3 Congestive heart failure / cardiac signal — reported honestly

A genuine cardiac safety signal emerged in the frail-elderly program. In a **multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase IIb trial in 123 patients ≥60 years recovering from hip fracture** (62 MK-0677 25 mg/day vs 61 placebo), the trial was **terminated early because of an excess of congestive heart failure: 4 CHF events (≈6.5%) in the MK-677 arm versus 1 (≈1.7%) in placebo.** The investigators concluded that the IGF-1 rise did not translate into functional benefit and that **MK-0677 has "an unfavorable safety profile in this patient population"** [15] (`rct`).

The mechanistically coherent explanation is GH/IGF-1-driven **sodium and fluid retention** (the same effect that produces the peripheral edema above) precipitating overt failure in elderly patients with reduced cardiac reserve or subclinical cardiac dysfunction. The large 563-patient Alzheimer's RCT (25 mg/day × 12 months) — another elderly, comorbid population — confirmed robust target engagement (IGF-1 +60% at 6 weeks, +72.9% at 12 months) but no cognitive benefit; it is the program's other large elderly dataset and the appropriate companion when judging cardiac/edema risk in older or comorbid users [14] (`rct`).

**Honest characterization of the CHF signal:** it is a real, trial-derived event imbalance serious enough to stop a trial — but it was observed specifically in **frail, acutely-ill elderly hip-fracture patients**, a population predisposed to fluid-overload heart failure. It should **not** be silently extrapolated as a quantified CHF rate for healthy young users; rather it establishes that MK-677's fluid-retaining, GH-driven physiology can decompensate a vulnerable heart — a legitimate contraindication trigger and monitoring rationale.

### 5.4 Theoretical / class risks (distinguished from trial-observed AEs)

- **Malignancy (IGF-1):** chronically elevated IGF-1 is epidemiologically associated with certain cancers (breast, prostate, colorectal). This is a **theoretical class concern from IGF-1 biology and observational epidemiology, NOT an adverse event observed in the MK-677 RCTs**, which were too short and small to detect cancer endpoints [17] (`mechanism_review`).
- **Acromegaly-like soft-tissue/skeletal effects** with very long-term supraphysiologic IGF-1 are a theoretical extrapolation from GH-excess states, not a documented MK-677 trial finding.

### 5.5 Contraindications, monitoring, and stopping criteria

**Contraindications (relative-to-absolute):**
- **Diabetes mellitus, impaired fasting glucose, or impaired glucose tolerance** — direct, trial-replicated worsening of glycemia [8][3][9].
- **Congestive heart failure or significant cardiac risk / reduced cardiac reserve** — fluid-retention CHF signal in the elderly trial [15].
- **Active or recent malignancy** — IGF-1 elevation is a theoretical proliferation risk [17].
- **Pregnancy and lactation** — no human data; should not be used [17].

**Objective monitoring (named assays):**
- **Fasting glucose + HbA1c** are the primary surveillance labs (baseline and on-treatment); add an **OGTT** if pre-diabetic, since the obese-male trial showed defects appearing only on glucose challenge [8][3][9].
- **IGF-1** to confirm/limit target engagement and bound the malignancy-relevant exposure [3][14].
- **Clinical surveillance for edema and cardiac decompensation** (weight gain, dyspnea, peripheral edema), particularly in older or cardiac-risk individuals [8][15].

**Stopping criteria:** discontinue for new or worsening edema with weight gain or dyspnea (possible CHF), for a clinically meaningful rise in fasting glucose/HbA1c into or toward the diabetic range, or for any sign of cardiac decompensation. The hip-fracture trial's early termination is the precedent: a CHF signal in a comorbid population warrants immediate cessation [8][15].

### 5.6 Coverage gaps (Section 5)

- **No long-term carcinogenicity data** — the malignancy concern is theoretical (IGF-1 biology + epidemiology), not a trial finding; trials were too short/small to detect cancer.
- **No pregnancy/lactation human data** — avoid by default.
- **HbA1c magnitude is review-level, not primary-trial-tabled here** — the fasting-glucose and OGTT signals are primary-trial-grounded; the HbA1c increase is carried from a review.
- **CHF rate is population-specific** — the ~6.5% vs ~1.7% figure is from frail hip-fracture elderly and must not be read as a general CHF rate.

## 6. Regulatory & Sport Status

**Load-bearing distinction:** MK-677 is governed by drug law as an **unapproved drug**, but it is **not a peptide** and therefore does **not** sit inside the §503A peptide-compounding framework that captures BPC-157, TB-500, and the library's other peptides (see §6.3). [type-tag: `regulatory` throughout]

### 6.1 FDA / EMA approval — never approved anywhere

MK-677 has **never been approved by the FDA (or EMA) for any indication**, in the United States or elsewhere. Developed by Merck Research Laboratories (as MK-0677) and advanced through Phase II for GH deficiency, frailty/sarcopenia, and Alzheimer's-associated indications, Merck **discontinued clinical development** and the compound was never submitted for or granted marketing approval [24][18]. The candidate has since changed hands and indication — a successor program licensed the molecule and renamed it **LUM-201** for pediatric GH deficiency — but this remains an **unapproved investigational drug**; no approved finished drug product containing ibutamoren mesylate exists [24]. The U.S. Department of Defense's Operation Supplement Safety (OPSS, Uniformed Services University CHAMP) states plainly that MK-677 "is not approved for human use, making it an unapproved drug" [18]. **Bottom line: unapproved everywhere.**

### 6.2 Dietary-supplement / sports-nutrition status — NOT a lawful dietary ingredient

MK-677 is **not a lawful dietary ingredient** and may not legally be sold as (or in) a dietary supplement. Two independent legal exclusions apply:

1. **Drug-exclusion (IND-preclusion) clause.** Because ibutamoren was authorized for investigation as a new drug and was the subject of substantial clinical investigations made public *before* it was marketed as a food/supplement, it is excluded from the statutory definition of a "dietary ingredient/dietary supplement" under FD&C Act §201(ff)(3)(B). A supplement product containing it is therefore **adulterated** [18][19].
2. **Unapproved-new-drug status.** Independently, because the products make drug-like (GH/IGF-1-raising) claims and contain an unapproved active, FDA treats them as **unapproved new drugs** [19][20].

FDA has **actually enforced** this. In December 2025 the agency issued warning letters to firms selling MK-677:
- **Prime Sports Nutrition (CMS #719433, dated 2025-12-12):** products marketed as "MK-677" / "Ibutamoren" cited as **unapproved new drugs** under FD&C Act §505(a); introduction into interstate commerce violates §§301(d) and 505(a) [19].
- **Agebox Inc. (CMS #718252, dated 2025-12-19):** two "iKids-Growth IGF-1 Support" children's products were found by laboratory analysis to contain **undeclared ibutamoren mesylate**; deemed unapproved new drugs in violation of §§505(a) and 301(d) [20][21].

OPSS/DoD corroborates that MK-677 "is also not legal for use as an ingredient in dietary supplements or any other consumer or commercial products," and lists it on the **DoD Prohibited Dietary Supplement Ingredients List**, noting vendors deceptively label it with "Supplement Facts" panels or as "Research Use Only" chemicals [18]. **Gray-market reality:** despite this, MK-677 is widely sold online as a "research chemical" / "not for human consumption" liquid or capsule, often alongside SARMs — gray-market commerce that confers no legal supplement or drug status [18][19].

### 6.3 §503A peptide-bulks list / April-2026 Federal Register action — DOES NOT apply to MK-677

This must be stated precisely to avoid conflation. In 2026 FDA opened a §503A bulk-drug-substances compounding process specifically for a set of **peptides**, and on **April 16, 2026** published the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) meeting notice — **FR Doc 2026-07361** (91 FR 20465; docket FDA-2025-N-6895) — listing the bulk substances the committee would consider [22]. The substances on that action are **peptides only** (BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Emideltide/DSIP, Semax, Epitalon; a further peptide cohort — e.g., GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, LL-37, dihexa, PEG-MGF — slated for a later PCAC session, with 12 peptides removed from Category 2) [22][23].

**Ibutamoren / MK-677 does NOT appear anywhere in FR Doc 2026-07361, and it is not a peptide** [22]. The §503A *peptide*-bulks framework and the April-2026 peptide action therefore **do not apply to MK-677 the way they apply to BPC-157, TB-500, etc.** — it is simply not in scope of that compounding pathway. This does **not** make MK-677 more permissible: it remains an unapproved drug and a non-lawful dietary ingredient regardless of the peptide proceeding. The two issues are distinct regulatory tracks — peptide compounding (§503A bulks) versus an unapproved small-molecule drug being sold illegally as a supplement/research chemical [19][20][22]. Consistent with this, a compounding-pharmacy aggregator states that MK-677 "is not FDA-approved and is not on the FDA 503A bulk substances list, so US compounding pharmacies cannot legally dispense it for human consumption" [25].

### 6.4 WADA — prohibited at all times (Class S2, growth-hormone secretagogues)

Ibutamoren / MK-677 is **prohibited at all times (in- and out-of-competition)** under the World Anti-Doping Code Prohibited List, within **Section S2 — Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics**, specifically the **growth-hormone-secretagogue (GHS) / ghrelin-mimetic** subgroup (S2.2 — GH and its releasing factors) [31][32]. Anti-doping authorities name ibutamoren explicitly: the independent national anti-doping organization **Sport Integrity Australia** (an Australian Government body) classifies "Ibutamoren (MK-677)" under **S2 — Prohibited at all times**, citing the WADA Prohibited List [31]; OPSS/DoD likewise states MK-677 "is on … the World Anti-Doping Agency Prohibited List" [18]. Other GHS/ghrelin-mimetic examples named alongside it in S2 include anamorelin, capromorelin, ipamorelin, lenomorelin, macimorelin, and tabimorelin [31][32].

> **Primary-source flag.** The exact wada-ama.org primary text could not be machine-fetched (the site returned blocked/empty bodies and the PDF would not render). The WADA-S2 naming above is therefore anchored to a **government anti-doping primary that quotes the WADA List** (Sport Integrity Australia, a national anti-doping organization) plus a **U.S. DoD primary** (OPSS). A reader should confirm the precise S2 subsection number against the live wada-ama.org 2026 Prohibited List; the substantive status (prohibited at all times, S2 GHS) is consistent across all opened sources [18][31].

### 6.5 EU status

There is no EMA marketing authorization for MK-677, and it is not an authorized medicine or a permitted novel-food/supplement ingredient in the EU; as in the U.S., EU-facing sale is gray-market "research chemical" commerce rather than any approved status. (Asserted by analogy from the global never-approved status [24][18]; no EU-primary regulatory document was opened for this specific point, so it is presented as a lower-confidence inference, not a verified EU-primary fact.)

## 7. Why evidence_tier B / risk_tier medium

**evidence_tier B (deliberate, conservative grade):**

1. **Most-studied GH secretagogue in long-term human RCTs.** Unlike the peptides in this library (which are largely preclinical or have no controlled human data at all), MK-677 has a 2-year RCT [Nass 2008], a 12-month n=563 Alzheimer's RCT [Sevigny 2008], an 18-month n=292 osteoporosis RCT [Murphy 2001], a Phase IIb hip-fracture RCT [Adunsky 2011], and several shorter dose-finding/mechanistic trials. This volume and duration of controlled human data is what lifts it well above the peptide tiers.
2. **But efficacy is biomarker/lean-mass only.** What replicates is GH↑, IGF-1↑, and a modest fat-free-mass gain (≈+1.1 kg). What does **not** appear is strength, physical function, BMD generalization, or any disease endpoint.
3. **The two major disease programs FAILED.** Alzheimer's and hip-fracture both missed primary endpoints despite full IGF-1 target engagement; the hip-fracture trial was halted for a CHF signal. A drug whose only large, hard-endpoint trials are negative cannot sit at S/A.
4. **Concentration in a single lineage.** ~75% of human primaries carry a Merck author/sponsor footprint; no Merck-independent disease-endpoint trial exists (§4).

What keeps MK-677 at **B** rather than lower is the genuine, durable, cross-trial-replicated pharmacology (GH/IGF-1 elevation, lean-mass gain) demonstrated in multiple long-term RCTs. What prevents anything higher is the biomarker-vs-function dissociation, the two FAILED programs, and the single-lineage concentration. **B = substantial long-term human data, but efficacy is surrogate-level and the hard-endpoint trials failed.**

**risk_tier medium:**

1. **Well-characterized** — a rich RCT safety record exists, so the harms are *known*, not merely speculative; this argues against the highest risk tier.
2. **Dominant metabolic harm** — raised fasting glucose, reduced insulin sensitivity, raised HbA1c, replicated across trials; intrinsic to chronic GH excess.
3. **A real CHF/fluid-retention signal** stopped a frail-elderly trial early (~6.5% vs ~1.7%).
4. **Unapproved** — no label, no pharmacovigilance, no quality assurance for gray-market product.

These place it at **medium**: not lowest-risk (it is not a wholly uncharacterized research chemical, and there is no acute-lethality signal in the trial record), not highest-risk (the harms are metabolic/cardiac and population-modulated rather than catastrophic at studied doses), but carrying a clear, replicated, mechanism-driven harm profile that mandates glucose/cardiac monitoring and excludes diabetic/cardiac/pregnant/malignancy populations.

## 8. Prescribing-Practice + Non-English Summary

> **Brief by design** — full detail lives in the sibling layer files in this directory. All efficacy/AE-rate magnitudes are deferred to §5 (Tier-1 clinical literature); practitioner, vendor, and anecdote sources here are used **only** for usage conventions, availability, and cost — never to ground efficacy or adverse-event rates. ### 8.1 Prescribing-practice layer

**Compounding gate — NEGATIVE (no compounding data sheets located).** MK-677 is an unapproved small-molecule drug, **not a peptide**, and is **not on the FDA §503A bulk drug substances list**, so US compounding pharmacies cannot legally compound or dispense it for human use; a compounding-pharmacy aggregator explicitly states this and routes no requests for it [25] (`regulatory`/availability). This contrasts with the injectable peptide secretagogues, several of which historically circulated through 503A pharmacies before the April-2026 Category-2 actions [26] (`regulatory`). **No Empower / Belmar / Tailormade / Hallandale / Strive compounding data sheet for MK-677** was located — consistent with the gate result. Some gray-market/telehealth pages assert MK-677 "is available through 503A compounding pharmacies with a prescription," but this conflicts with the FDA list and with FDA enforcement; treat the affirmative claim as unreliable vendor marketing [27] (`vendor_label`). FDA enforcement is active (Agebox warning letter, 19 Dec 2025; see §6.2) [21] (`regulatory`).

**Dosing conventions (bodybuilding / anti-aging gray-market practice — not a named clinical protocol).** The dominant practical convention is **~10–25 mg orally, once daily**:
- Clinical-reference dose carried into practice is **25 mg/day** (the dose used in the published trials) [28] (`vendor_label`/usage).
- For anti-aging / general-wellness use, practice pages converge on **lower doses (~10–15 mg/day)** as "sufficient," reserving 25 mg for body-composition goals [28][29] (`vendor_label`/usage).
- **Timing:** because once-daily dosing sustains IGF-1 around the clock (elimination t½ ~4–6 h, but PD GH/IGF-1 effect ~24 h — see §1.5; do not confuse the two), timing is flexible; many users dose **at night** to align with the nocturnal GH pulse and push appetite/somnolence into sleep, with the trade-off of possible morning grogginess/water retention; others split the dose [28][29] (usage).
- **Cycle length:** practice clusters at **8–16 weeks on**, sometimes a 4–8-week break, or continuous low-dose (~10 mg) with periodic labs; a first cycle is often described as 10 mg/day for 8–12 weeks [29][30] (usage).
- **No named-prescriber protocol** (name + venue + date) was located for MK-677; telehealth pages describe a generic clinician workflow (baseline labs, prescription, monitoring) with **no attributable prescriber name/venue/date**, so they do not qualify as `practitioner_protocol` cites and are reported as `vendor_label`/usage only [30].

**Glucose-monitoring practical caveat.** The dominant practitioner caution is **glucose/insulin monitoring**: obtain **baseline fasting glucose, HbA1c, and IGF-1**, recheck glucose metabolism at ~4–8 weeks and periodically thereafter, and treat pre-diabetics, high-BMI, edema-prone, or cardiovascular-risk individuals as poor or closely-monitored candidates [30][33] (practitioner caution).
