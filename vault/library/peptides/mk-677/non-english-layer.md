# MK-677 (Ibutamoren) — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 non-English-literature survey for **MK-677 (ibutamoren / ibutamoren mesylate; Merck MK-0677 / L-163,191)**, an **oral, non-peptide small molecule** GHS-R1a agonist, companion to `vault/library/peptides/mk-677/research-report.md`. Non-English peer-reviewed sources are admissible under the SAME tier rules as English; primary publication language is noted on every entry. Every citation below was checked for a retrievable identifier (patent number / stable database URL). No citation is fabricated; items found only as secondhand mentions with no locatable record are reported as "none located," not cited.

**Critical admissibility distinction enforced throughout (non-English-LANGUAGE vs non-English-AUTHOR).** A study **authored in Japan/China/Russia but published in English** (PubMed/DOI-indexed English article) is NOT a "genuinely non-English source" — it is already reachable by the English layer and is already in the MK-677 corpus. Only works whose **primary publication language is Russian, Chinese, or Japanese** are counted here as genuine non-English primaries. Merck's originator program and the UVA/Thorner and Göteborg academic work are **English-published** and therefore do not count here, regardless of author nationality.

**Headline result: ZERO independent non-English-LANGUAGE clinical primaries located** across all three required tracks (Russian / Chinese / Japanese). Every non-English-language hit is gray-market / encyclopedic (bodybuilding wikis, vendor shops, reagent catalogs, translated SARM blogs), not independent non-English-language primary research. **The one genuine non-English-language primary *document* is a Japanese patent (JP5336349B2) — a patent, NOT a clinical primary.** This is a clean, well-characterized null: the non-English MK-677 footprint is consumption/marketing of the English corpus, not independent non-English-language primary research. Reported honestly as "none located," not fabricated.
