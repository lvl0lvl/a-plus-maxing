# Section G — Concentration Audit + Sourcing + Prescribing Practice (TB-500 / Tβ4)

**Compound:** TB-500 / Thymosin Beta-4 (Tβ4), a 43-amino-acid actin-sequestering peptide.
**Mode:** DEEP. **Owner:** Section G retrieval specialist.
**Method note:** All citations confirmed via WebSearch + WebFetch (Tavily not connected — sanctioned substitution). Author+year+title+verifiable ID (PMID/DOI/URL) confirmed for every Tier-1/regulatory source. Tier 2.7/3/4 sources are scoped to admin/sourcing/prescribing only; no efficacy claims are drawn from them.

**Naming/identity caveat carried throughout:** "TB-500" is a gray-market trade name. Vendors describe it inconsistently as (a) the **full-length 43-aa Tβ4** (CAS 77591-33-4, MW ~4963 Da) or (b) a short **actin-binding fragment** (commonly the LKKTETQ region / residues ~17–23, or the Ac-SDKP N-terminal fragment). This full-length-vs-fragment ambiguity is the single most important identity issue in this section and is flagged at every relevant point. The peer-reviewed efficacy literature studied **full-length Tβ4**; gray-market "TB-500" may or may not be that molecule.

## Concentration audit (dominant labs; estimated single-lab share of efficacy primaries; method)

**Method.** I characterized the in-vivo efficacy literature for Tβ4 by (1) identifying the foundational reviews and the clinical-translation program, (2) tracing the principal preclinical author clusters across the major application domains (cardiac, corneal/ophthalmic, dermal/diabetic wound, renal/hepatic fibrosis), and (3) using a recent comprehensive review's reference base (Xing et al. 2021, ~133 refs) as a proxy for breadth and institutional diversity. This is an estimate, not a systematic count — see Coverage gaps.

**Dominant author clusters identified:**

- **Goldstein / RegeneRx-affiliated cluster (Tβ4 "discovery + translation" group).** Allan L. Goldstein is the historical discoverer/champion of Tβ4 and is **Chairman, Chief Scientific Advisor, patent inventor, and stockholder of RegeneRx Biopharmaceuticals** [10, regulatory/COI]. This cluster owns the foundational multi-application reviews and the entire human clinical program (RGN-259 ophthalmic, RGN-352 systemic/cardiac). Hannappel, Sosne, and Kleinman recur in this cluster.
- **Cardiac cluster — Bock-Marquette / Srivastava / Dimaio (and, separately, Riley/Smart, Oxford).** Bock-Marquette et al. 2004 *Nature* [4] and Srivastava et al. 2007 *Ann NY Acad Sci* [5] established cardioprotection/ILK-Akt. Smart…Riley 2007 *Nature* (epicardial progenitor mobilization) is an **independent UK group** [6, mechanism_review/animal].
- **Corneal/ophthalmic cluster — Sosne & Kleinman.** Sosne et al. 2002 *Exp Eye Res* [7] is the founding corneal study; this cluster feeds the RegeneRx ophthalmic program (overlaps the Goldstein cluster).
- **Independent international groups (no RegeneRx tie evident).** Kim & Kwon, Chonbuk National University, Korea — diabetic burn-wound healing in db/db mice [8, animal]; Guizhou Provincial People's Hospital, China — renal fibrosis (UUO rat); plus numerous Chinese/Korean dermal, hepatic, and renal groups surveyed in the Xing 2021 review [9, mechanism_review].

**Estimated single-lab share of in-vivo efficacy primaries: ~20–35% for the single largest cluster (Goldstein/RegeneRx + its corneal/dermal sub-groups). NO single lab reaches the ≥70% HALT threshold.** The Xing et al. 2021 review cites ~133 references spanning North America (US/NIH), Europe (France/INSERM), and Asia (predominantly China, plus Korea), drawn from many independent investigator groups with no single institution monopolizing the literature [9, mechanism_review]. The field is **decentralized at the primary-data level.**

**HALT decision: concentration-HALT NOT triggered (estimated max single-lab share well below 70%).**

**However — a distinct, narrower flag MUST be carried downstream (this is the real provenance concern):**

> ⚠ **COI/translation-concentration flag (not a concentration-HALT).** While the *preclinical primary literature* is diverse, the **foundational synthesizing reviews and 100% of the human clinical-translation program** are concentrated in the **Goldstein/RegeneRx commercial cluster**, where the lead figure is a patent-holder and stockholder [10, regulatory/COI]. Any "Tβ4 works in humans" inference rests disproportionately on a single commercially-conflicted nexus, and the marquee cardiac human trial (RGN-352, NCT01311518) was **withdrawn/never initiated** [11, regulatory]. Treat human-efficacy framing accordingly; do not let preclinical diversity launder the clinical-evidence concentration.

## Sourcing landscape (compounding vs vendor; purity/identity caveats) — claims [N, tag]

- TB-500 is **not FDA-approved for any human indication** [12, regulatory]. Compounding-regulatory posture (per Section F, verified against the FDA Law Blog summary and the Apr 16 2026 Federal Register notice, FR doc 2026-07361): TB-500 / thymosin beta-4 was placed on the FDA interim **503A Category 2** bulks list in **September 2023**, but the FDA **REMOVED it from interim Category 2 in April 2026** (notice ~Apr 15–16, 2026; effective within 7 calendar days). Removal is a procedural step — it is **not** FDA approval and **not** an affirmative green-light for compounding; TB-500 remains **non-compoundable** pending rulemaking (neither FDA-approved nor on the 503A Category-1 permitted list). A Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) review is scheduled for **July 23–24, 2026** to evaluate TB-500 (alongside BPC-157, KPV, MOTs-C) for possible **Category-1** listing; that decision had not occurred as of this writing. **Net effect: not lawfully available as a standard FDA-approved drug; not currently compoundable.** *(Canonical regulatory status per Section F, FR doc 2026-07361.)*
- **Compounding-pharmacy tier (Tier 3).** Some peptide-focused compounding pharmacies have historically listed Tβ4/TB-500, but the contested 503A bulk status (above) constrains lawful compounding. I did **not** retrieve an authenticated, manufacturer-issued compounding **data sheet** (reconstitution/admin) from a named 503A/503B pharmacy (Empower, Tailor Made) on this pass — searches surfaced product/marketing pages, not formal monograph data sheets [admin gap, see Coverage gaps]. **No compounding_data_sheet efficacy or admin claim is asserted that I could not verify.**
- **Research-chemical / gray-market tier (Tier 4).** TB-500 is widely sold by research-chem vendors as "Research-Use-Only / Not for human use." Example vendor label (Empower Peptides storefront — a research-chem storefront, NOT the 503B compounder): lyophilized powder, **≥98% purity claimed**, 5 mg and 10 mg vials, CAS 77591-33-4, MW 4963.49 g/mol; page states "Not for injection. Not for clinical or therapeutic use" [13, vendor_label — purity/identity only; gray-market flag]. **Vendor purity claims are self-reported and used here for identity/reconstitution math only, never efficacy.**
- **Purity/identity issues (the core sourcing risk).** Independent third-party reporting on research-chem TB-500 describes **variability in actual peptide content vs label, sequence identity, purity, and microbial/endotoxin contamination** across vendors; products may contain less peptide than labeled, a different sequence than labeled, or synthesis-process contaminants [14, anecdote_aggregate — flagged gray-market; not efficacy]. *(This is a secondary-aggregate characterization. For product **identity** there is now a Tier-1 analytical primary — Esposito et al. 2012 found a marketed "TB-500" to be the Ac-LKKTETQ 17–23 fragment by HPLC/HRMS [19, lab_analysis] — but for the **contamination/underdosing** claim specifically, I did NOT locate a single named, methodologically-described multi-vendor third-party purity/assay audit dataset; the underdosing/contamination characterization rests on secondary-aggregate reporting only — see Disclosed limitations, do not over-cite.)*
- **Full-length vs fragment mislabeling.** Marketing copy and even some vendor science pages conflate full-length 43-aa Tβ4 with a short active fragment (LKKTETQ region, or Ac-SDKP). Buyers cannot assume "TB-500" = the molecule used in the cited preclinical efficacy literature (full-length Tβ4) [identity caveat, vendor_label].
- **Reconstitution math (admin only, from dosing-aggregator pages — Tier 4).** Common convention: a 10 mg lyophilized vial reconstituted with 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water → **5 mg/mL** (or 3.0 mL → ~3.33 mg/mL); refrigerate 2–8 °C after reconstitution, use within ~2–6 weeks [15, anecdote_aggregate — admin/reconstitution only; NOT a pharmacy data sheet]. *(Re-tag note: this source was previously mislabeled `compounding_data_sheet`; it is a community dosing-aggregator site [peptidedosingprotocols.com, operated by Garret Grant; peptidedosages.com], NOT an authenticated 503A/503B compounding-pharmacy clinical data sheet. No compounding/admin claim is grounded on an authenticated pharmacy monograph — that source does not exist on this pass; see Disclosed limitations.)*
- **Product-identity assay (what gray-market "TB-500" actually is — Tier-1 primary).** A peer-reviewed analytical-chemistry study characterized a marketed "TB-500" product by HPLC/high-resolution MS (Orbitrap) and identified its active constituent as the **N-terminal acetylated 17–23 fragment of Tβ4 (Ac-LKKTETQ), NOT full-length 43-aa Tβ4** [19, lab_analysis, tier=1]. This is direct analytical confirmation of the full-length-vs-fragment mislabeling risk at the product level: at least one marketed "TB-500" was the short fragment, not the molecule studied in the efficacy literature. *(This study assayed product identity; it is not a multi-vendor purity/underdosing audit — see Disclosed limitations.)*
