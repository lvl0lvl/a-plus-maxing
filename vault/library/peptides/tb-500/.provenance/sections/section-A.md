## Claims

### 1. Molecular identity — full-length Thymosin β4 (Tβ4)

1. Thymosin β4 is a 43-amino-acid peptide with the sequence Ac-SDKPDMAEIEKFDKSKLKKTETQEKNPLPSKETIEQEKQAGES, an acetyl-serine at the N-terminus, and an authoritative average molecular weight of **~4,963 Da** for the native N-terminally acetylated mature chain (UniProt P62328 / Kelleher proteoform repository: 4,963.50 Da, average mass, acetylation at the N-terminal serine) [3, mechanism_review]. The spread of circulating values is fully explained by convention: **~4,921 Da** = unmodified/free-N-terminus peptide (average, no acetyl, the value Wikipedia states for the bare 43-mer); **~4,963 Da** = the same chain with the native N-terminal acetylation (+42 Da), i.e. the actual endogenous molecule; **~4,982 Da** = the value reported in the original 1982 isolation/sequencing paper (which also fixed the isoelectric point at 5.1) [1, mechanism_review]. The canonical figure for the native acetylated peptide used throughout this report is therefore **~4,963 Da (average, acetylated; UniProt P62328)** [3, mechanism_review].
2. Tβ4 carries a Whitepaper-level identity record: gene TMSB4X (Xp22.2; synonyms FX, PTMB4, TB4X, TMSB4), CAS 77591-33-4, molecular formula approximately C212H350N56O78S, with the mature peptide N-terminally acetylated — a modification present on the native molecule [3, mechanism_review].
3. The N-terminus of mature Tβ4 is acetylserine, established by automated Edman degradation in the original chemical characterization, and the peptide contains two predicted helical regions; sequence comparison showed no statistically significant homology to other then-known thymic hormones [1, mechanism_review].

### 2. Endogenous biology of Tβ4 (identity context)

4. Tβ4 is the principal intracellular G-actin–sequestering peptide: it forms a 1:1 complex with monomeric (globular) actin and thereby buffers the free G-actin pool that feeds filament (F-actin) polymerization, per the equilibrium F-actin ↔ G-actin + Tβ4 ↔ (G-actin·Tβ4) [3, mechanism_review].
5. The actin-binding activity is localized to a short internal motif spanning residues 17–23, sequence LKKTETQ [4, mechanism_review].
6. Tβ4 is a major cellular constituent across many tissues, with intracellular concentrations reaching the high-micromolar to ~0.5 mM range and highest expression in monocytes, granulocytes, and lymphoid tissue; it is also present in extracellular fluids including tears [3, mechanism_review].

### 3. The "TB-500" vs Thymosin β4 disambiguation (CRITICAL)

7. "TB-500" sold as a research chemical is NOT full-length Tβ4: analytical characterization of a commercial TB-500 product identified its active ingredient as the N-terminally acetylated 17–23 fragment of thymosin β4, i.e. Ac-LKKTETQ — a 7-residue peptide, confirmed by HPLC/high-resolution Orbitrap MS and reproduced by solid-phase synthesis [2, mechanism_review].
8. Independent doping-control work on a commercial TB-500 preparation likewise reports the contents as N-acetylated LKKTETQ (the actin-binding "active region" of Tβ4), supplied as ~10 mg per vial, and developed an LC-MS detection method for it in equine urine and plasma [5, mechanism_review].
9. The two entities are therefore chemically distinct molecules with different size (43-mer full protein vs 7-mer fragment), different mass, and different regulatory/analytical histories; vendor materials that equate "TB-500" with "Thymosin Beta-4" conflate them [2, mechanism_review][5, mechanism_review].
10. The substantive human clinical efficacy/safety evidence base was generated with FULL-LENGTH Tβ4 (e.g., topical Tβ4 in venous ulcers; IV Tβ4 in healthy volunteers; ophthalmic Tβ4), NOT with the Ac-LKKTETQ 7-mer fragment that vendors market as "TB-500" [6, rct][7, open_label][8, regulatory]. (No completed, published human RCT of the Ac-LKKTETQ fragment itself was located — see Coverage gaps.)

### 4. Chemistry / synthesis / formulation / stability / reconstitution

11. The Ac-LKKTETQ fragment is producible by standard solid-phase peptide synthesis (Fmoc/Boc chemistry), as demonstrated when authentic reference material was synthesized to confirm the identity of the TB-500 product [2, mechanism_review].
12. Full-length Tβ4 is formulated for clinical use as an aqueous solution; an ophthalmic formulation (RGN-259) buffers to a near-physiological tear pH (working ranges cited as pH 5.5–7.8) [8, regulatory]. (See gaps: lyophilization/reconstitution math for injectable powders is covered only by vendor sheets, which cannot ground numerical claims.)

### 5. Pharmacokinetics (full-length Tβ4; species + n + route tagged)

13. Human, IV, full-length synthetic Tβ4: in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 1, four cohorts of n=10 healthy volunteers each received single IV doses of 42, 140, 420, or 1,260 mg, then the same dose daily for 14 days; PK was dose-proportional with an increasing half-life at higher doses, and the drug was well tolerated with no dose-limiting toxicity or serious adverse events [7, open_label].
14. Human, IV, recombinant human Tβ4 (NL005): a first-in-human randomized, double-blind, single-/multiple-ascending-dose Phase 1 in n=54 (SAD) Chinese volunteers dosed IV at 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 2.0, 5.0, 12.5, 25.0 µg/kg showed dose-proportional Cmax and AUC, Tmax ~3–15 min, terminal half-life ~0.5–2.08 h, and clearance ~155–189 mL/h/kg; the multiple-dose arm (n=30; 0.5/2.0/5.0 µg/kg daily ×10 d) showed no accumulation [9, open_label].
15. Animal (rat), IV: a Sprague-Dawley rat single-dose IV PK study of a Tβ4 product (1 mg/kg IV, n=4 male rats, sampling to 60 min) reported a plasma half-life of ~6.9 ± 3.8 min, indicating rapid systemic elimination [10, animal]. NOTE: this value is sourced to a clinical-trial regulatory protocol document (NCT05232175, ALT-BB4) rather than a peer-reviewed primary; tagged accordingly and treated as regulatory-grade, not Tier-1 primary.
16. [route-extrapolation] Vendor and practitioner materials commonly recommend SC/IM dosing for "TB-500," but the retrievable human PK data above are for IV full-length Tβ4; SC/IM bioavailability and half-life for the marketed fragment are NOT established by admissible primary sources — any SC/IM dose guidance is an extrapolation across both route and molecule and should be treated as unsupported [route-extrapolation].

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

1. **"17 amino acids" marketing claim is wrong, but I want to flag the confusion.** Vendor/blog copy frequently calls TB-500 a "17-amino-acid fragment." The primary analytical literature ([2], [5]) is unambiguous that the marketed product is the **7-residue** Ac-LKKTETQ corresponding to **residues 17–23** of Tβ4. The "17-aa" figure is almost certainly a misreading of "residues 17–23." I did not find any primary source supporting an actual 17-amino-acid TB-500 species. Flagged so downstream sections do not repeat the vendor error.

2. **Exact MW of full-length Tβ4 — three circulating values, now reconciled (Phase-4.25 cross-section fix).** The convention spread is resolved against UniProt P62328: **~4,921 Da** = the unmodified free-N-terminus 43-mer (average; the value Wikipedia states for the bare sequence); **~4,963 Da** = that chain with native N-terminal acetylation (+42 Da; UniProt/Kelleher average mass 4,963.50 Da) — the actual endogenous molecule and the value commercial vendors also quote (~4,963.49); **~4,982 Da** = the figure reported in the original 1982 isolation paper ([1]). Claim 1 now canonicalizes on **~4,963 Da (average, acetylated; UniProt P62328)** — consistent with Section G — and records the convention mapping rather than leaving the values unreconciled. (Monoisotopic masses differ from these average values and are not the quoted convention here.)

3. **No admissible PK for the Ac-LKKTETQ fragment ("TB-500") itself in humans.** All human PK ([7], [9]) and the rat figure ([10]) are for full-length Tβ4 (synthetic or recombinant). I found NO completed, published human RCT or human PK study of the 7-mer fragment. Any half-life/dose extrapolation from full Tβ4 to the fragment crosses both molecule and (usually) route and is unsupported.

4. **SC/IM PK is essentially absent from admissible sources.** Marketed dosing is SC/IM, but the retrievable primaries are IV (human) and IV (rat). Cynomolgus monkey IV/SC PK appears to exist only inside a clinical protocol PDF (NCT05232175) that did not render cleanly; I did not find a peer-reviewed primate PK primary, so SC bioavailability/half-life is a genuine gap.

5. **Rat PK record is regulatory-grade, not peer-reviewed.** The 6.9 min rat half-life ([10]) comes from a trial protocol document and its body did not fully render; I corroborated it only via the search engine's extraction of that document. Treat as provisional pending a peer-reviewed source.

6. **NCT00743769 (RegeneRx IV Tβ4 cardioprotection Phase 1) was WITHDRAWN with 0 enrollment** (manufacturing issues) — it is NOT evidence and is deliberately excluded from the bibliography despite appearing in searches.

7. **Reconstitution/stability math** for injectable lyophilized powder is covered only by Tier-4 vendor sheets, which by rule cannot ground numerical claims; I therefore did not state vial concentrations or shelf-life numbers for injectable product beyond the ophthalmic pH range from the regulatory record ([8]).

8. **Two key full-texts were paywalled/blocked** ([2] Wiley 403, [4] Wiley 403, [6] PubMed CAPTCHA, Esposito CORE/Donike PDFs 403/404). Bibliographic metadata and the load-bearing facts were corroborated across multiple independent search records and abstract mirrors, but I could not read the full methods sections of [2], [4], and [6] directly. No numerical claim rests on an unread full text beyond what abstracts/records stated.
