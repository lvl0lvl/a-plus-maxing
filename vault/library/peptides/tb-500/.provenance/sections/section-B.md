# Section B — Mechanism of Action (TB-500 / Thymosin Beta-4)

> Scope note: "TB-500" is a research/compounding designation for thymosin β4 (Tβ4) — most commercial "TB-500" is sold as the full 43-residue Tβ4 peptide or as an actin-binding fragment derived from it. The mechanistic literature is overwhelmingly on endogenous Tβ4 itself; mechanism claims below are for the Tβ4 molecule. Where a claim rests on a fragment (e.g., the LKKTET actin-binding motif, or the N-terminal Ac-SDKP tetrapeptide), this is stated explicitly.

## Claims

### 1. G-actin sequestration (primary biochemical role)

1.1. Tβ4 is the principal intracellular G-actin–sequestering protein: it binds monomeric (G-)actin in a 1:1 complex and inhibits salt-induced polymerization into F-actin, thereby buffering the pool of unpolymerized actin monomers available for filament elongation. [1, mechanism_review][6, mechanism_review]

1.2. Structurally, Tβ4 sequesters actin by capping **both ends** of the actin monomer (longitudinal contacts at the barbed/pointed faces), as resolved at 2 Å in the gelsolin-G1–Tβ4:actin complex; the same structure shows that exchange of actin between Tβ4 and profilin is mediated by a minor overlap in their binding sites — the molecular basis for cells dynamically shifting actin between a sequestered pool and a polymerizable pool. [2, in_vitro] (X-ray crystallography, recombinant constructs; n = single high-resolution structure.)

1.3. The actin-buffering function is consistent with the WH2 (WASP-homology 2) domain family: Tβ4 belongs to the β-thymosin/WH2 structural class, which informs how it engages the actin monomer. [2, in_vitro]

### 2. Cell migration / wound-healing signaling

2.1. Secreted/exogenous Tβ4 stimulates directional cell migration of keratinocytes, endothelial cells and fibroblasts, enabling cytoskeletal reorganization and movement toward a wound edge; this is the cellular basis of its wound-repair activity. [3, animal][6, mechanism_review]

2.2. Tβ4 accelerates re-epithelialization in vivo: topical or intraperitoneal Tβ4 increased re-epithelialization by ~42% over saline controls at day 4 and up to ~61% at day 7, with increased collagen deposition and angiogenesis in treated wounds. (Rat full-thickness dermal wound model; species = rat, including normal and healing-impaired/steroid-treated animals.) In vitro, keratinocyte/cell migration was stimulated 2–3-fold with as little as ~10 pg Tβ4 added. [3, animal] (Note: numeric in-vivo magnitudes are from an animal model, not human data.)

2.3. Tβ4 functions as a "moonlighting" protein: the same intracellular actin regulator becomes an extracellular repair signal when released by platelets, macrophages and other cells after injury, contributing to migration, survival and reduced apoptosis/inflammation at the wound site. [4, mechanism_review][7, mechanism_review]

### 3. Angiogenesis and endothelial effects

3.1. Tβ4 is pro-angiogenic: it promotes endothelial cell migration, adhesion, tubule formation and aortic/aortic-arch ring sprouting, and the **seven–amino-acid actin-binding motif** of Tβ4 is necessary and largely sufficient for this angiogenic activity (the isolated motif reproduces angiogenic effects; constructs lacking it lose activity). [5, in_vitro] (Human umbilical vein endothelial cells [HUVEC] migration assays; chick aortic-arch sprouting assays; activity ~50 nM; n not specified.)

3.2. Tβ4 up-regulates VEGF by stabilizing HIF-1α protein in an oxygen-independent (normoxic) manner rather than by increasing HIF-1α transcription — i.e., it raises VEGF expression indirectly via increased HIF-1α protein stability. [8, in_vitro] (Colon cancer; Tβ4/HIF-1α co-localization shown in a colon cancer tissue microarray plus tumor cell-line experiments; the abstract does not name a specific in-vitro line; n not specified.)

3.3. Tβ4 promotes angiogenic behavior of endothelial cells through Notch signaling: blocking Notch1 or Notch4 significantly prevented Tβ4-induced tube formation and transendothelial migration, indicating Notch receptors are required for this angiogenic response. [9, in_vitro] (HUVEC model; n not specified.)

### 4. Anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic mechanisms

4.1. Tβ4 suppresses NF-κB–driven inflammation by directly targeting the NF-κB RelA/p65 subunit: overexpressed Tβ4 interferes with TNF-α–induced NF-κB activation and downstream IL-8 transcription, blocking RelA/p65 nuclear translocation and its binding to the IL-8 promoter; this effect is independent of actin binding. Its focal-adhesion partners PINCH-1 and ILK conversely *enhance* NF-κB activity, and Tβ4 counteracts that sensitization. [10, in_vitro] (Human corneal epithelial cells, corneal fibroblasts, plus HEKa/COS-7/A7r5 and related lines; cell-culture model.)

4.2. In cardiac injury, Tβ4 reduces inflammation and fibrosis by suppressing NF-κB and collagen synthesis, activating Akt phosphorylation and the ILK–PINCH–Parvin complex, reducing macrophage infiltration and pro-fibrotic signaling, and limiting fibroblast-to-myofibroblast conversion. [11, mechanism_review]

4.3. The N-terminal tetrapeptide of Tβ4, **Ac-SDKP** (N-acetyl-Ser-Asp-Lys-Pro), is an endogenous anti-inflammatory/anti-fibrotic fragment liberated from Tβ4 by a two-enzyme cascade: meprin-α first cleaves the 43-residue Tβ4 into shorter intermediates (<30 aa), then prolyl oligopeptidase (POP) releases Ac-SDKP (POP cannot act on the intact 43-mer directly). [12, in_vitro] (Enzymatic/biochemical study using renal enzymes; demonstrates the generation pathway, not a clinical effect.)

### 5. Tissue repair / stem-progenitor activation; ILK–PINCH–Akt axis

5.1. Tβ4 activates a pro-survival/pro-migratory signaling module by forming a functional complex with PINCH1 and integrin-linked kinase (ILK), leading to Akt (PKB) activation; this enhances cardiomyocyte and endothelial cell migration and survival. In vivo, after coronary artery ligation, Tβ4 up-regulated ILK and Akt activity in the heart, improved early myocyte survival and improved cardiac function. [13, animal] (Embryonic + postnatal cardiomyocyte cultures; mouse coronary-ligation MI model.)

5.2. Across animal models, Tβ4's repair program integrates cell migration, blood-vessel development, cell survival, stem/progenitor cell maturation, and down-regulation of inflammatory chemokines/cytokines — documented in dermal, corneal and cardiac repair. [14, mechanism_review]

### 6. Receptor / target ambiguity (honest status)

6.1. Tβ4's best-established molecular target is intracellular G-actin (Claims 1.1–1.3); this is structurally proven. By contrast, a definitive cell-surface receptor mediating Tβ4's extracellular signaling effects has NOT been firmly established. [4, mechanism_review][6, mechanism_review]

6.2. Candidate/partial targets proposed in the literature include: (a) **Ku80** (subunit of ATP-dependent DNA helicase II) — co-immunoprecipitates with Tβ4 in endothelial cells via its C-terminal arm domain and mediates an intracellular activity (PAI-1 induction) that is distinct from G-actin sequestering and independent of the Ac-SDKP fragment [15, in_vitro] (endothelial cells); and (b) downstream pathway components such as ILK/PINCH (Claim 5.1) and Notch (Claim 3.3). These are best read as binding partners / pathway intermediaries, not a validated canonical Tβ4 receptor. The receptor question remains open. [6, mechanism_review][15, in_vitro]

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

- **Numeric magnitudes are non-human.** The only quantitative efficacy numbers (re-epithelialization %, fold-migration) come from a rat model [3]; cardiac functional improvement is from a mouse MI model [13]. No human-derived numeric mechanism endpoints were located for Section B. Vendor/anecdote sources were excluded entirely from numeric grounding (none used).
- **"TB-500" vs Tβ4.** Essentially all mechanism research is on endogenous full-length Tβ4 (or defined fragments). Claims here apply to the Tβ4 molecule; commercial "TB-500" preparations vary (full peptide vs actin-binding fragment) and that compounding/identity question belongs to other sections (compounding_data_sheet / vendor_label — out of scope for B).
- **Receptor remains genuinely unresolved.** G-actin binding is structurally proven [2]; the extracellular signaling receptor is not. Ku80 [15] is the strongest candidate for one intracellular branch but is not a validated canonical surface receptor. "Actin as a surface receptor" is a hypothesis only [6]. Claim 6 is deliberately conservative.
- **n / sample sizes** are frequently unreported in the in-vitro angiogenesis and HIF-1α papers ([5], [8], [9]) — annotated as "n not specified" rather than guessed.
- **Two reviews ([4]/[7], [1]) are Tier-2 aggregators.** Their specific factual claims are also independently grounded in Tier-1 sources ([2], [3], [13]) wherever a hard claim is made; they were not used to carry any standalone numeric or novel mechanistic assertion.
- **Not pursued (time/scope):** S1P/S1PR1 endothelial-barrier work and several oligodendrocyte/p38-MAPK CNS-repair papers surfaced in searches but were not fully verified; they could deepen the angiogenesis/neuro-repair subsections if Section B is expanded later.
