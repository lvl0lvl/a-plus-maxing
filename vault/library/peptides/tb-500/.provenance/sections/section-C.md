# Section C — Preclinical Efficacy: Musculoskeletal & Wound (TB-500 / Tβ4)

> Scope note: TB-500 is a synthetic peptide marketed as "thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4)." Vendor TB-500 is commonly the actin-binding fragment/full peptide; most peer-reviewed preclinical work uses **full-length Tβ4 (43 aa)** or the **LKKTETQ actin-binding heptapeptide**, NOT a product labeled "TB-500." Where the cited study used full Tβ4 or LKKTETQ, that is stated. No admissible peer-reviewed efficacy study in this section used a product called "TB-500"; vendor/product-identity equivalence to research Tβ4 is itself a coverage gap (see limits).

## Claims

### 1. Tendon healing / tendinopathy

1. There is **no admissible in-vivo tendon-injury efficacy study** (Achilles, patellar, rotator-cuff, flexor) for Tβ4/TB-500. The closest tendon-directed evidence is **in vitro tissue-engineering** work [1, in_vitro]. This is a real, load-bearing gap, not an oversight (search terms in "Coverage gaps").
2. In vitro, Tβ4 incorporated into electrospun PLGA/PLA nanofiber/microfiber yarns dose-dependently increased tenogenic markers (scleraxis SCX, tenomodulin TNMD, tenascin-C TNC) and promoted migration/proliferation of human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (HADMSC); the topography + Tβ4 combination was more effective than either alone [1, in_vitro]. **No tensile/biomechanical or in-vivo tendon outcome** — this is cell-culture differentiation data only.

### 2. Ligament injury

3. Local Tβ4 (1 µg in 100 µL fibrin sealant placed in the ligament gap) improved healing of a sharply transected **medial collateral ligament (MCL)** in **rats (Sprague-Dawley; n not reported in abstract)** [2, animal] [route-extrapolation: local fibrin depot, not systemic SC injection as TB-500 is typically self-administered].
4. Tβ4-treated MCL showed **more uniform, evenly-spaced collagen fiber bundles** and **significantly increased collagen fibril diameter** within granulation tissue vs control (rat MCL, n not reported) [2, animal].
5. Tβ4-treated femur-MCL-tibia complexes had **significantly better biomechanical (tensile) properties at 4 weeks** vs control (rat MCL, n not reported) [2, animal]. This is the single strongest musculoskeletal efficacy signal in the corpus, but rests on **one study from one lab** and the abstract does not report n or effect-size magnitudes.

### 3. Skeletal muscle injury / repair / regeneration

6. After skeletal muscle injury in mice, endogenous Tβ4 (and Tβ10) mRNA is **up-regulated early** in regenerating fibers and infiltrating hematopoietic/inflammatory cells (**mice, C57BL; n not reported**) [3, animal/mechanism].
7. Tβ4 — and more potently its **sulfoxidized form** — acts as a **chemoattractant for myoblasts**: both accelerated scratch-wound closure and increased chemotaxis of C2C12 myoblasts; the sulfoxidized form attracted primary satellite-cell-derived myoblasts (**in vitro**, C2C12 + primary mouse myoblasts) [3, in_vitro]. Mechanistic basis for a muscle-regeneration role; **not a functional in-vivo efficacy endpoint**.
8. Chronic systemic Tβ4 (**150 µg IP, twice weekly, 6 months**) in the **mdx dystrophic mouse** (Duchenne model; female C57BL/10ScSn-Dmd^mdx^/J) **significantly increased regenerating skeletal muscle fibers** (11.6 ± 13.5 vs 2.6 ± 1.1 untreated; p = 0.03) [4, animal]. **The regenerating-fiber histology that anchors this efficacy claim is n = 9 treated mdx / n = 7 untreated mdx** (gastrocnemius, Table S3, footnote ^^; WT comparators n = 15/14). This is a DIFFERENT cohort from the serum creatine-kinase measurement (n = 8 treated / n = 10 untreated mdx) — do not conflate the two. [population_annotation: verified per-endpoint n from full-text Table S3; prior "n = 8–14/group" was the CK cohort mislabeled onto the histology claim.]
9. In that same mdx study, Tβ4 produced **NO significant improvement in grip strength, skeletal muscle function, cardiac systolic function, or fibrosis** (mdx mice; gastrocnemius fibrosis n = 3/group, cardiac fibrosis n = 3 mdx/4 WT-treated, diaphragm fibrosis n = 5/group; grip-strength/echo n not separately tabulated in the abstract-level extract) [4, animal]. **Histological regeneration did not translate to functional benefit** — a critical honest qualifier for any muscle-recovery claim. [population_annotation: the n = 3/group fibrosis cohort is distinct from both the n = 8/10 CK cohort and the n = 9/7 regenerating-fiber cohort.]

### 4. Skin / dermal / full-thickness / diabetic / burn wounds

10. Topical or intraperitoneal Tβ4 increased **reepithelialization by 42% (day 4) and up to 61% (day 7)** and increased wound contraction by **≥11% (day 7)** vs saline in a **rat full-thickness (8 mm punch) wound model** (dose 5 µg/50 µL PBS; **rat; n not reported in abstract**) [5, animal]. Also increased collagen deposition, angiogenesis, and keratinocyte migration (2–3-fold in vitro) [5, animal/in_vitro].
11. Tβ4 accelerated full-thickness dermal wound repair in **db/db diabetic mice AND aged mice**, with significantly increased wound contracture and collagen deposition; the **LKKTETQ actin-binding heptapeptide alone reproduced the effect in aged mice** (**mice, db/db + aged; n not reported in abstract**) [6, animal]. Second independent demonstration that impaired-healing models respond.
12. Intradermal Tβ4 (**5 mg/kg, twice weekly × 2 weeks**) improved **deep second-degree burn** healing (wound closure, granulation, vascularization) in **db/db diabetic mice (n not reported)**, associated with downregulation of RAGE [7, animal] [route-extrapolation: mg/kg intradermal dosing not comparable to fixed-mass human SC TB-500 dosing].
13. A **dimeric Tβ4 (DTβ4)** outperformed monomeric Tβ4 on topical wound closure in **Sprague-Dawley rats (n = 8/group; 4M/4F)**: mid-dose DTβ4 reached 50% healing ~3 days faster than hydrogel control and ~1 day faster than monomeric Tβ4 [8, animal]. Relevant as dose/format-finding and as an independent rat dermal replication; the marketed entity is monomeric, so the DTβ4 advantage does not transfer to TB-500.
14. A peer-reviewed **review** (Kleinman & Sosne) summarizes the dermal preclinical corpus (diabetic, aged, burn models) plus phase-2 human ulcer/epidermolysis-bullosa trials as evidence Tβ4 accelerates skin repair [9, mechanism_review]. Used for context/framing only — does not independently ground numerical claims.

### 5. Dose-finding summary (route + species + n, where reported)

15. Effective preclinical doses span routes and are **not interconvertible**: local fibrin depot 1 µg (rat MCL) [2]; topical/IP 5 µg/50 µL (rat dermal) [5]; intradermal 5 mg/kg (mouse burn) [7]; topical 0.125–0.5 mg/mL hydrogel (rat dermal, DTβ4) [8]; systemic 150 µg IP 2×/wk (mouse mdx) [4]. **No dose-response study establishes a canonical systemic SC dose** matching how TB-500 is actually used; all systemic-vs-local and mg/kg-vs-fixed-mass comparisons are [route-extrapolation].

## Lab-provenance note

Critical for the downstream single-lab concentration audit. The corpus clusters into a small number of groups, and the dermal evidence in particular is **heavily concentrated in one lineage**:

- **Goldstein / Kleinman (NIH / Uniformed Services University) lineage** — [5] Malinda 1999, [6] Philp 2003, and the [9] Kleinman & Sosne 2016 review. This single lineage originated and dominates the dermal full-thickness/diabetic/aged-mouse wound corpus. Goldstein was also a co-founder/scientific figure behind commercial Tβ4 development (RegeneRx), so this lineage carries a **commercial-interest flag**.
- **Xu B / Jiang (China)** — [2], the lone in-vivo ligament (MCL) study. Independent of the NIH lineage.
- **Nagaraju (Children's National / GWU)** — [4], the mdx muscle study; notable for reporting **negative functional results** despite histological regeneration (lower fabrication/bias risk profile).
- **Hara lab (Japan)** — [3], muscle myoblast chemoattraction mechanism. Independent.
- **Kwon lab (Chonbuk, Korea)** — [7], diabetic burn. Independent.
- **M. Li / Fourth Military Medical Univ (China)** — [8], dimeric Tβ4 dermal. Independent, but tests an engineered variant.
- **Duan lab (Nebraska)** — [1], in-vitro tendon scaffold. Independent.

Summary for audit: **dermal efficacy is single-lineage-dominated (Goldstein/Kleinman) with a commercial-interest flag**; musculoskeletal claims (ligament, muscle) are each single-study but come from **independent, non-overlapping labs**. No claim in this section is corroborated by ≥2 fully independent in-vivo studies on the *same* tissue endpoint.

## Coverage gaps / honest limits

1. **No in-vivo tendon study exists** (Achilles, patellar, rotator-cuff, flexor) for Tβ4/TB-500. Despite TB-500's heavy marketing for tendinopathy, the ONLY tendon-directed data is in-vitro stem-cell differentiation on a scaffold [1]. This is the single largest gap relative to the compound's reputation. Search terms used (all returned no admissible in-vivo tendon efficacy study): "thymosin beta-4 Achilles tendon rat", "thymosin beta-4 rotator cuff in vivo rat rabbit", "Tβ4 tendinopathy / tenocyte / tendon rupture repair", "thymosin beta-4 patellar/flexor tendon".
2. **No replication on any single MSK tissue endpoint.** Ligament = 1 study [2]; in-vivo muscle regeneration = 1 study [4]; muscle chemoattraction = 1 study [3]. Single-lab, single-study claims do not meet a replication bar.
3. **Sample sizes (n) are largely unreported in abstracts.** Only [4] (per-endpoint n verified from open-access full text + Table S3: CK n=8/10 mdx, regenerating-fiber histology n=9/7 mdx, fibrosis n=3–5/group) and [8] (n=8) report n; [2], [5], [6], [7] n could not be confirmed from abstracts and full text is paywalled (ScienceDirect/Wiley/BBA) — "n not extractable from abstract", not fabricated. Effect-size magnitudes are reported for [4], [5], [8]; absent or qualitative for [2], [6], [7].
4. **Histology ≠ function.** The best-controlled muscle study [4] showed histological regeneration but explicitly NO functional improvement (strength, cardiac function, fibrosis). This caution should propagate to any "muscle recovery" claim.
5. **Dermal corpus is single-lineage-dominated** (Goldstein/Kleinman) with a commercial-interest flag (RegeneRx). Independent dermal replication exists [7][8] but in different models (burn, dimer) — not a clean independent replication of the Malinda full-thickness result.
6. **Route and entity extrapolation.** Effective doses span local-depot, intradermal mg/kg, and IP — none matches the fixed-mass subcutaneous self-administration TB-500 users employ. No study used a product labeled "TB-500"; research used full-length Tβ4 or LKKTETQ. Product-identity equivalence is unverified.
7. **HALT issue:** none. No fabrication risk encountered; all 9 sources verified by PMID/DOI via PubMed/PMC. Paywall blocks on full-text ([2] ScienceDirect, [6] Wiley) limited n/effect-size extraction but did not affect citation admissibility.
