# TB-500 / Thymosin Beta-4 (Tβ4)

## Metadata

- **class:** peptide
- **evidence_tier:** C — Mechanistically rich and broadly studied in animals, but the entire *human* evidence base rests on a handful of small, mostly company-run trials of a *different molecule* (full-length Tβ4), none of which met its pre-specified primary endpoint, with literally zero human data for the marketed athletic use case.
- **risk_tier:** experimental — Not FDA-approved for any indication; not lawfully compoundable; a real, bidirectional tumor signal (pro-metastatic in solid-tumor animal models); thin and indirect human safety data; gray-market product-identity and purity problems; WADA-prohibited at all times.
- **status:** researching
- **last_verified:** 2026-06-19

> **Read this first — the honest evidence state.**
>
> The product sold on the gray market as "TB-500" is, by direct analytical assay of at least one marketed preparation, the **N-terminally acetylated Ac-LKKTETQ fragment** — residues 17–23 of thymosin β4 (Tβ4), a *7-amino-acid peptide* — **NOT** the full-length 43-amino-acid Tβ4 molecule. This matters enormously, because **almost all studied evidence — every mechanism paper, every animal efficacy model, and every human trial — was generated with full-length Tβ4 (synthetic or recombinant), not with the marketed fragment.** When a buyer reads "thymosin beta-4 is clinically studied," they are reading about a molecule they are very likely not buying.
>
> Beyond the identity problem, the clinical picture is sobering. **There is no completed human randomized controlled trial that met its pre-specified primary endpoint for ANY Tβ4 indication.** The flagship Phase 3 dry-eye trial (ARISE-3, n=700) missed its co-primary endpoints; the Phase 3 neurotrophic-keratopathy trial (SEER-1, n=18) *narrowly* missed (p=0.0656); the Chinese cardiac Phase 2/2b trials are completed but have posted no results. And there is **ZERO human evidence — not a single completed or even registered trial — for the athletic muscle/tendon/ligament use case** that drives TB-500's reputation. All musculoskeletal data are preclinical, in rodents.
>
> On regulation: in **April 2026 the FDA REMOVED TB-500 / thymosin beta-4 from the 503A interim Category 2 bulk-substances list** (Federal Register notice, FR doc 2026-07361). **This is NOT approval.** Removal from Category 2 is a procedural step; TB-500 remains **non-compoundable** — it is neither FDA-approved nor on the Category-1 (permitted) list — pending the **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) review scheduled July 23–24, 2026**. It is **WADA-prohibited at all times**, in and out of competition, under class S2.3 "Growth Factors and Growth Factor Modulators."
>
> And there is a real, *bidirectional* tumor signal: Tβ4 over-expression was strongly pro-metastatic in a mouse melanoma model (46.7 vs 10.9 lung nodules), yet acted as a tumor *suppressor* in multiple myeloma. The conservative read for a non-approved, self-administered, pro-angiogenic peptide is that active or recent solid malignancy is a contraindication.
>
> This is a **goal-agnostic library entry**: operator/dosing-for-a-specific-person fields are deliberately left blank. The purpose here is to state the evidence honestly, not to recommend use.

## 1. Identity, Chemistry & the Molecular Identity Problem

### 1.1 Full-length thymosin β4 (Tβ4) — the molecule the science is about

Thymosin β4 is a **43-amino-acid peptide** with the sequence **Ac-SDKPDMAEIEKFDKSKLKKTETQEKNPLPSKETIEQEKQAGES**, bearing an **acetyl-serine at the N-terminus**. Its authoritative average molecular weight, for the native N-terminally acetylated mature chain, is **~4,963 Da** (UniProt P62328 / Kelleher proteoform repository: 4,963.50 Da, average mass, acetylation at the N-terminal serine) [3].

The several molecular-weight values that circulate in the literature and vendor copy are fully explained by convention, not by contradiction:

- **~4,921 Da** — the unmodified / free-N-terminus 43-mer (average; the value Wikipedia states for the bare sequence, i.e. without the acetyl group);
- **~4,963 Da** — the same chain *with* the native N-terminal acetylation (+42 Da). This is the actual endogenous molecule and the value commercial vendors also quote (~4,963.49 g/mol);
- **~4,982 Da** — the figure reported in the original 1982 isolation/sequencing paper, which also fixed the isoelectric point at 5.1 [1].

The canonical figure used throughout this report is therefore **~4,963 Da (average, acetylated; UniProt P62328)** [3]. (Monoisotopic masses differ from these average values and are not the convention quoted here.)

Identity record:

- **Gene:** TMSB4X (Xp22.2; synonyms FX, PTMB4, TB4X, TMSB4)
- **CAS:** 77591-33-4
- **Molecular formula (approx.):** C212H350N56O78S
- **INN:** "Timbetasin"
- **N-terminus:** acetylserine, established by automated Edman degradation in the original chemical characterization; the peptide contains two predicted helical regions, and sequence comparison showed no statistically significant homology to other then-known thymic hormones [1, 3].

### 1.2 Endogenous biology as identity context

Tβ4 is the **principal intracellular G-actin–sequestering peptide**: it forms a 1:1 complex with monomeric (globular) actin and thereby buffers the free G-actin pool that feeds filament (F-actin) polymerization, per the equilibrium F-actin ↔ G-actin + Tβ4 ↔ (G-actin·Tβ4) [3]. Its **actin-binding activity is localized to a short internal motif spanning residues 17–23, sequence LKKTETQ** [4] — a fact that will matter immediately below, because that very motif is what the gray-market product turns out to be.

Tβ4 is a major cellular constituent across many tissues, reaching intracellular concentrations in the high-micromolar to ~0.5 mM range, with highest expression in monocytes, granulocytes, and lymphoid tissue; it is also present in extracellular fluids, including tears [3].

### 1.3 The "TB-500" vs Tβ4 disambiguation (CRITICAL)

This is the single most important fact in the entire entry, and it propagates through every downstream section.

**"TB-500" sold as a research chemical is NOT full-length Tβ4.** Analytical characterization of a commercial TB-500 product identified its active ingredient as the **N-terminally acetylated 17–23 fragment of thymosin β4 — Ac-LKKTETQ, a 7-residue peptide** — confirmed by HPLC/high-resolution Orbitrap mass spectrometry and reproduced by solid-phase synthesis [2]. Independent doping-control work on a commercial TB-500 preparation likewise reports the contents as **N-acetylated LKKTETQ** (the actin-binding "active region" of Tβ4), supplied as ~10 mg per vial, with an LC-MS detection method developed for it in equine urine and plasma [5].

The two entities are therefore **chemically distinct molecules**: a 43-mer full protein vs a 7-mer fragment, with different mass and different regulatory/analytical histories. Vendor materials that equate "TB-500" with "Thymosin Beta-4" conflate them [2, 5].

Crucially, the **substantive human clinical efficacy/safety evidence base was generated with full-length Tβ4** — topical Tβ4 in venous ulcers, IV Tβ4 in healthy volunteers, ophthalmic Tβ4 — **not** with the Ac-LKKTETQ 7-mer fragment that vendors market as "TB-500" [6, 7, 8]. No completed, published human RCT of the Ac-LKKTETQ fragment itself has been located (the single registered fragment trial, NCT07487363, is recruiting as of 2026 — see §3).

A note on a persistent vendor error: marketing copy frequently calls TB-500 a "17-amino-acid fragment." The primary analytical literature ([2], [5]) is unambiguous that the marketed product is the **7-residue** Ac-LKKTETQ corresponding to **residues 17–23** of Tβ4. The "17-aa" figure is almost certainly a misreading of "residues 17–23." No primary source supports an actual 17-amino-acid TB-500 species.

### 1.4 Chemistry, synthesis, formulation, stability, reconstitution

The Ac-LKKTETQ fragment is producible by standard solid-phase peptide synthesis (Fmoc/Boc chemistry), as demonstrated when authentic reference material was synthesized to confirm the identity of a TB-500 product [2]. Full-length Tβ4 is formulated for clinical use as an aqueous solution; an ophthalmic formulation (RGN-259) buffers to a near-physiological tear pH (working ranges cited as pH 5.5–7.8) [8].

For injectable lyophilized product, reconstitution/stability arithmetic is covered only by Tier-4 vendor and community dosing-aggregator pages, which by rule cannot ground numerical claims. A common community convention (admin-only, not a pharmacy data sheet) reconstitutes a 10 mg vial with 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water → 5 mg/mL (or 3.0 mL → ~3.33 mg/mL), refrigerated 2–8 °C, used within ~2–6 weeks [15]. No authenticated 503A/503B compounding-pharmacy clinical data sheet exists or was retrievable — an honest limit of the real-world evidence, compounded by the fact that TB-500 is currently non-compoundable (see §4).

### 1.5 Pharmacokinetics (full-length Tβ4 only; species + n + route tagged)

All admissible PK data are for **full-length Tβ4**. There is **no admissible PK for the marketed Ac-LKKTETQ fragment in humans**, and essentially none by the subcutaneous/intramuscular routes vendors recommend.

- **Human, IV, full-length synthetic Tβ4:** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 1 dosed four cohorts of **n=10 healthy volunteers each** with single IV doses of **42, 140, 420, or 1,260 mg**, then the same dose daily for 14 days. PK was dose-proportional with an increasing half-life at higher doses; the drug was well tolerated with no dose-limiting toxicity or serious adverse events [7].
- **Human, IV, recombinant human Tβ4 (NL005):** A first-in-human randomized, double-blind, single-/multiple-ascending-dose Phase 1 in **n=54 (SAD)** Chinese volunteers dosed IV at 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 2.0, 5.0, 12.5, 25.0 µg/kg showed dose-proportional Cmax and AUC, **Tmax ~3–15 min, terminal half-life ~0.5–2.08 h, clearance ~155–189 mL/h/kg**; the multiple-dose arm (**n=30**; 0.5/2.0/5.0 µg/kg daily ×10 d) showed no accumulation [9].
- **Animal (rat), IV:** A Sprague-Dawley rat single-dose IV PK study of a Tβ4 product (**1 mg/kg IV, n=4 male rats**, sampling to 60 min) reported a plasma half-life of **~6.9 ± 3.8 min**, indicating rapid systemic elimination [10]. *Note:* this value is sourced to a clinical-trial regulatory protocol document (NCT05232175, ALT-BB4) rather than a peer-reviewed primary, and is treated as regulatory-grade/provisional, not Tier-1.
- **[route-extrapolation]** Vendor and practitioner materials commonly recommend SC/IM dosing, but the retrievable human PK data above are for **IV full-length Tβ4**. SC/IM bioavailability and half-life for the marketed fragment are NOT established by admissible primary sources — any SC/IM dose guidance crosses both route *and* molecule and should be treated as unsupported [route-extrapolation].

## 2. Mechanism of Action

The mechanistic literature is overwhelmingly on endogenous **full-length Tβ4**. Where a claim rests on a defined fragment (the LKKTET actin-binding motif, or the N-terminal Ac-SDKP tetrapeptide), this is stated explicitly. The honest summary: the *intracellular* actin biology is structurally proven; the *extracellular* signaling biology — which is what would have to underlie injected repair effects — runs through pathways whose **canonical cell-surface receptor remains unidentified.**

### 2.1 G-actin sequestration (the proven primary role)

Tβ4 is the principal intracellular G-actin–sequestering protein: it binds monomeric (G-)actin in a 1:1 complex and inhibits salt-induced polymerization into F-actin, buffering the pool of unpolymerized actin monomers available for filament elongation [1, 6]. Structurally, Tβ4 sequesters actin by **capping both ends** of the actin monomer (longitudinal contacts at the barbed and pointed faces), as resolved at 2 Å in the gelsolin-G1–Tβ4:actin complex; the same structure shows that exchange of actin between Tβ4 and profilin is mediated by a minor overlap in their binding sites — the molecular basis for cells dynamically shifting actin between a sequestered pool and a polymerizable pool [2] (X-ray crystallography, recombinant constructs; single high-resolution structure). Tβ4 belongs to the **β-thymosin/WH2 (WASP-homology 2) structural class**, which informs how it engages the actin monomer [2].

### 2.2 Cell migration / wound-healing signaling

Secreted/exogenous Tβ4 stimulates **directional migration** of keratinocytes, endothelial cells and fibroblasts, enabling cytoskeletal reorganization and movement toward a wound edge — the cellular basis of its wound-repair activity [3, 6]. In a rat full-thickness dermal wound model, topical or intraperitoneal Tβ4 increased re-epithelialization by ~42% at day 4 and up to ~61% at day 7, with increased collagen deposition and angiogenesis; in vitro, keratinocyte/cell migration was stimulated 2–3-fold with as little as ~10 pg Tβ4 [3]. (These numeric magnitudes are from an animal model, not human data.) Tβ4 functions as a **"moonlighting" protein**: the same intracellular actin regulator becomes an extracellular repair signal when released by platelets, macrophages and other cells after injury [4, 7].

### 2.3 Angiogenesis and endothelial effects

Tβ4 is **pro-angiogenic**: it promotes endothelial cell migration, adhesion, tubule formation and aortic-arch ring sprouting, and the **seven-amino-acid actin-binding motif** is necessary and largely sufficient for this angiogenic activity (the isolated motif reproduces angiogenic effects; constructs lacking it lose activity) [5] (HUVEC migration assays; chick aortic-arch sprouting; activity ~50 nM; n not specified). Tβ4 up-regulates **VEGF** by stabilizing **HIF-1α** protein in an oxygen-independent (normoxic) manner — raising VEGF indirectly via increased HIF-1α protein stability rather than transcription [8] (colon cancer; Tβ4/HIF-1α co-localization in a colon cancer tissue microarray plus tumor cell-line work; no specific in-vitro line named; n not specified). It also promotes angiogenic endothelial behavior through **Notch signaling**: blocking Notch1 or Notch4 significantly prevented Tβ4-induced tube formation and transendothelial migration [9] (HUVEC; n not specified). This pro-angiogenic, VEGF-inducing biology is the same biology that underlies the tumor-promotion concern in §8 — benefit and risk share a mechanism.

### 2.4 Anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic mechanisms

Tβ4 suppresses **NF-κB-driven inflammation** by directly targeting the RelA/p65 subunit: overexpressed Tβ4 interferes with TNF-α–induced NF-κB activation and downstream IL-8 transcription, blocking RelA/p65 nuclear translocation and binding to the IL-8 promoter; this effect is **independent of actin binding**. Its focal-adhesion partners PINCH-1 and ILK conversely *enhance* NF-κB activity, and Tβ4 counteracts that sensitization [10] (human corneal epithelial cells, corneal fibroblasts, plus HEKa/COS-7/A7r5 and related lines). In cardiac injury, Tβ4 reduces inflammation and fibrosis by suppressing NF-κB and collagen synthesis, activating Akt phosphorylation and the ILK–PINCH–Parvin complex, reducing macrophage infiltration and limiting fibroblast-to-myofibroblast conversion [11].

A distinct mechanistic branch involves the **N-terminal tetrapeptide Ac-SDKP** (N-acetyl-Ser-Asp-Lys-Pro), an endogenous anti-inflammatory/anti-fibrotic fragment liberated from Tβ4 by a two-enzyme cascade: meprin-α first cleaves the 43-residue Tβ4 into shorter intermediates (<30 aa), then prolyl oligopeptidase (POP) releases Ac-SDKP (POP cannot act on the intact 43-mer directly) [12] (renal-enzyme biochemical study). This is mechanistically important because much of Tβ4's anti-fibrotic benefit in some organ models (notably kidney, §7) is attributable to the **Ac-SDKP metabolite**, not the parent peptide — and the marketed fragment (Ac-LKKTETQ, residues 17–23) is *not* the source of Ac-SDKP (which comes from the N-terminus).

### 2.5 Tissue repair / the ILK–PINCH–Akt axis

Tβ4 activates a pro-survival/pro-migratory module by forming a functional complex with **PINCH1 and integrin-linked kinase (ILK), leading to Akt (PKB) activation**, enhancing cardiomyocyte and endothelial migration and survival. In vivo, after coronary artery ligation, Tβ4 up-regulated ILK and Akt activity in the heart, improved early myocyte survival and improved cardiac function [13] (embryonic + postnatal cardiomyocyte cultures; mouse coronary-ligation MI). Across animal models, Tβ4's repair program integrates cell migration, blood-vessel development, cell survival, stem/progenitor maturation, and down-regulation of inflammatory chemokines/cytokines, documented in dermal, corneal and cardiac repair [14].

### 2.6 Receptor / target ambiguity (honest status)

Tβ4's best-established molecular target is **intracellular G-actin** (§2.1) — structurally proven. By contrast, **a definitive cell-surface receptor mediating Tβ4's extracellular signaling effects has NOT been firmly established** [4, 6]. Candidate/partial targets include **Ku80** (a subunit of ATP-dependent DNA helicase II), which co-immunoprecipitates with Tβ4 in endothelial cells via its C-terminal arm domain and mediates an intracellular activity (PAI-1 induction) distinct from G-actin sequestering and independent of the Ac-SDKP fragment [15] (endothelial cells); plus downstream pathway components such as ILK/PINCH (§2.5) and Notch (§2.3). These are best read as **binding partners / pathway intermediaries, not a validated canonical Tβ4 receptor**. The receptor question remains genuinely open — a meaningful caveat for any claim that an injected peptide produces specific receptor-mediated repair.

## 3. Human Clinical Evidence

Nearly all human clinical work uses **full-length Tβ4** — the 43-aa peptide (RegeneRx programs RGN-259 / RGN-352 / RGN-137) or a 44-aa recombinant version (NL005, Beijing Northland). Until 2026 there were **zero registered human trials of the "TB-500" fragment.** The one fragment trial that now exists (NCT07487363) is cardiovascular, Phase 1/2, recruiting, with no results.

### 3.1 The bottom line, stated first

**There is NO completed human RCT with posted/published positive efficacy results that met its pre-specified primary endpoint for ANY Tβ4 indication.** Across the 20 registered studies catalogued in the §3.9 trial table (19 distinct NCTs + 1 non-NCT EU Guarnera RCT), the strongest signals (dry eye, neurotrophic keratopathy, cardiac) are either small Phase 2 trials, Phase 3 trials that *missed* their primary/co-primary endpoints, or completed trials whose results have not been posted [1, 2, 6].

### 3.2 Ophthalmic — RGN-259, dry eye (the largest program; Phase 3 co-primaries NOT met)

ARISE-1 (NCT02597803, Phase 2/3, n=317, completed 2016) and ARISE-2 (NCT02974907, Phase 3, n=601, completed 2018) showed dose-dependent symptom signals; **ARISE-3 (NCT03937882, Phase 3, n=700, completed Oct 2021) failed its pre-specified co-primary endpoints**, with only some secondary/subgroup signs (ocular grittiness, corneal staining in subgroups) reaching significance. Sponsor: ReGenTree (a RegeneRx joint venture). No posted registry results for ARISE-3 [2, 3, 4].

### 3.3 Ophthalmic — neurotrophic keratopathy (SEER-1 narrowly missed)

**SEER-1 (NCT02600429, Phase 3, n=18 [10 drug / 8 placebo], terminated for a business decision)** reported **60% complete corneal healing on 0.1% RGN-259 vs 12.5% placebo at Day 29, p=0.0656 — not significant** on the pre-specified test (an alternative chi-square gave p=0.040). Published peer-reviewed [5]. Confirmatory trials SEER-2 (NCT05555589, Phase 3, n≈70, recruiting, est. completion 2026) and SEER-3 are ongoing.

### 3.4 Ophthalmic — severe dry eye / GVHD (positive small Phase 2)

A Phase 2 randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled trial (**n=9 patients, 12 eyes treated**) of 0.1% Tβ4 drops showed significant improvement vs vehicle: **35.1% reduction in ocular discomfort (p=0.0141)** and **59.1% reduction in corneal fluorescein staining (p=0.0108)** by day 56 [6]. Tiny n; hypothesis-generating. The publication (PMID 25826322) cites no NCT; its most plausible registry counterpart is NCT01393132 (best-fit inference, not a registry-confirmed mapping; NCT01387347 is a separate larger ReGenTree dry-eye study, not this n=9 publication).

### 3.5 Dermal — RGN-137 (press-release-only efficacy)

- **Venous stasis ulcers:** NCT00832091 (Phase 2, n=72, completed Jan 2009) — drug safe/well-tolerated (primary objective met); mid-dose "appeared most active" per the sponsor *press release*; no registry results, no peer-reviewed trial paper [7, 8].
- **Pressure ulcers:** NCT00382174 (Phase 2, n=72, completed Dec 2008) — safe/well-tolerated; mid-dose most active per sponsor *press release*; no registry results, no peer-reviewed paper [9, 10].
- **Epidermolysis bullosa:** NCT00311766 (Phase 2, n=30 target, terminated 2012 for lack of patients / drug expiry) — orphan-designated; no usable efficacy results [11].

The strongest dermal datapoint is the earlier **European topical Tβ4 venous-ulcer work (Guarnera)** — an 8-site European (Italy/Poland) prospective randomized double-blind **dose-escalation** study reporting safety/tolerability and a healing-enhancement signal, across two publications (2007, 2010) [12, 13]. But it was a dose-escalation/safety study with efficacy only as a secondary aim — not a powered efficacy RCT.

### 3.6 The "TB-500" fragment — exactly ONE registered human trial, and it is cardiovascular

**NCT07487363 (Hudson Biotech): "Phase 1/2, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, sequential dose-escalation study of TB-500 (Thymosin Beta 4 17-23 Fragment)"** for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease / endothelial dysfunction. Phase 1/2, **n=80 (estimated), status: recruiting (started 5 Feb 2026)**; primary endpoints are safety (TEAEs/SAEs); exploratory secondaries = flow-mediated dilation, hs-CRP, NT-proBNP, vascular stiffness. **No results posted.** This is the first-ever registered human trial of the actual "TB-500" molecule — and it is **NOT** for muscle/tendon/athletic use [14].

### 3.7 Cardiac — RGN-352 (West) and NL005 (China)

- **RGN-352 (RegeneRx, injectable Tβ4):** NO completed Phase 2 in the West. Two registered RGN-352 records — NCT00743769 (Phase 1, healthy volunteers) and NCT01311518 (Phase 2, acute MI/STEMI) — are both listed **Withdrawn (n=0)**. The U.S. RGN-352 Phase 2 was placed on FDA clinical hold [15, 16].
- **NL005 (Beijing Northland, recombinant 44-aa Tβ4):** A published Phase 1 (SAD n=54 / MAD n=30) in 2021 reported no SAEs/DLTs and dose-proportional PK (NCT04555824 / NCT04555850) [17]. Phase 2/2b AMI trials **NCT05485818 (n=62, completed 2021)** and **NCT05984134 (n=90, completed May 2023, primary = % change in infarct area by cardiac MRI)** are **completed but have NO posted results (hasResults=false)** [18, 19]; a further Phase 2 (NCT07586865, n=189) is not yet recruiting.

### 3.8 The athletic use case — no human evidence at all

**There is NO human efficacy evidence — no completed trial, no registered trial — for the athletic / musculoskeletal "TB-500" use case** (muscle tear, tendon/ligament repair, sports recovery). All musculoskeletal data are preclinical (rodent/rabbit). Tβ4/TB-500 is not FDA-approved for any human therapeutic use [20, 14].

### 3.10 The conflation trap

Buyers and vendors cite full-length Tβ4 clinical trials (RGN-259 dry eye, NL005 cardiac) as if they validate the injectable "TB-500" fragment. They do not — **different molecule, different (non-athletic) indications, and even the full-peptide trials have not produced an endpoint-meeting, results-posted efficacy win.**

## 4. Regulatory Status

### 4.1 FDA — not approved; removed from 503A Category 2 but not approved or compoundable

**Tβ4 is not FDA-approved for any indication** (as of 2026). RGN-259 (ophthalmic) holds FDA orphan-drug designations (neurotrophic keratopathy; epidermolysis bullosa) and has completed Phase II/III ophthalmic trials without marketing approval. The systemic IV program for acute MI (RGN-352, NCT01311518, Phase 2) was withdrawn / never initiated (enrollment 0) — there is no completed controlled human trial of systemic Tβ4 for a systemic indication [3, 2].

On compounding: in **April 2026 the FDA REMOVED thymosin beta-4 / TB-500 from the 503A interim Category 2 bulk-substances list** (notice published ~Apr 15–16, 2026; effective within 7 calendar days). TB-500 had been placed on **Category 2 in September 2023** over significant-safety-risk concerns (immunogenicity, impurities, limited human clinical data); that listing is now rescinded. **Removal from Category 2 is a procedural step — it is NOT FDA approval and NOT an affirmative green-light for compounding.** TB-500 remains **non-compoundable** pending rulemaking, because it is neither FDA-approved nor on the 503A **Category-1 (permitted)** list. A **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) meeting is scheduled for July 23–24, 2026** (Federal Register notice Apr 16, 2026, **FR doc 2026-07361**) to evaluate TB-500 (alongside BPC-157, KPV, MOTs-C) for potential Category-1 listing; that decision had not occurred as of this writing. (This mirrors the parallel FDA action on BPC-157 in the same window.) [9, 12]

**Net effect: not lawfully available as a standard FDA-approved drug; not currently compoundable.**

### 4.2 WADA — prohibited at all times

Tβ4/TB-500 is **prohibited at all times (in- and out-of-competition)** on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List, class **S2.3 "Growth Factors and Growth Factor Modulators."** Verbatim list text: *"Thymosin-ß4 and its derivatives e.g. TB-500."* The S2 class is designated **non-Specified Substances**; the list is valid from 1 January 2026 [10]. A positive test carries sanctions. (A detection window in the ~30–45 day range appears in secondary anti-doping commentary only and is not asserted here as fact.)

## 5. Concentration Audit & Commercial Conflict-of-Interest (FIRST-CLASS)

This is surfaced ahead of the efficacy detail because it materially conditions how the human evidence should be read.

### 5.1 Method and finding

The in-vivo efficacy literature was characterized by (1) identifying the foundational reviews and the clinical-translation program, (2) tracing the principal preclinical author clusters across the major domains (cardiac, corneal/ophthalmic, dermal/diabetic wound, renal/hepatic fibrosis), and (3) using a recent comprehensive review's reference base (Xing et al. 2021, ~133 refs) as a proxy for breadth and institutional diversity. This is a reasoned estimate, not a PRISMA-style enumeration.

**Estimated single-lab share of in-vivo efficacy primaries: ~20–35% for the single largest cluster (Goldstein/RegeneRx + its corneal/dermal sub-groups). NO single lab reaches the ≥70% HALT threshold.** The Xing 2021 review spans ~133 references across North America (US/NIH), Europe (France/INSERM), and Asia (predominantly China, plus Korea), drawn from many independent investigator groups with no single institution monopolizing the literature [9, deduped single-lab share]. **The field is decentralized at the primary-data level — concentration-HALT NOT triggered.**

### 5.2 The real provenance concern: clinical-translation concentration + COI

> ⚠ **COI / translation-concentration flag (not a concentration-HALT).** While the *preclinical primary literature* is diverse, the **foundational synthesizing reviews and 100% of the human clinical-translation program are concentrated in the Goldstein/RegeneRx commercial cluster**, where the lead figure — Allan L. Goldstein — is **Chairman, Chief Scientific Advisor, patent inventor, and stockholder of RegeneRx Biopharmaceuticals**. Any "Tβ4 works in humans" inference rests disproportionately on a single commercially-conflicted nexus, and the marquee cardiac human trial (RGN-352, NCT01311518) was **withdrawn / never initiated**. Treat human-efficacy framing accordingly; do not let preclinical diversity launder the clinical-evidence concentration.

So the honest distinction is: the *animal* literature is decentralized (good), but the *human-translation* story is single-nexus and commercially conflicted (a serious caveat). The decentralization of the preclinical base should not be read as independent confirmation of any human-efficacy claim.

### 5.3 Author-cluster detail

- **Goldstein / RegeneRx cluster** (the "discovery + translation" group): owns the foundational multi-application reviews and the entire human clinical program (RGN-259 ophthalmic, RGN-352 systemic/cardiac, RGN-137 dermal). Hannappel, Sosne, and Kleinman recur here.
- **Cardiac:** Bock-Marquette / Srivastava / Dimaio (ILK-Akt cardioprotection) and, separately, the **independent UK Riley/Smart group** (epicardial progenitor mobilization).
- **Corneal/ophthalmic:** Sosne & Kleinman (overlaps the Goldstein cluster).
- **Independent international groups (no RegeneRx tie evident):** Kim & Kwon (Chonbuk National University, Korea — diabetic burn-wound healing); Chinese renal-fibrosis groups; numerous Chinese/Korean dermal/hepatic/renal groups surveyed in Xing 2021.

## 6. Why This Lands at evidence_tier C

The grading reflects a genuine split between a *rich, broadly-replicated mechanistic/animal base* and a *thin, unconvincing, commercially-concentrated human base for a molecule that is probably not even what is sold.*

Reasons it is not higher than C:

1. **No endpoint-meeting human RCT for any indication.** The flagship Phase 3 (ARISE-3 dry eye, n=700) missed its co-primaries; the Phase 3 NK trial (SEER-1, n=18) narrowly missed (p=0.0656); positive trials are small Phase 2s (n=9). Cardiac Phase 2/2b trials are completed with no posted results.
2. **Zero human evidence for the marketed use case.** No completed or registered human trial exists for muscle/tendon/ligament/sports recovery.
3. **Molecular-identity gap.** The marketed product is, by assay, the Ac-LKKTETQ 7-mer fragment, while essentially all evidence is for full-length Tβ4 — so even the indirect evidence may not transfer.
4. **Commercial concentration of the human program** in the Goldstein/RegeneRx nexus, with the marquee systemic trial withdrawn.

Reasons it is not lower than C:

1. **Structurally proven core mechanism** (G-actin sequestration) and a coherent, repeatedly-demonstrated pro-migratory/pro-angiogenic/anti-inflammatory repair program in animals across multiple independent labs and tissues.
2. **Some cross-lab and independent-group preclinical support** in cardiac (anti-fibrotic/vascular), ophthalmic, and dermal domains.
3. **A clean human PK/safety Phase 1 base** (IV full-length Tβ4) showing mild-to-moderate, self-limiting AEs with no DLTs to 25 µg/kg.

Net: mechanistically plausible and animal-supported, but **human efficacy is unproven for everything and entirely absent for the use it is sold for** — a textbook evidence_tier C.

## 7. Preclinical Efficacy by Domain

The published preclinical literature studies **full-length Tβ4** (and in some cardiac/eye work, recombinant human Tβ4 or clinical-grade RGN-259) — or, occasionally, the **LKKTETQ heptapeptide**. **No admissible peer-reviewed efficacy study used a product called "TB-500."** Every animal claim carries species + n + route where reported; extrapolation to the gray-market product is, in every case, an unproven leap.

### 7.1 Musculoskeletal

**Tendon — the headline gap.** **There is NO admissible in-vivo tendon-injury efficacy study** (Achilles, patellar, rotator-cuff, flexor) for Tβ4/TB-500, despite TB-500's heavy marketing for tendinopathy. The closest tendon-directed evidence is **in-vitro tissue-engineering** work: Tβ4 incorporated into electrospun PLGA/PLA nanofiber/microfiber yarns dose-dependently increased tenogenic markers (scleraxis SCX, tenomodulin TNMD, tenascin-C TNC) and promoted migration/proliferation of human adipose-derived MSCs, with topography + Tβ4 more effective than either alone [1]. This is **cell-culture differentiation data only — no tensile/biomechanical or in-vivo tendon outcome.** This is the single largest gap relative to the compound's reputation.

**Ligament (single study, independent lab).** Local Tβ4 (1 µg in 100 µL fibrin sealant placed in the ligament gap) improved healing of a sharply transected **medial collateral ligament (MCL)** in **rats (Sprague-Dawley; n not reported)** [2]; treated MCL showed more uniform, evenly-spaced collagen bundles and significantly increased collagen fibril diameter, and femur-MCL-tibia complexes had significantly better biomechanical (tensile) properties at 4 weeks vs control [2] [route-extrapolation: local fibrin depot, not systemic SC]. This is the single strongest musculoskeletal efficacy signal in the corpus, but rests on **one study from one lab**, with no n or effect-size magnitudes in the abstract.

**Skeletal muscle — histology without function.** After skeletal muscle injury in mice, endogenous Tβ4 (and Tβ10) mRNA is up-regulated early in regenerating fibers and infiltrating cells (**mice, C57BL; n not reported**) [3]; Tβ4 — more potently its sulfoxidized form — acts as a **chemoattractant for myoblasts** in vitro (C2C12 + primary mouse myoblasts) [3]. The best-controlled in-vivo muscle study: chronic systemic Tβ4 (**150 µg IP, twice weekly, 6 months**) in the **mdx dystrophic mouse** (Duchenne model) significantly increased regenerating skeletal muscle fibers (**11.6 ± 13.5 vs 2.6 ± 1.1 untreated; p = 0.03; histology n = 9 treated mdx / 7 untreated mdx**, gastrocnemius) [4]. **But in the same study Tβ4 produced NO significant improvement in grip strength, skeletal muscle function, cardiac systolic function, or fibrosis** (fibrosis n = 3–5/group; CK cohort n = 8/10 mdx — a distinct cohort) [4]. **Histological regeneration did not translate to functional benefit** — a critical honest qualifier for any "muscle recovery" claim.

### 7.2 Wound / dermal

Topical or intraperitoneal Tβ4 increased **re-epithelialization by 42% (day 4) and up to 61% (day 7)** and wound contraction by ≥11% (day 7) vs saline in a **rat full-thickness (8 mm punch) model** (5 µg/50 µL PBS; **rat; n not reported**), plus increased collagen deposition, angiogenesis, and 2–3-fold keratinocyte migration in vitro [5]. Tβ4 also accelerated repair in **db/db diabetic AND aged mice**, with the **LKKTETQ heptapeptide alone reproducing the effect in aged mice** (**mice; n not reported**) [6]; improved **deep second-degree burn** healing in db/db mice (5 mg/kg intradermal, twice weekly × 2 weeks; RAGE downregulation; n not reported) [7] [route-extrapolation]; and a **dimeric Tβ4 (DTβ4)** outperformed monomeric Tβ4 on topical wound closure in **Sprague-Dawley rats (n = 8/group)** [8] — though the marketed entity is monomeric, so the DTβ4 advantage does not transfer.

**Provenance caveat:** the dermal corpus is **single-lineage-dominated (Goldstein/Kleinman, with a RegeneRx commercial flag)**; independent dermal replication exists [7, 8] but in *different* models (burn, dimer), not a clean independent replication of the foundational full-thickness result. Effective doses span local-depot, intradermal mg/kg, IP, and topical — **none matches the fixed-mass subcutaneous self-administration TB-500 users employ** [route-extrapolation].

### 7.3 Cardiac — including the reprogramming replication FAILURE

The cardiac story is where the most important integrity signal lives. In the **foundational study**, Tβ4 mobilized adult epicardium-derived progenitor cells, induced neovascularization, and was shown essential for embryonic coronary vessel development (**mouse; Smart/Riley group, UCL; n not enumerated**) [1-D] — the origin of the "Tβ4 regrows heart muscle" narrative. Subsequent positive results: sustained **intramyocardial** delivery of Tβ4 (10 µg) from a self-assembling peptide scaffold increased epicardial EPDCs, microvessel density, reduced scar and improved ejection fraction 4 weeks post-MI (**transgenic mice; n≥8/group; intramyocardial, peri-infarct**) [2-D]; and systemic **AAV-delivered** Tβ4 reduced post-MI oxidative stress/fibrosis and improved function (**LVEF 74.3±3.2% vs 66.8±5.4%; male C57BL/6J; n=7/group; IP AAV, 4×10¹⁰ vg**) [3-D].

**The replication failure:** a genetic lineage-tracing study found that Tβ4 given **AFTER MI** increased epicardial thickness and coronary capillary density but did **NOT** reprogram epicardium-derived cells into cardiomyocytes — treated cells became fibroblasts and smooth muscle, not myocytes (**mice, Wt1CreERT2 lineage tracing; clinical-grade Tβ4 daily ×1 week post-MI; independent Pu/Zhou lab, Boston Children's**) [4-D]. The authors note Smart's reprogramming claim involved **PRE-MI "priming,"** and post-MI dosing did not reproduce it. **This is a documented cross-lab contradiction of the strongest mechanistic cardiac claim.** The surviving cardiac benefits are vascular/anti-fibrotic/anti-oxidant — **not myocyte regeneration** — and the strongest positive results used AAV gene delivery or an intramyocardial scaffold, not the systemic injection route TB-500 users employ.

### 7.4 Neurological — single-lab (Chopp)

Essentially all neuro evidence comes from the **Chopp group (Henry Ford Hospital)** — un-replicated across labs. Delayed Tβ4 reduced infarct volume in aged rats (**12.77±3.61% vs 26.04±4.29%, p<0.05**) but produced **no behavioral improvement at day 56** (aged male Wistar rats; 12 mg/kg IP; Tβ4 n=11, control n=12) [5-D] — anatomic benefit without functional benefit. Tβ4 initiated 6 h after **traumatic brain injury** reduced cortical lesion volume (30 mg/kg), hippocampal cell loss, lowered mNSS and improved Morris water maze learning (young adult male Wistar rats; saline n=7, Tβ4 6 mg/kg n=8, 30 mg/kg n=8, sham n=6; IP) [6-D]. In **EAE (MS model)**, Tβ4 produced ~50% relative functional recovery, reduced inflammatory infiltrates (3.6±0.3 vs 5±0.5/slide, p<0.05), and increased oligodendrocyte progenitors and mature oligodendrocytes (SJL/J mice; Tβ4 n=10, saline n=11; 6 mg/kg) [7-D], mechanistically via p38 MAPK upregulation [8-D]. **Treat all neuro effect sizes as un-replicated-across-labs.**

### 7.5 Ophthalmic

**RGN-259 (clinical-grade topical Tβ4)** matched or exceeded prescription dry-eye drugs in a desiccation-stress model: corneal fluorescein staining reduced 51.8% (4×/day) and 33.6% (2×/day) vs 17.6% cyclosporine A / 15.1% diquafosol; tear production raised ~9×; goblet cells 2.0–2.4× (**NOD.B10-H2b mice; n=4–5/group; topical 5 µL/eye, 10 days**) [9-D]. Recombinant human Tβ4 eye drops alleviated benzalkonium-chloride-induced dry eye (improved tear volume, reduced corneal staining, increased goblet-cell density, reduced CD4+ T cells and pro-inflammatory cytokines, via NF-κB inhibition; **female C57BL/6 mice; n=6/group; topical 0.05%/0.1%, 4×/day, 7 days**) [10-D]. Ophthalmic has some cross-lab support (independent Chinese group + Sosne/RegeneRx-affiliated work).

### 7.6 Other organ systems

**Liver:** Tβ4 (1 mg/kg IP daily ×1 wk) prevented oxidative stress, inflammation and fibrosis in ethanol+LPS-induced injury (ROS down ~41–54%, Sirius Red down 80–88%, hydroxyproline down 28–50%; **female C57BL/6 mice; n=4/group**) [11-D] (very small n). **Kidney:** Tβ4 and its degradation product **Ac-SDKP** are reparative in renal fibrosis (UUO model) — but **much of the benefit is attributable to the Ac-SDKP metabolite**, and the outcome is PAI-1-status-dependent: Ac-SDKP reduced fibrosis in both WT and PAI-1-KO mice; Tβ4 alone reduced late fibrosis only in WT; and Tβ4 + a prolyl-oligopeptidase inhibitor (blocking Ac-SDKP generation) **increased** fibrosis (**WT C57BL and PAI-1 KO mice; n=5–10/group; IP**) [12-D]. Not a clean "Tβ4 is renoprotective" story — and again, the Ac-SDKP-dependent benefit would not transfer to the marketed Ac-LKKTETQ fragment, which is not the Ac-SDKP source.

### 7.7 Preclinical honest-limits summary

Small n throughout (frequently n=4–8/group); single-lab dominance in neuro and in dermal; the flagship cardiac claim is a replication failure; route/modality mismatch with how TB-500 is used; and an unresolved product-identity gap (no cited study used a verified gray-market "TB-500" product).

## 8. Safety, Adverse Events & Contraindications

**Human safety data is thin and indirect.** No controlled human trial of the gray-market "TB-500" fragment itself exists; human safety is inferred from full-length Tβ4, and that inference is itself a limitation.

### 8.1 Human adverse-event data (full-length Tβ4)

- **IV Phase 1 (recombinant hTβ4), single ascending dose** (54 healthy adults, 0.05–25 µg/kg IV, 4:1 drug:placebo): AEs in **25/44 (56.8%) Tβ4 vs 7/10 (70%) placebo; all mild–moderate; no SAEs, no dose-limiting toxicities** to 25 µg/kg [1].
- **Multiple ascending dose arm** (30 healthy adults, 0.5/2.0/5.0 µg/kg IV daily ×10 days): AEs in **16/24 (66.7%) Tβ4 vs 5/6 (83.3%) placebo; all mild–moderate, self-resolving; no SAEs, no DLT** [1].
- **Phase 3 ophthalmic NK** (0.1% RGN-259, n=18; 10 active, 8 placebo): **16 AEs across 7 subjects; treatment-group burden (40%) ≈ placebo (37.5%); 1 treatment-related AE; 1 (non-ocular, unrelated) serious AE; all resolved** [2].
- **Aggregate ophthalmic program** (~425 subjects across 5 RGN-259 studies, a sponsor-reported pooled figure): reported safe/well-tolerated, predominantly mild, transient, local (ocular) AEs [2].
- **Early systemic Phase 1:** three uncommon, possibly-related events — **dizziness, headache, pyrexia (1 subject each)** [4] (Tier-3 lead; corroborates the mild-systemic-AE pattern).

### 8.2 The tumor signal — real and bidirectional

This is the most consequential safety consideration.

**Pro-tumor direction:** In a mouse model, Tβ4 over-expression (adenoviral) increased **B16-F10 melanoma metastatic lung nodules to mean 46.7 (95% CI 35.0–57.7) vs 10.9 (6.2–15.6) in controls (P<.001)**; increased 20-day tumor size (21.7 mm vs 13.3 mm, P=.036); increased tumor-cell migration 2.3-fold and intratumoral blood-vessel density 4.4-fold, associated with VEGF induction (**mouse, C57BL/6, n≈20 IV cohort; in-vitro migration assays**) [5]. This is mechanism-consistent: the same pro-angiogenic, VEGF-inducing, pro-migratory biology that drives wound repair (§2.3) can drive metastasis.

> **Mechanism-of-evidence caveat (Cha et al. 2003 [5]).** This pro-metastatic signal comes from a **genetic over-expression construct** — tumor cells were engineered (adenovirally) to *constitutively over-express Tβ4 from within the cell*, producing sustained, supraphysiologic intracellular/autocrine levels. That is **not** the same exposure as **exogenous, intermittent, subcutaneous dosing of a peptide** (and, for the gray-market product, dosing of the *Ac-LKKTETQ fragment*, not full-length Tβ4 at all). Continuous tumor-intrinsic over-expression and pulsed extracellular dosing differ in level, duration, cellular compartment, and molecule, so the **quantitative human relevance of the 46.7-vs-10.9 effect to a person injecting TB-500 is genuinely uncertain** — it may overstate or understate real-world risk. The contraindication below is retained as the **prudent** default for a non-approved, self-administered, pro-angiogenic peptide, but it rests on a mechanism-plausibility / over-expression inference, **not** on demonstrated exogenous-dosing carcinogenicity in humans (for which there is no data at all).

**Tumor-suppressor direction (counter-evidence):** In **multiple myeloma**, Tβ4 behaves as a tumor *suppressor*: expression significantly lower in myeloma vs normal plasma cells (P<0.001); over-expression decreased proliferation/migration and increased apoptosis sensitivity; below-median expression predicted shorter disease-free survival in the high-dose-therapy/ASCT survival subset (**n=209** of the 298-patient cohort; **26.2 vs 37.6 months, P<0.05**); Tβ4-over-expressing myeloma cells prolonged mouse survival (**88.9 vs 65.9 days, P<0.05**) [6].

So the direction is **genuinely context-dependent and cancer-type-dependent** — but the net signal in **solid tumors leans pro-metastatic.** There is **no human carcinogenicity / longitudinal cancer-incidence data** for exogenous Tβ4. The conservative read for a non-approved, self-administered, pro-angiogenic peptide is that **active or recent solid malignancy and undiagnosed masses are a contraindication.**

### 8.3 Immunogenicity, preclinical tox, anti-fibrotic context

A peptide carries inherent immunogenicity potential, and gray-market "research-only" vials are not quality-controlled (impurities, mislabeling, no sterility assurance). Immunogenicity/impurities/limited-human-data were the **historical** rationale for FDA's Sept-2023 interim Category-2 listing — a listing **removed in April 2026** — so the caveat is anchored to peptide/product biology, not to a current restriction [9]. A documented human neutralizing-antibody clinical AE rate was **not** located; it appears as a theoretical/regulatory concern, not a measured clinical event rate.

Sponsor/program documentation describes ~23–24 completed nonclinical toxicology and safety-pharmacology studies (including rat and rabbit developmental tox with no maternal or embryo/fetal effects reported) supporting intended clinical use; specific NOAEL values are redacted in public filings, and the underlying reports are not independently published (Tier-3, sponsor-asserted) [4, 8]. The **anti-fibrotic** activity (reduced fibrosis in lung/kidney/heart animal models via lower macrophage infiltration, TGF-β/CTGF, fibroblast→myofibroblast conversion) [7] shares the same vascular/remodeling biology that underlies both the stated benefit and the tumor concern.

### 8.4 Contraindications & risk-floor (harm-reduction framing, not endorsement)

- **Active or history of solid malignancy / elevated cancer risk / undiagnosed masses → avoid** (pro-angiogenic; pro-metastatic in a solid-tumor animal model) [5, 6]. *Prudent-default caveat:* the pro-metastatic datum [5] is a **genetic over-expression** construct, not exogenous peptide dosing, so its human-dosing relevance is uncertain (see §8.2) — the contraindication is retained as conservative risk-floor, not as proven exogenous-dosing harm.
- **Anyone subject to anti-doping testing → categorical contraindication** (WADA-banned at all times, non-Specified) [10].
- **Pregnancy/lactation → avoid** (no human reproductive safety data) [3, 4].
- **No established human dosing / no systemic safety regulatory file.** The only completed systemic human safety data is one healthy-volunteer Phase 1 to 25 µg/kg IV [1]; the systemic therapeutic Phase 2 was withdrawn [3]; there is **no long-term human safety data** and **no human data on the gray-market fragment** specifically — treat any systemic self-administration as unstudied [1, 3].
- **Product-quality risk:** monitor for hypersensitivity/injection-site reactions; stop on systemic allergic signs [9].
- **If used despite the above:** baseline and periodic clinical review for new/growing lesions given the angiogenesis signal; watch for dizziness, headache, pyrexia and mild–moderate self-limited AEs seen in trials. **No validated lab-monitoring panel exists** [1, 5].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics, Dose, Route & Formulation

### 9.1 PK — full-length Tβ4 only

(See §1.5 for the primary data.) Summary: human IV PK exists for full-length synthetic Tβ4 (42–1,260 mg, dose-proportional, increasing t½) [7] and recombinant hTβ4 (NL005: Tmax ~3–15 min, terminal t½ ~0.5–2.08 h, CL ~155–189 mL/h/kg, no accumulation) [9]; rat IV t½ ~6.9 min (regulatory-grade, provisional) [10]. **There is NO human PK for the marketed Ac-LKKTETQ fragment, and essentially no SC/IM PK for any form.** Any half-life or dose extrapolation from full Tβ4 to the fragment crosses both molecule and (usually) route and is unsupported.

### 9.2 Dose / route — practitioner conventions, explicitly NOT efficacy

The following are **prescribing conventions only** — dose/cycle/route as stated by named practitioners/venues. **No efficacy or AE-rate is inferred from these.** [practitioner_protocol]

- **Route:** subcutaneous injection (most common; IM also described).
- **Loading phase:** ~2–5 mg twice weekly (≈4–10 mg/week) for 4–6 weeks.
- **Maintenance phase:** ~2 mg (≤2.5 mg) once weekly (or every 1–2 weeks).
- **Cycling (per A4M):** do not use continuously >3 months without a break — e.g., "3 months on, 6 weeks off" or "6 weeks on, 6 weeks off" [17].
- Because TB-500 is not FDA-approved, practitioners universally state there is **no standardized/validated dose**; any protocol is individualized and physician-supervised.

Sources: William A. Seeds, MD, *Peptide Protocols: Volume One* (2020) [16]; A4M "Thymosin beta 4" professional monograph + Peptide Therapy Certification [17]; general A4M/IPS-adjacent practitioner convention [18]. Provenance of all three is verified; the verbatim dose tables were **not** machine-extractable, so the mg/cycle figures above are attributed to the broader practitioner/A4M *convention*, not quoted from any primary text. (A requested Kent Holtorf protocol could not be verified to a citable venue+date and was deliberately not fabricated.)

### 9.3 Formulation / reconstitution (admin-only, Tier-4)

Ophthalmic clinical product (RGN-259) is full-length Tβ4 buffered to near-physiological tear pH 5.5–7.8 [8]. For injectable product, a community reconstitution convention is 10 mg vial + 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water → 5 mg/mL, refrigerated 2–8 °C, used within ~2–6 weeks [15] — **not** an authenticated pharmacy data sheet (none exists/was retrievable). Vendor purity claims (e.g., ≥98%, 5/10 mg vials, "Not for injection / clinical use") are self-reported and used for identity/reconstitution math only, never efficacy [13]. Independent reporting describes **variability in actual peptide content vs label, sequence identity, purity, and microbial/endotoxin contamination** across gray-market vendors — though for the contamination/underdosing claim specifically, no single named, methodologically-described multi-vendor purity-audit dataset was located; it rests on secondary-aggregate reporting [14]. The one Tier-1 *identity* assay (Esposito 2012) found a marketed "TB-500" to be the Ac-LKKTETQ fragment [2].

## 10. N=1 / Trial-Design Considerations (goal-agnostic)

For anyone evaluating self-experimentation, the structural problems are severe enough that a meaningful personal trial is close to impossible:

1. **You cannot verify what you have.** Gray-market "TB-500" is, by assay, often the Ac-LKKTETQ fragment, not full-length Tβ4 — and content/purity vary by vendor. An N=1 on an unverified molecule has no interpretable input. Independent third-party HPLC/MS identity and purity testing of the actual vial would be a minimum prerequisite, and even that does not validate sterility/endotoxin.
2. **No validated endpoint exists for the marketed use.** There is no human efficacy precedent for muscle/tendon/ligament/sports recovery, so there is no established, sensitive, time-anchored outcome measure to track — only subjective recovery impressions, which are highly placebo-susceptible for an injected peptide.
3. **No human dose-response, no SC/IM PK.** The practitioner conventions are not efficacy-anchored; there is no validated dose, route, or cycle, so any self-protocol is uncalibrated.
4. **The relevant risk is latent and serious.** The dominant safety concern (solid-tumor pro-metastatic signal) would not surface within any short personal trial window, making "I felt fine" uninformative about the actual risk.
5. **Confounded by legality/eligibility.** WADA-prohibited at all times; non-compoundable; not approved. Tested athletes are categorically excluded.

If, despite all of this, use is contemplated under physician supervision, the only defensible design elements are: documented third-party identity/purity assay of the exact lot; baseline malignancy screening and exclusion of active/recent solid cancer; pre-registered, objective, time-anchored outcome(s); injection-site and hypersensitivity monitoring; and a defined stop rule. None of this overcomes the absence of an interpretable efficacy endpoint.

## 11. Bottom Line — Supported vs Marketed

**What is supported:**

- Tβ4 is, mechanistically, a genuine actin-sequestering, pro-migratory, pro-angiogenic, anti-inflammatory/anti-fibrotic repair peptide — structurally proven at the actin level and broadly demonstrated in animals across dermal, corneal, cardiac, neuro, hepatic and renal injury models, often by independent labs.
- Full-length Tβ4 has a clean short-term human IV safety/PK profile (mild–moderate self-limiting AEs, no DLTs to 25 µg/kg).
- Some human ophthalmic signals exist (a small positive Phase 2 in severe dry eye; a narrow Phase 3 miss in neurotrophic keratopathy).

**What is marketed but NOT supported:**

- That "TB-500" is "clinically studied thymosin beta-4" — the marketed product is, by assay, the Ac-LKKTETQ fragment, a different molecule from the one in the literature.
- That it heals tendons, ligaments, and muscle tears or speeds athletic recovery — there is **zero** human evidence and **no in-vivo tendon study at all**; the best muscle study showed histological regeneration **without** functional benefit.
- That its preclinical breadth implies human efficacy — no Tβ4 trial has met its primary endpoint for any indication, and the human-translation program is concentrated in a single commercially-conflicted nexus.
- That it is "FDA-cleared" or now "legal to compound" — it is neither approved nor compoundable; April-2026 removal from Category 2 is not approval.

**One sentence:** TB-500 is a mechanistically plausible, animal-supported tissue-repair peptide whose marketed gray-market product is a *different molecule* (the Ac-LKKTETQ fragment) than the full-length Tβ4 actually studied, for which **no human trial has ever met a primary endpoint, there is zero human evidence for the athletic use it is sold for, a real bidirectional (net solid-tumor pro-metastatic) cancer signal exists, and it is non-approved, non-compoundable, and WADA-banned at all times.**
