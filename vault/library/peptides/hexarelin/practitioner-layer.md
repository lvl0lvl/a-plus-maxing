# Hexarelin (Examorelin) — Prescribing-Practice Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how hexarelin is actually prescribed, dosed, and reconstituted in practice* — the dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions circulating among peptide clinics and vendor channels. It is **not** an efficacy document and **must not** be read as one. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol`, `compounding_data_sheet`, and `vendor_label` sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy or adverse-event-rate claim, and none appears here for that purpose. The efficacy state — acute GH-release pharmacology, tachyphylaxis (~45% GH-AUC decline over 16 weeks with no IGF-1/IGFBP-3/body-composition change), the non-selective cortisol/ACTH/prolactin profile, and the preclinical-dominant cardiac line — lives in [[library/peptides/hexarelin/research-report]] (§2–§3) and is unaffected by anything below.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** Hexarelin (examorelin) is **not an FDA-approved drug** for any indication (Phase II discontinued ~2005, never marketed). It does **not** appear on the FDA §503A authorized-bulks list, the Category-2 list, or any PCAC agenda, and it was **NOT among the 12 peptides removed from Category 2 in April 2026** (a distinct nominated-then-withdrawn set). It was apparently never nominated as a §503A bulk drug substance — so it sits **outside the legitimate compounding pathway** and is **not lawfully compoundable**. It is **WADA-prohibited at all times** under S2.2.4 (named "examorelin (hexarelin)"). What supply exists is **gray-market research-chemical vendor**, not patient-specific compounding.

## Dose / route conventions

With no compounding data sheet to anchor on (see gate below), the conventions here come from wellness-clinic pages and vendor/peptide-information sites, reported strictly as *practice convention*, not validated dosing.

- **Vendor / peptide-info convention** (`vendor_label`, gray-market — convention only, never efficacy): **~100 mcg SC, 1–3×/day**, doses spaced ~4–6 h apart, empty-stomach / bedtime timing; common total **200–300 mcg/day**; beginner 50–100 mcg once daily. These converge on **~100 mcg SC 2–3×/day, cycled** [9, vendor_label][10, vendor_label].
- **Wittmer Rejuvenation Clinic** (downgraded — see below) recommends **0.2 mg (200 mcg) SC at bedtime**, nightly for month 1 then **every other night**, run **12–16 weeks consecutively followed by a 4-week break** [6, practitioner_protocol_downgraded].
- **Revolution Health & Wellness** (Sapulpa, OK; downgraded) gives no mcg dosing, only the qualitative practice sentiment that hexarelin "has the highest potency for GH release but may also lead to quicker receptor desensitization if not cycled properly," and advises against stacking multiple GHRPs [8, practitioner_protocol_downgraded].

**Desensitization-driven cycling.** The practice layer explicitly frames hexarelin's cycling around **tachyphylaxis / receptor desensitization** — e.g. cycle off for ~4–8 weeks after ~8–12 weeks of use "to restore receptor sensitivity" — not calendar convenience [9, vendor_label]. **The actual desensitization magnitude must come from the research report's §2.4 primary literature (~45% GH-AUC decline over 16 weeks); the 40–60% / 50–75% figures circulating on vendor pages are NOT grounded here.** The only point retained from this layer is that the cycling *convention itself* is built around desensitization, consistent with the defining fact.

**Stacking (commercial convention, NOT efficacy — kept distinct).** Practice pages routinely pair hexarelin (a **GHRP / ghrelin-receptor agonist**) with a **GHRH analogue — CJC-1295 (no DAC) / Mod GRF 1-29 / sermorelin / tesamorelin** — for "synergistic" GH release [9, vendor_label][8, practitioner_protocol_downgraded]. Reported as commercial convention only. Hexarelin's own dosing (~100 mcg SC) is **kept distinct** from the GHRH-analogue partners and is **not cross-attributed** to them. (Hexarelin is also **less selective than ipamorelin** — the relevant practice contrast for a clean-GH alternative within the same GHRP class.)

> **No human dose-finding study backs any of these numbers.** No published human Phase 2/3 dose-ranging trial for hexarelin exists; every figure here is a *convention* extrapolated from gray-market vendor practice and clinic marketing, not an established therapeutic dose.
