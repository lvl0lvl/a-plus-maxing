# Hexarelin

## Metadata
- **class:** peptide — synthetic **hexapeptide growth-hormone-releasing peptide (GHRP)**; an agonist of the **ghrelin receptor (growth hormone secretagogue receptor type 1a, GHS-R1a)** and a binder of the cardiac scavenger receptor **CD36**. Sequence His-D-2-methyl-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂ (His-D-Mrp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂); CAS 140703-51-1; INN **examorelin**; developmental codes EP-23905 / MF-6003. Developed in the early 1990s by **Romano Deghenghi and colleagues** (Europeptides / the Italian endocrine lineage) and advanced clinically by **Mediolanum Farmaceutici** [1, rct][3, animal][5, vendor_label][30, regulatory].
- **evidence_tier: C** — hexarelin is **unapproved**, never reached Phase III, and has **no registrational program**. The human evidence base is **acute pharmacodynamic** provocation plus **two small, short clinical studies** (8 prepubertal short children; 12 healthy elderly) and a **preclinical-dominant cardiac line** (human cardiac data = acute inotropy only). The only multi-week human study (16 weeks, twice-daily SC) showed **desensitization of the GH response with NO sustained IGF-1, IGFBP-3, or body-composition change**. There is **no Phase 3 efficacy trial for any indication.**
- **risk_tier: experimental** — unapproved drug with no product label and no long-term human safety dataset; it is **non-selective** (it co-stimulates cortisol, ACTH, and prolactin, unlike ipamorelin), it has **measurable acute cardiac/hemodynamic effects** (including a mean-arterial-pressure rise), the GH effect **self-desensitizes within weeks**, and the GH→IGF-1 axis it activates carries class-level theoretical risks. Real-world supply is confined to the research-chemical gray market.
- **status:** researching
- **last_verified:** 2026-06-20

> **Read this first.** Hexarelin is a **GHRP — a ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist and a CD36 binder, NOT a GHRH analogue.** It belongs to the same receptor class as GHRP-6, GHRP-2, and ipamorelin; it must **not** be confused with the GHRH-receptor agonists sermorelin, CJC-1295, or tesamorelin, which act through an entirely separate receptor and signaling cascade [1, rct][6, mechanism_review]. Its **defining limitation is TACHYPHYLAXIS**: the GH response down-regulates under sustained dosing. In the longest human study (1.5 µg/kg SC **twice daily for 16 weeks**), the GH AUC fell from **19.1 ± 2.4 to 10.5 ± 1.8 µg/L·h — an ~45% decline — while serum IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 did NOT change** (P = 0.24 and P = 0.74); the attenuation was reversible (recovered to 19.4 ± 3.7 µg/L·h four weeks after stopping) [7, prospective_trial][8, clinical_trial]. The practical consequence is decisive: **acute GH spikes do not equal durable effect.** Hexarelin is also **less selective than ipamorelin** — it raises **cortisol, ACTH, and prolactin** to clinically relevant magnitudes [9, interventional][2, rct][11, interventional]. Its evidenced human data are acute pharmacodynamics plus two small short studies; a distinct **CD36-mediated cardiac** line is GH-independent but **mostly preclinical** (the only human cardiac data are acute inotropy, and the one diseased-heart human population — dilated cardiomyopathy — did **not** respond acutely) [14, human_acute][15, human_acute][17, animal]. **Body-composition, anti-aging, and athletic efficacy have ZERO robust human data.** Hexarelin was **never approved** (Mediolanum discontinued at Phase II); it is **NOT among the April-2026 removed-12** §503A peptides; and it is **WADA-prohibited at all times** under S2.2.4, where "examorelin (hexarelin)" is named explicitly [29, regulatory][30, regulatory][36, regulatory]. ## TL;DR

Hexarelin (examorelin) is a synthetic hexapeptide GH secretagogue of the **GHRP / ghrelin-receptor-agonist** class — chemically and mechanistically distinct from the GHRH analogues it is routinely confused with. Acutely it is one of the most potent GH releasers known: a single IV dose releases roughly **twice the GH of an equimolar dose of GHRH**, with subcutaneous bioavailability ~77% [1, rct]. But its acute potency is the trap. The single most important fact about its human pharmacology is **tachyphylaxis**: under sustained twice-daily dosing the GH response down-regulates by ~45% within weeks, and — critically — the downstream **IGF-1/IGFBP-3 axis never rose and body composition never changed** over a 16-week course in healthy elderly subjects [7, prospective_trial][8, clinical_trial]. There is therefore **no human evidence that chronic hexarelin produces a durable anabolic GH/IGF-1 signal.** It is also **not GH-selective**: it co-stimulates cortisol, ACTH (via arginine-vasopressin, a true central stress-axis activation), and prolactin [9, interventional][2, rct]. A separate, scientifically interesting **CD36-mediated cardiac** line shows GH-independent positive inotropy — but the human data are acute-only and the diseased-heart population did not respond, leaving every therapeutic cardiac claim **investigational and preclinical-dominant** [14, human_acute][15, human_acute]. For **body composition, fat loss, lean mass, anti-aging, and athletic performance, the robust human evidence is ABSENT.** Regulatorily, hexarelin was never approved (Phase II discontinued ~2005), is effectively unlisted under §503A and **not among the April-2026 removed-12**, and is WADA-prohibited at all times under S2.2.4 as a named GHRP. Real-world availability is confined to the gray-market "research chemical" channel. **Net: a potent acute GH releaser that does not convert acute spikes into durable, measurable benefit, bundled with a cortisol/prolactin/ACTH and cardiac-hemodynamic liability — clinically unproven for every marketed use.**

## 1. Identity, Structure & Mechanism

### 1.1 What hexarelin is — and what it is not

Hexarelin (INN **examorelin**; developmental code EP-23905, also MF-6003) is a **synthetic hexapeptide growth hormone secretagogue (GHS)** of the **growth-hormone-releasing-peptide (GHRP) family** [1, rct][3, animal]. Its defining pharmacological identity — the single most important fact for situating it correctly — is that it is an **agonist of the ghrelin receptor, the growth hormone secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHS-R1a)**: the same seven-transmembrane G-protein-coupled receptor targeted by the other peptidyl secretagogues of its class (GHRP-6, GHRP-2, ipamorelin) and by the endogenous hormone ghrelin [1, rct][6, mechanism_review][4, mechanism_review].

**Hexarelin is a GHRP / ghrelin-receptor agonist, NOT a GHRH (growth-hormone-releasing-hormone) analogue.** It must not be conflated with **sermorelin, CJC-1295, or tesamorelin**, which are GHRH-*receptor* agonists acting through an entirely separate receptor and second-messenger cascade. This is not a pedantic distinction: it determines everything about how hexarelin behaves, why it synergizes with GHRH analogues rather than competing with them, and why "stacking" two GHRH analogues would be redundant whereas a GHRP + a GHRH analogue is complementary (§1.5). The mechanistic separation between GHRP and GHRH was established experimentally well before hexarelin: in normal men, GHRP and GHRH stimulate GH release through **distinct, non-cross-desensitizing receptor sites**, and their combined administration is synergistic rather than merely additive [2, rct].

### 1.2 Discovery and research lineage

The peptidyl GHS class originated with C.Y. Bowers and F.A. Momany, whose late-1980s structure–activity work on enkephalin-derived peptides produced **GHRP-6 (His-D-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂)** as the prototype potent GH releaser [2, rct]. Hexarelin was developed in the early 1990s by **Romano Deghenghi and colleagues** (Europeptides / the Italian research lineage centered on Eugenio Müller, Vittorio Locatelli, and the Turin endocrine group of Ezio Ghigo) as a **structural modification of GHRP-6**: the position-2 tryptophan is replaced with **D-2-methyl-tryptophan (D-Mrp)**, conferring enhanced enzymatic stability and increased GH-releasing potency relative to the parent [3, animal]. The peptide's biological target was an "orphan" until **Howard, Smith, van der Ploeg and colleagues cloned the growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHS-R)** in 1996, identifying it as a 7-transmembrane GPCR of pituitary and hypothalamus; three years later ghrelin was identified as the receptor's endogenous ligand, retrospectively recasting the GHRPs — hexarelin included — as **synthetic ghrelin-receptor agonists** [6, mechanism_review][4, mechanism_review].

### 1.3 Structure

Hexarelin is a six-residue peptide:

**His – D-2-methyl-Trp – Ala – Trp – D-Phe – Lys – NH₂** (His-D-Mrp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂) [3, animal][5, vendor_label].

- CAS number: 140703-51-1 [5, vendor_label]
- Molecular formula: C₄₇H₅₈N₁₂O₆; molecular weight ≈ 887 Da [5, vendor_label]
- C-terminal amidation (–NH₂) and two D-amino-acid substitutions (D-Mrp at position 2, D-Phe at position 5) are the structurally load-bearing features that protect the peptide from rapid proteolysis [3, animal]

The structure is corroborated by both the primary developmental literature and a chemical-supplier identity record; the vendor source is used **only** for non-numeric structural identity (CAS, sequence, formula), and no numeric pharmacology claim rests on it [3, animal][5, vendor_label].

### 1.4 Mechanism — the GH-releasing pathway

Hexarelin's canonical action is to **stimulate endogenous pulsatile GH release** by agonizing GHS-R1a on **anterior-pituitary somatotrophs** and on hypothalamic neurons [1, rct][4, mechanism_review]. The intracellular cascade is the classic **Gq/11 → phospholipase C (PLC)** pathway:

1. GHS-R1a (Gq/11-coupled) activation stimulates PLC [1, rct][4, mechanism_review]
2. PLC hydrolyzes PIP₂ to **inositol-1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP₃)** and **diacylglycerol (DAG)** [4, mechanism_review]
3. IP₃ mobilizes **intracellular Ca²⁺**, raising cytosolic calcium [1, rct][4, mechanism_review]
4. The Ca²⁺ rise triggers **exocytosis of GH-containing secretory vesicles** from somatotrophs [4, mechanism_review]

A nitric-oxide-synthase / NO → guanylate cyclase / cGMP arm has also been implicated as a secondary contributor to GHS-R1a-driven GH release [4, mechanism_review].

Beyond the direct somatotroph effect, hexarelin (like ghrelin and the other GHRPs) acts **at two levels**: directly on pituitary somatotrophs, and indirectly within the hypothalamus, where it functions as a **functional antagonist of somatostatin** (the tonic inhibitor of GH) and facilitates endogenous GHRH tone [4, mechanism_review]. Because part of the effect requires an intact hypothalamic GHRH system, the maximal GH response to GHRP-class peptides depends on endogenous GHRH availability [2, rct][4, mechanism_review].

### 1.5 Synergy with GHRH — a two-receptor convergence

The GHRP/GHRH synergy is mechanistically a **two-receptor convergence onto the somatotroph**. GHRH acts via the **GHRH receptor** (Gs-coupled → adenylate cyclase → cAMP/PKA), priming GH synthesis and release; hexarelin acts via **GHS-R1a** (Gq → PLC → IP₃/Ca²⁺) while simultaneously withdrawing somatostatin restraint [2, rct][4, mechanism_review]. Because the two converge through **independent receptors and independent second-messenger systems** (cAMP vs IP₃/Ca²⁺), co-administration yields a GH pulse markedly greater than either agent alone — Bowers et al. demonstrated this synergy in normal men [2, rct]. This is the pharmacological rationale for clinical and protocol-level GHRP+GHRH "stacks." The synergy is *only* coherent if hexarelin is understood as a non-GHRH agent: a second GHRH analogue added to a GHRH analogue would compete at one receptor, whereas a GHRP plus a GHRH analogue engage two complementary receptors.

### 1.6 The second receptor — CD36 and a GH-independent cardiac action

Hexarelin has a **second, GH-independent molecular target: CD36**, a scavenger-receptor glycoprotein. Bodart et al. radiolabeled rat cardiac membranes with a photoactivatable hexarelin derivative and purified a binding protein of Mr ≈ 84,000 whose N-terminal sequence was identical to **rat CD36**, expressed in cardiomyocytes and microvascular endothelial cells; activation of cardiac CD36 by hexarelin in perfused hearts produced a **dose-dependent increase in coronary perfusion pressure** [17, animal]. This CD36 pathway is the basis for hexarelin's reported cardiovascular effects occurring **independently of growth-hormone secretion** (developed in §3). Hexarelin's affinity is **higher for GHS-R1a than for CD36** — published binding shows preferential GHS-R binding over CD36 by roughly two orders of magnitude — consistent with GH release being its dominant high-affinity action and the CD36/cardiac action engaging at the second, lower-affinity site [5, vendor_label][17, animal].

### 1.7 Pharmacokinetics and routes of administration

The most rigorous human PK data come from Ghigo et al., who studied hexarelin after **intravenous, subcutaneous, intranasal, and oral** administration in healthy young volunteers (n=12) [1, rct]:

- Hexarelin produced **strong, reproducible, dose-dependent GH release by all four routes** [1, rct]
- IV hexarelin at 1 µg/kg produced GH responses roughly **double those of GHRH** at an equivalent dose [1, rct]
- Intranasal hexarelin at 20 µg/kg gave GH responses comparable to 1 µg/kg IV [1, rct]
- **Biological bioavailability** (relative to IV): **≈ 77.0 ± 10.5% subcutaneous, ≈ 4.8 ± 0.9% intranasal, ≈ 0.3 ± 0.1% oral** [1, rct] — i.e., SC is the efficient parenteral route, intranasal is modest, and oral bioavailability of the intact peptide is negligible

The plasma GH response peaks at roughly 30 minutes and returns toward baseline within ~3–4 hours; the peptide itself is short-lived in circulation (a short plasma half-life on the order of tens of minutes, typical of small peptides), which is why dosing is parenteral and the GH effect is pulsatile rather than sustained [1, rct].

### 1.8 Potency relative to GHRP-6 and GHRP-2

In the rat, hexarelin and GHRP-6 showed **similar effectiveness** at most tested IV doses, but **subcutaneous hexarelin elicited a longer-lasting GH release and was slightly more effective than GHRP-6** — attributable to the D-2-methyl-Trp stabilization [3, animal]. In humans, hexarelin is among the **most potent acute GH releasers** of the peptidyl GHRP class [1, rct]. GHRP-2 is generally regarded as comparably potent within the same ghrelin-receptor-agonist family; all three (GHRP-6, GHRP-2, hexarelin) share the **identical receptor mechanism (GHS-R1a / Gq-PLC-Ca²⁺)** and differ mainly in potency, duration, and off-target (cortisol/prolactin/ACTH) liability rather than in fundamental pathway [1, rct][2, rct][4, mechanism_review].

### Coverage gaps (Section 1)
- The preferential GHS-R>CD36 binding ratio is stated qualitatively (favoring GHS-R by ~two orders of magnitude); the precise IC₅₀ values rest on a vendor identity record [5] for the ratio and on [17] for the quantitative cardiac CD36 evidence — no numeric pharmacology claim is grounded on the vendor source alone.
- The plasma half-life is characterized qualitatively ("tens of minutes") from the PK behavior in [1]; no exact human terminal-half-life constant is carried.
- One mechanism review [6] does not name hexarelin and is cited only for shared receptor-biology/discovery context of the GHRP class.

## 2. Human GH Pharmacology + Tachyphylaxis

> **Class note (applies throughout):** "synergy with GHRH" means synergy between two pharmacologically distinct secretagogues acting on two different receptors — not a property intrinsic to the hexarelin molecule.

### 2.1 Acute GH-release magnitude and route dependence (healthy adults)

In the foundational human pharmacodynamic study (12 healthy young volunteers), hexarelin was a potent and reproducible acute GH secretagogue across routes. After IV dosing, the GH area-under-curve rose dose-dependently from **3175 ± 506 µg·min/L (1 µg/kg) to 4422 ± 626 µg·min/L (2 µg/kg)**; SC dosing produced comparable AUCs (3180 ± 392 at 1.5 µg/kg; 4459 ± 566 at 3 µg/kg), with absolute SC bioavailability of 77.0 ± 10.5%. Intranasal and oral routes were far weaker (4.8 ± 0.9% and 0.3 ± 0.1%). Critically, IV hexarelin 1 µg/kg released roughly **twice the GH of an equimolar dose of GHRH** (~2-fold higher, P < 0.001), establishing hexarelin as a stronger acute GH stimulus than GHRH itself [1, rct]. `[rct, n=12 healthy adults]`

A formal IV dose-response study in healthy adult males quantified the ceiling: the GH response rose with dose to a **plateau of ~140 mU/L reached at 1.0 µg/kg, with an ED50 of 0.48 ± 0.02 µg/kg** — i.e., the acute GH effect **saturates at low microgram-per-kg doses** and higher doses add little [2_massoud, rct]. `[rct, healthy adult males]` This dose-ceiling is corroborated by the original multiroute study, where escalating beyond ~2 µg/kg IV / 3 µg/kg SC gave diminishing incremental GH [1, rct].

### 2.2 Synergy with GHRH (two-receptor, not intrinsic)

Hexarelin and GHRH act through distinct receptors and are functionally complementary. In 12 healthy male volunteers, IV hexarelin 2 µg/kg produced markedly higher GH than GHRH 2 µg/kg (AUC 4693 ± 691 vs 1494 ± 102 µg·min/L, P < 0.01), and **coadministration was synergistic** rather than additive (combined AUC 7395 ± 450 µg·min/L, P < 0.05 vs hexarelin alone). Exogenous somatostatin abolished the GHRH response but only partially blunted the hexarelin response — direct evidence the two operate by different mechanisms [10, rct]. `[rct, n=12 healthy adults]` Consistent with a partly somatostatin-independent mechanism, in subjects with glucocorticoid excess the GHRH-evoked GH response was severely blunted (median ΔGH 0.9 vs 7.1 µg/L in controls) whereas the **hexarelin response was preserved** (median ΔGH 15.5 vs 17.9 µg/L; no significant patient-vs-control difference) [12, rct_crossover]. `[rct cross-over, n=14 (8 glucocorticoid-excess + 6 controls)]`

### 2.3 INTEGRITY FLAG — hexarelin is NOT GH-selective (cortisol, prolactin, ACTH co-secretion)

Unlike ipamorelin (the selective GHRP), hexarelin **meaningfully co-stimulates the HPA axis and prolactin**. This is a defining liability, documented in tier-1 human studies:

- In 15 healthy young men, IV hexarelin produced significant ACTH release (AUC 3444 ± 696 ng/L·125 min) and cortisol release (45,844 ± 2,925 nmol/L·125 min), plus significant GH and prolactin release. The HPA effect was **CRH-independent and appears to be mediated by arginine vasopressin (AVP)** — a true central activation of the stress axis, not an artifact [9, interventional]. `[interventional, n=15 healthy young men]`
- In the IV dose-response study, prolactin rose to a plateau of ~180% above baseline (ED50 0.39 ± 0.02 µg/kg) and cortisol stepped up ~40% at 0.5 µg/kg [2_massoud, rct]. `[rct, healthy adult males]`
- Head-to-head against GHRH, TRH and hCRH, hexarelin's prolactin-releasing activity was lower than TRH's but its **ACTH/cortisol-releasing activity was comparable to that of hCRH** — i.e., hexarelin stimulates the adrenal axis about as strongly as a corticotropin-releasing stimulus does [11, interventional]. `[interventional comparison, healthy young adults]`

**Bottom line for the wiki:** the GH spike from hexarelin comes bundled with a cortisol/ACTH/prolactin spike of clinically relevant magnitude; it should not be presented as a clean GH agent.

### 2.4 DEFINING LIMITATION — tachyphylaxis / desensitization (acute ≠ sustained)

The single most important fact about hexarelin's human pharmacology is that **the acute GH response does not survive continued dosing — the response is progressively down-regulated (GHS-R desensitization).** Two tier-1 reports establish this directly, and they describe the **same 16-week, 1.5 µg/kg SC twice-daily course in healthy elderly subjects** (n=12):

- **Long-term therapy study (verified key finding).** GH AUC fell from a baseline of **19.1 ± 2.4 µg/L·h to 13.1 (week 1) → 12.3 (week 4, P < 0.05) → 10.5 ± 1.8 µg/L·h (week 16, P < 0.01) — an ~45% attenuation**, most of it within the first 1–4 weeks. The attenuation was **reversible**: four weeks after stopping (week 20) the AUC recovered to 19.4 ± 3.7 µg/L·h, not significantly different from baseline. Crucially, **serum IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 did NOT change significantly over the 20-week period (P = 0.24 and P = 0.74)** [7, prospective_trial]. `[prospective clinical trial, n=12 healthy elderly, 16 wk SC BID]`
- **Companion desensitization analysis** (same paradigm and population): mean GH AUC decreased from 19.1 µg/L·h (week 0) to 10.5 µg/L·h (week 16, P < 0.01), then rebounded to 19.4 ± 3.7 µg/L·h four weeks post-course (P < 0.05 vs week 16) — confirming **partial, reversible desensitization** [8, clinical_trial]. `[clinical trial, n=12 healthy elderly]`

> **Population reconciliation note.** The Rahim/Shalet 1998 16-week course was conducted in **healthy elderly subjects (n=12)** — this report uses that descriptor consistently for [7] and [8] throughout (§2, §3, §5, §8). Where earlier loose phrasing might say "adults," the corpus-supported population is *healthy elderly.*

The mechanistic interpretation across this literature is **GHS-R1a desensitization/down-regulation with sustained agonist exposure**. The practical consequence is decisive: **acute GH spikes overstate durable effect.** Because the downstream IGF-1/IGFBP-3 axis did not rise even during 16 weeks of twice-daily dosing — while the GH spike itself shrank by ~45% — **there is no human evidence that chronic hexarelin produces a sustained anabolic GH/IGF-1 signal.** Reported "cycling" regimens (§8) are extrapolations from this reversibility finding, not from durable-efficacy trials.

### 2.5 IGF-1 with short courses, and population annotation

Short single-dose human studies measure acute GH only; the longest controlled human course (16 weeks, twice-daily SC) showed **no significant IGF-1 or IGFBP-3 change** despite real acute GH release [7, prospective_trial]. The GH response is also strongly **age/developmental-stage dependent**: in 60 subjects given 2.0 µg/kg IV, GH AUC was low in prepubertal children (769.5 µg·min/L), strikingly higher at puberty (1960.2), high in young adults, and **lower in the elderly (951.1)**; prolactin and cortisol responses showed no significant age variation, while ACTH was enhanced at puberty [13, interventional]. `[interventional, n=60 across 4 age groups]` Thus quantitative GH numbers must be read against population: **pubertal > young-adult > elderly ≈ prepubertal.**

### Coverage gaps (Section 2)
- All chronic human GH/IGF-1 data come from a single 16-week course (n=12 healthy elderly), reported across two papers [7][8]; no other multi-week human dataset exists.
- No human course beyond 16 weeks exists; the long-term trajectory of desensitization and of IGF-1 is unknown.
- The dose-ceiling/ED50 data [2_massoud] are in healthy adult males; ED50 may differ by age/population per [13].
- Acute studies measure GH release, not durable physiological endpoints.

## 3. Clinical Evidence + Cardiac / CD36

> Throughout this section, evidence is demarcated **EVIDENCED** (peer-reviewed human or animal data exists), **INVESTIGATIONAL** (mechanism/preclinical only, no human efficacy endpoint), or **ABSENT** (no robust data exists). Every figure is annotated with its source population.

### 3.1 GH-axis clinical evidence (small, short, old)

The human GH-axis clinical literature for hexarelin consists of a handful of **small, short-duration** studies. There is **no large-scale, long-term efficacy trial** and **no regulatory approval** for any indication.

- **EVIDENCED — Short children (open-label, non-randomized, n=8).** Laron et al. gave **intranasal hexarelin 60 µg/kg three times daily for up to 8 months** to **8 prepubertal short children** (aged 4–11.6 y). IGF-I rose from 10.4 ± 3.9 to 14.1 ± 4.6 nmol/L (P < 0.004) and **linear growth velocity** increased from 5.3 ± 0.8 to 8.3 ± 1.7 cm/year (P < 0.0001) [18, open_label]. **Caveats:** uncontrolled, tiny n, surrogate/short-term endpoints (no final-height data); this is a **pediatric short-stature population**, not a body-composition or anti-aging population.

- **EVIDENCED — Healthy elderly, long-term GH (n=12), with the desensitization caveat.** Rahim, O'Neill & Shalet gave **SC hexarelin 1.5 µg/kg twice daily for 16 weeks** to **12 healthy elderly subjects**. The GH response **attenuated** progressively — mean AUC-GH fell from 19.1 ± 2.4 to 10.5 ± 1.8 µg/L·h by week 16 (P < 0.01) — while **serum IGF-I and IGFBP-3 did not change significantly**, and **body composition and bone mineral density were unchanged** [7, prospective_trial]. The companion analysis confirmed the attenuation is **partial and reversible** (recovery to ~19.4 µg/L·h four weeks after stopping) [8, clinical_trial]. **This is the central desensitization caveat:** chronic GHRP dosing blunts its own GH-releasing effect and, critically, **failed to move IGF-I or body composition over 16 weeks in the elderly** — i.e., the GH-axis "anti-aging" rationale is **not** supported even by the only multi-week human study.

**Net GH-axis read:** Hexarelin reliably and **acutely** releases GH (§2). Beyond acute provocation, the human chronic evidence is **two small studies** (8 children; 12 elderly), both with surrogate endpoints, and the elderly study explicitly showed **no sustained IGF-I or body-composition benefit plus self-desensitization.**

### 3.2 Cardiac / CD36 research line (GH-independent — mostly preclinical)

A distinct and scientifically interesting line shows hexarelin has **direct cardiac actions independent of GH release**, mediated largely by **CD36** on cardiomyocytes and microvascular endothelium (in addition to GHS-R1a). Human cardiac data are **acute, hemodynamic, and small**; durable clinical outcomes are **animal-only**.

- **EVIDENCED (human, acute, n=7 GHD + 9 controls) — GH-independent inotropy.** Bisi et al. (Turin/Ghigo group) gave **IV hexarelin** to **7 GH-deficient hypopituitary adult men + 9 healthy male controls**, measuring LVEF by equilibrium radionuclide angiocardiography. Hexarelin **acutely raised LVEF** in both groups (GHD: 50 ± 1% → 57 ± 2%; controls: 63 ± 2% → 70 ± 2%) **without** changing blood pressure, heart rate, catecholamines, or cardiac output [14, human_acute]. Because the effect occurred in GHD subjects (who cannot mount a GH rise), it is **GH-independent** — but this is an **acute, single-dose hemodynamic** effect, not a treated-outcome trial.

- **EVIDENCED (human, acute, n=7+7+12) — confirmation + the cardiomyopathy ceiling.** Broglio et al. (Turin/Ghigo group) gave **acute IV hexarelin 2.0 µg/kg** to **7 normal adults, 7 GH-deficient patients, and 12 dilated-cardiomyopathy patients**. LVEF rose significantly in normal and GHD subjects but **NOT in the dilated-cardiomyopathy group**, again without BP/HR change [15, human_acute]. The positive inotropic effect is real and GH-independent in intact myocardium, but **the diseased-heart population — the one of clinical interest — did not respond acutely.** No follow-on chronic efficacy trial in heart failure exists.

- **EVIDENCED (animal — receptor identity) — CD36 is the cardiac target.** Bodart et al. cross-linked a photoactivatable hexarelin derivative in **rat cardiac membranes** and identified the binding protein as **CD36 (84 kDa)**. In perfused rat hearts, hexarelin raised coronary perfusion pressure dose-dependently; the action tracked CD36 [17, animal]. **Preclinical (rat), receptor-mechanism — no human outcome.**

- **EVIDENCED (animal — cardioprotection, GH-independent) — Locatelli/Müller group.** Locatelli et al. gave **hexarelin 80 µg/kg SC for 7 days** to **hypophysectomized rats** (no GH capacity) and subjected explanted hearts to **ischemia-reperfusion**. Hexarelin prevented the hypophysectomy-worsened I/R damage — limiting LV end-diastolic and coronary perfusion pressure rises, blunting angiotensin-II reactivity, and reducing creatine-kinase release — while an inactive GHRP analogue (EP 51389) did not, confirming a **specific cardiac receptor, GH-independent** mechanism [19, animal]. **Preclinical (rat), n not stated in abstract.**

- **EVIDENCED (animal — chronic heart failure) — PTEN/Akt-mTOR.** Agbo et al. gave **hexarelin 100 µg/kg SC twice daily for 30 days** to **male Sprague-Dawley rats (96 total; n=6/measurement group)** after coronary-artery-ligation heart failure. Hexarelin **improved LV function, reduced remodeling/fibrosis (TGF-β1, procollagen-I) and oxidative stress**, via PTEN upregulation and Akt/mTOR downregulation [20, animal]. **Preclinical (rat); independent Chinese group — useful external replication of the cardioprotective signal, but still animal.**

**Net cardiac read:** A coherent, GH-independent, CD36-mediated cardioprotective mechanism is **well-supported preclinically** (multiple rat models, ≥3 groups) and shows an **acute** GH-independent positive inotropic effect in **small human studies** — but with **no chronic human cardiac trial**, and the only diseased-heart human data (dilated cardiomyopathy) showed **no acute LVEF response**. This domain is **INVESTIGATIONAL for any therapeutic claim.**

### 3.3 ABSENT domains (state plainly)

- **Body composition / fat loss / muscle gain: ABSENT.** No RCT shows hexarelin produces durable fat loss or lean-mass gain in humans. The only multi-week human study (16 wk, elderly) found **no change in body composition** [7, prospective_trial].
- **Anti-aging / longevity / IGF-1 elevation as a benefit: ABSENT.** Chronic dosing did **not** raise IGF-I/IGFBP-3 and **self-desensitizes** [7, prospective_trial][8, clinical_trial].
- **Athletic / ergogenic performance: ABSENT.** No controlled performance trial exists; hexarelin is a WADA-prohibited GHRP with no efficacy evidence for performance (§6).
- **Phase 3 efficacy / regulatory approval, any indication: ABSENT.** Hexarelin never advanced to a registrational program; it is an unapproved research peptide (§6).

### Coverage gaps (Section 3)
- The entire chronic-human GH-axis base is two small studies (n=8 children; n=12 elderly); no controlled body-composition or anti-aging endpoint exists.
- All human cardiac data are acute/single-dose; the durable cardioprotection signal is animal-only, and the one diseased-heart human population did not respond acutely.
- The human inotropy confirmations [14][15] both come from the same Turin lab — consistent but not cross-lab-replicated in humans (see §4).
- A short tolerance/desensitization observation (short-term intranasal/oral hexarelin not desensitizing over brief exposure, Ghigo group) is noted in the corpus as duration-dependence context; the full acute pharmacodynamics are handled in §1–§2, and no separate admissible primary is broken out here.

## 5. Safety & Adverse Effects

> **Defining fact for this section.** Hexarelin has **no regulatory approval and no product label** in any jurisdiction; it never advanced past Phase II. There is therefore **no approved-label adverse-event table.** The safety picture is built from (a) adverse events actually observed in the short human pharmacodynamic/clinical studies (largely the Turin group and the Manchester group), and (b) **class/mechanism reasoning extrapolated from the GH/IGF-1 axis it activates.** Off-label "research-peptide" use is at doses, frequencies, and durations never formally studied — the trial AE profile below should be read as a *floor*, not a ceiling. `[dose-extrapolation]`

### 5.1 Adverse effects observed in human studies

The human literature is small (single-dose or short multi-week pharmacodynamic studies) and was powered to measure hormone release, not safety. Within that limit, the consistently reported acute events are mild and transient.

- **Transient facial flushing / warmth** is the most frequently noted acute event after IV/SC hexarelin and is captured as an indexed adverse-effect term in the comparative GHRP pharmacology work; it is short-lived and self-limiting [11, interventional]. This is a recognized class effect of GHRP-6-family secretagogues.
- **Cortisol / ACTH / prolactin elevation (the key non-selectivity issue).** Unlike the selective secretagogue ipamorelin, hexarelin is **not GH-selective**: acutely it also stimulates the lactotroph and corticotroph axes. In direct human comparison, hexarelin's **ACTH/cortisol-releasing activity was similar to that of human CRH (hCRH)**, with a **slight prolactin rise** (lower than the TRH-driven response) [11, interventional]. A dose-response study reported a **stepwise cortisol increase (~40% above baseline at 0.5 µg/kg)** and a **prolactin peak ~180% above baseline (ED50 ≈ 0.39 µg/kg)** alongside the GH response [2_massoud, rct]. These are pharmacologic (on-mechanism) endocrine responses, not toxic events, but they are the distinctive feature separating hexarelin from selective GHRPs and the basis for the prolactin/cortisol monitoring note below.
- **Chronic dosing does NOT cause sustained over-stimulation of the cortisol/prolactin axes.** In the longest human study (**1.5 µg/kg SC twice daily for 16 weeks in 12 healthy elderly subjects**), there was **no significant change in ACTH AUC, no rise in 24-h urinary free cortisol, and no significant prolactin change**; cortisol if anything transiently *fell* at week 16 and normalized by week 20. The authors concluded over-stimulation of the pituitary-adrenal axis and prolactin "do not occur" with chronic use and that any changes were unlikely to be clinically significant [21, open_label]. This is reassuring but applies to a *trial dose in elderly subjects* — not to high-dose off-label cycling. `[dose-extrapolation]`
- **Cardiac / hemodynamic effects (the "double-edged" item).** Hexarelin acts on the myocardium partly through **CD36** (a non-GHS-R1a cardiac receptor), underlying a **cardioprotective / positive-inotropic signal** but also meaning it has measurable *acute hemodynamic effects*. In 24 male CAD patients (mean age ~60) given **2.0 µg/kg IV during bypass surgery**, hexarelin produced a prompt rise in LV ejection fraction, cardiac index, and cardiac output with a fall in wedge pressure — i.e., *improved* cardiac performance — but this occurred with a **measurable rise in mean arterial pressure (P < 0.05)** and a transient drop in central venous pressure. **Heart rate was unchanged** (similar to placebo) and systemic vascular resistance index did not change [22, open_label]. The honest reading: the cardiac signal is genuinely **double-edged** — a cardioprotection rationale on one hand, a real acute pressor/hemodynamic effect on the other. There is **no long-term cardiovascular outcome data in humans.**
- **Sleep architecture.** Hexarelin given to healthy volunteers **decreased slow-wave sleep** while stimulating overnight GH/ACTH/cortisol/prolactin secretion [11, interventional]-adjacent class data; a documented pharmacodynamic effect, relevant if dosed at night.
- **Injection-site reactions** are not systematically quantified in the trial literature; SC dosing was used for weeks without reported local intolerance in the chronic study [21, open_label], but **no numerical injection-site AE rate exists from an admissible source.** No vendor or anecdote source is used to assign any numerical AE rate.

### 5.2 Tolerance / desensitization (a use-pattern risk, not a toxicity)

Repeated hexarelin dosing produces **partial, reversible desensitization of the GH response.** In the 16-week SC study (1.5 µg/kg BID), GH AUC fell progressively — **19.1 → 13.1 → 12.3 → 10.5 µg/L·h at weeks 0, 1, 4, 16** (most of the attenuation within the first 1–4 weeks) — and **recovered to baseline ~4 weeks after stopping** [8, clinical_trial]. Mechanistically this reflects GHS-R1a downregulation with sustained agonism. **Practical implication:** continuous off-label dosing loses efficacy within weeks while continuing to expose the user to the endocrine and hemodynamic effects above — an argument for cycling rather than continuous use.

### 5.3 GH/IGF-1-axis class risks (theoretical / extrapolated — not all observed for hexarelin)

These are **class risks inferred from activating the GH→IGF-1 axis**, drawn from the GH-replacement literature; they are **not** demonstrated hexarelin trial AEs and must be read as such. `[dose-extrapolation]`

- **Glucose intolerance / insulin resistance.** GH is counter-regulatory, and GH therapy can mildly raise fasting glucose and insulin, especially early and in older/obese individuals [23, mechanism_review]. **Importantly, acute hexarelin itself did NOT modify glucose or insulin** in healthy young adults despite a marked GH rise — in contrast to ghrelin, which raised glucose and lowered insulin in the same paradigm [24, rct]. So the glucose risk here is a **sustained-exposure / class** concern via chronic IGF-1 elevation, **not an acute hexarelin effect** — relevant mainly to prolonged off-label use. `[dose-extrapolation]`
- **Fluid retention, peripheral edema, arthralgia/myalgia, carpal tunnel, paresthesia** are the **most common GH-therapy side effects**, dose-dependent, more frequent in older/obese/female patients, usually reversible on dose reduction [23, mechanism_review]. Plausible at supraphysiologic IGF-1 exposure from aggressive hexarelin dosing; not quantified for hexarelin specifically.
- **IGF-1 elevation and theoretical malignancy risk (associational — flag).** GH replacement has **not** been shown to increase de-novo or recurrent cancer, but **higher circulating IGF-1 is epidemiologically associated** with increased risk of several cancers (breast, prostate, colorectal) in large prospective cohorts (modest hazard ratios, on the order of ~1.07–1.10 per unit increase) [23, mechanism_review][25, cohort]. This is an **association, not established causation by exogenous secretagogues**, and is the conservative basis for the active-malignancy precaution — not evidence that hexarelin causes cancer.

### 5.4 Contraindications (precautionary — derived from mechanism + AE profile)

- **Active or recent malignancy** — avoid; GH/IGF-1 axis activation is a theoretical concern given the IGF-1–cancer association [23, mechanism_review][25, cohort] (precautionary).
- **Uncontrolled diabetes / significant glucose intolerance** — caution/avoid; GH-axis activation can worsen glycemic control with sustained use [23, mechanism_review].
- **Cardiac conditions / hemodynamically fragile patients** — caution given the documented acute rise in mean arterial pressure and the CD36-mediated hemodynamic effects [22, open_label]; no long-term CV safety data.
- **Hyperprolactinemia** — caution; hexarelin acutely raises prolactin [11, interventional][2_massoud, rct].
- **Pregnancy and lactation** — avoid; **no human data exist.**
- **Known hypersensitivity** to the peptide or excipients.

### 5.5 Monitoring & stopping criteria (objective assays named)

- **IGF-1** (interpreted against an **age- and sex-specific reference range**; [[biomarkers/igf-1]]) — baseline and periodically; the central biomarker of cumulative GH-axis exposure.
- **Fasting glucose ± HbA1c** — baseline and periodically, especially with prolonged use or metabolic risk factors [23, mechanism_review].
- **Prolactin and morning cortisol** — consider with **chronic** dosing given the non-selective profile, though the 16-week trial found no sustained axis disturbance [21, open_label].
- **Blood pressure / heart rate** — reasonable given acute hemodynamic effects, particularly in anyone with cardiac risk [22, open_label].
- **Stopping criteria:** IGF-1 above the upper end of the age/sex range; new/worsening hyperglycemia; significant fluid retention, arthralgia, or carpal-tunnel-type symptoms; sustained blood-pressure rise; any new cardiac symptom; pregnancy. Because efficacy desensitizes within weeks, **scheduled cycling/washout** is advisable independent of AEs [8, clinical_trial].

> These are standard objective endocrine/metabolic/hemodynamic assays, **NOT** a validated surrogate of hexarelin activity; no hexarelin-specific validated biomarker of effect exists. IGF-1 is the closest objective marker of cumulative GH-axis exposure.

### Coverage gaps (Section 5)
- No human AE *rate* can be quoted — all human AE statements are existence-of-AE in small short studies; off-label dosing is unstudied.
- GH/IGF-1 class risks (§5.3) are extrapolated from GH-replacement literature, not demonstrated for hexarelin.
- The IGF-1–cancer link [25] is associational, not causal, and not specific to secretagogues.
- No long-term human cardiovascular safety data exist despite measurable acute hemodynamic effects.
- The "no sustained HPA/prolactin over-stimulation" reassurance [21] applies to a trial dose in elderly subjects, not high-dose cycling.

## 6. Regulatory & Sport Status

### 6.1 FDA / EMA marketing approval — never approved, anywhere

Hexarelin (INN **examorelin**; FDA UNII 09QF37C617) has **never received marketing approval from the FDA, the EMA, or any other regulatory authority** for any indication [26, regulatory]. It is an investigational GH-releasing hexapeptide developed under the codes **MF-6003 / EP-23905** by **Mediolanum Farmaceutici** (with Europeptides as co-developer) for somatotropin (growth-hormone) deficiency [26, regulatory]. The US government's authoritative substance database (NCATS Inxight Drugs) lists examorelin's development status as **"Investigational,"** approval year **"Unknown,"** with **no approved indication in any jurisdiction** [26, regulatory].

Clinical development reached **Phase II** (growth-hormone deficiency and congestive heart failure) but was **discontinued without yielding a marketed product** — secondary sources place the strategic discontinuation around 2005 [27, mechanism_review]. A targeted check of the FDA orange-book/approval ecosystem and EMA medicines register surfaced **no NDA, BLA, or EU marketing authorisation** [26, regulatory]. **Verification note:** the "never approved" claim is anchored to the NCATS primary substance record [26]; the specific Phase-II-then-discontinued narrative rests on secondary/encyclopedic sources [27] and is flagged accordingly — directionally consistent with investigational status but not independently confirmed against a primary clinical-trials-registry entry in this pass.

### 6.2 FDA §503A compounding status — NOT among the April-2026 removed-12; effectively unlisted / not eligible

Hexarelin's compounding standing must be stated precisely because the 2026 landscape shifted, and it is **easy to misattribute the removed-12 action to hexarelin — it does not apply.**

**What FR Doc 2026-07361 actually is.** Published in the Federal Register on **April 16, 2026**, FR Doc **2026-07361** is the **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) Notice of Meeting** for **July 23–24, 2026** — it is *not* itself "the removal of 12 peptides" [28, regulatory]. The govinfo primary text confirms the PCAC will evaluate **seven** bulk substances for possible inclusion on the §503A Bulks List: **Day 1 (July 23)** — BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c; **Day 2 (July 24)** — Emideltide/DSIP, Semax, Epitalon (each in free-base and acetate forms). **Hexarelin / examorelin is explicitly NOT on this agenda** [28, regulatory].

**The "removed-12."** Separately, in April 2026 the FDA removed **12 peptides from Category 2** of the interim §503A bulks list (nominations withdrawn): (1) BPC-157, (2) TB-500/Thymosin β4 fragment, (3) KPV, (4) MOTS-c, (5) DSIP/Emideltide, (6) Semax, (7) Epitalon, (8) GHK-Cu, (9) Melanotan-II, (10) Dihexa, (11) PEG-MGF, (12) LL-37/Cathelicidin [29, regulatory]. **Hexarelin is NOT one of the 12** — it is not named in the removal action and is not referenced anywhere in the practitioner/legal coverage of it [29, regulatory].

**Hexarelin's actual standing.** Hexarelin does **not appear** on (a) the FDA §503A Bulks List of substances that *can* be compounded, (b) the Category 2 "significant safety risks" list, or (c) the PCAC July-2026 or follow-up agendas. It was **not among the nominated-then-withdrawn 12**, and no nomination record for hexarelin surfaced in this pass. The most defensible characterization is therefore: **hexarelin is effectively unlisted / not a nominated bulk drug substance under §503A.** Under the statutory framework, a compounder may use a bulk drug substance only if it (i) has an applicable USP/NF monograph, (ii) is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or (iii) appears on the §503A bulks list — hexarelin satisfies **none** of these [30, regulatory]. The practical consequence is that hexarelin is **not legally eligible for §503A compounding**, the same end-state as the closely related GHRPs **GHRP-2 and GHRP-6** (which FDA placed in Category 2 citing immunogenicity/aggregation risk and, for GHRP-2, serious-adverse-event reports) [30, regulatory]. **Honest hedge:** this pass could not confirm from a primary FDA list whether hexarelin was ever formally *nominated and categorized* versus simply *never nominated*; either way it is not on the eligible bulks list and not compoundable, but the precise mechanism (Category 2 vs. never-nominated) is **not established** and should not be over-stated.

### 6.3 WADA — prohibited at all times under S2.2.4 (GH-releasing factors / GHRPs)

Hexarelin is **prohibited at all times** (both in- and out-of-competition) under the **WADA 2026 Prohibited List** (in force 1 January 2026) [36, regulatory]. It falls within **Section S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances, and Mimetics)**, headed verbatim **"PROHIBITED AT ALL TIMES (IN- AND OUT-OF-COMPETITION)"** and **"All prohibited substances in this class are non-Specified Substances"** [36, regulatory]. Within S2.2 (Peptide Hormones and Their Releasing Factors), subsection **S2.2.4 ("Growth hormone releasing factors")** names it explicitly under the GHRP bullet, quoted verbatim from the official List: *"GH-releasing peptides (GHRPs) [e.g. alexamorelin, **examorelin (hexarelin)**, GHRP-1, GHRP-2 (pralmorelin), GHRP-3, GHRP-4, GHRP-5 and GHRP-6]"* [36, regulatory]. Because the entire S2 class is **non-Specified**, an anti-doping rule violation involving hexarelin carries the strictest sanctioning posture [36, regulatory].

**Subsection numbering — primary-confirmed.** The **S2.2.4** decimal is taken directly from the official WADA 2026 Prohibited List PDF (extracted from the wada-ama.org primary [36]), not from a mirror or inference — it is the actual official numbering, where S2.2.4 sits beneath S2.2.

### 6.4 EU status and gray-market reality

There is **no EU marketing authorisation** for hexarelin (consistent with the never-marketed investigational status above); it is not an approved medicine in any EU member state [26, regulatory]. In practice hexarelin circulates almost entirely through the **"research chemical" gray market** — sold by online peptide vendors labelled "for research/laboratory use only, not for human consumption" [37, anecdote_aggregate]. This labelling is the standard vendor mechanism for distributing an unapproved drug outside the medicines framework; such product is not subject to FDA/EMA quality oversight, and identity/purity rest solely on vendor-supplied certificates of analysis (qualitative observation only) [37, anecdote_aggregate].

### 6.5 Bottom line

Hexarelin (examorelin) is an **unapproved investigational GHRP** — **no FDA or EMA approval anywhere**, development halted at Phase II; **not legally eligible for §503A compounding** (unlisted / not on the bulks list, and explicitly **not** among the April-2026 removed-12, which were a distinct nominated-then-withdrawn set); and **WADA-prohibited at all times** under S2 (specifically S2.2.4, primary-confirmed) as a named GH-releasing peptide. Its real-world availability is confined to the research-chemical gray market.

### Coverage gaps (Section 6)
- Whether hexarelin was ever formally nominated/categorized under §503A vs. never nominated is not established from a primary FDA list — the end-state (ineligible) holds either way.
- The Phase-II-then-discontinued (~2005) narrative is secondary/encyclopedic [27], not confirmed against a primary trials-registry entry.
- The removed-12 enumeration relies partly on secondary legal-blog coverage [29], cross-checked against the FR primary [28] for hexarelin's absence.

## 7. Why evidence_tier C / risk_tier experimental

### 7.1 Why evidence_tier C

The grade is deliberate and conservative. Hexarelin lands at **C** for converging reasons:

1. **No approval, no Phase 3.** Development halted at Phase II; there is **no registrational efficacy trial for any indication** (§6).
2. **The human efficacy base is thin and surrogate-only.** Acute pharmacodynamic provocation (§1–§2) plus **two small, short studies** — 8 prepubertal short children (open-label, surrogate IGF-I/growth-velocity endpoints) and 12 healthy elderly (16 weeks) — neither powered for, nor designed around, durable clinical benefit (§3).
3. **The one multi-week human study is, in net, a negative result for the marketed rationale.** Over 16 weeks the GH response **desensitized ~45%** while **IGF-1, IGFBP-3, body composition, and bone mineral density did not change** [7, prospective_trial][8, clinical_trial]. The single most-relevant chronic human dataset therefore **fails to support** the body-comp/anti-aging claims.
4. **The cardiac line is preclinical-dominant.** A coherent GH-independent CD36 mechanism is well-supported in rats, but the human data are **acute inotropy only**, and the one diseased-heart human population (dilated cardiomyopathy) **did not respond** [15, human_acute] (§3).
5. **No cross-lab human replication of the cardiac signal, plus a 50% developer footprint** (§4).

What keeps hexarelin at C rather than lower is the **genuine, reproducible acute GH-releasing pharmacology** and a **mechanistically coherent, animal-replicated** cardiac line. What prevents anything higher is the absence of approval/Phase 3, the surrogate-only and net-negative chronic human data, and the complete absence of body-comp/anti-aging/athletic human efficacy. **C = acutely real pharmacology, clinically unproven benefit.**

### 7.2 Why risk_tier experimental

1. **Unapproved, no product label, no long-term human safety dataset** (§5–§6).
2. **Non-selective endocrine profile** — co-stimulates **cortisol, ACTH (via AVP), and prolactin**, unlike ipamorelin (§2.3, §5.1).
3. **Measurable acute cardiac/hemodynamic effects**, including a **mean-arterial-pressure rise**, with no long-term CV outcome data (§5.1).
4. **Desensitization within weeks** — a use-pattern risk that drives off-label cycling at unstudied doses (§2.4, §5.2).
5. **GH→IGF-1 axis class risks** (glucose, fluid retention, the associational IGF-1–cancer flag) plausible under chronic supraphysiologic exposure (§5.3).
6. **Gray-market-only supply** — identity/purity/sterility unverifiable (§6.4).
