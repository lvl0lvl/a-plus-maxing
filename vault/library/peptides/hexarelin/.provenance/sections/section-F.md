# Section F — Practitioner-practice + non-English

> **Tier discipline reminder (applies to this entire section):** Every source below is `practitioner_protocol`, `compounding_data_sheet`, `vendor_label`, or `regulatory`. **None of these may ground an efficacy or adverse-event-rate claim.** Where a clinical effect (e.g., GH-release magnitude, desensitization percentage, cardiovascular ejection-fraction change) appears in a practitioner/vendor source, it is reported here **only as evidence of what the practice layer believes/asserts** — the underlying physiology must be sourced from Sections B/C primary literature, not from anything in this section. The one non-English item that *is* a peer-reviewed review (CyberLeninka [7]) is itself secondary (a literature review), not a primary efficacy source.

## Prescribing-practice layer

### Compounding-pharmacy availability — gate result: effectively NEGATIVE

The brief asked whether any compounding pharmacy carries hexarelin. **Searched and not found** at the major 503A/503B houses checked. Empower Pharmacy's peptide pages list sermorelin acetate and other secretagogues but **no hexarelin product page was located** `[regulatory/compounding_data_sheet]` [1][2]. Tailor Made, Strive, Hallandale, and Belmar surfaced only generic compounding/peptide-program pages — **no hexarelin-specific product or data sheet** was found at any of them `[compounding_data_sheet]` [3].

This negative result is corroborated structurally by the **regulatory** picture. The FDA's April 2026 action on the interim 503A bulks list and the scheduled PCAC reviews enumerate the peptides actually in the compounding pipeline — BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-C, DSIP, Semax, Epitalon (July 2026 meeting) and GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, LL-37, dihexa, PEG-MGF (by Feb 2027) `[regulatory]` [4][5]. **Hexarelin appears on none of these lists** and was not among the September 2023/2024 Category-2 cohort. Practical reading: hexarelin was apparently never nominated as a bulk drug substance, so it sits **outside the legitimate 503A compounding pathway** — which explains why it is far less available from compounding pharmacies than sermorelin or CJC-1295. What supply exists is **gray-market research-chemical vendors**, not patient-specific compounding. (Self-check 1 satisfied: this is an availability/regulatory observation, not an efficacy claim.)

### What the practice layer asserts for dosing/cycle/route

With no compounding data sheet to anchor on, the dosing conventions below come from **wellness-clinic pages and vendor/peptide-information sites** and are reported strictly as *practice convention*, not as validated dosing:

- **Wittmer Rejuvenation Clinic** (`practitioner_protocol` candidate) recommends **0.2 mg (200 mcg) SC at bedtime**, nightly for month 1 then **every other night**, run **12–16 weeks consecutively followed by a 4-week break** [6]. **Attribution gap (honest disclosure, self-check 2):** the page carries a **clinic name (Wittmer) and an April-2025 image date but NO named prescribing physician** and no explicit author credential. By the brief's own rule this therefore **fails the name+venue+date bar for a citable practitioner_protocol** — it is a clinic marketing page, downgraded accordingly. It also gives only a route/dose, **no desensitization rationale**.
- **Revolution Health & Wellness** (Sapulpa, OK), dated **May 11, 2025**, has a **venue + date but no named author**; it gives *no* mcg dosing, only the qualitative claim that hexarelin "has the highest potency for GH release but may also lead to quicker receptor desensitization if not cycled properly," and advises against stacking multiple GHRPs [8]. Again **fails name+venue+date** (no prescriber named) → not a groundable protocol, reported as practice sentiment only.
- **Vendor/peptide-info convention** (`vendor_label`, gray-market — dosing reported as convention only, never efficacy): **100 mcg SC, 1–3×/day**, doses spaced 4–6 h apart, empty-stomach/bedtime timing; common total **200–300 mcg/day**; beginner 50–100 mcg once daily [9][10]. These sites converge on **~100 mcg SC 2–3×/day cycled**, matching the defining fact.

**Desensitization-driven cycling (self-check 3):** the practice layer explicitly frames hexarelin's cycling around **tachyphylaxis/receptor desensitization**, not calendar convenience. Vendor/info pages state users "cycle off for 4–8 weeks after 8–12 weeks of use to restore receptor sensitivity" [9] and cite a marked drop in GH-releasing potency over 2 weeks of daily dosing [11]. **Per self-check 1, the actual desensitization magnitude must come from Section B/C primary literature — the 40–60% / 50–75% figures floating around vendor pages are NOT grounded here.** The point retained from this section is only that *the practice convention itself is built around desensitization-driven on/off cycling.*

**Stacking (commercial convention, not efficacy — self-check 3 boundary):** practice pages routinely pair hexarelin (a GHRP / ghrelin-receptor agonist) with a **GHRH analogue — CJC-1295 (no DAC) or Mod GRF 1-29 / sermorelin / tesamorelin** — for "synergistic" GH release [9][8]. **Reported as commercial convention only.** Hexarelin's own dosing (~100 mcg SC) is **kept distinct** from the GHRH-analogue partners and is **not cross-attributed** to them.

### Reconstitution (vendor_label / info-site — MATH ONLY)

- **5 mg vial + 2.5 mL bacteriostatic water → 2 mg/mL (2000 mcg/mL)**; therefore **0.05 mL = 5 IU on a U-100 insulin syringe = 100 mcg**; 200 mcg = 10 IU `[vendor_label]` [9].
- **2 mg vial + 1 mL BAC water → 2 mg/mL** `[vendor_label]` [11].
- **6 mg vial + 6 mL BAC water → 1 mg/mL** (the Wittmer clinic mixing instruction; 0.2 mL = 200 mcg) `[practitioner_protocol, downgraded]` [6].
- Post-reconstitution storage convention: refrigerate, use within ~28 days `[vendor_label]` [11].

### Cost (qualitative)

Pricing is **gray-market vendor only** (no legitimate compounded price exists, given the availability gate above). Indicative figures: a **2 mg research vial ~$20**, ten-vial kits **~$160** `[vendor_label]` [11]. Qualitatively **inexpensive per vial**, but the **short desensitization-limited course + every-other-night maintenance** means modest total spend per cycle. ## Non-English literature

**Method:** searched Russian (CyberLeninka / eLibrary), Chinese (CNKI / Wanfang / VIP-Qikan), and Italian (originator-country) sources, distinguishing **non-English-LANGUAGE primaries** from English-published work by non-English authors.

- **Russian — ONE genuine Russian-language secondary source located.** Brylëv MI, Kozin SV, Korolëv VL, Lotorev DS, Pavlova LA, Alekseev AA. *"Пептиды, повышающие секрецию соматотропного гормона, и их влияние на сердечно-сосудистую систему"* ("Peptides enhancing somatotropin secretion and their effects on the cardiovascular system"), **Ученые записки КГУ (Scholarly Notes, Kursk State University), 2013** — full text in Russian `[regulatory/academic-secondary; review, NOT primary]` [7]. It discusses hexarelin's cardiovascular effects (left-ventricular ejection-fraction improvement in ischemic cardiomyopathy, anti-ischemic protection, multiple administration routes). **It is a literature review, so it cannot ground efficacy here (self-check 1) — listed as evidence that a Russian-language secondary literature exists, not as a primary finding.** Other Russian hits (SportWiki, russianpeptide.com, aasraw, eko-tec) are **wiki/vendor and excluded** (SportWiki is wiki-type → HALT-adjacent; vendors don't ground anything).

- **Chinese — NO hexarelin-specific Chinese-language primary located.** CNKI/Wanfang/VIP searches returned Chinese GH and ghrelin (脑肠肽 Ghrelin) literature and recombinant-hGH clinical papers, but **no Chinese-language clinical or primary study specifically on hexarelin (六肽/海沙瑞林) was located.** Honestly reported as **none located** (self-check 5). Hexarelin appears in Chinese sources only as a referenced GH-secretagogue, not as the subject of a located primary.

- **Italian (originator country) — NO Italian-LANGUAGE primary located.** Hexarelin originated at **Mediolanum Farmaceutici (Milan/Turin), with R. Deghenghi as principal investigator, early 1990s** — but consistent with the brief's caveat, **the originator group's work was largely English-published** (e.g., dose-response and GH-secretagogue papers in English-language journals). Italian-language searches surfaced only **vendor pages (Peptidy, SCBT-IT), a generic SIF/GH magazine piece, and general GH-deficiency reviews — no Italian-language hexarelin primary** [12]. Reported as **none located** for Italian-language primaries; the substantive originator literature is in English (belongs to Sections B/C, not here).

**Non-English net result:** one Russian-language **review** (secondary, non-groundable) located; **no Chinese-language and no Italian-language primary located.** No fabrication — gaps reported as gaps (self-check 5).
