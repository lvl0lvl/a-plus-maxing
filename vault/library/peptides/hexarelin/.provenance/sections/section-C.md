## Section C — Clinical + cardiac/CD36 + concentration audit

**Class note:** Hexarelin is a synthetic hexapeptide **growth hormone secretagogue / growth-hormone-releasing peptide (GHRP)** — His-D-2-methyl-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂. It is **not** a GHRH analogue: it acts on the GHS-R1a (ghrelin) receptor (and, in heart, on CD36), not on the GHRH receptor. Every figure below is annotated with its source population. Throughout, evidence is demarcated as **EVIDENCED** (peer-reviewed human or animal data exists), **INVESTIGATIONAL** (mechanism/preclinical only, no efficacy endpoint in humans), or **ABSENT** (no robust data exists).

### GH-axis clinical evidence (small, short, old)

The human GH-axis literature for hexarelin consists of a handful of **small, short-duration** studies. There is **no large-scale, long-term efficacy trial** and **no regulatory approval** for any indication.

- **EVIDENCED — Short children (open-label, non-randomized, n=8).** Laron et al. gave intranasal hexarelin **60 µg/kg three times daily for up to 8 months** to **8 prepubertal short children** (aged 4–11.6 y). IGF-I rose from 10.4±3.9 to 14.1±4.6 nmol/L (P<0.004) and **linear growth velocity** increased from 5.3±0.8 to 8.3±1.7 cm/year (P<0.0001) `[1]`. **Caveats:** uncontrolled, tiny n, surrogate/short-term endpoints (no final-height data); this is a pediatric short-stature population, **not** a body-composition or anti-aging population.

- **EVIDENCED — Healthy elderly, long-term GH (n=12), with the desensitization caveat.** Rahim, O'Neill & Shalet gave **subcutaneous hexarelin 1.5 µg/kg twice daily for 16 weeks** to **12 healthy elderly subjects**. The GH response **attenuated** progressively — mean AUC-GH fell from 19.1±2.4 to 10.5±1.8 µg/L·h by week 16 (P<0.01) — while **serum IGF-I and IGFBP-3 did not change significantly**, and body composition and bone mineral density were unchanged `[2]`. The companion analysis ("Does desensitization to hexarelin occur?") confirmed the attenuation is **partial and reversible**: four weeks after stopping, AUC-GH recovered to ~19.4 µg/L·h, not significantly different from baseline `[3]`. **This is the central desensitization caveat:** chronic GHRP dosing blunts its own GH-releasing effect and, critically, **failed to move IGF-I or body composition over 16 weeks in the elderly** — i.e., the GH-axis "anti-aging" rationale is **not** supported even by the only multi-week human study.

- **EVIDENCED (mechanistic, GHD adults) — short tolerance/desensitization work.** A separate short-term study found that short-term intranasal or oral hexarelin did **not** desensitize GH responsiveness in human aging over brief exposure, contrasting with the 16-week finding and underscoring that desensitization is dose/duration-dependent (Ghigo group; see Section A/B for full handling of acute pharmacodynamics).

**Net GH-axis read:** Hexarelin reliably **acutely** releases GH (acute pharmacodynamics covered in Section A). Beyond acute provocation, the human chronic evidence is **two small studies (children; elderly)**, both with surrogate endpoints, and the elderly study explicitly showed **no sustained IGF-I or body-composition benefit plus self-desensitization**.

### Cardiac / CD36 research line (GH-independent — mostly preclinical)

A distinct and scientifically interesting line shows hexarelin has **direct cardiac actions independent of GH release**, mediated largely by the scavenger receptor **CD36** on cardiomyocytes and microvascular endothelium (in addition to GHS-R1a). Human cardiac data are **acute, hemodynamic, and small**; durable clinical outcomes are **animal-only**.

- **EVIDENCED (human, acute, n=7+7) — GH-independent inotropy.** Bisi et al. (Turin/Ghigo group) gave **IV hexarelin** to **7 GH-deficient hypopituitary adult men + 9 healthy male controls**, measuring LVEF by equilibrium radionuclide angiocardiography. Hexarelin **acutely raised LVEF** in both groups (GHD: 50±1%→57±2%; controls: 63±2%→70±2%) **without** changing blood pressure, heart rate, catecholamines, or cardiac output `[4]`. Because the effect occurred in GHD subjects (who cannot mount a GH rise), it is **GH-independent** — but this is an **acute, single-dose hemodynamic** effect, not a treated-outcome trial.

- **EVIDENCED (human, acute, n=7+7+12) — confirmation + the cardiomyopathy ceiling.** Broglio et al. (Turin/Ghigo group) gave **acute IV hexarelin 2.0 µg/kg** to **7 normal adults, 7 GH-deficient patients, and 12 dilated-cardiomyopathy patients**. LVEF rose significantly in normal and GHD subjects but **NOT in the dilated-cardiomyopathy group**, again without BP/HR change `[5]`. The positive inotropic effect is real and GH-independent in intact myocardium, but **the diseased-heart population — the one of clinical interest — did not respond acutely.** No follow-on chronic efficacy trial in heart failure exists.

- **EVIDENCED (animal — receptor identity) — CD36 is the cardiac target.** Bodart et al. cross-linked a photoactivatable hexarelin derivative in **rat cardiac membranes** and identified the binding protein as **CD36** (84 kDa). In perfused rat hearts, hexarelin raised coronary perfusion pressure dose-dependently; the action tracked CD36 `[6]`. **Preclinical (rat), receptor-mechanism — no human outcome.**

- **EVIDENCED (animal — cardioprotection, GH-independent) — Locatelli/Müller group.** Locatelli et al. gave **hexarelin 80 µg/kg SC for 7 days** to **hypophysectomized rats** (no GH capacity) and subjected explanted hearts to **ischemia-reperfusion**. Hexarelin prevented the hypophysectomy-worsened I/R damage — limiting LV end-diastolic and coronary perfusion pressure rises, blunting angiotensin-II reactivity, and reducing creatine-kinase release — while an inactive GHRP analogue (EP 51389) did not, confirming a **specific cardiac receptor, GH-independent** mechanism `[7]`. **Preclinical (rat), n not stated in abstract.**

- **EVIDENCED (animal — chronic heart failure) — PTEN/Akt-mTOR.** Agbo et al. gave **hexarelin 100 µg/kg SC twice daily for 30 days** to **male Sprague-Dawley rats (96 total; n=6/measurement group)** after coronary-artery-ligation heart failure. Hexarelin **improved LV function, reduced remodeling/fibrosis (TGF-β1, procollagen-I) and oxidative stress**, via PTEN upregulation and Akt/mTOR downregulation `[8]`. **Preclinical (rat); independent Chinese group — useful external replication of cardioprotective signal, but still animal.**

**Net cardiac read:** A coherent, GH-independent, CD36-mediated cardioprotective mechanism is **well-supported preclinically (multiple rat models, ≥3 groups)** and shows an **acute** GH-independent positive inotropic effect in **small human studies** — but with **no chronic human cardiac trial**, and the only diseased-heart human data (dilated CMP) showed **no acute LVEF response**. This domain is **INVESTIGATIONAL for any therapeutic claim**.

### ABSENT domains (state plainly)

- **Body composition / fat loss / muscle gain:** **ABSENT.** No RCT shows hexarelin produces durable fat loss or lean-mass gain in humans. The only multi-week human study (16 wk, elderly) found **no change in body composition** `[2]`.
- **Anti-aging / longevity / IGF-1 elevation as a benefit:** **ABSENT.** Chronic dosing did **not** raise IGF-I/IGFBP-3 and **self-desensitizes** `[2,3]`.
- **Athletic / ergogenic performance:** **ABSENT.** No controlled performance trial exists; hexarelin is a WADA-prohibited GHRP with no efficacy evidence for performance.
- **Phase 3 efficacy / regulatory approval, any indication:** **ABSENT.** Hexarelin has never advanced to a registrational program; it is an unapproved research peptide.
