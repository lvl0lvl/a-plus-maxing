## Section E — Regulatory & sport status

### FDA / EMA marketing approval — never approved, anywhere

Hexarelin (INN **examorelin**; FDA UNII 09QF37C617) has **never received marketing approval from the FDA, the EMA, or any other regulatory authority** for any indication. `[regulatory]` The compound is an investigational growth-hormone-releasing hexapeptide (His-D-2-methyl-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂), a synthetic analogue of GHRP-6 developed under the codes **MF-6003 / EP-23905** by **Mediolanum Farmaceutici** (with Europeptides as co-developer) for somatotropin (growth-hormone) deficiency. `[regulatory]` The US government's authoritative substance database (NCATS Inxight Drugs, a National Center for Advancing Translational Sciences resource) lists examorelin's development status as **"Investigational"** with approval year **"Unknown"** and records **no approved indication in any jurisdiction** [1]. `[regulatory]`

Clinical development reached **Phase II** (growth-hormone deficiency and congestive heart failure) but was **discontinued without yielding a marketed product** — secondary sources place the strategic discontinuation around 2005 [2]. `[mechanism_review]` A targeted check of the FDA orange-book/approval ecosystem and EMA medicines register surfaced **no NDA, BLA, or EU marketing authorisation** for hexarelin/examorelin; the consistent picture across the substance-level record is that it never advanced past investigational status [1]. `[regulatory]` **Verification note:** the "never approved" claim is anchored to the NCATS primary substance record [1]; the specific Phase-II-then-discontinued narrative rests on secondary/encyclopedic sources [2] and is flagged accordingly — directionally consistent with the investigational status but not independently confirmed against a primary clinical-trials registry entry in this pass.

### FDA §503A compounding status — NOT among the Apr-2026 removed-12; effectively unlisted / not eligible

Hexarelin's compounding standing must be stated precisely because the 2026 regulatory landscape shifted.

**What FR Doc 2026-07361 actually is.** Published in the Federal Register on **April 16, 2026**, FR Doc **2026-07361** is the **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) Notice of Meeting** for **July 23–24, 2026** — it is *not* itself "the removal of 12 peptides." `[regulatory]` The govinfo primary text confirms the PCAC will evaluate **seven** bulk substances for possible inclusion on the §503A Bulks List: **Day 1 (July 23)** — BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c; **Day 2 (July 24)** — Emideltide/DSIP, Semax, Epitalon (each in free-base and acetate forms) [3]. `[regulatory]` **Hexarelin / examorelin is explicitly NOT on this agenda** [3]. `[regulatory]`

**The "removed-12."** Separately, in April 2026 the FDA removed **12 peptides from Category 2** of the interim §503A bulks list (nominations withdrawn). The enumerated 12 are: **(1) BPC-157, (2) TB-500/Thymosin β4 fragment, (3) KPV, (4) MOTS-c, (5) DSIP/Emideltide, (6) Semax, (7) Epitalon, (8) GHK-Cu, (9) Melanotan-II, (10) Dihexa, (11) PEG-MGF (pegylated mechano growth factor), (12) LL-37/Cathelicidin** [4]. `[regulatory]` **Hexarelin is NOT one of the 12** — it is not named in the removal action and is not referenced anywhere in the practitioner/legal coverage of it [4]. `[regulatory]` (The 7 on the July agenda plus 5 more — GHK-Cu, Melanotan-II, LL-37, Dihexa, PEG-MGF — slated for a later PCAC meeting together make up the 12.)

**Hexarelin's actual standing.** Hexarelin does **not appear** on (a) the FDA §503A Bulks List of substances that *can* be compounded, (b) the Category 2 "significant safety risks" list, or (c) the PCAC July-2026 or follow-up agendas. `[regulatory]` It was **not among the nominated-then-withdrawn 12**, and no nomination record for hexarelin surfaced in this pass. The most defensible characterization is therefore: **hexarelin is effectively unlisted / not a nominated bulk drug substance under §503A.** `[regulatory]` Under the §503A statutory framework, a compounder may use a bulk drug substance only if it (i) has an applicable USP/NF monograph, (ii) is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or (iii) appears on the §503A bulks list — hexarelin satisfies **none** of these [5]. `[regulatory]` The practical consequence is that hexarelin is **not legally eligible for §503A compounding**, the same end-state as the closely related GHRPs **GHRP-2 and GHRP-6**, which FDA placed in Category 2 citing immunogenicity/aggregation risk and (for GHRP-2) reports of serious adverse events [5]. `[regulatory]` **Uncertainty flagged honestly:** I could not confirm from a primary FDA list whether hexarelin was ever formally *nominated and categorized* versus simply *never nominated*; either way it is not on the eligible bulks list and not compoundable, but the precise mechanism (Category 2 vs. never-nominated) is **not established** in this pass and should not be over-stated.

### WADA — prohibited at all times under S2.2.4 (GH-releasing factors / GHRPs)

Hexarelin is **prohibited at all times** (both in-competition and out-of-competition) under the **WADA 2026 Prohibited List** (entry into force 1 January 2026). `[regulatory]` It falls within **Section S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances, and Mimetics)**, the heading of which reads verbatim **"PROHIBITED AT ALL TIMES (IN- AND OUT-OF-COMPETITION)"** and **"All prohibited substances in this class are non-Specified Substances"** [6]. `[regulatory]` Within S2.2 (Peptide Hormones and Their Releasing Factors), subsection **S2.2.4 ("Growth hormone releasing factors")** names it explicitly under the **GH-releasing peptides (GHRPs)** bullet, quoted verbatim from the official List: *"GH-releasing peptides (GHRPs) [e.g. alexamorelin, **examorelin (hexarelin)**, GHRP-1, GHRP-2 (pralmorelin), GHRP-3, GHRP-4, GHRP-5 and GHRP-6]"* [6]. `[regulatory]` Because the entire S2 class is **non-Specified** [6], an anti-doping rule violation involving hexarelin carries the strictest sanctioning posture. `[regulatory]`

**Subsection numbering — primary-confirmed.** The **S2.2.4** decimal is taken directly from the official WADA 2026 Prohibited List PDF (text extracted from the wada-ama.org primary [6]), not from a mirror or inference — it is the actual official numbering, where S2.2.4 ("Growth hormone releasing factors") sits beneath S2.2 ("Peptide Hormones and Their Releasing Factors"). The off-whitelist mirrors (suek.fi / drugs.com) now serve only as secondary corroboration of the same wording.

### EU status and gray-market reality

There is **no EU marketing authorisation** for hexarelin (consistent with the never-marketed investigational status above) [1]; it is not an approved medicine in any EU member state. `[regulatory]` In practice hexarelin circulates almost entirely through the **"research chemical" gray market** — sold by online peptide vendors labelled **"for research/laboratory use only, not for human consumption."** `[anecdote_aggregate]` This labelling is the standard vendor mechanism for distributing an unapproved drug outside the medicines framework; such product is not subject to FDA/EMA quality oversight, and identity/purity rest solely on vendor-supplied certificates of analysis (qualitative observation only, not a regulatory authorization of any kind). `[anecdote_aggregate]`

### Bottom line

Hexarelin (examorelin) is an **unapproved investigational GHRP** — **no FDA or EMA approval anywhere**, development halted at Phase II; **not legally eligible for §503A compounding** (unlisted / not on the bulks list, and explicitly **not** among the April-2026 removed-12, which were a distinct nominated-then-withdrawn set); and **WADA-prohibited at all times** under S2 (specifically S2.2.4, primary-confirmed) as a named GH-releasing peptide. Its real-world availability is confined to the research-chemical gray market.
