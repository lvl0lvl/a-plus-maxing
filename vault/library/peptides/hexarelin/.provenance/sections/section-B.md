## Section B — Human GH pharmacology + tachyphylaxis

**Compound class note.** Hexarelin (His-D-2-methyl-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2) is a synthetic **growth hormone-releasing peptide (GHRP)** — a GHS-R1a (ghrelin-receptor) agonist — **not a GHRH analogue**. Throughout this section "synergy with GHRH" means synergy between two pharmacologically distinct secretagogues acting on two different receptors, not a property of the molecule itself.

### B.1 — Acute GH-release magnitude and route dependence (healthy adults)

In the foundational human pharmacodynamic study, hexarelin was a potent and reproducible acute GH secretagogue across multiple routes in 12 healthy young volunteers. After IV dosing, the GH area-under-curve rose dose-dependently from 3175 ± 506 µg·min/L (1 µg/kg) to 4422 ± 626 µg·min/L (2 µg/kg); subcutaneous dosing produced comparable AUCs (3180 ± 392 at 1.5 µg/kg; 4459 ± 566 at 3 µg/kg), with absolute SC bioavailability of 77.0 ± 10.5%. Intranasal and oral routes were far weaker (bioavailability 4.8 ± 0.9% and 0.3 ± 0.1%, respectively). Critically, IV hexarelin 1 µg/kg released roughly **twice the GH of an equimolar dose of GHRH** (about 2-fold higher, P < 0.001), establishing hexarelin as a stronger acute GH stimulus than GHRH itself [1]. `[rct, n=12 healthy adults]`

A formal IV dose-response study in healthy adult males quantified the ceiling: the GH response rose with dose to a **plateau of ~140 mU/L reached at 1.0 µg/kg, with an ED50 of 0.48 ± 0.02 µg/kg** — i.e., the acute GH effect saturates at low microgram-per-kg doses and higher doses add little [2]. `[rct, healthy adult males]` This dose-ceiling is corroborated by the original multiroute study, where escalating beyond ~2 µg/kg IV / 3 µg/kg SC gave diminishing incremental GH [1].

### B.2 — Synergy with GHRH (two-receptor, not intrinsic)

Hexarelin and GHRH act through distinct receptors and are functionally complementary. In 12 healthy male volunteers, IV hexarelin 2 µg/kg produced markedly higher GH than GHRH 2 µg/kg (AUC 4693 ± 691 vs 1494 ± 102 µg·min/L, P < 0.01), and **coadministration was synergistic** rather than additive (combined AUC 7395 ± 450 µg·min/L, P < 0.05 vs hexarelin alone). Exogenous somatostatin abolished the GHRH response but only partially blunted the hexarelin response — direct evidence the two operate by different mechanisms [3]. `[rct, n=12 healthy adults]` Consistent with a partly somatostatin-independent mechanism, in subjects with glucocorticoid excess the GHRH-evoked GH response was severely blunted (median ΔGH 0.9 vs 7.1 µg/L in controls) whereas the **hexarelin response was preserved** (median ΔGH 15.5 vs 17.9 µg/L; no significant patient-vs-control difference) [4]. `[rct cross-over, n=14 (8 glucocorticoid-excess + 6 controls)]`

### B.3 — INTEGRITY FLAG: Hexarelin is NOT GH-selective — cortisol, prolactin, and ACTH co-secretion

Unlike ipamorelin (the selective GHRP), hexarelin **meaningfully co-stimulates the HPA axis and prolactin**. This is a defining liability and is documented in tier-1 human studies:

- In 15 healthy young men, IV hexarelin produced significant ACTH release (AUC 3444 ± 696 ng/L·125 min) and cortisol release (45,844 ± 2,925 nmol/L·125 min), plus significant GH and prolactin release. The HPA effect was **CRH-independent and appears to be mediated by arginine vasopressin (AVP)** — a true central activation of the stress axis, not an artifact [5]. `[interventional, n=15 healthy young men]`
- In the IV dose-response study, prolactin rose to a plateau of ~180% above baseline (ED50 0.39 ± 0.02 µg/kg) and cortisol stepped up ~40% at 0.5 µg/kg [2]. `[rct, healthy adult males]`
- Head-to-head against GHRH, TRH and hCRH, hexarelin's prolactin-releasing activity was lower than TRH's but its **ACTH/cortisol-releasing activity was comparable to that of hCRH** — i.e., hexarelin stimulates the adrenal axis about as strongly as a corticotropin-releasing stimulus does [6]. `[interventional comparison, healthy young adults]`

**Bottom line for the wiki:** the GH spike from hexarelin comes bundled with a cortisol/ACTH/prolactin spike of clinically relevant magnitude; it should not be presented as a clean GH agent.

### B.4 — DEFINING LIMITATION: Tachyphylaxis / desensitization (acute ≠ sustained)

The single most important fact about hexarelin's human pharmacology is that **the acute GH response does not survive continued dosing — the response is progressively down-regulated (GHS-R desensitization).** Two tier-1 studies establish this directly:

- **Long-term therapy study (verified key finding):** In adults given hexarelin 1.5 µg/kg SC **twice daily for 16 weeks**, the GH AUC fell from a baseline of **19.1 ± 2.4 µg/L·h to 13.1 (week 1) → 12.3 (week 4, P < 0.05) → 10.5 ± 1.8 µg/L·h (week 16, P < 0.01) — an ~45% attenuation.** The attenuation was **reversible**: 4 weeks after stopping (week 20) the AUC recovered to 19.4 ± 3.7 µg/L·h, not significantly different from baseline. Crucially, **serum IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 did NOT change significantly over the 20-week period (P = 0.24 and P = 0.74)** [7]. `[prospective clinical trial, adults]`
- **Companion desensitization study** in 12 healthy elderly subjects (same 16-week twice-daily SC paradigm): mean GH AUC decreased from 19.1 µg/L·h (week 0) to 10.5 µg/L·h (week 16, P < 0.01), then rebounded to 19.4 ± 3.7 µg/L·h four weeks post-course (P < 0.05 vs week 16) — confirming **partial, reversible desensitization** [8]. `[clinical trial, n=12 healthy elderly]`

The mechanistic interpretation across this literature is GHS-R1a desensitization/down-regulation with sustained agonist exposure. The practical consequence is decisive: **acute GH spikes overstate durable effect.** Because the downstream IGF-1/IGFBP-3 axis did not rise even during 16 weeks of twice-daily dosing — while the GH spike itself shrank by ~45% — there is no human evidence that chronic hexarelin produces a sustained anabolic GH/IGF-1 signal. Reported "cycling" regimens are extrapolations from this reversibility finding, not from durable-efficacy trials.

### B.5 — IGF-1 with short courses, and population annotation

Short single-dose human studies measure acute GH only; the longest controlled human course (16 weeks, twice-daily SC) showed **no significant IGF-1 or IGFBP-3 change** despite real acute GH release [7] `[prospective trial, adults]`. The GH response is also strongly **age/developmental-stage dependent**: in 60 subjects given 2.0 µg/kg IV, GH AUC was low in prepubertal children (769.5 µg·min/L), strikingly higher at puberty (1960.2), high in young adults, and **lower in the elderly (951.1)**; prolactin and cortisol responses showed no significant age variation, while ACTH was enhanced at puberty [9]. `[interventional, n=60 across 4 age groups]` Thus quantitative GH numbers must be read against population: pubertal > young-adult > elderly ≈ prepubertal.
