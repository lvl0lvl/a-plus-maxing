## TL;DR

Tesamorelin (Egrifta / Egrifta SV / Egrifta WR; Theratechnologies) is a synthetic, DPP-IV-resistant analogue of the full-length human GHRH peptide that stimulates the body's own pulsatile growth-hormone secretion, raising IGF-1 toward the physiologic range. Mechanistically it is a clean, physiologic secretagogue: it works **upstream** at the pituitary GHRH receptor, preserves feedback and pulsatility (unlike flat exogenous rhGH), and its effects are **fully reversible** on stopping. Its one FDA-approved use — **reduction of excess visceral abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy** — is backed by two pivotal Phase 3 RCTs and a pooled/extension analysis showing a ~15% VAT reduction that is **visceral-selective and weight-neutral** (subcutaneous fat, body weight, and BMI do not change), with a 2026 meta-analysis independently confirming the VAT and hepatic-fat effects **in that same HIV population**. That is the entire proven story. Everything beyond it is either **INVESTIGATIONAL** — NAFLD/hepatic fat (real signal, but every datapoint is in HIV-positive patients) and cognition (one positive but small, short, surrogate-endpoint RCT in HIV-negative older adults/MCI) — or **ABSENT**: there is **no human efficacy data of any kind** for bodybuilding, athletic performance, general/healthy-adult fat loss, anti-aging, or fracture prevention, despite heavy off-label and gray-market use. The efficacy evidence is single-sponsor-concentrated (100% Theratechnologies-linked study drug/sponsorship; no independent replication outside the HIV setting). Safety is well-characterized for an approved drug: the common AEs are injection-site reactions, arthralgia/myalgia, peripheral edema, and paresthesia; IGF-1 rises into supraphysiologic ranges in a meaningful minority and must be monitored; and there is an honestly two-sided glucose signal (mean glucose parameters did not shift in trials, but the label reports a higher proportion of patients crossing the diabetic HbA1c threshold, OR 3.3). It is contraindicated in HPA-axis disruption, active malignancy, hypersensitivity (incl. the mannitol excipient), and pregnancy. Regulatory reality: FDA-approved (2010) for the narrow indication only; **not approved in the EU**; **WADA-prohibited at all times**; ~$3,000/month brand price that drives a gray-market channel. **Net:** tesamorelin is a genuinely approved, evidence-backed drug **for one narrow indication** — and an evidence-free extrapolation for every popular off-label use.

## 1. Identity, Structure & Pharmacokinetics

### 1.1 What tesamorelin actually is

Tesamorelin (developmental code **TH9507**; brands **Egrifta → Egrifta SV → Egrifta WR**) is a synthetic analogue of human growth-hormone-releasing factor (GRF, also called growth-hormone-releasing hormone, GHRH). It was developed by **Theratechnologies Inc.** (Montréal) and is the **only FDA-approved GHRH analogue** in the growth-hormone-secretagogue cluster; it received U.S. approval in **November 2010** for the reduction of excess abdominal (visceral) fat in HIV-infected patients with antiretroviral-therapy–associated lipodystrophy [1][23]. `regulatory`

The defining structural fact is that tesamorelin retains the **full 44-amino-acid sequence of human GRF (hGRF(1-44)NH2)** and adds a single N-terminal modification. The manufacturer's label describes it precisely: "It is comprised of the 44 amino acid sequence of human GRF and a hexenoyl moiety, a C6 chain with a double bond at position 3, attached to the tyrosine residue at the N-terminal part of the molecule" [1]. This **trans-3-hexenoyl** group is anchored on the amino-terminal **Tyr1** residue — hence the chemical synonym **N-hexenoyl-Tyr1-hGRF(1-44)** [6]. Reported molecular characteristics from the label are molecular formula **C₂₂₁H₃₆₆N₇₂O₆₇S** (free base, distributed as an acetate salt) and a molecular weight of approximately **5136 Da** [1]. `regulatory`/`mechanism_review`

**Critical structural distinction (get this right):** tesamorelin is built on the **complete GRF(1-44)** backbone, NOT on the truncated **GRF(1-29)** core that sermorelin and CJC-1295 use (see §1.5). The N-terminal cap on tesamorelin is the trans-3-hexenoyl lipid group, not the internal amino-acid substitutions or albumin-binding tether used by other analogues.

### 1.2 Mechanism of action

Tesamorelin reproduces the physiology of endogenous GHRH. It binds and activates the **GHRH receptor (GHRH-R / GRF receptor)**, a **class B (secretin-family) G-protein-coupled receptor** expressed on the **somatotroph cells of the anterior pituitary**. The label states that "in vitro, tesamorelin binds and stimulates human GRF receptors with similar potency as the endogenous GRF," acting on pituitary somatotrophs to "stimulate the synthesis and pulsatile release of endogenous growth hormone (GH)," which in turn "increases IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 levels" [1]. `regulatory`

The canonical downstream signalling for this receptor is **Gsα → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → protein kinase A (PKA)**, with PKA-driven transcriptional activation of the GH gene and stimulated exocytosis of GH from somatotroph granules [5][7]. The therapeutically important consequence is that tesamorelin **augments the body's own pulsatile GH axis** rather than replacing it with a flat, exogenous GH signal. Bedimo's review emphasizes that, unlike recombinant human GH (rhGH) administration, tesamorelin acts "through the hypothalamic-pituitary axis," generating "a more physiologic pattern of GH secretion" and thereby avoiding the sustained supraphysiologic GH exposure characteristic of rhGH therapy [5]. `mechanism_review`

Two mechanistic features follow directly from working upstream of the pituitary:

1. **Preserved feedback / pulsatility.** Because GH is released episodically and the negative-feedback loop (GH and IGF-1 feeding back on the hypothalamus and pituitary) remains intact, GH secretion is self-limiting. Population PK/PD modelling found that tesamorelin stimulates GH "in an 'episodic' manner, i.e., for a finite duration of time," consistent with augmentation of native pulses rather than continuous elevation [8]. `cohort`
2. **Reversibility on discontinuation.** Effects depend on continued dosing. In the Phase 3 program the improvement in VAT "was not sustained among patients who were re-randomized to placebo after 26 weeks of therapy"; VAT reaccumulated toward baseline, so "chronic therapy therefore appears to be necessary to maintain the reductions" [5]. This reversibility is a direct corollary of the physiologic, secretagogue mechanism — there is no persistent depot or irreversible pathway change. `rct`/`mechanism_review`

GH released into the circulation acts on peripheral tissues including hepatocytes, where it stimulates production of **insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1)**; IGF-1 mediates much of the anabolic signalling, while GH itself drives lipolysis (the basis for the visceral-fat indication) [5]. `mechanism_review`

### 1.3 Pharmacokinetics

Tesamorelin is administered by **subcutaneous (SC) injection** (the approved regimen is a once-daily SC dose into the abdomen; the original Egrifta dose was 2 mg, with the Egrifta SV reformulation delivering a bioequivalent 1.4 mg — see §8) [1]. The pharmacokinetics are dominated by **very rapid absorption and very rapid elimination**, exactly as expected for a peptide secretagogue: `regulatory`

- **Tmax:** median peak plasma concentration is reached at **0.15 h (~9 minutes)** in both healthy subjects and HIV-infected patients [1].
- **Elimination half-life:** the label reports a mean t½ of **~8 minutes in healthy subjects** after a single SC dose [1]. In HIV-infected patients the mean elimination half-life is somewhat longer — on the order of **~18 minutes (single dose)** rising to **~37 minutes (multiple doses)** — which brackets the commonly cited "~26–38 min" range and explains the variability in secondary sources [1].
- **Disposition:** population PK analysis described tesamorelin with a one-compartment model with combined first- and zero-order absorption and linear elimination, estimating a plasma clearance of ~**1060 L/h** and volume of distribution of ~**200 L** [8]. `cohort`
- **Bioavailability:** SC bioavailability is low (the absolute fraction absorbed is small), but high intrinsic potency means a low-milligram dose achieves the target pharmacodynamic GH/IGF-1 response [1].

### 1.4 Stabilization rationale vs native GHRH

Native GRF/GHRH is degraded within minutes in plasma, principally by **dipeptidyl-peptidase IV (DPP-IV / DPP4)**, which cleaves the N-terminal dipeptide and inactivates the hormone. The purpose of the trans-3-hexenoyl cap on Tyr1 is to block this cleavage. In the primary non-clinical pharmacology study, TH9507 "was found to be resistant to dipeptidyl aminopeptidase-IV deactivation"; the modification "slowed the in vitro degradation of the peptide in rat, dog and human plasma" relative to unmodified hGRF(1-44)NH2 and prolonged in vivo elimination kinetics (apparent t½ ~21–45 min in dogs), with plasma GH and IGF-1 markedly increased in pigs, rats and dogs after repeat SC/IV dosing [3] (rat, dog, pig) `animal`/`in_vitro`. Thus tesamorelin's measured human half-life of minutes — still short, but meaningfully longer than the seconds-to-minutes survival of native GHRH — is sufficient to trigger a physiologic GH pulse before the peptide clears.

### 1.5 Contrast with related compounds

Within the growth-hormone-secretagogue space there are **two mechanistically distinct receptor targets**, and three structural sub-types of GHRH analogue. The three key contrasts:

1. **Tesamorelin vs sermorelin (both GHRH-R agonists, different backbone).** Sermorelin is **GRF(1-29)** — the truncated 29-residue minimal active fragment of GHRH with no stabilizing cap, giving it the shortest half-life and the closest match to native GHRH PK. Tesamorelin is the **full GRF(1-44) plus the trans-3-hexenoyl cap**, conferring DPP-IV resistance and a longer (though still short) half-life [1][5].
2. **Tesamorelin vs CJC-1295 (both GHRH-R agonists, different stabilization strategy).** CJC-1295 is a **modified GRF(1-29)** analogue. In a peer-reviewed human study, CJC-1295 is characterized as "a long-acting analog of GH-releasing hormone" with an estimated **half-life of 5.8–8.1 days** and GH/IGF-1 elevation persisting for days to weeks — a profile achieved via an albumin-binding drug-affinity-complex (DAC) tether on a GRF(1-29) core [9]. This is the opposite design philosophy from tesamorelin: CJC-1295 sacrifices pulsatility for a long, sustained signal, whereas tesamorelin's minutes-long half-life **preserves the physiologic pulse**. `rct` (used only to ground the contrast)
3. **GHRH analogues vs ghrelin-receptor secretagogues (different receptor entirely).** Ipamorelin and related "-relin" GH-releasing peptides act NOT on the GHRH receptor but on the **growth-hormone-secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHS-R1a), i.e., the ghrelin receptor** [5][7]. They work through a parallel pathway and are frequently combined with GHRH analogues for synergistic GH release. Tesamorelin has no meaningful activity at the ghrelin receptor; its entire mechanism is GHRH-R mediated.

In summary: tesamorelin is uniquely the **FDA-approved, full-length GRF(1-44), trans-3-hexenoyl-stabilized GHRH-receptor agonist** that augments the endogenous, reversible, pulsatile GH→IGF-1 axis.

### 1.6 Coverage gaps (Section 1)

- **Human PK is characterized but population-narrow** — the half-life/Tmax/clearance values come from healthy subjects and HIV-infected patients; PK in non-HIV off-label users at off-label doses is not separately characterized.
- **The CJC-1295/ipamorelin contrasts** are grounded in single representative sources for orientation, not exhaustive head-to-head PK comparisons.
- **No human dose-response PK** outside the approved 1.4–2 mg SC daily regimen.

## 2. Approved-Indication Efficacy (HIV-associated lipodystrophy)

**Scope / integrity boundary (read before any number).** Every efficacy figure in this section was generated in the **single** population in which tesamorelin's efficacy is FDA-established: **HIV-infected adults on antiretroviral therapy (ART) with lipodystrophy and excess visceral/abdominal adipose tissue (VAT)**. The FDA-approved indication is "the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy," and the label explicitly states the drug is "**not indicated for weight loss management as it has a weight neutral effect**" [1]. None of the numbers below should be read as evidence for general anti-aging, bodybuilding, or non-HIV fat-loss efficacy; such uses are off-label extrapolation (see §3, §6). The proven endpoint is **selective visceral (intra-abdominal) fat reduction in HIV lipodystrophy** — not weight, not subcutaneous fat, and not lean mass.

### 2.1 The pivotal trial: Falutz 2007 (NEJM) — LIPO-010

The pivotal Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial randomized **412 HIV-infected patients** (86% male) with ART-associated abdominal fat accumulation to **tesamorelin 2 mg subcutaneously once daily** or placebo for **26 weeks**; the prespecified primary endpoint was percent change in VAT by CT [10]. Results (all in HIV-lipodystrophy patients): `rct`

- **VAT: −15.2% with tesamorelin vs. +5.0% with placebo** at 26 weeks (P<0.001) — i.e., the drug reversed the ongoing visceral-fat accumulation seen in the placebo arm [10].
- **IGF-1: +81.0% vs. −5.0%**, confirming on-target GHRH-axis stimulation [10].
- **Triglycerides: −50 mg/dL vs. +9 mg/dL**, with a significant improvement in the total-cholesterol/HDL ratio [10].
- **No significant difference in glycemic measures** between arms at 26 weeks; more tesamorelin patients withdrew for adverse events, though overall AE rates did not differ significantly [10].

The trial's own framing is that the reduction was "highly significant and selective" for visceral fat — establishing the visceral-specific signature that distinguishes this approved endpoint from generalized fat or weight loss [10].

### 2.2 The confirmatory trial + durability/reversal: Falutz 2010 (JAIDS)

The confirmatory Phase 3 trial randomized **404 HIV-infected patients with excess abdominal fat** across **two sequential 26-week phases (52 weeks total)** [12]. The design is the critical part for the honesty of this section: Phase 1 (months 0–6) randomized 2:1 to tesamorelin 2 mg SC daily vs. placebo; Phase 2 (months 6–12) **re-randomized** the tesamorelin responders to continue or **switch to placebo**, while the original placebo arm crossed to active drug [12]. `rct`

- At 6 months, **VAT fell −10.9% (−21 cm²) with tesamorelin vs. −0.6% (−1 cm²) with placebo** [12].
- With continued treatment to 12 months, VAT reduction deepened to **roughly −18%** [12].
- **Effect reverses on discontinuation (key honest point):** in patients switched from tesamorelin to placebo at week 26, "the initial improvements over 6 months in VAT were rapidly lost" — visceral fat re-accumulated toward baseline [12]. This is corroborated by independent summaries reporting return to near-baseline VAT within ~3 months of stopping [14]. Tesamorelin is therefore a **maintenance therapy, not a one-time fix**: the benefit is contingent on continued dosing.
- IGF-1 increased significantly (P<0.001) without adverse glucose changes [12].

### 2.3 Pooled analysis of both Phase 3 trials: Falutz 2010 (JCEM)

The two coordinated pivotal trials were pooled (**N = 806 ART-treated HIV patients** with excess abdominal fat, randomized 2:1 to tesamorelin 2 mg SC daily [n=543] vs. placebo [n=263]; primary endpoint percent change in VAT at week 26) [11]. `rct/pooled`

- **VAT: −24 cm² (tesamorelin) vs. −2 cm² (placebo)**, treatment effect **−15.4%** at week 26 (P<0.001) [11].
- **IGF-1: +108 ± 112 vs. −7 ± 64 ng/mL** (P<0.001) — confirming the axis effect across the combined population [11].
- **Triglycerides: −37 mg/dL vs. +6 mg/dL**, treatment effect **−12.3%** [11].
- **Subcutaneous fat: NO significant change** (−2 ± 32 vs. +2 ± 29 cm², P=0.08) — the formal demonstration that the effect is **visceral-selective and weight-neutral**, not generalized fat or weight loss [11].
- At 52 weeks, continued-treatment patients **maintained** VAT reduction (−35 cm²) with no tachyphylaxis [11].
- "No clinically meaningful differences … in glucose parameters at wk 26 and 52"; generally well tolerated [11].

### 2.4 The pivotal program enumerated

There are exactly **2** pivotal Phase 3 efficacy trials underpinning the approval:

1. **LIPO-010** — n=412 — NCT00123253 — primary report Falutz 2007 NEJM [10][11].
2. **Confirmatory Phase 3 trial** — n=404 — 52-week 2-phase design — Falutz 2010 JAIDS [12]; pooled with #1 in Falutz 2010 JCEM [11].

(Related extension registration NCT00608023 covered an open-label safety extension; it is not a separate pivotal efficacy trial.) Both trials were 26-week main + 26-week extension, 2 mg SC daily, VAT-by-CT primary endpoint, **all in HIV-associated lipodystrophy** [10][11][12].

### 2.5 Independent confirmation: 2026 meta-analysis

A 2026 meta-analysis (Badran et al., *Obes Res Clin Pract*) of **five RCTs** of tesamorelin **in HIV-associated lipodystrophy** found a significant pooled VAT reduction (**MD = −27.71 cm², P<0.001**), a significant reduction in **hepatic fat (MD = −4.28%, P<0.001)**, improved IGF-1, and — consistent with the pivotal data — **no significant reduction in subcutaneous adipose tissue**, with no glucose perturbation and no serious safety signal beyond arthralgia/myalgia/paresthesia/injection-site reactions [13]. This reinforces the visceral-selective, weight-neutral profile **in the HIV-lipodystrophy population** and adds liver-fat reduction as a downstream visceral correlate. `meta_analysis` **Population annotation: HIV-lipodystrophy throughout — this is NOT a general-population meta-analysis.**

### 2.6 Magnitude, durability, reversal, IGF-1 targeting — summary (all in HIV lipodystrophy)

- **Magnitude:** ~15% VAT reduction at 26 weeks, deepening to ~18% by 52 weeks on continued dosing [10][11][12]; pooled treatment effect −15.4% / −24 cm² [11]; meta-analytic MD −27.71 cm² [13].
- **Selectivity (endpoint discipline):** the proven endpoint is **visceral/intra-abdominal fat by CT**. Subcutaneous fat, body weight, and BMI are **unchanged** (label: "weight neutral") [1][11] — so this is not generalized fat loss.
- **Durability:** maintained through 52 weeks without tachyphylaxis [11].
- **Reversal on stopping:** VAT re-accumulates rapidly toward baseline once tesamorelin is discontinued — efficacy requires ongoing therapy [12][14].
- **IGF-1 normalization-range targeting:** therapy raises IGF-1 from the often-low baseline of lipodystrophy toward the physiologic range; the FDA label directs clinicians to **monitor IGF-1 and consider discontinuation for persistent elevation (e.g., >3 SDS)** — i.e., dosing aims for normalization rather than supraphysiologic IGF-1 [1].
- **Dose/formulation note:** pivotal trials used the original **2 mg SC daily**; the reformulated Egrifta SV is dosed **1.4 mg SC once daily**, bioequivalent to the original 2 mg [1] (see §8 for all three formulations).

### 2.7 Coverage gaps (Section 2)

- **Single population** — every efficacy datapoint is in HIV-associated lipodystrophy; the meta-analysis does not change that.
- **VAT is an imaging surrogate** — the trials proved VAT/CT reduction and lipid improvement, not hard cardiovascular outcomes (the label states long-term CV safety is not established; see §6).
- **No head-to-head** vs other VAT-reducing strategies (diet, rhGH, GLP-1 agonists) in this population was carried in the corpus.
- **Reversal magnitude/kinetics** rest partly on a secondary summary [14] alongside the trial [12].

## 3. Beyond-Label Evidence (INVESTIGATIONAL vs ABSENT)

This section covers human evidence **outside** the single approved indication. The approved indication (§2) is the ONLY domain with a regulatory label and confirmatory pivotal RCTs. Everything below is demarcated into **INVESTIGATIONAL** (a real signal exists, but no approval and no confirmation for general use) vs **ABSENT** (no human efficacy data exists at all). Every trial's population is annotated; note that the human evidence base for tesamorelin remains almost entirely confined to people with HIV.

### 3.1 Domain 1 — NAFLD / hepatic fat in HIV — INVESTIGATIONAL

This is NOT an approved use. The signal is real but the population is HIV throughout, so it cannot be assumed to generalize to HIV-negative NAFLD/MASLD.

- **Anchor RCT.** In a 12-month, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre trial of **61 people with HIV and hepatic fat fraction ≥5%** (30 tesamorelin 2 mg/day, 30 placebo; double-blind phase plus a 6-month open-label phase), tesamorelin reduced hepatic fat fraction relative to placebo (absolute effect −4.1%, 95% CI −7.6 to −0.7, p=0.018), with **35% of the tesamorelin arm reaching hepatic fat fraction <5% vs 4% on placebo** [15]. Population: ALL participants HIV-positive; no HIV-negative arm. `rct`
- **Fibrosis.** In the same trial, paired liver biopsies showed tesamorelin **limited fibrosis progression** over 12 months vs placebo — a histological endpoint, not merely imaging [15]. This is the strongest beyond-label efficacy claim, but it is a secondary biopsy outcome in 61 HIV patients, not a powered fibrosis trial. `rct`
- **Earlier liver-fat signal.** An earlier 6-month double-blind RCT of **HIV-infected patients with abdominal fat accumulation on ART** (48 treated; 28 tesamorelin 2 mg/day, 22 placebo) found a liver-fat reduction (median −2.0% [IQR −6.4 to 0.1] vs +0.9% placebo, p=0.003) as a co-endpoint alongside visceral fat [16]. Population: HIV-positive. This is the proof-of-concept that motivated the 2019 dedicated NAFLD trial. `rct`
- **Mechanism.** A secondary mechanistic analysis using the paired biopsies from the 2019 trial (HIV-positive NAFLD) reported that tesamorelin **upregulated oxidative-phosphorylation gene sets and downregulated inflammation/tissue-repair/cell-division gene sets**, with fibrosis-gene-score improvement tracking these shifts [17]. This is hypothesis-supporting, not independent efficacy evidence. `mechanism_review`

**Bottom line:** a coherent INVESTIGATIONAL package (proof-of-concept RCT → dedicated NAFLD RCT with biopsy → mechanism), but **every datapoint is in HIV-positive patients.** No evidence in HIV-negative NAFLD/MASLD exists. (Note: a China-sited Phase 2 MASLD trial, NCT07481734, is newly registered — see §8 — but is unstarted/unpublished.)

### 3.2 Domain 2 — Cognition — INVESTIGATIONAL

This is the one beyond-label domain with **non-HIV human data**.

- **Anchor RCT.** In a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, **152 adults aged 55–87 (mean 68) — both cognitively healthy older adults and adults with amnestic mild cognitive impairment (MCI); no HIV involvement** — received tesamorelin 1 mg/day or placebo for 20 weeks (plus a 10-week washout). Tesamorelin improved **executive function (p=0.005)** with a non-significant trend for verbal memory (p=0.08); visual memory was unchanged [18]. Population: community-dwelling older adults / MCI, HIV-negative. `rct`
- **Caveats.** Effects were modest, on a 20-week horizon, with surrogate cognitive endpoints — not dementia incidence or function. No Phase 3 confirmation followed. A separate Phase 2 trial of tesamorelin for cognition in *aging HIV-infected* persons (NCT02572323) was registered, keeping the cognition program alive but unconfirmed [19]. `rct`/`regulatory`

**Bottom line:** a single positive but small/short RCT in HIV-negative older adults/MCI. Genuinely INVESTIGATIONAL — and the only beyond-label efficacy signal generated outside the HIV population. It does NOT support clinical use for cognitive enhancement or dementia prevention.

### 3.3 Domain 3 — General population / muscle / athletic / anti-aging — ABSENT

There is **NO human efficacy data** for tesamorelin in any of the following. State this as a finding, not an omission:

- **Bodybuilding / physique / athletic performance — ZERO human efficacy trials.** All "benefit" claims for these uses trace to vendor/coaching content, not to trials in healthy or athletic populations. Off-label and gray-market use is widespread, but no RCT, cohort, or open-label study has tested tesamorelin for muscle gain, strength, body recomposition, or performance in healthy adults. (No admissible primary exists to cite; the absence is itself the finding.) `regulatory`
- **Muscle wasting outside HIV — one TERMINATED trial, no positive data.** A sponsor (Theratechnologies) trial of tesamorelin + exercise for lean body mass in **COPD patients with muscle wasting (HIV-negative)**, NCT01388920, was **terminated** with no published efficacy results [20]. So even the one non-HIV muscle-wasting attempt produced no usable efficacy evidence. `regulatory`
- **Anti-aging / longevity — ZERO outcome data.** Tesamorelin reliably raises IGF-1 by roughly 50–100% (a pharmacodynamic effect, well established in the HIV trials and pharmacology reviews) [16], but **no human study links tesamorelin to any aging, mortality, frailty, or fracture outcome.** IGF-1 elevation is a surrogate, not an anti-aging endpoint. `mechanism_review`
- **Fracture / bone outcomes — ZERO trials.** No human study has tested fracture or bone-density endpoints.

**Bottom line:** bodybuilding, athletic performance, general/healthy-adult fat loss, anti-aging, and fracture prevention are all ABSENT domains — used in the wild, but with **no human efficacy evidence whatsoever.**

### 3.4 Coverage gaps (Section 3)

- **NAFLD evidence is 100% HIV-positive** — no HIV-negative NAFLD/MASLD efficacy data exist (the China MASLD trial is unstarted).
- **Cognition rests on a single small/short surrogate-endpoint RCT** — unconfirmed by Phase 3.
- **The ABSENT domains have no primary to cite** — by design; the absence is the finding, and it is the demarcation that matters most for off-label readers.

## 5. Safety & Adverse Effects

Because tesamorelin is an approved drug, its safety profile is unusually well-characterized for a peptide: it rests on a current FDA prescribing label [2] and on the two pivotal Phase 3 RCTs (~806 patients pooled) plus a 52-week safety/extension analysis [10][11][14]. This section grounds AE rates and contraindications in those sources, type-tags every claim, and separates label/RCT findings from theoretical GH-axis class risks.

**Defining safety fact:** tesamorelin's effects — including its pharmacodynamic AEs and the IGF-1 elevation that drives several of them — **reverse on discontinuation.** The VAT benefit re-accumulates and the GH/IGF-1 axis returns toward baseline when the drug is stopped, and fluid-retention-related effects (edema, arthralgia, carpal-tunnel symptoms) are described as transient or resolving on discontinuation [2][14]. `regulatory`/`rct`

**Dose/route caveat (off-label use).** The FDA-approved regimen is 1.4–2 mg SC once daily into the abdomen [2][10]; the pivotal trials studied 2 mg/day [10][12]. All AE rates below are anchored to that approved SC daily regimen **in an HIV-lipodystrophy population.** Real-world off-label use (general visceral-fat reduction, body composition, non-HIV populations) frequently occurs at non-label doses and in populations never studied; any extrapolation of these AE rates to such use is a **[route/dose-extrapolation]** not supported by the label data. `regulatory`

### 5.1 Most common adverse reactions (RCTs + label)

The most common adverse reactions reported at ≥5% and more frequently than placebo were **arthralgia, injection-site erythema, injection-site pruritus, pain in extremity, peripheral edema, and myalgia** [2]. From the integrated 26-week Phase 3 safety dataset in the label (placebo N=263; tesamorelin N=543), reactions occurring at ≥1% and more frequently than placebo [2]: `regulatory`

- **Injection-site reaction** (erythema, pruritus, rash, urticaria, pain, swelling, irritation, hemorrhage): tesamorelin **17%** vs placebo 6%. Counting all injection-site events, the label cites a **25%** incidence vs 14% on placebo over the first 26 weeks — the single most characteristic AE [2].
- **Arthralgia:** 13% vs 11% [2].
- **Pain in extremity:** 6% vs 5% [2].
- **Myalgia:** 6% vs 2% [2].
- **Peripheral edema:** 6% vs 2% [2].
- **Paresthesia:** 5% vs 2% [2].
- **Hypoesthesia:** 4% vs 2% [2].
- **Rash:** 4% vs 2%; **pruritus:** 2% vs 1% [2].
- **Dyspepsia, vomiting (3% vs 0%), nausea-spectrum GI events:** generally low single-digit incidence [2].
- **Musculoskeletal stiffness:** 2% vs 0%; **musculoskeletal pain:** 2% vs 1%; **carpal tunnel syndrome:** 1% vs 0%; **blood creatine phosphokinase increased:** 1% vs 0% [2].

Many of the musculoskeletal and edema-type AEs (arthralgia, myalgia, peripheral edema, carpal tunnel) reflect the expected pharmacology of GH-axis stimulation and fluid retention, which the label attributes to fluid retention that is transient or resolves on discontinuation [2]. In the pivotal trials themselves, **overall AE rates did not differ significantly between tesamorelin and placebo**, although more tesamorelin patients withdrew because of an AE [10]; the most frequent reasons for discontinuation across the program were headache and arthralgias [14]. `rct`/`mechanism_review`

### 5.2 IGF-1 elevation and monitoring

Tesamorelin works by stimulating endogenous GH, which raises serum IGF-1 — the on-target effect, but also the principal safety signal requiring monitoring. IGF-1 rose **81.0%** in the NEJM trial (vs −5.0% placebo) [10]; in the pooled analysis it increased by a mean of **108 ± 112 ng/mL** vs −7 ± 64 ng/mL placebo (P<0.001) [11]. `rct`

The label quantifies how many patients reach supraphysiologic IGF-1: among 26-week patients, **47% had IGF-1 >2 SDS and 36% had >3 SDS**; at 52 weeks, **34% had >2 SDS and 23% had >3 SDS** [2]. The label directs clinicians to **monitor IGF-1 during therapy and consider discontinuing tesamorelin for persistent IGF-1 elevations (e.g., >3 SDS)** [2]. The pivotal trials did not identify a discrete clinical adverse outcome correlated with the IGF-1 elevation at the studied dose, but sustained supraphysiologic IGF-1 is the basis for the malignancy-monitoring and discontinuation warnings below [2][10]. `regulatory`/`rct`

### 5.3 Glucose metabolism and diabetes risk (reported honestly, both ways)

This is the most nuanced part of the profile, because GH stimulation is intrinsically counter-regulatory to insulin, yet the trial glucose data are largely reassuring — with one important caveat.

- **NEJM pivotal trial:** "no significant differences were observed in glycemic measures" across fasting glucose, insulin, and oral glucose tolerance [10]. `rct`
- **Pooled Phase 3 analysis (n=806):** "no clinically meaningful differences were observed between groups in glucose parameters" at weeks 26 and 52 [11]. `rct`
- **Dedicated 12-week RCT in type 2 diabetics (n=53; Clemmons DR et al., PMID 28617838):** tesamorelin did not alter insulin response or glycemic control — HbA1c change at week 12 was +0.1% (2 mg) vs −0.5% (placebo), no significant fasting-glucose difference (p=0.44), and **no patient discontinued for loss of diabetes control** [21]. `rct`

**The honest counter-signal — from the label.** Despite the trial-level reassurance, the label reports that elevated HbA1c (≥6.5%) from baseline to week 26 occurred in **5% of tesamorelin patients vs 1% of placebo**, and states there was "an increased risk of developing diabetes with Egrifta (HbA1c ≥6.5%) relative to placebo … intent-to-treat hazard odds ratio of 3.3 (CI 1.4, 9.6)" [2]. So while **mean** glucose parameters did not shift meaningfully, the **proportion** of patients crossing the diabetic HbA1c threshold was higher on drug, and the label treats glucose intolerance/new-onset diabetes as a formal warning [2]. Importantly, the label characterizes glucose intolerance as transient or resolving on discontinuation [2]. `regulatory`/`rct`

### 5.4 Hypersensitivity (including the mannitol excipient) and immunogenicity

Hypersensitivity reactions occurred in **4% of tesamorelin-treated patients** in clinical trials (pruritus, erythema, flushing, urticaria, rash); patients are advised to seek prompt medical attention and discontinue if a serious reaction is suspected [2]. Tesamorelin is **contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to tesamorelin or to the excipients, which include mannitol** [2]. Anti-tesamorelin (anti-drug) antibodies developed in roughly **50% of patients at 26 weeks and 47% at 52 weeks**, with no observed impact on efficacy [2]. `regulatory`

### 5.5 Contraindications (grounded directly in the FDA label)

The Egrifta WR label lists four contraindications [2]: `regulatory`

1. **Disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis** — patients with HPA disruption due to **hypophysectomy, hypopituitarism, pituitary tumor/surgery, head irradiation, or head trauma** (tesamorelin acts on the GHRH→pituitary axis, which must be intact) [2].
2. **Active malignancy** — any preexisting malignancy must be inactive and its treatment complete before starting [2].
3. **Known hypersensitivity** to tesamorelin or the excipients (including **mannitol**) [2].
4. **Pregnancy** — modifying VAT offers no benefit in a pregnant woman and could result in fetal harm [2].

### 5.6 Warnings, precautions, monitoring & stopping criteria

Warnings/precautions [2]: increased risk of neoplasms / IGF-1 (evaluate before initiating given elevated malignancy risk in HIV-positive patients; do not treat active malignancy; **discontinue for any evidence of recurrent malignancy**); elevated IGF-1 (monitor; consider discontinuing for persistent >3 SDS); fluid retention (edema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel — generally transient or resolves on discontinuation); glucose intolerance/diabetes (evaluate before initiating, monitor periodically); hypersensitivity (discontinue immediately if serious); injection-site reactions (rotate sites); and **increased mortality in acute critical illness** (pharmacologic GH dosing increased mortality in critically ill patients — consider discontinuation in critically ill patients). `regulatory`

**Monitoring:** evaluate glucose status (fasting glucose / HbA1c) and screen for malignancy before initiation; on therapy, monitor **IGF-1** (keep within normal range), **fasting glucose / HbA1c**, and clinical signs of fluid retention; monitor diabetics for retinopathy [2]. **Stopping criteria:** persistent IGF-1 elevation (e.g., >3 SDS), new or recurrent malignancy, uncontrolled glucose intolerance/diabetes, pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity, or acute critical illness; also consider discontinuation in patients without a clear efficacy response [2]. `regulatory`

### 5.7 Demonstrated AEs vs theoretical class risks (distinction)

The **demonstrated** AEs (label + RCTs): injection-site reactions, arthralgia/myalgia/extremity pain, peripheral edema, paresthesia/hypoesthesia, hypersensitivity (4%), supraphysiologic IGF-1, and a modest excess of patients crossing the diabetic HbA1c threshold [2][10][11]. The **theoretical/class risks** of sustained GH-axis stimulation — neoplasm promotion via IGF-1, frank diabetes, fluid-overload phenomena, and the critical-illness mortality signal extrapolated from high-dose GH — drive the label's warnings and monitoring even though the pivotal trials did not demonstrate a clinical malignancy or net glycemic harm at 26–52 weeks [2][10][11]. **Long-term cardiovascular safety has not been established** [2]. Off-label use outside the studied HIV-lipodystrophy population, or at non-label doses, is a **[route/dose-extrapolation]** for all of the above. `regulatory`/`rct`/`mechanism_review`

### 5.8 Coverage gaps (Section 5)

- **AE rates are HIV-lipodystrophy-specific** — no validated AE rates for off-label/non-HIV populations or non-label doses.
- **No long-term (>1–2 yr) CV or malignancy outcome data** — the label explicitly leaves long-term CV safety unestablished.
- **The glucose signal is direction-clear but magnitude-modest** — trial means vs label HbA1c-threshold proportions must be read together, not in isolation.

## 6. Regulatory & Sport Status

### 6.1 FDA approval — the defining regulatory fact

Tesamorelin is the **one FDA-approved GHRH analogue** in this cluster. The original brand, **EGRIFTA (tesamorelin for injection)**, received FDA approval in **2010** [1][22]; the FDA's review action was dated **November 10, 2010**, and the manufacturer/marketing partner announced approval on **November 11, 2010** [1][23]. The **Initial U.S. Approval** year on the current prescribing information is **2010** [1]. `regulatory`

**Exact approved-indication wording (verified verbatim against the DailyMed label):**

> "EGRIFTA SV is indicated for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy." [1]

The original 2010 EGRIFTA approval used the same substantive indication — "the first and only treatment indicated to reduce excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy" [23]; the newest formulation, EGRIFTA WR, is described as approved for "reduction of excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV who have lipodystrophy" [24].

**Limitations of Use (verbatim) — these bound the approval and explicitly exclude general fat-loss / body-recomposition uses [1]:**

> 1. "Long-term cardiovascular safety of EGRIFTA SV has not been established."
> 2. "EGRIFTA SV is not indicated for weight loss management as it has a weight neutral effect."
> 3. "There are no data to support improved compliance with anti-retroviral therapies in HIV-positive patients taking EGRIFTA SV."

**Critical scope point.** The FDA approval is **ONLY** for reduction of excess abdominal fat in **HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy.** It is **NOT** an approval for general anti-aging, bodybuilding, athletic enhancement, or non-HIV fat loss. Any use outside the labeled HIV-lipodystrophy population is **off-label** — the existence of an approval for one narrow indication does not constitute approval for any other purpose. `regulatory`

**Manufacturer / commercial history.** Tesamorelin is developed and held by **Theratechnologies Inc.** (Montréal; U.S. License No. 2091) [1]. At the original 2010 launch the U.S. marketing partner was **EMD Serono, Inc.** (an affiliate of Merck KGaA, Darmstadt) [23]; U.S. commercialization arrangements changed over time, and Theratechnologies has since marketed the product directly in the U.S. `regulatory`

**Three FDA-approved formulations exist** [1][22][24]: `regulatory`
1. **EGRIFTA** — original, approved **2010** (two-step reconstitution) [1][23].
2. **EGRIFTA SV ("Single-Vial," F4)** — reformulation approved **2019**, simplifying reconstitution to a single vial / single daily reconstitution step [22][24].
3. **EGRIFTA WR ("Weekly Reconstitution," F8)** — approved via supplemental BLA on **March 25, 2025**; still a daily injection but requiring only **weekly** reconstitution, with less than half the administration volume of the F4 (SV) formulation [24].

### 6.2 EU / EMA — NOT approved (withdrawn)

Tesamorelin is **not approved in the European Union.** A centralised marketing authorisation application for **Egrifta (tesamorelin), 2 mg, powder for solution for injection** was submitted to the EMA on **31 May 2011** by the applicant **Ferrer Internacional, S.A.**, and was subsequently **withdrawn** while under CHMP review [25][26]. The intended EU indication was treatment of **excess visceral adipose tissue (VAT)** (>130 cm² by imaging) in treatment-experienced HIV-infected patients [25]. `regulatory`

The withdrawal followed a **negative provisional CHMP position**: the CHMP considered that the data did not allow a conclusion of a **positive benefit-risk balance**, was provisionally of the opinion that Egrifta **could not have been approved**, and was specifically concerned about how the drug would be used in practice because it would be difficult to differentiate excess abdominal fat due to lipodystrophy from excess fat due to obesity [25]. Net effect: tesamorelin has **no EU marketing authorisation** — the application was abandoned in the face of an effectively negative opinion, in contrast to its U.S. approval. `regulatory`

### 6.3 503A compounding / gray-market supply

Because tesamorelin is an **approved brand drug** (Egrifta SV / Egrifta WR), copies and unapproved sources exist and should be flagged. Three practical supply routes are described in the consumer market: (1) a genuine prescription for FDA-approved Egrifta SV/WR; (2) telehealth clinics shipping **compounded** tesamorelin from U.S. **503A/503B** pharmacies; and (3) **research-chemical vendors** selling lyophilized "tesamorelin" labeled "for laboratory and research use only, not for human use" [27][28]. `anecdote_aggregate`/`practitioner_protocol`

A regulatory complication: in **late 2023 the FDA recategorized more than a dozen peptides** (a bulk-substances determination affecting compounding eligibility), restricting which peptides 503A compounding pharmacies may make [28]. The research-chemical channel ($30–$80-range product) is **not** compounded to pharmaceutical standards, is not prescription-dispensed, and carries no clinical oversight — it is gray-market and falls outside the FDA approval entirely [27][28]. (On bulks-list specifics, the corpus did not place tesamorelin definitively among added/removed Category-2 peptides; its compoundability is constrained chiefly by its approved-drug/biologic status — see §8.) `regulatory/secondary`

### 6.4 WADA — prohibited at all times

Tesamorelin is **prohibited at all times** (both In-Competition and Out-of-Competition) under the WADA Prohibited List, **Section S2** ("Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances, and Mimetics") [29]. Within S2, the relevant entry is **S2.2.4 Growth hormone releasing factors**, and **tesamorelin is named explicitly** in the primary list text (verified verbatim against the WADA 2026 Prohibited List) [29]: `regulatory`

> "S2.2.4 Growth hormone releasing factors, including, but not limited to:
> • growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) and its analogues (e.g. CJC-1293, CJC-1295, sermorelin and tesamorelin)
> • growth hormone secretagogues (GHS) and their mimetics [e.g. anamorelin, capromorelin, ibutamoren (MK-677), ipamorelin, lenomorelin (ghrelin), macimorelin and tabimorelin];
> • GH-releasing peptides (GHRPs) [e.g. alexamorelin, examorelin (hexarelin), GHRP-1, GHRP-2 (pralmorelin), GHRP-3, ...]"

So tesamorelin is banned for athletes subject to WADA at all times, with no in-competition-only carve-out [29]. `regulatory`

### 6.5 Cost / access (qualitative)

The FDA-approved brand is an **expensive specialty Rx**: Egrifta SV (brand) is reported to run on the order of **~$3,000 per month**, typically billed through specialty pharmacies in the HIV-lipodystrophy setting [27]; other cost aggregators place a 30-day brand supply in roughly the **$2,400–$5,500/month** range before insurance, with manufacturer patient-support/copay programs cited for eligible patients [34]. By contrast, research-chemical "tesamorelin" is quoted far cheaper (single 10 mg vials ~$60–$150, dropping per-mg with multi-packs), but that price reflects an **unapproved, non-clinical** product [27]. The cost gulf is itself a driver of the gray-market channel. `anecdote_aggregate`/`anecdote`

- **Bulks-list placement is unsettled in the corpus** — tesamorelin was not definitively located among added/removed Category-2 peptides; its compounding constraint is its approved/biologic status.
- **Cost figures are price-aggregator/anecdote** — not a clinical claim and source-variable.

## 7. Why this lands at evidence_tier B / risk_tier medium

### 7.1 evidence_tier B (not S/A, not C)

Tesamorelin sits **higher** than the unapproved peptides in this library because it has what they lack: **FDA approval plus two coordinated pivotal Phase 3 RCTs** (n≈806 pooled), a 52-week safety extension showing maintained effect without tachyphylaxis, and an independent 2026 meta-analysis confirming the VAT and hepatic-fat effects [10][11][12][13]. That is a genuine, replicated, regulator-vetted efficacy base — clearly above tier C.

It does **not** reach S/A for three converging reasons:
1. **Narrow population/endpoint.** The proven efficacy is **only** for reduction of excess VAT in **HIV-associated lipodystrophy** — a single narrow population and a single imaging-defined endpoint (§2). Hard outcomes (CV events, mortality) are not established, and the label explicitly leaves long-term CV safety open (§6).
2. **Single-sponsor concentration.** 100% of the efficacy primaries used Theratechnologies study drug or sponsorship; there is **no manufacturer-independent confirmatory program** and no independent replication of the beyond-label signals (§4).
3. **Zero evidence for the off-label uses people actually want.** Anti-aging, bodybuilding, athletic performance, and healthy-adult fat loss have **no human efficacy data at all** (§3.3); NAFLD and cognition are merely INVESTIGATIONAL (§3.1–3.2).

**B = approved and genuinely evidence-backed for one narrow indication; unproven everywhere people actually want to use it.**

### 7.2 risk_tier medium (not experimental)

Tesamorelin is **not** "experimental": it is an **approved Rx with a well-characterized, monitorable, and reversible** safety profile derived from the label and RCTs (§5). Common AEs are mild and pharmacologically predictable (injection-site, arthralgia/myalgia, edema, paresthesia); the effects reverse on discontinuation. That places it **below** the experimental tier of the unapproved peptides.

It is **not** low-risk, however: it elevates IGF-1 into supraphysiologic ranges in a meaningful minority (requiring IGF-1 monitoring), carries an honestly two-sided glucose/diabetes signal (label OR 3.3 for crossing the diabetic HbA1c threshold), has four formal contraindications, and inherits the GH-axis class concerns (neoplasm promotion, critical-illness mortality) that drive its warnings. These keep it **doctor-required** with mandatory IGF-1 + fasting-glucose/HbA1c monitoring. **medium = approved, monitorable, reversible — but with real GH-axis/glucose risks that require clinician oversight.**

## 8. Prescribing-Practice + Non-English Summary

> Scope note: the FDA-label doses below are `regulatory` (authoritative). Full detail lives in the layer files; this is the synthesized summary.

### 8.1 Authoritative label doses (`regulatory`)

Tesamorelin is approved for one indication only — reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adults with lipodystrophy — across all three marketed formulations [1][3-orig]. The three FDA-labeled doses, each verified against DailyMed / accessdata:

- **Original EGRIFTA (1 mg/vial):** **2 mg SC once daily**, into the abdomen; each single-use vial contains 1 mg, so the 2 mg dose requires reconstituting **two vials** [3-orig][3-orig2]. `regulatory`
- **EGRIFTA SV (2 mg/vial):** **1.4 mg SC once daily**, delivered as **0.35 mL** of reconstituted solution into the abdomen [1]. `regulatory`
- **EGRIFTA WR (11.6 mg multi-dose vial):** **1.28 mg SC once daily**, delivered as **0.16 mL** [31]. `regulatory`

The successive reformulations (SV, then WR) lowered the milligram dose and injection volume while delivering comparable tesamorelin exposure — the "2 mg → ~1.4 mg → 1.28 mg" reduction reflects formulation/concentration changes, **not** a deliberate de-escalation of the active. Route (SC, abdomen, rotate sites) and once-daily frequency are identical across all three [1][31]. `regulatory`

### 8.2 Reconstitution (math only)

- **EGRIFTA SV (label):** reconstitute one 2 mg lyophilized vial with **0.5 mL Sterile Water for Injection** → 2 mg/0.5 mL (4 mg/mL); roll gently 30 s, do not shake; draw **0.35 mL = 1.4 mg**; use immediately, discard remainder [1]. `regulatory`
- **EGRIFTA WR (label):** reconstitute the 11.6 mg vial with **1.3 mL Bacteriostatic Water for Injection** → seven daily doses of **1.28 mg / 0.16 mL**; one vial mixed per week; store at room temperature, do not freeze/refrigerate after mixing [31]. `regulatory`
- **Original EGRIFTA (label):** each 1 mg vial reconstituted with **2.2 mL** diluent; two vials per 2 mg dose [3-orig][3-orig2]. `regulatory`
- **Off-label/vendor reconstitution math (no efficacy):** vendor/clinic guides commonly describe a **10 mg "research" vial reconstituted with ~2.5 mL bacteriostatic water**, where "25 units" on an insulin syringe ≈ **1 mg** — gray-market arithmetic only, flagged as such [32]. `vendor_label`

### 8.3 Off-label practitioner conventions (`practitioner_protocol` — UNATTRIBUTED)

Tesamorelin is widely discussed in the anti-aging / body-composition prescriber space for visceral-fat reduction in non-HIV adults. Recurring conventions: starting at **1 mg SC daily** and titrating toward the labeled **2 mg daily**; dosing at night / on an empty stomach; intermittent schedules such as **5 days on / 2 days off**, run in **~3-month cycles** with rest intervals, alongside baseline + follow-up IGF-1, fasting glucose/HbA1c, and lipid monitoring [27a][27][27b]. **Honest attribution gap:** the aplus-research standard requires every `practitioner_protocol` claim to carry a **named prescriber + venue + date**, and none of the off-label sources meet that bar — they are anonymous med-spa / clinic blog pages and peptide-vendor "dosing guides" with no identified author, institution, or date [27a][27][27b]. ### 8.4 Compounding gate result

No live tesamorelin product page on a major 503A/503B compounder's own catalog was located (Empower's published catalog surfaced sermorelin acetate and other GH-axis peptides, but no tesamorelin page was retrievable) [35].
