# Tesamorelin (GHRH(1-44) analog; Egrifta / Egrifta SV / Egrifta WR)

## Metadata
- **Class:** peptide — stabilized synthetic analog of human growth-hormone-releasing hormone, GHRH(1-44)
- **Evidence tier:** **A** for its STUDIED indication (HIV-associated lipodystrophy — multiple Phase-3 RCTs + a 2026 meta-analysis). **Effectively D for the user's intended use** (general / post-illness / non-HIV body composition) — see §4, the population-transfer caveat.

## TL;DR

Tesamorelin is a DPP-4-resistant analog of human GHRH that stimulates the pituitary to release **pulsatile** endogenous growth hormone, raising IGF-1 and selectively reducing **visceral** abdominal fat. It is **FDA-approved (2010)** — but for exactly one population: **HIV-associated lipodystrophy**. The evidence there is genuinely strong (two pivotal Phase-3 RCTs, a single-center mechanistic RCT, a NAFLD RCT, and a 2026 meta-analysis of 5 RCTs). The decisive caveat for this wiki's operator: **there is NO randomized evidence for tesamorelin in healthy, general, or post-illness HIV-negative adults for body composition or recovery** — the one non-HIV RCT (Baker 2012) measured cognition, not body comp. Using it for the recovery/body-comp goal is a direct population transfer onto an unstudied population. Safety is well-characterized (it is a marketed drug): the watch-items are IGF-1 elevation (~36% exceed +3 SD at 26 wk), a real glycemic signal (new HbA1c ≥6.5% in 5% vs 1% placebo), and an active-malignancy contraindication. The benefit reverses when you stop. WADA-banned (S2.2.4). US compounding is restricted (503A Category 2).

## 1. Identity & Molecular Mechanism

### 1.1 What tesamorelin actually is
A synthetic peptide comprising the full 44-amino-acid sequence of human GHRH (a.k.a. GRF, growth-hormone-releasing factor) with a **trans-3-hexenoyl group attached to the N-terminal tyrosine** `[regulatory: FDA Egrifta SV label, NDA 022505]`. Free-base MW ≈ 5135.9 Da.

### 1.2 Why the modification — DPP-4 resistance
Native GHRH is cleaved and inactivated in plasma within minutes by dipeptidyl-peptidase-4 (DPP-4). The N-terminal hexenoyl modification confers resistance to that cleavage, markedly extending the effective plasma exposure and raising GH/IGF-1 after once-daily dosing `[mechanism_review: Bedimo 2011, PMID 22096409]`.

### 1.3 Mechanism of action — pulsatile GH, preserved feedback
Tesamorelin binds the pituitary GHRH receptor with potency similar to endogenous GRF `[regulatory: FDA label §12.1]` and stimulates synthesis and **pulsatile** release of endogenous GH, which raises hepatic IGF-1 `[regulatory: FDA label §12.1]`. In healthy men (2 mg SC daily × 14 d, n=13), it augmented basal AND pulsatile GH and raised IGF-1 by +181 ± 22 µg/L while preserving insulin-stimulated glucose uptake `[open_label: Stanley 2011, PMID 20943777]`. This is the key contrast with exogenous rhGH, which produces sustained (non-pulsatile) levels and escapes normal negative feedback — the basis for tesamorelin's claimed better tolerability `[mechanism_review: Bedimo 2011]`.

### 1.4 Why visceral fat specifically
GH is lipolytic, and pulsatile GH mobilizes fat more than continuous delivery `[mechanism_review: Bedimo 2011]`. In vivo this is VAT-selective: the pivotal RCT reduced **visceral** adipose tissue with no significant change in subcutaneous fat `[rct: Falutz 2007, PMID 18057338]`.

## 2. Human Clinical Evidence (by population)

### 2.1 HIV-associated lipodystrophy — the approved indication, the bulk of the evidence
- **Falutz 2007 (NEJM, PMID 18057338), n=412, 26 wk, 2 mg/d SC:** VAT **−15.2%** vs **+5.0%** placebo; IGF-1 +81.0% vs −5.0% (P<0.001); triglycerides −50 vs +9 mg/dL (P<0.001). `[rct]`
- **Falutz 2010 (JAIDS, PMID 20101189):** VAT −10.9% (−21 cm²) vs −0.6% placebo at 6 mo; ~18% reduction maintained at 12 mo. `[rct]`
- **Stanley 2014 (JAMA, PMID 25038357), n=50:** VAT treatment effect **−42 cm²** (95% CI −71 to −14; P=.005); liver fat −2.9% net (P=.003). `[rct]`
- **Loss-of-effect on discontinuation:** in the pivotal program, VAT reduction was rapidly lost when patients switched from tesamorelin to placebo — continuous therapy is required `[rct: Falutz 2010; pooled review Stanley & Grinspoon 2012, PMID 22495074]`.

### 2.2 NAFLD / hepatic fat (in HIV)
- **Stanley 2019 (Lancet HIV, PMID 31611038), n=61, 12 mo:** hepatic fat fraction −4.1% absolute / **−37% relative** (P=0.016); 35% vs 4% reached HFF <5% (P=0.0069). `[rct]`

### 2.3 The ONE non-HIV RCT — and it isn't body composition
- **Baker 2012 (Arch Neurol, PMID 22869065), n=152, age 55–87 (healthy + MCI), 1 mg/d × 20 wk:** favorable effect on executive-function composite (P=.005); IGF-1 +117% (within physiologic range). This is the rare non-HIV RCT — but its endpoint is **cognition, not body composition**. `[rct]`

### 2.4 Meta-analysis (2026)
- **Badran 2026 (Obes Res Clin Pract, PMID 41545261):** pooled across **5 RCTs (all HIV)**: VAT MD **−27.71 cm²** (95% CI −38.37 to −17.06; P<0.001); hepatic fat −4.28% (P<0.001); improved lean mass + IGF-1. `[meta_analysis]` (Pooled total n not stated in the abstract — flagged.)

## 3. Regulatory Status

- **FDA:** approved **2010-11-10**, NDA 022505 (Theratechnologies), for **reduction of excess visceral abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy** — and that indication ONLY `[regulatory: FDA approval / Egrifta SV label]`. Reformulations: **Egrifta SV** (2019), **Egrifta WR** (Complete Response Letter Jan 2024 → approved Mar 2025), all same indication.
- **EMA (Europe):** **never approved** — the marketing application (Ferrer Internacional) was **withdrawn June 2012** (CHMP notification 2012-06-21) during review, with the agency signaling the long-term benefit-risk data were insufficient `[regulatory: EMA withdrawal notice]`.
- **Health Canada:** approved/marketed for the same HIV-lipodystrophy indication `[regulatory: CADTH clinical review, NBK539137]`.
- **WADA / sport:** **prohibited at all times** under **S2.2.4 (Growth Hormone-Releasing Factors)** — tesamorelin is named by token under this class (alongside sermorelin, CJC-1295) `[regulatory: WADA Prohibited List + GHRF guidance]`.
- **US compounding (restricted):** tesamorelin is a **deemed biologic** — under the BPCI Act it transitioned to regulation as a licensed biologic (deemed-BLA effective **2020-03-23**), which bars it from bulk 503A/503B compounding; it had earlier appeared in FDA's interim 503A bulks review as **Category 2** (significant-safety-concern), a framework FDA restructured in early 2025. Either way US compounded supply is restricted, not freely available `[regulatory: FDA biologics-transition + 503A framework]`.

## 4. The Population-Transfer Problem (FIRST-CLASS CAVEAT)

This is the tesamorelin analog of BPC-157's single-lab concentration: the evidence is real and strong, but it is **all in one population that is not the operator**.

### 4.1 The headline
**Every** body-composition / visceral-fat / hepatic-fat RCT (Falutz 2007, Falutz 2010, Stanley 2014, Stanley 2019, and all 5 trials in the Badran 2026 meta-analysis) enrolled **antiretroviral-treated HIV patients with lipodystrophy / abnormal fat accumulation**. The only non-HIV RCT (Baker 2012) tested **cognition**, not body composition.

### 4.2 What this means
- **NO ADMISSIBLE PRIMARY FOUND** for tesamorelin improving body composition, visceral fat, recovery, or deconditioning in **healthy, general, or post-illness HIV-negative adults** (verified absence, not an oversight — the retrieval pass specifically hunted for it and confirmed the aggregator-claimed "Stanley non-HIV obesity RCT" does not exist).
- HIV-associated lipodystrophy is a *specific* pathophysiology (antiretroviral-driven fat redistribution, GH-axis blunting). Efficacy there does not establish efficacy in a metabolically different population.
- Therefore any non-HIV use is a **direct population transfer** from an HIV-lipodystrophy evidence base onto an unstudied population — biologically plausible (the GH→VAT mechanism is general), but **unproven**.

### 4.3 Why this lands at evidence_tier A (for the indication) but D (for the operator's goal)
For the studied HIV-lipodystrophy indication, the evidence is RCT- and meta-analysis-grade → **A**. For the operator's actual goal (post-illness / general body composition & recovery), there is zero direct randomized evidence → that *use* is effectively **D**. The compound entry carries both, with the goal-relevant grade flagged prominently.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics & Dosing

- **Approved dose/route:** original Egrifta **2 mg SC once daily** (abdomen); Egrifta SV **1.4 mg**; Egrifta WR **1.28 mg** — all SC once daily, **exposure-matched** (the lower mg of WR delivers Cmax/AUC ≈ original 2 mg), NOT interchangeable `[regulatory: FDA label]`.
- **Bioavailability (SC):** **<4%** `[regulatory: FDA label §12.3]`.
- **Half-life:** **26 min (healthy) / 38 min (HIV)** multi-dose, original formulation; **11 min** single-dose for Egrifta WR — do not state a single universal t½; all are well under 1 h `[regulatory: FDA label §12.3]`.
- **Cmax ≈ 2875 pg/mL; Tmax (median) 0.15 h; AUC ~34% higher in HIV** `[regulatory: FDA label §12.3]`.
- **Population PK:** clearance ~1060 L/h, Vd ~200 L (n=38) `[open_label/pop-PK: González-Sales 2015, PMID 25358450]`.
- **Reconstitution (Egrifta SV):** lyophilized powder + 0.5 mL Sterile Water → 2 mg/0.5 mL, roll 30 s (do not shake), inject 0.35 mL immediately, discard remainder; room-temp storage, protect from light `[regulatory: Egrifta SV label]`. (Egrifta WR uses a different diluent — bacteriostatic water.)
- **Duration:** trials dosed 26–52 wk; the VAT effect **reverses on discontinuation** (re-accumulation; specific regain % is in an extension publication not independently re-verified here).

## 6. Safety, Adverse Events, Contraindications, Monitoring

All grounded in the FDA Egrifta SV label of record (DailyMed setid 3d783378…), the authoritative label.

### 6.1 Contraindications
- Disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis (hypophysectomy, hypopituitarism, pituitary tumor/surgery, head irradiation/trauma)
- **Active malignancy** (GH/IGF-1 stimulation concern); weigh pre-existing benign neoplasms
- **Pregnancy** (discontinue if pregnancy occurs)
- Known hypersensitivity

### 6.2 Common adverse reactions (26-wk pooled, tesamorelin N=543 vs placebo N=263)
- Injection-site reactions 25% vs 14%; arthralgia 13% vs 11%; myalgia 6% vs 2%; peripheral edema 6% vs 2%; paresthesia 5% vs 2%; hypersensitivity reactions ~4%. Fluid-retention/GH-class effects (edema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel).

### 6.3 IGF-1 elevation (the headline lab signal)
At 26 wk, **47% exceeded +2 SDS and 36% exceeded +3 SDS**; at 52 wk, 34% / 23%. Label rule: **monitor IGF-1; consider discontinuing on persistent elevation (e.g. >+3 SDS)** (no fixed interval specified).

### 6.4 Glycemic signal
New **HbA1c ≥6.5% in 5% vs 1%** placebo by week 26 (mean baseline 5.3% both arms); hazard ratio for developing diabetes ~3.3 (95% CI 1.4–9.6). Evaluate glucose before starting and monitor periodically.

### 6.5 Monitoring plan (synthesis)
Baseline + interval **IGF-1** and **fasting glucose / HbA1c**; discontinue on persistent IGF-1 >+3 SDS or emergent dysglycemia; discontinue in acute critical illness; do not use with active malignancy or pregnancy.

## 7. Sourcing & Cost (honest state)

- Legitimate access is **on-label Rx for HIV-lipodystrophy only** (specialty tier, prior auth requiring imaging-confirmed excess VAT) `[payer policy]`. Off-label (body-comp/anti-aging) use is **not insurance-covered**.
- Branded cost is high (~$1,964/30 d at 2010 launch; current branded ≈ $2,400–$3,100+/mo per pharmacy aggregators — approximate, not regulatory).
- US **compounding is restricted** (503A Category 2). Gray-market/"research" vendor tesamorelin exists (~$800–$1,200/mo) but is `vendor_label`/`anecdote` — not quality-assured, not legal-status evidence, and may never ground efficacy.

## 9. Bottom line for the operator

Tesamorelin is one of the **most evidence-backed peptides in this library** — but the evidence is for a population the operator is not in. If the goal is visceral-fat / metabolic and the operator is (or becomes) clinically appropriate, it is a *real drug with a real Rx pathway and cheap monitoring*, and the July-2026 primary-care visit is the right venue to raise it. But for general "recovery / body composition" in a healthy HIV-negative adult, the honest status is **mechanistically plausible, regulatorily real, clinically unproven in this population**, off-label, costly, WADA-banned, and requiring IGF-1 + glucose monitoring. It belongs on the doctor-handout queue, not in a self-sourced gray-market protocol.
