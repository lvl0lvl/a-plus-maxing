## Section D — Safety & adverse effects

Tesamorelin (Egrifta / Egrifta SV / Egrifta WR) is an FDA-approved synthetic analogue of human growth-hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), indicated for the reduction of excess abdominal visceral adipose tissue (VAT) in HIV-infected adults with lipodystrophy [1]. Because it is an approved drug, its safety profile is unusually well-characterized for a peptide: it rests on a current FDA prescribing label [1] and on two pivotal Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials totaling ~806–816 patients, plus a pooled safety/extension analysis to 52 weeks [2][3][4]. This section grounds adverse-event (AE) rates and contraindications in those sources, type-tags every claim, and separates label/RCT findings from theoretical GH-axis class risks.

**Defining safety fact:** tesamorelin's effects — including its pharmacodynamic AEs and the IGF-1 elevation that drives several of them — **reverse on discontinuation.** The VAT benefit re-accumulates and the GH/IGF-1 axis returns toward baseline when the drug is stopped, and fluid-retention-related effects (edema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel symptoms) are described as transient or resolving on discontinuation [1][4]. `regulatory`/`rct`

**Dose/route caveat (off-label use):** the FDA-approved regimen is 1.4–2 mg subcutaneously once daily into the abdomen [1][2]. The pivotal trials studied 2 mg/day [2][3]. All AE rates below are anchored to that approved subcutaneous daily regimen in an HIV-lipodystrophy population. Real-world off-label use (e.g., for general visceral-fat reduction, body composition, or in non-HIV populations) frequently occurs at non-label doses and in populations never studied; any extrapolation of these AE rates to such use is a **[route/dose-extrapolation]** and is not supported by the label data. `regulatory`

### Most common adverse reactions (RCTs + label)

The most common adverse reactions reported at ≥5% and more frequently than placebo were **arthralgia, injection-site erythema, injection-site pruritus, pain in extremity, peripheral edema, and myalgia** [1]. `regulatory`

From the integrated 26-week Phase 3 safety dataset in the FDA label (placebo N=263; tesamorelin N=543), reactions occurring at ≥1% and more frequently than placebo [1]: `regulatory`

- **Injection-site reaction** (erythema, pruritus, rash, urticaria, pain, swelling, irritation, hemorrhage): tesamorelin **17%** vs placebo 6% [1]. Injection-site reactions are the single most characteristic AE; counting all injection-site events, the label cites a **25%** incidence in tesamorelin-treated patients vs 14% on placebo over the first 26 weeks [1]. `regulatory`
- **Arthralgia:** 13% vs 11% [1]. `regulatory`
- **Pain in extremity:** 6% vs 5% [1]. `regulatory`
- **Myalgia:** 6% vs 2% [1]. `regulatory`
- **Peripheral edema:** 6% vs 2% [1]. `regulatory`
- **Paresthesia:** 5% vs 2% [1]. `regulatory`
- **Hypoesthesia:** 4% vs 2% [1]. `regulatory`
- **Rash:** 4% vs 2%; **pruritus:** 2% vs 1% [1]. `regulatory`
- **Dyspepsia, vomiting (3% vs 0%), nausea-spectrum GI events:** generally low single-digit incidence [1]. `regulatory`
- **Musculoskeletal stiffness:** 2% vs 0%; **musculoskeletal pain:** 2% vs 1%; **carpal tunnel syndrome:** 1% vs 0%; **blood creatine phosphokinase increased:** 1% vs 0% [1]. `regulatory`

Many of the musculoskeletal and edema-type AEs (arthralgia, myalgia, peripheral edema, carpal tunnel syndrome) reflect the expected pharmacology of GH-axis stimulation and fluid retention, and the label explicitly attributes them to fluid retention that is transient or resolves on discontinuation [1]. `regulatory`

In the pivotal trials themselves, **overall AE rates did not differ significantly between tesamorelin and placebo**, although more tesamorelin patients withdrew because of an adverse event [2]. The most frequent reasons for discontinuation across the program were headache and arthralgias [5]. `rct`/`mechanism_review`

### IGF-1 elevation and monitoring

Tesamorelin works by stimulating endogenous GH secretion, which raises serum IGF-1 — this is the on-target effect, but it is also the principal safety signal requiring monitoring. In the pivotal NEJM trial, mean IGF-1 rose **81.0%** in the tesamorelin group versus a 5.0% decrease on placebo [2]; in the pooled Phase 3 analysis, IGF-1 increased by a mean of **108 ± 112 ng/mL** versus −7 ± 64 ng/mL on placebo (P<0.001) [3]. `rct`

The label quantifies how many patients reach supraphysiologic IGF-1: among 26-week patients, **47% had IGF-1 >2 standard-deviation score (SDS) and 36% had >3 SDS**; at 52 weeks, **34% had >2 SDS and 23% had >3 SDS** [1]. The label directs clinicians to **monitor IGF-1 during therapy and consider discontinuing tesamorelin in patients with persistent IGF-1 elevations (e.g., >3 SDS)** [1]. The pivotal trials did not identify a discrete clinical adverse outcome correlated with the IGF-1 elevation at the studied dose, but sustained supraphysiologic IGF-1 is the basis for the malignancy-monitoring and discontinuation warnings below [1][2]. `regulatory`/`rct`

### Glucose metabolism and diabetes risk (reported honestly)

This is the most nuanced part of tesamorelin's safety profile, because GH stimulation is intrinsically counter-regulatory to insulin, yet the trial glucose data are largely reassuring with one important caveat.

- In the **NEJM pivotal trial**, "no significant differences were observed in glycemic measures" between groups across fasting glucose, insulin, and oral glucose tolerance [2]. `rct`
- The **pooled Phase 3 analysis (n=806)** likewise concluded "no clinically meaningful differences were observed between groups in glucose parameters" at weeks 26 and 52 [3]. `rct`
- A dedicated **12-week RCT in type 2 diabetics (n=53; Clemmons DR et al., PMID 28617838)** found tesamorelin did not alter insulin response or glycemic control: HbA1c change at week 12 was +0.1% (2 mg) vs −0.5% (placebo), with no significant difference in fasting glucose (p=0.44), and **no patient discontinued for loss of diabetes control** [6]. `rct`

**The honest counter-signal — from the label:** despite the trial-level reassurance, the FDA label reports that elevated HbA1c (≥6.5%) from baseline to week 26 occurred in **5% of tesamorelin patients vs 1% of placebo**, and states there was "an increased risk of developing diabetes with Egrifta (HbA1c ≥6.5%) relative to placebo … intent-to-treat hazard odds ratio of 3.3 (CI 1.4, 9.6)" [1]. So while mean glucose parameters did not shift meaningfully, the proportion of patients crossing the diabetic HbA1c threshold was higher on drug, and the label treats glucose intolerance/new-onset diabetes as a formal warning [1]. Importantly, the label characterizes glucose intolerance as transient or resolving on discontinuation [1]. `regulatory` `rct`

### Hypersensitivity reactions (including to the mannitol excipient)

Hypersensitivity reactions occurred in **4% of tesamorelin-treated patients** in clinical trials, including pruritus, erythema, flushing, urticaria, and rash; patients are advised to seek prompt medical attention and discontinue if a serious hypersensitivity reaction is suspected [1]. Tesamorelin is **contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to tesamorelin or to the excipients**, which include **mannitol** [1]. `regulatory`

### Immunogenicity

Anti-tesamorelin (anti-drug) antibodies developed in roughly **50% of patients at 26 weeks and 47% at 52 weeks**; the label reports no observed impact of these antibodies on efficacy [1]. `regulatory`

### Contraindications (grounded directly in the FDA label)

The Egrifta WR label lists four contraindications [1]: `regulatory`

1. **Disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis** — patients with HPA disruption due to **hypophysectomy, hypopituitarism, pituitary tumor/surgery, head irradiation, or head trauma** (tesamorelin acts on the GHRH→pituitary axis, which must be intact) [1].
2. **Active malignancy** — contraindicated; any preexisting malignancy must be inactive and its treatment complete before starting therapy [1].
3. **Known hypersensitivity** to tesamorelin or the excipients (including **mannitol**) [1].
4. **Pregnancy** — modifying visceral adipose tissue offers no benefit in a pregnant woman and could result in fetal harm [1].

### Warnings and precautions (label)

- **Increased risk of neoplasms / IGF-1:** evaluate carefully before initiating given elevated malignancy risk in HIV-positive patients; do not treat active malignancy; **discontinue if there is any evidence of recurrent malignancy** [1].
- **Elevated IGF-1:** monitor during therapy; consider discontinuing for persistent elevations (>3 SDS) [1].
- **Fluid retention:** may manifest as edema, arthralgia, and carpal tunnel syndrome; generally transient or resolves on discontinuation [1].
- **Glucose intolerance / diabetes:** evaluate glucose status before initiating and monitor periodically [1].
- **Hypersensitivity reactions:** discontinue immediately if a serious reaction is suspected [1].
- **Injection-site reactions:** rotate injection sites to mitigate [1].
- **Increased mortality in acute critical illness:** pharmacologic GH dosing increased mortality in critically ill patients; consider discontinuation in critically ill patients [1].

### Monitoring and stopping criteria

- **Baseline & ongoing:** evaluate glucose status (fasting glucose / HbA1c) before initiation and periodically; evaluate for malignancy before starting [1].
- **On-therapy monitoring:** IGF-1 levels (keep within the normal range), fasting glucose / HbA1c, and clinical signs of fluid retention; monitor diabetics for retinopathy [1].
- **Stopping criteria:** persistent IGF-1 elevation (e.g., >3 SDS), evidence of new or recurrent malignancy, uncontrolled glucose intolerance/diabetes, pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity reaction, or acute critical illness; also consider discontinuation in patients without a clear efficacy response [1].

### Theoretical class risks vs. demonstrated AEs (distinction)

The **demonstrated** AEs (from label + RCTs) are: injection-site reactions, arthralgia/myalgia/extremity pain, peripheral edema, paresthesia/hypoesthesia, hypersensitivity (4%), supraphysiologic IGF-1, and a modest excess of patients crossing the diabetic HbA1c threshold [1][2][3]. The **theoretical/class risks** of sustained GH-axis stimulation — neoplasm promotion via IGF-1, frank diabetes, fluid-overload phenomena, and the critical-illness mortality signal extrapolated from high-dose GH — drive the label's warnings and monitoring even though the pivotal trials did not demonstrate a clinical malignancy or net glycemic harm at 26–52 weeks [1][2][3]. Long-term cardiovascular safety has not been established [1]. Off-label use outside the studied HIV-lipodystrophy population, or at non-label doses, is a **[route/dose-extrapolation]** for all of the above. `regulatory`/`rct`/`mechanism_review`
