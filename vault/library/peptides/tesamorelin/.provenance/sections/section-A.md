## Section A — Identity, structure, mechanism

### Identity

Tesamorelin (developmental code **TH9507**; brand name **Egrifta**, later reformulated as **Egrifta SV** / **Egrifta WR**) is a synthetic analogue of human growth-hormone-releasing factor (GRF, also called growth-hormone-releasing hormone, GHRH). It was developed by **Theratechnologies Inc.** (Montreal) and is the **only FDA-approved GHRH analogue** in the growth-hormone-secretagogue cluster; it received U.S. approval in **November 2010** for the reduction of excess abdominal (visceral) fat in HIV-infected patients with antiretroviral-therapy–associated lipodystrophy `[regulatory]` [1][2].

The defining structural fact is that tesamorelin retains the **full 44-amino-acid sequence of human GRF (hGRF(1-44)NH2)** and adds a single N-terminal modification. The manufacturer's label describes it precisely: "It is comprised of the 44 amino acid sequence of human GRF and a hexenoyl moiety, a C6 chain with a double bond at position 3, attached to the tyrosine residue at the N-terminal part of the molecule" `[regulatory]` [1]. This **trans-3-hexenoyl** group is anchored on the amino-terminal **Tyr1** residue — hence the chemical synonym **N-hexenoyl-Tyr1-hGRF(1-44)** `[mechanism_review]` [3]. Reported molecular characteristics from the label are molecular formula **C₂₂₁H₃₆₆N₇₂O₆₇S** (as the free base, distributed as an acetate salt) and a molecular weight of approximately **5136 Da** `[regulatory]` [1].

**Critical distinction (get the structure right):** tesamorelin is built on the **complete GRF(1-44)** backbone, NOT on the truncated **GRF(1-29)** core that sermorelin and CJC-1295 use (see "Contrast" below). The N-terminal cap on tesamorelin is the trans-3-hexenoyl lipid group, not the internal amino-acid substitutions or albumin-binding tether used by other analogues.

### Mechanism of action

Tesamorelin reproduces the physiology of endogenous GHRH. It binds and activates the **GHRH receptor (GHRH-R / GRF receptor)**, a **class B (secretin-family) G-protein-coupled receptor** expressed on the **somatotroph cells of the anterior pituitary**. The label states that "in vitro, tesamorelin binds and stimulates human GRF receptors with similar potency as the endogenous GRF," acting on pituitary somatotrophs to "stimulate the synthesis and pulsatile release of endogenous growth hormone (GH)," which in turn "increases IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 levels" `[regulatory]` [1].

The canonical downstream signalling for this receptor is **Gsα → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → protein kinase A (PKA)**, with PKA-driven transcriptional activation of the GH gene and stimulated exocytosis of GH from somatotroph granules `[mechanism_review]` [2][4]. The therapeutically important consequence is that tesamorelin **augments the body's own pulsatile GH axis** rather than replacing it with a flat, exogenous GH signal. Bedimo's review emphasizes that, unlike recombinant human GH administration, tesamorelin acts "through the hypothalamic-pituitary axis," generating "a more physiologic pattern of GH secretion" and thereby avoiding the sustained supraphysiologic GH exposure characteristic of rhGH therapy `[mechanism_review]` [2].

Two mechanistic features follow directly from working upstream of the pituitary:

1. **Preserved feedback / pulsatility.** Because GH is released episodically and the negative-feedback loop (GH and IGF-1 feeding back on the hypothalamus and pituitary) remains intact, GH secretion is self-limiting. Population PK/PD modelling found that tesamorelin stimulates GH "in an 'episodic' manner, i.e., for a finite duration of time," consistent with augmentation of native pulses rather than continuous elevation `[cohort]` [5].
2. **Reversibility on discontinuation.** Effects depend on continued dosing. In the phase-3 program, the improvement in visceral adipose tissue "was not sustained among patients who were re-randomized to placebo after 26 weeks of therapy"; VAT reaccumulated toward baseline, so "chronic therapy therefore appears to be necessary to maintain the reductions" `[rct]` [2]. This reversibility is a direct corollary of the physiologic, secretagogue mechanism — there is no persistent depot or irreversible pathway change.

GH released into the circulation acts on peripheral tissues including hepatocytes, where it stimulates production of **insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1)**; IGF-1 mediates much of the anabolic signalling, while GH itself drives lipolysis (the basis for the visceral-fat indication) `[mechanism_review]` [2].

### Pharmacokinetics

Tesamorelin is administered by **subcutaneous (SC) injection** (the approved Egrifta/Egrifta SV regimen is a once-daily SC dose; the original Egrifta dose was 2 mg, with the Egrifta SV reformulation delivering a bioequivalent 1.4 mg) `[regulatory]` [1].

The pharmacokinetics are dominated by **very rapid absorption and very rapid elimination**, exactly as expected for a peptide secretagogue:

- **Tmax:** median peak plasma concentration is reached at **0.15 h (~9 minutes)** in both healthy subjects and HIV-infected patients `[regulatory]` [1].
- **Elimination half-life:** the label reports a mean t½ of **8 minutes in healthy subjects** after a single SC dose `[regulatory]` [1]. In HIV-infected patients the mean elimination half-life is somewhat longer — on the order of **~18 minutes (single dose)** rising to **~37 minutes (multiple doses)** — which brackets the commonly cited "~26–38 min" range and explains the variability in secondary sources `[regulatory]` [1].
- **Disposition:** population PK analysis described tesamorelin with a one-compartment model with combined first- and zero-order absorption and linear elimination, estimating a plasma clearance of ~**1060 L/h** and volume of distribution of ~**200 L** `[cohort]` [5].
- **Bioavailability:** SC bioavailability is low (the absolute fraction absorbed is small), but high intrinsic potency means a low-milligram dose achieves the target pharmacodynamic GH/IGF-1 response `[regulatory]` [1].

**Stabilization rationale vs native GHRH.** Native GRF/GHRH is degraded within minutes in plasma, principally by **dipeptidyl-peptidase IV (DPP-IV / DPP4)**, which cleaves the N-terminal dipeptide and inactivates the hormone. The whole point of the trans-3-hexenoyl cap on Tyr1 is to block this cleavage. In the primary non-clinical pharmacology study, TH9507 "was found to be resistant to dipeptidyl aminopeptidase-IV deactivation"; the modification "slowed the in vitro degradation of the peptide in rat, dog and human plasma" relative to unmodified hGRF(1-44)NH2 and prolonged in vivo elimination kinetics (apparent t½ ~21–45 min in dogs), with plasma GH and IGF-1 markedly increased in pigs, rats and dogs after repeat SC/IV dosing `[animal]` (rat, dog, pig) [3]. Thus tesamorelin's measured human half-life of minutes — still short, but meaningfully longer than the seconds-to-minutes survival of native GHRH — is sufficient to trigger a physiologic GH pulse before the peptide clears.

### Contrast with related compounds

Within the growth-hormone-secretagogue space there are **two mechanistically distinct receptor targets**, and three structural sub-types of GHRH analogue. Enumerated below are the **three key contrasts**:

1. **Tesamorelin vs sermorelin (both GHRH-R agonists, different backbone).** Sermorelin is **GRF(1-29)** — the truncated 29-residue minimal active fragment of GHRH with no stabilizing cap, giving it the shortest half-life and the closest match to native GHRH PK. Tesamorelin is the **full GRF(1-44) plus the trans-3-hexenoyl cap**, conferring DPP-IV resistance and a longer (though still short) half-life `[regulatory]` [1] `[mechanism_review]` [2].

2. **Tesamorelin vs CJC-1295 (both GHRH-R agonists, different stabilization strategy).** CJC-1295 is a **modified GRF(1-29)** analogue. In the peer-reviewed human study, CJC-1295 is characterized as "a long-acting analog of GH-releasing hormone" with an estimated **half-life of 5.8–8.1 days** and GH/IGF-1 elevation persisting for days to weeks — a profile achieved via an albumin-binding drug-affinity-complex (DAC) tether on a GRF(1-29) core `[rct]` [6]. This is the opposite design philosophy from tesamorelin: CJC-1295 sacrifices pulsatility for a long, sustained signal, whereas tesamorelin's minutes-long half-life **preserves the physiologic pulse**.

3. **GHRH analogues vs ghrelin-receptor secretagogues (different receptor entirely).** Ipamorelin and related "-relin" GH-releasing peptides act NOT on the GHRH receptor but on the **growth-hormone-secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHS-R1a), i.e., the ghrelin receptor** `[mechanism_review]` [2][4]. They therefore work through a parallel pathway and are frequently combined with GHRH analogues for synergistic GH release. Tesamorelin has no meaningful activity at the ghrelin receptor; its entire mechanism is GHRH-R mediated.

In summary: tesamorelin is uniquely the **FDA-approved, full-length GRF(1-44), trans-3-hexenoyl-stabilized GHRH-receptor agonist** that augments the endogenous, reversible, pulsatile GH→IGF-1 axis.
