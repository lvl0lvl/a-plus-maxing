## Section E — Regulatory & sport status

### FDA approval — the defining fact

Tesamorelin is the **one FDA-approved GHRH analogue** in this cluster. The original brand, **EGRIFTA (tesamorelin for injection)**, received FDA approval in **2010** [regulatory] [1][2]. The FDA's review action was dated **November 10, 2010**, and the manufacturer/marketing partner announced the approval on **November 11, 2010** [regulatory] [1][3]. The **Initial U.S. Approval** year stated on the current prescribing information is **2010** [regulatory] [1].

**EXACT approved-indication wording (verified verbatim against the DailyMed label):**

> "EGRIFTA SV is indicated for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy." [regulatory] [1]

The original 2010 EGRIFTA approval used the same substantive indication — "the first and only treatment indicated to reduce excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy" [regulatory] [3]. The newest formulation, EGRIFTA WR, is described as approved for "reduction of excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV who have lipodystrophy" [regulatory] [4].

**Limitations of Use (verbatim from the label) — these bound the approval and explicitly exclude general fat-loss / body-recomposition uses:** [regulatory] [1]

> 1. "Long-term cardiovascular safety of EGRIFTA SV has not been established."
> 2. "EGRIFTA SV is not indicated for weight loss management as it has a weight neutral effect."
> 3. "There are no data to support improved compliance with anti-retroviral therapies in HIV-positive patients taking EGRIFTA SV."

**Critical scope point:** The FDA approval is **ONLY** for reduction of excess abdominal fat in **HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy**. It is **NOT** an approval for general anti-aging, bodybuilding, athletic enhancement, or non-HIV fat loss. The label's own Limitations of Use state the drug is **not indicated for weight loss management** and is **weight-neutral** [regulatory] [1]. Any use outside the labeled HIV-lipodystrophy population is **off-label**, which is not an FDA-approved use — the existence of an approval for one narrow indication does not constitute approval for any other purpose.

**Manufacturer / commercial history:** Tesamorelin is developed and held by **Theratechnologies Inc.** (Montréal, Québec, Canada; U.S. License No. 2091) [regulatory] [1]. At original 2010 launch the U.S. marketing partner was **EMD Serono, Inc.** (an affiliate of Merck KGaA, Darmstadt) [regulatory] [3]. (Note: U.S. commercialization arrangements changed over time; Theratechnologies has since marketed the product directly in the U.S. — the EMD Serono relationship reflects the original-launch period.)

**Reformulations (three FDA-approved formulations exist):** [regulatory]
1. **EGRIFTA** — original formulation, approved 2010 (two-step reconstitution) [1][3].
2. **EGRIFTA SV ("Single-Vial," F4)** — reformulation approved **2019**, simplifying reconstitution to a single vial / single daily reconstitution step [2][4].
3. **EGRIFTA WR ("Weekly Reconstitution," F8 formulation)** — approved via supplemental BLA on **March 25, 2025**; still a daily injection but requiring only **weekly** reconstitution, with less than half the administration volume of the F4 (EGRIFTA SV) formulation [regulatory] [4].

### EU / EMA status — NOT approved in the EU

Tesamorelin is **not approved in the European Union.** A centralised marketing authorisation application for **Egrifta (tesamorelin), 2 mg, powder for solution for injection** was submitted to the EMA on **31 May 2011** by the applicant **Ferrer Internacional, S.A.**, and was subsequently **withdrawn** while under review by the CHMP [regulatory] [5][6]. The intended EU indication was treatment of **excess visceral adipose tissue (VAT)** (defined as >130 cm² by imaging) in treatment-experienced HIV-infected patients [regulatory] [5].

The withdrawal followed a **negative provisional CHMP position**: the CHMP considered that the provided data did not allow it to conclude on a **positive benefit-risk balance**, was of the provisional opinion that Egrifta **could not have been approved**, and was specifically concerned about how the drug would be used in clinical practice because it would be difficult to differentiate excess abdominal fat due to lipodystrophy from excess fat due to obesity [regulatory] [5]. Net effect: tesamorelin has **no EU marketing authorisation** — the application was abandoned in the face of an effectively negative opinion, in contrast to its U.S. approval.

### 503A compounding / gray-market supply

Because tesamorelin is an **approved brand drug** (Egrifta SV / Egrifta WR), copies and unapproved sources exist and should be flagged. Three practical supply routes are described in the consumer market: (1) a genuine prescription for FDA-approved Egrifta SV/WR; (2) telehealth clinics shipping **compounded** tesamorelin from U.S. **503A/503B** pharmacies; and (3) **research-chemical vendors** selling lyophilized "tesamorelin" labeled "for laboratory and research use only, not for human use" [anecdote_aggregate / practitioner_protocol — qualitative] [7][8].

A regulatory complication: in **late 2023 the FDA recategorized more than a dozen peptides** (a bulk-substances determination affecting eligibility for compounding), restricting which peptides 503A compounding pharmacies may make [regulatory/secondary] [8]. The research-chemical channel ($30–$80-range product) is **not** compounded to pharmaceutical standards, is not prescription-dispensed, and carries no clinical oversight — it is gray-market and falls outside the FDA approval entirely [anecdote_aggregate — qualitative] [7][8].

### WADA — prohibited at all times

Tesamorelin is **prohibited at all times** (both **In-Competition and Out-of-Competition**) under the WADA Prohibited List, **Section S2** ("Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances, and Mimetics") [regulatory] [9]. The S2 class header states all prohibited substances in this class are prohibited at all times, where "Prohibited at all times" means "the substance or method is prohibited In- and Out-of-Competition as defined in the Code" [regulatory] [9].

Within S2, the relevant entry is **S2.2.4 Growth hormone releasing factors**, and **tesamorelin is named explicitly** in the primary list text (verified verbatim against the WADA 2026 Prohibited List) [regulatory] [9]:

> "S2.2.4 Growth hormone releasing factors, including, but not limited to:
> • growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) and its analogues (e.g. CJC-1293, CJC-1295, sermorelin and tesamorelin)
> • growth hormone secretagogues (GHS) and their mimetics [e.g. anamorelin, capromorelin, ibutamoren (MK-677), ipamorelin, lenomorelin (ghrelin), macimorelin and tabimorelin];
> • GH-releasing peptides (GHRPs) [e.g. alexamorelin, examorelin (hexarelin), GHRP-1, GHRP-2 (pralmorelin), GHRP-3, ...]"

So tesamorelin is banned for athletes subject to WADA at all times, with no in-competition-only carve-out [regulatory] [9].

### Cost / access (qualitative)

The FDA-approved brand is an **expensive specialty Rx**: Egrifta SV (brand) is reported to run on the order of **~$3,000 per month**, typically billed through specialty pharmacies in the HIV-lipodystrophy setting [anecdote_aggregate — qualitative] [7]. By contrast, research-chemical "tesamorelin" is quoted far cheaper (single 10 mg vials ~$60–$150, dropping per-mg with multi-packs), but that price reflects an **unapproved, non-clinical** product [anecdote_aggregate — qualitative] [7]. The cost gulf is itself a driver of the gray-market channel.
