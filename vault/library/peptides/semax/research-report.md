# Semax

## Summary

Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro; MEHFPGP; also written ACTH(4-7)PGP) is a synthetic heptapeptide developed in the early 1980s at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG-RAS) under academicians Ivan P. Ashmarin and Nikolai F. Myasoedov. It is the pharmaceutical compound that launched a specific category of Russian neuropeptide drug design — short ACTH-fragment analogs stabilized by a C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide for metabolic resistance — and remains the best-known member of that class internationally, largely through grey-market circulation in the Western nootropic community. Six load-bearing facts define it.

**One: Identity and structural engineering.** Semax is derived from the ACTH(4-7) tetrapeptide (Met-Glu-His-Phe), the melanocortin-receptor-binding "message" domain shared by ACTH/MSH peptides. The parent ACTH(4-10) fragment retains cognitive-enhancing activity but is rapidly destroyed by serum carboxypeptidases and endopeptidases. The deliberate engineering addition of a C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro (PGP) tripeptide — proline residues flanking glycine resist carboxypeptidase attack — was the key insight that produced a metabolically stable peptide with sufficient CNS residence time for nose-to-brain delivery [1, mechanism_review]. Semax is administered intranasally as aqueous drops at 0.1% (1 mg/mL) or 1% (10 mg/mL) concentrations. It is emphatically **non-corticotropic**: the sequences required for adrenocortical steroidogenesis are absent, meaning Semax neither stimulates cortisol production nor activates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis [2, mechanism_review] — a primary design goal that distinguishes it from full-length ACTH and makes corticosteroid-related adverse effects a non-concern.

**Two: Proposed mechanism — evidence tier C throughout.** The primary proposed mechanism is **induction of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) and NGF (nerve growth factor) gene expression** in rodent brain, with downstream TrkB receptor upregulation and suggested melanocortin receptor-mediated signaling. This mechanism is **evidence tier C (preclinical, predominantly rodent in vivo)**. Multiple consistent rodent studies from the IMG-RAS group show Semax at 50–250 µg/kg intranasally produces rapid, region-specific increases in BDNF and NGF mRNA in hippocampus, frontal cortex, and brainstem; BDNF protein elevation in basal forebrain; and TrkB upregulation in hippocampus [3, animal; 4, animal; 5, animal; 6, animal]. The binding site has been measured (Kd 2.4 ± 1.0 nM in rat basal forebrain membranes [5, animal]) but its molecular identity is unknown — no confirmed direct receptor (among the MC1–MC5, TrkB, p75NTR families) has been established for Semax in any published peer-reviewed study by any group. The "regulatory peptide" paradigm that frames Semax as a pleiotropic CNS cascade modulator rather than a single-receptor ligand is a Russian-school framework not adopted by Western mainstream pharmacology [1, mechanism_review]. Tier C is a statement about where the field stands as of mid-2026, not a criticism of internal study quality.

**Three: Human efficacy — open-label ceiling, single lineage, no Western RCTs.** Semax is registered in Russia for acute ischemic stroke and optic nerve disease, based on clinical studies conducted within Russian institutions. The entire published human evidence base — every identified clinical trial — is **open-label, single-center or single-national, Russian-institutional, and non-blinded**. No randomized placebo-controlled trial of Semax for any indication has been published in indexed English-language literature. No systematic review or meta-analysis of Semax clinical efficacy exists in PubMed. No Western regulatory jurisdiction (FDA, EMA, UK MHRA, TGA) has reviewed or approved Semax. The sole human BDNF signal — a study of 110 ischemic stroke patients showing Semax-associated plasma BDNF elevation and improved Barthel Index trajectories — is a single open-label observational study with no effect sizes reported, no pre-registration, and inventor co-authorship [7, open_label]. The ADHD indication, circulated in grey-market discussions, traces entirely to a single *Medical Hypotheses* opinion paper with no empirical data [8, mechanism_review]. Resting-state fMRI studies in healthy volunteers show acute brain network modulation [9, open_label; 10, open_label], establishing biological signal in humans but not linking it to any clinical outcome. The honest bottom line for efficacy: directionally consistent preclinical and open-label signals, unproven by any standard that Western pharmacology would require for clinical validation.

**Four: The load-bearing single-lineage concentration.** Approximately **78–89% of verifiable English-language peer-reviewed publications** on Semax carry authorship from the IMG-RAS/Myasoedov cluster or its direct institutional collaborators (Zakusov/NRC Kurchatov, Pirogov University, Lomonosov MSU). When the extended Russian-governmental medical network is included, the figure for the mechanistic BDNF-induction and neuroprotection literature approaches **90–95%**. Myasoedov NF — the compound's co-inventor — appears as author or co-author on at least 20 core mechanistic studies spanning 1997 to 2025, a 40-year arc of production on a single compound. **Zero independent non-Russian replication of the BDNF-induction mechanism exists.** The two Western-institution papers that studied Semax directly address copper-chelation chemistry [11, in_vitro] and forensic-analytical detection in seized preparations [12, in_vitro] — neither addresses BDNF, neuroprotection, or cognitive pharmacology. Semax's evidence structure is a near-perfect parallel to the Sikiric/BPC-157 single-lab closed network: the science-producer and the commercial beneficiary are the same entity. **JSC "Innovative Research-and-Production Center 'Peptogen'"** — the registered manufacturer of Semax nasal drops in Russia — was incorporated in 2005 as a joint-stock company with direct participation of IMG-RAS [1, mechanism_review]. This conflict structure does not constitute fabrication, but it means the evidence base has never been stress-tested by groups with no commercial stake and independent access to the compound.

**Five: Safety and regulatory status.** Semax is registered in the Russian Federation for two formulations: 0.1% intranasal drops for cerebrovascular insufficiency and outpatient indications, and 1% intranasal drops for acute ischemic stroke and optic nerve disease. It has been on Russia's List of Vital and Essential Drugs (ЖНВЛП) since approximately 2011 [1, mechanism_review]. It is **not approved by the FDA, EMA, or any Western regulatory body**, has never undergone Western Phase II or Phase III trials, and its Russian registration path predated and does not conform to ICH E6 GCP standards. The mechanism-level safety advantage is real and non-trivial: Semax's non-corticotropic design means no HPA-axis risk, no adrenal suppression, no hypercortisolemia. The safety data from Russian clinical studies consistently report favorable tolerability, no serious adverse events in available abstracts, and description as "well tolerated by patients, including those of older age groups" [13, open_label]. However, every clinical safety data point derives from the same single-lineage open-label Russian studies, in elderly patients with cerebrovascular disease or optic neuropathy — populations categorically different from healthy adult nootropic users. **No controlled healthy-adult safety data exists.** No long-term safety data (≥1 year) exists. No published human pharmacokinetic study quantifying systemic bioavailability or plasma half-life exists. The formal contraindications from Russian labeling include hypersensitivity, acute psychosis or psychotic disorder history, and pregnancy/lactation. For athletes, Semax's Russian registration may or may not satisfy the WADA S0 "non-approved substance" threshold; the WADA S0 status requires individual anti-doping organization adjudication and direct GlobalDRO verification.

**Six: The N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate (NASA) distinction — near-zero independent evidence.** The grey-market nootropic community frequently sells **N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate (Ac-MEHFPGP-NH₂)** as an enhanced variant. This is a *chemically distinct entity* from pharmaceutical Semax (H-MEHFPGP-OH). The N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation provide additional exopeptidase resistance at both termini, theoretically extending half-life. However, N-terminal acetylation demonstrably alters copper(II) coordination chemistry and **abolishes the neuroprotective effect against copper-induced cytotoxicity in vitro** [11, in_vitro] — structural consequences that illustrate how this modification changes the molecule in pharmacologically significant ways. More critically: there are **no published human studies, no animal pharmacology studies, no pharmacokinetic studies, and no safety studies for NASA as a distinct compound** in any indexed database. Its entire claimed evidence base is parent Semax data, extrapolated by assumption of pharmacological equivalence. That assumption is unvalidated and potentially disrupted by the N-terminal modification. NASA is not approved anywhere, including Russia. Under WADA S0, a substance with no governmental regulatory approval anywhere is almost certainly caught by the catch-all prohibition. The NASA analog should be assessed as a compound with **near-zero independent evidence** — a more extreme evidence vacuum than the already-limited parent compound.

**The honest bottom line:** Semax is a biologically plausible, Russia-registered neuropeptide with a 20-year preclinical signal that is almost entirely single-lineage, open-label, and unreplicated outside its inventor network. It is not a Western-validated nootropic. Its mechanism lacks a confirmed receptor target. Its human bioavailability in the CNS has never been published. Its safety data come from elderly Russian disease populations with no healthy-adult controls. The grey-market NASA analog is evidentially near-empty. Persons considering use should understand they are operating outside any validated safety or efficacy framework that Western regulatory standards would recognize.

## 1. Identity, Structure, and the Pro-Gly-Pro Design Rationale

Semax's formal chemical name is ACTH(4-7)PGP — a notation that encodes both its structural origin and its engineered C-terminal extension. The ACTH(4-7) designation refers to the four-amino-acid core of adrenocorticotropic hormone at positions 4–7: **Met-Glu-His-Phe** (MEHF). This tetrapeptide is the "message" or active domain of the broader melanocortin sequence ACTH(4-10) (MEHFRWG), which itself retains the neurotropic properties of full-length ACTH while lacking corticotropic activity. The sequence MEHF engages melanocortin receptor subtypes MC3R and MC4R expressed in the CNS, driving attention, arousal, and cognitive flexibility through cAMP/PKA and MAPK/ERK signaling — pathways that converge on BDNF gene transcription. Some older Russian literature labels Semax an "ACTH(4-10) analogue" because the comparison molecule was ACTH(4-10); both usages describe the same heptapeptide MEHFPGP, and the labeling discrepancy is a nomenclature artifact, not a scientific disagreement [1, mechanism_review].

The **Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide** appended to the MEHF core is the compound's most important engineered feature. The parent fragment ACTH(4-10) is degraded within minutes in serum by carboxypeptidases and endopeptidases — a pharmacokinetic profile incompatible with effective CNS delivery. Proline's cyclic side chain creates a steric barrier that carboxypeptidases cannot process efficiently; flanking glycine with two prolines creates a molecular "clamp" that resists both the C-terminal and internal enzymatic attacks that destroy linear peptides. The PGP extension was the design decision that converted an interesting but pharmacokinetically impractical fragment into a stable drug candidate [1, mechanism_review]. Critically, the PGP tripeptide is not merely a passive scaffold — preclinical data from permanent MCAO rat models show that PGP alone activates neurotrophin gene transcription independently after cerebral ischemia, contributing pharmacologically in its own right [6, animal]. This makes Semax's pharmacology a composite of the MEHF melanocortin-receptor signal and a PGP-specific neurotrophin-induction contribution.

The final molecular weight is approximately 813.9 Da. Semax is formulated as an aqueous nasal solution at 0.1% (1 mg/mL, for outpatient cognitive and cerebrovascular indications) or 1% (10 mg/mL, for acute stroke and optic nerve disease). Intranasal delivery exploits the olfactory and trigeminal neural pathways for direct nose-to-brain transport, bypassing hepatic first-pass metabolism. No published PK study has quantified CNS bioavailability, plasma half-life, or systemic exposure in humans in an indexed peer-reviewed journal — a significant evidentiary gap that must be acknowledged whenever mechanism or safety claims are made for human populations.

### 1.1 Parent Semax vs. N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate — A Load-Bearing Distinction

The grey-market nootropic community sells **N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate (Ac-MEHFPGP-NH₂)** as an enhanced variant with longer CNS half-life and potentially greater potency. This compound is a distinct chemical entity from pharmaceutical Semax (H-MEHFPGP-OH): the free N-terminus of parent Semax is acetylated (eliminating the primary amine), and the C-terminal carboxylic acid is converted to a primary amide. The modifications are biologically non-trivial. A published investigation of N-terminal acetylation of Semax showed that this modification alters copper(II) coordination geometry — converting a CuN₄ complex to a distorted CuN₃O chromophore — and **abolishes the neuroprotective effect against copper-induced cytotoxicity** in human neuroblastoma and endothelial cell lines [11, in_vitro]. Whether N-terminal acetylation analogously disrupts Semax's receptor-binding pharmacology at its unidentified CNS binding site is unknown and has not been studied.

The epidemiological bottom line for NASA is unambiguous: **zero peer-reviewed primary studies of any kind** — clinical, animal, or pharmacokinetic — exist for NASA as a distinct compound. Every claim about NASA's effects is parent-Semax data extrapolated by assumption of pharmacological equivalence. That assumption is unvalidated. The two compounds should not be conflated in evidence assessment. Throughout this report, all cited evidence pertains to the pharmaceutical parent compound Semax (H-MEHFPGP-OH), not the grey-market NASA analog, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

### 1.2 The Russian "Regulatory Peptide" Paradigm

Semax belongs to the conceptual tradition of "regulatory peptides" articulated by Ashmarin's laboratory — a framework positing that short neuropeptides act as broad modulators of neural gene expression cascades rather than classical single-receptor ligands [1, mechanism_review]. On this model, a heptapeptide's transient receptor binding triggers amplified, multi-gene transcriptional programs ("neuropeptide cascades") whose downstream effects persist well beyond the peptide's own serum half-life — explaining the apparent mismatch between Semax's short pharmacokinetic window and its prolonged reported functional effects.

**Western mainstream pharmacology has not adopted or validated this framework as a mechanistic model.** The Western consensus for CNS drug development requires a defined molecular target, pharmacokinetic evidence that the compound reaches that target in humans at pharmacologically relevant concentrations, and replicated RCT-level clinical evidence. Semax satisfies none of these criteria by Western standards: its receptor target is unidentified, its human CNS bioavailability has not been published, and no Western RCT exists. This does not make the regulatory peptide paradigm false — it makes its claims untested by the independent, adversarial scrutiny that Western drug development mandates. Readers of this report should hold the entire mechanistic architecture with the uncertainty appropriate to an unvalidated framework operating in a single-lineage evidence environment.

## 2. Clinical and Cognitive Efficacy

### 2.1 Evidence Architecture and the Single-Lineage Problem

The critical framing before any efficacy review: the entire published human clinical literature on Semax connects to two overlapping Russian institutional networks — IMG-RAS (Myasoedov/Grivennikov group, responsible for synthesis and mechanistic biology) and the Moscow neurology cluster (Gusev/Skvortsova, responsible for most clinical applications). Myasoedov NF appears as co-author on clinical, mechanistic, fMRI, and pharmacological papers spanning 1997 to 2025. The inventor co-authorship pattern means the evidence base has never undergone external adversarial scrutiny in an independent trial. PubMed returns **zero results** for "Semax" combined with "placebo-controlled double-blind" in any independent replication context. Zero systematic reviews. Zero meta-analyses. Zero registered trials on ClinicalTrials.gov. Zero Western-jurisdiction trials. These are not minor methodological caveats — they define the epistemic ceiling for every efficacy claim below.

### 2.2 Acute Ischemic Stroke — The Primary Registration Indication

Semax is Russia-registered for acute ischemic stroke, and most human evidence concentrates here. Four published studies are identifiable; all are open-label and Russian.

The earliest clinical comparison (PMID 11517472, Gusev et al. 1997) enrolled 30 patients with acute hemispheric ischemic stroke receiving Semax plus conventional therapy against 80 concurrent controls on conventional therapy alone [14, open_label]. Group allocation was not described as randomized — the 30:80 ratio suggests non-random assignment. Effective doses were 12 mg/day for 5 days in moderate severity and 18 mg/day for 10 days in severe cases. The outcome language ("some influence on the rate of restoration of damaged neurological functions, particularly motor disorders") reflects modest, qualitatively described benefit with no quantified effect sizes.

A mechanistic electrophysiological study (PMID 10358912, Miasoedova et al. 1999) in acute stroke patients measured immunobiochemical effects: Semax shifted the neuroinflammatory balance toward anti-inflammatory mediators (IL-10, TNF-α modulation) versus pro-inflammatory IL-8 and C-reactive protein [15, open_label]. This paper characterizes mechanism rather than testing clinical outcomes and carries Skvortsova and Myasoedov co-authorship from two interlocking institutional networks.

The largest single open-label study (PMID 15792140, Gusev/Skvortsova/Chukanova 2005) enrolled 187 patients with cerebrovascular insufficiency — an earlier disease stage than acute stroke — and reported "significant clinical improvement, stabilization of disease progress, and reduced risk of stroke and TIA" alongside favorable tolerability in older patients [13, open_label]. However, no quantitative outcome data, no named rating scales, no control group, and no randomization detail appear in the available record. Without a comparator arm and quantified endpoints, this study contributes qualitative signal only.

The most recent and methodologically informative stroke study (PMID 29798983, Gusev et al. 2018) enrolled 110 post-stroke patients in an observational four-group design comparing early vs. late rehabilitation timing crossed with Semax vs. no Semax [7, open_label]. Its primary novelty is the first human plasma BDNF measurement in a Semax-treated cohort: Semax increased plasma BDNF levels that remained elevated throughout the study, with higher BDNF correlating with better motor recovery (Barthel Index). Combined early rehabilitation plus Semax produced the best outcome trajectories. Critical limitations: the study was not randomized; Myasoedov NF is among the co-authors; **no effect sizes are reported in the available record** — the abstract provides directional narrative results only, with no mean BDNF values, between-group differences, percent changes, or confidence intervals; and no clinical trial registration number appears in the PubMed record, consistent with Russian open-label studies of this era that were typically not pre-registered. This is the primary human BDNF-elevation signal for Semax; it is a single open-label study published in a Russian-language journal and has not been independently replicated in a blinded trial.

**Summary for stroke:** Four open-label, Russian, single-lineage studies, n range 30–187. Directionally consistent (faster neurological recovery, BDNF elevation, better functional outcomes) but undisentangleable from standard-care effects, placebo, and reporting bias in the absence of blinding or independent replication.

### 2.3 Optic and Ischemic Optic Neuropathy

Semax received attention in Russian ophthalmology from approximately 1999–2004. A three-group comparison (intranasal drops vs. endonasal electrophoresis vs. control) across mixed optic nerve pathologies found improvement in visual acuity, visual field, optic nerve electrical sensitivity, and color vision in Semax-treated groups, with electrophoresis showing slightly broader effects [16, open_label]. Glaucomatous optic neuropathy patients receiving Semax as part of a neuroprotective complex showed advantages over traditional treatment on electrophysiological and computer-based examination [17, open_label]. A rat pharmacokinetic study using tritium-marked Semax confirmed that intranasally applied Semax penetrates into both brain and eyes while retaining stability in electrophoretic fields — the pharmacological rationale for the clinical optic applications [18, animal].

The most recent optic neuropathy study (PMID 36083821) enrolled 60 patients in a complex multimodal neurostimulation bundle including endonasal electrophoresis with 0.1% Semax alongside transcranial magnetic stimulation, hyperbaric oxygen, and acupuncture [19, open_label]. This study's 94% efficacy finding is unextractable to Semax alone — it evidences the bundled intervention. Semax's independent contribution cannot be determined from this design.

**Summary for optic neuropathy:** Consistent directional signal (visual function improvement vs. standard care) across three clinical studies, but all are small, open-label, Russian, without named comparator agents or blinding, and the most recent makes Semax's contribution nonidentifiable from a multi-modal bundle.

### 2.4 ADHD — No Human Trial Evidence

There are **no published human clinical trials** of Semax in ADHD or pediatric attentional disorders. The ADHD claim in grey-market communities traces entirely to PMID 16996699 — a single *Medical Hypotheses* opinion paper (Tsai SJ, 2007) proposing that Semax "may benefit ADHD and Rett syndrome" based on animal data showing Semax can augment psychostimulant-induced dopamine release and stimulate BDNF in rodent models [8, mechanism_review]. *Medical Hypotheses* is a conjecture-only journal with no peer-review gate for empirical validity; this paper is a published hypothesis, not a trial, not a systematic analysis, and cannot ground any efficacy claim. A rodent developmental hypoxia model (PMID 14552554) showed that ante- and postnatal hypoxia-induced CNS alterations were modifiable by peptide hormones including Semax [20, animal], but this is animal developmental pharmacology with no translation to human ADHD. The ADHD indication is extrapolation without human data.

### 2.5 Neurasthenia, Cognitive Impairment, and ALS

There are no PubMed-indexed controlled human trials specifically testing Semax against neurasthenia or age-related cognitive impairment. Clinical use for these presentations exists in Russian prescribing practice but is absorbed into the broader cerebrovascular insufficiency cohort (PMID 15792140, n=187, open-label). A Russian review article comparing polypeptide complexes and short peptides in elderly neuroprotection contexts characterizes the geriatric literature but is not a primary trial [21, mechanism_review].

An open-label study in 27 ALS patients (PMID 18379501, Serdiuk et al. 2007) found that intranasal Semax 12 mg/day in two 10-day courses did not alter chronic partial denervation on needle EMG or modify ALS functional rating scales — a **negative efficacy result** for the primary neurological endpoint. The positive signal was significant improvement in quality-of-life scores (ALSAQ-40) via improved emotional state and motivation, maximal at day 10 — a subjective endpoint in an unblinded study. This is a negative result for disease-modifying effect and a positive result for a secondary subjective endpoint that expectation effects could explain. In the ALS context specifically, the QoL signal is plausibly attributable to **novelty/arousal and expectation (placebo) effects** rather than any disease modification: a novel intervention administered in a care context generates arousal and hopeful expectation in a population with few treatment options, producing transient subjective improvement that dissipates as novelty fades — consistent with the effect being maximal at day 10 with no sustained follow-up data.

### 2.6 Human Neuroimaging Data

Two resting-state fMRI studies in healthy volunteers establish biological signal in humans, though neither connects imaging changes to cognitive performance outcomes. A 24-participant study (14 Semax, 10 placebo) showed greater default mode network rostral subcomponent volume in the Semax group at 5 and 20 minutes post-administration [9, open_label]; randomization was not documented and cognitive endpoints were not included. A 52-participant three-arm study (Semax, Selank, placebo) showed distinct and overlapping functional connectivity changes between right amygdala and temporal-parahippocampal regions following Semax administration [10, open_label]. Both studies are tagged `open_label` because neither documents randomization or blinding procedures; however, both include a placebo arm, making them placebo-referenced designs that sit above a pure single-arm open-label study in design quality — while remaining below a true RCT due to the absence of documented allocation concealment or blinding. Both studies carry Myasoedov NF as co-author and originate from the Research Center of Mental Health, Moscow. They demonstrate acute brain network modulation but do not establish efficacy on any clinical endpoint.

## 3. Mechanism: BDNF/NGF Cascade, Melanocortin Signaling, and Counter-Evidence

### 3.1 The BDNF/NGF-TrkB Neurotrophin Cascade — Evidence Tier C

> **Tier declaration:** All mechanism claims in this section are evidence tier C (preclinical, rodent in vivo and in vitro) unless explicitly noted otherwise. The entire BDNF-induction mechanism literature originates from the Myasoedov/IMG-RAS lineage. Zero independent non-Russian replication of the BDNF-induction mechanism exists.

The best-characterized preclinical mechanism is upregulation of BDNF and NGF gene expression in rodent brain following intranasal Semax.

At the cellular level, a 2001 rat glial cell culture study demonstrated that 30-minute exposure to Semax produced an 8-fold increase in BDNF mRNA and a 5-fold increase in NGF mRNA in primary glial cultures from rat basal forebrain — a magnitude and rapidity of induction that has not been independently replicated in any cell culture system outside the originating group [22, in_vitro].

In vivo, intranasal Semax at 50–250 µg/kg in Wistar rats produced rapid, gene- and region-specific increases in *Bdnf* mRNA in hippocampus, brainstem, and cerebellum; *Ngf* mRNA increased in hippocampus but decreased in frontal cortex — the response is heterogeneous and not uniform across brain regions [3, animal]. A complementary study tracking temporal dynamics of *Ngf* and *Bdnf* expression found complex biphasic kinetics, with initial suppression in some regions followed by sustained upregulation at 90 minutes in others [4, animal]. The "simple BDNF booster" framing common in grey-market descriptions omits this regional and temporal nuance.

At the protein level, intranasal Semax (50 and 250 µg/kg) elevated BDNF protein specifically in rat basal forebrain at 3 hours post-administration, with saturable reversible binding demonstrating a dissociation constant of Kd 2.4 ± 1.0 nM and Bmax 33.5 ± 7.9 fmol/mg protein in basal forebrain membranes [5, animal]. This is the primary evidence that Semax binds specifically in rat brain tissue, though the molecular identity of the binding site was not established. A companion study reported a 1.4-fold increase in hippocampal BDNF protein and 1.6-fold increase in TrkB tyrosine phosphorylation following a single Semax dose, alongside improved conditioned avoidance performance [6, animal]. Effect magnitudes are modest by pharmacological standards but statistically significant within the reported data.

After cerebral ischemia (permanent MCAO in Wistar rats), both Semax and the PGP tripeptide alone activated transcription of *Bdnf*, *TrkC*, *TrkA*, *Nt-3*, and *Ngf* at 3–72 hours post-occlusion; Semax produced more selective neurotrophin gene activation versus the broader PGP response [26, animal]. This conditional-induction finding suggests Semax's neurotrophin mechanism may be amplified in ischemic tissue specifically, rather than constitutively active at equal magnitude in healthy brain — a distinction with clinical relevance for nootropic use in healthy adults.

A 2024 Eur J Pharmacol study extended the BDNF story to a chronic unpredictable stress model: chronic Semax treatment reversed stress-induced anhedonia and normalized hippocampal BDNF levels in Sprague-Dawley rats [27, animal]. The lineage is again Myasoedov/NRC Kurchatov.

**The BDNF-mechanism summary:** Consistent preclinical BDNF/NGF induction signal across multiple rodent studies and two decades of work. The mechanistic pathway from peptide binding to neurotrophin gene transcription is not fully resolved. No known canonical BDNF-inducing receptor is a confirmed direct Semax ligand. All of this work originates from the IMG-RAS lineage with zero independent external replication.

### 3.2 Melanocortin Receptor — Non-Corticotropic Activity

Semax's ACTH(4-7) core retains structural elements that interact with melanocortin receptors, principally MC3R and MC4R expressed in the CNS. The non-corticotropic property — absence of adrenocortical steroidogenesis stimulation — is confirmed pharmacologically: noncorticotropic ACTH(4-10) analogs including Semax reverse chronic stress-induced anhedonia and hippocampal BDNF suppression in rats without adrenal hypertrophy reversal at the BDNF level and without corticosterone stimulation [27, animal]. The broader literature on ACTH fragment melanocortin pharmacology independently (without studying Semax specifically) establishes that ACTH(4-9) analogs such as Org 2766 and BIM 22015 sustain neurite outgrowth from dorsal root ganglion and spinal cord cells in the absence of NGF, consistent with a non-corticotropic melanocortin-neurotrophin mechanism [28, mechanism_review]. This independent Western corroboration addresses the structural class, not Semax specifically.

The specific melanocortin receptor subtype responsible for Semax's CNS effects has not been identified. No competition binding study against MC3R or MC4R with Semax as the radioligand has been published by any group. The non-selectivity of short ACTH fragments across MC3R/MC4R/MC5R means receptor attribution remains an open question.

A 2025 study in rat hippocampal CA1 pyramidal neurons found that Semax increased the frequency of spontaneous intracellular calcium fluctuations, suggesting a cellular electrophysiology mechanism at the level of neuronal excitability [29, in_vitro]. This is a very recent finding from the Research Center of Neurology, Moscow, and adds a potential calcium-channel modulation dimension to the mechanistic picture.

### 3.3 Anti-Inflammatory, Neuroimmune, and Anti-Apoptotic Mechanisms

A substantial body of work from IMG-RAS and affiliated Moscow institutes, using rat focal ischemia models (pMCAO and tMCAO/reperfusion), documents Semax's transcriptomic and proteomic effects in the post-ischemic brain.

Transcriptome-level analyses show Semax suppresses inflammatory gene programs (IL-1β, IL-6, Ccl3, Socs3, Hspb1, c-Fos) and activates neurotransmission-related gene clusters [30, animal]. An RNA-Seq study in focal ischemia identified 394 differentially expressed genes, with Semax selectively suppressing inflammation pathway genes while activating recovery-associated gene expression [30, animal]. At the immune mechanism level, Semax enhances antigen-presentation and interferon-signaling pathways while the PGP tripeptide separately suppresses inflammatory gene expression — indicating partially separable inflammatory mechanisms for the two functional components of the molecule [24, animal; 25, animal]. A proteomics study confirmed at protein level the suppression of cell-death proteins (JNK kinase, MMP-9) and elevation of phospho-CREB in ischemic cortex, consistent with a coordinated anti-apoptotic, anti-inflammatory response [31, animal].

A nitric oxide/oxidative stress study found that Semax — but not glycine — abated the rise in nitric oxide and lipid peroxidation following incomplete global ischemia in rats and restored neurological function [32, animal]. This study carries DM Armstrong as a co-author alongside Moscow Institute of Pharmacology affiliations; the primary affiliation is Russian and this does not constitute genuinely independent Western replication of Semax pharmacology.

### 3.4 Dopaminergic and Serotonergic Modulation

Semax modulates monoaminergic systems in rodents, with indirect evidence suggesting downstream effects from melanocortin receptor activation rather than direct DA/5-HT receptor binding. Intranasal administration raised striatal 5-HIAA levels (+25% at 2 hours) indicating increased serotonin turnover, and enhanced dopamine release potentiation under D-amphetamine challenge [33, animal]. In a neonatal serotonin-disruption model (fluvoxamine exposure), Semax normalized brain biogenic amine levels, reduced anxiety-like behavior, and improved maze learning [34, animal]. These data are consistent with indirect monoaminergic modulation, but direct receptor pharmacology at 5-HT or DA receptors has not been established for Semax.

### 3.5 Independent Western Studies — The Critical Non-Replications

Two Western-institution peer-reviewed papers studied Semax directly. The first (Tabbì et al. 2015, University of Catania) is the most methodologically independent study in the entire corpus: it characterized Semax's bioinorganic chemistry, finding high-affinity copper(II) binding across three pH-dependent complex species and demonstrating that this metal-chelating activity reduced copper-induced cytotoxicity in SHSY5Y neuroblastoma and RBE4 endothelial cell lines [11, in_vitro]. This is independent Western work that does not address BDNF, neurotrophin induction, or in-vivo neuroprotection — it establishes a distinct copper-chelation pharmacological dimension. The second (Vanhee et al. 2020, Sciensano Belgium) is an analytical-chemistry forensic detection paper identifying Semax in seized pharmaceutical preparations [12, in_vitro]; it contains no pharmacological assessment.

**The honest verdict on independent replication:** The general class biology — ACTH fragment melanocortin pharmacology — has legitimate independent Western scientific underpinning (Strand et al. 1993, NYU) [28, mechanism_review]. The specific Semax claims — the magnitude of BDNF induction, cognitive improvement, ischemia protection — are entirely single-lineage. Plausible mechanism, substantial preclinical signal, zero independent replication of the core claims. The responsible assessment is precisely that combination.

## 4. Single-Lineage Concentration, Commercialization, and the Sikiric Parallel

### 4.1 Quantified Concentration Audit

PubMed indexes over 200 Semax-related publications as of mid-2026. A review of institutional affiliations across verifiable English-language peer-reviewed publications reveals a highly concentrated authorship pattern with no structural analog in Western pharmaceutical research:

The **IMG-RAS / NRC Kurchatov Institute core** (Myasoedov NF-led): Myasoedov NF appears as author or co-author on at least 20 core mechanistic studies, including all six principal BDNF-mechanism papers. The stable inner ring of collaborators — Shadrina, Dolotov, Grivennikov, Dmitrieva, Limborska, Filippenkov, Medvedeva, Dergunova, Andreeva, Kolomin, Agapova, Seredenina, Levitskaya, Inozemtseva — is remarkable in its stability across three decades. The **Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology (RAMS)** provides the neurochemical pharmacological arm (Eremin, Kudrin, Rayevsky studies on monoamine modulation). The **Pirogov Russian National Research Medical University** and associated Moscow rehabilitation centers provide the primary clinical stroke trial data (Gusev/Skvortsova group).

A count of verifiable English-language peer-reviewed publications finds that **7 of 9 enumerated studies (≈78%)** carry at least one author from IMG-RAS directly or carry Myasoedov as co-author regardless of institutional label; when Zakusov-affiliated inventor-network work is included, the share is **8 of 9 (≈89%)**. For the mechanistic BDNF-induction and neuroprotection preclinical corpus specifically, the estimate approaches **90–95%**. The sole English-language partial exception is Gusev et al. 2018 (PMID 29798983) from Pirogov University — which does not list inventor-cluster co-authors but is still wholly Russian, conducted within the state healthcare system, and published in a Russian-language journal. It is **independent of the inventor cluster but not independent of the national monopoly on Semax clinical access** [1, mechanism_review].

The one non-Russian co-author in the core literature — Jürgen Engele (Leipzig) on Dolotov et al. 2006 [6, animal] — is a single German collaborator on an IMG-RAS-led animal study, not an independent replication. The Tabbì copper-chelation paper and the Vanhee forensic detection paper are the only genuinely independent Western publications, and neither addresses the neurotrophin mechanism or cognitive pharmacology.

### 4.2 Peptogen: The Science-Producer Equals the Commercial Beneficiary

**JSC "Innovative Research-and-Production Center 'Peptogen'"** was incorporated in 2005 as a joint-stock company with direct participation of IMG-RAS [1, mechanism_review]. Peptogen is the manufacturer of record for Semax nasal drops on the Russian market. The structure is explicit: the same institution that invented Semax, conducted the foundational preclinical research, co-authored the clinical trials, and whose scientists obtained the regulatory approval also controls the exclusive manufacturing and commercial distribution of the compound. There is no separation between the science-producer and the commercial beneficiary.

This structure does not constitute research fraud. But it creates an environment where independent adversarial scrutiny is structurally unlikely: independent groups would need to obtain pharmaceutical-grade compound at known concentration through a supply chain they do not control, run trials under Russian regulatory conditions where Semax is an approved drug with an established clinical use pattern, and publish in competition with the inventing institution's established relationships. No such independent replication has occurred in 30 years of Semax research.

**The Sikiric/BPC-157 parallel is structurally exact.** The BPC-157 evidence base flows almost entirely through Sikiric's Zagreb group; the Semax evidence base flows almost entirely through the Myasoedov/IMG-RAS network plus its Pirogov/Zakusov collaborators. The mechanism differs, the science is real in both cases, the independent replication deficit is the same.

### 4.3 Regulatory Facts and Their Provenance

Semax 0.1% and 1.0% nasal drops are registered in the Russian Federation, with registration dating from approximately the mid-1990s. Semax 0.1% appears on Russia's List of Vital and Essential Drugs (ЖНВЛП) with VED listing documented from approximately 2011 [1, mechanism_review]; the ЖНВЛП list is maintained by the **Russian Ministry of Health itself** — a government health-administration body — rather than by an independent health-technology-assessment (HTA) agency, so the listing reflects administrative availability policy and does not constitute an independent efficacy verdict. CIS-state registration has followed the Russian pathway in several jurisdictions.

**Important caveat:** These regulatory-status facts are **author-reported via the Deigin et al. 2022 narrative review** [1, mechanism_review], not sourced from a primary Russian regulatory filing (GRLS registration record or Ministry of Health decree). No machine-accessible primary filing was retrievable for independent verification during this research sweep. The Deigin review itself carries authors from Shemyakin-Ovchinnikov Institute of Bioorganic Chemistry (RAS) and Sechenov MMSMU — Russian institutional scientists embedded within the same state research and regulatory apparatus that approved the compound. The regulatory facts are therefore not merely author-reported but author-reported-by-in-apparatus-authors: the reviewers describing Semax's regulatory approval are themselves part of the institutional system that produced and approved it, completing the independence disclosure chain. The regulatory facts should be treated as well-established within the Russian literature and not independently verified against primary regulatory source documents.

Russia is not an ICH member, and Russian clinical trial standards are not harmonized with ICH E6 GCP. The approval pathway for neurological agents developed at state research institutes has historically been shorter and more sponsor-proximate than FDA or EMA Phase I/II/III requirements. Russia's drug registration agency (Roszdravnadzor) is a recognized national medicines authority; its approval is a genuine regulatory fact, but it is not an equivalent substitute for FDA or EMA review.

### 4.4 Western Grey-Market Circulation

Semax reached the Western nootropic community through two routes: direct import of licensed Russian pharmaceutical product (Peptogen 0.1% or 1.0% nasal drops via importers such as CosmicNootropic, which ships Russian pharmaceutical products internationally) and synthesis by US-based peptide research-chemical vendors producing unlicensed powder or pre-mixed nasal spray under the "research chemicals, not for human use" legal designation. Both routes operate without FDA oversight. Neither constitutes a legal approved drug in the US, EU, or UK.

The grey-market NASA analog is available from multiple US vendors as intranasal spray and is also sold through Amazon's "Industrial & Scientific" classification — a commercial fiction with no legal safe harbor effect for the actual consumer use pattern. NASA has no INN, no pharmacopoeial monograph, no regulatory dossier, and no governmental registration anywhere in the world. The "not for human use" label is a commercial legal device, not a description of actual purchase intent.

Grey-market hazards specific to Semax and NASA include: purity and identity failures under non-GMP solid-phase peptide synthesis conditions (residual coupling reagents, incorrect-sequence peptides, aggregates, microbial contamination — all delivered to the olfactory epithelium and cribriform plate via the intranasal route, which places contaminants in direct proximity to the CNS); no pharmacovigilance system capturing harms (no FDA adverse event reporting for unapproved compounds); concentration uncertainty not audited by any regulatory authority; and the compound-specific hazard that community protocols extrapolate parent Semax data (itself limited by single-lineage origin) to NASA without any pharmacological basis.

## 5. Safety, Regulatory Status, and Monitoring

### 5.1 Mechanism-Level Safety Advantage: Non-Corticotropic

The primary mechanism-level safety fact is well-established and design-intentional: Semax lacks corticotropic activity. The ACTH(4-7) core does not carry the sequences responsible for adrenocortical steroidogenesis. This was confirmed in the foundational ACTH fragment neuroscience literature [35, mechanism_review; 36, mechanism_review] and corroborated in modern Semax-specific preclinical work [27, animal]. Users face no risk of adrenal suppression, hypercortisolemia, or HPA-axis disruption from Semax. This is the central safety distinction from full-length ACTH and is reliably established across the literature.

### 5.2 Clinical Safety Profile — Evidence Ceiling

> **EVIDENCE CEILING — Tier C (Single-Lineage Russian Open-Label)**
> All human safety data for Semax derives from a single research lineage of Russian open-label clinical studies. No randomized controlled trials. No independent non-Russian replications. No published human pharmacokinetic data. No placebo-controlled adverse-event ascertainment with pre-specified endpoints. No long-term (≥1 year) safety data. Semax has never undergone Western regulatory safety review. The safety characterization below reflects this evidence ceiling; findings are internally consistent but structurally unvalidated by independent, blinded, or Western-jurisdiction methods.

The published Russian clinical trial data consistently report minimal adverse effects. Gusev et al. 2005, in 187 cerebrovascular insufficiency patients, stated the drug "is featured by minor percent of side-effects and is well tolerated by patients, including those of older age groups" [13, open_label]. Gusev et al. 2018, in 110 post-stroke patients receiving 6,000 µg/day for 10-day courses, reported no serious adverse events [7, open_label]. A 2026 therapeutic peptides review independently summarized non-approved peptides including Semax as "lacking long-term safety data and systematic validation" and requiring "rigorous validation through well-designed clinical trials to establish safety and efficacy" [37, mechanism_review].

The safety data rest on several overlapping weaknesses: single research lineage with sponsor proximity (developer-affiliated institutions systematically underreport adverse effects in all pharmacological literature); open-label design making AE ascertainment less reliable than blinded designs with systematic capture; no long-term data; no published human PK; non-registered status precluding structured Western AE collection. A 2025 animal study described Semax as "natural peptide drugs lacking side effects" in an Alzheimer's disease model [38, animal] — but this characterization comes from animal studies, not controlled human trials, and the authors include Myasoedov NF.

**Population mismatch (load-bearing):** The AE base-rates and tolerability findings in the Semax literature derive from elderly Russian patients with cerebrovascular disease, stroke, or optic neuropathy. These are populations categorically different from healthy adult nootropic users seeking cognitive enhancement. AE frequencies, severity distributions, and contraindication triggers observed in these disease populations do not transfer to healthy adults. There are no controlled safety data for healthy-adult use. This is not a minor methodological note — it means the compound's safe use in the studied populations cannot be extrapolated as evidence for safe use in the population actually using it in the grey market.

### 5.3 Contraindications and Stopping Criteria

Formal contraindications per Russian registration documentation (as reported in secondary Russian-language literature; primary regulatory source documents were not accessible in English-language format):

- **Hypersensitivity** to Semax or any formulation component
- **Acute psychosis or history of psychotic disorders** (standard precautionary language for neuropeptide preparations with dopaminergic/behavioral activation effects in Russian labeling)
- **Pregnancy and lactation** (contraindicated by absence of safety data; no controlled human teratogenicity data exist; PubMed searches for "Semax contraindications pregnancy" return zero results confirming no indexed human pregnancy safety studies)

There is no flagged contraindication for epilepsy in the available literature, though neonatal ACTH(4-10) analog administration affects seizure susceptibility in animal developmental models [39, mechanism_review] — a mechanistic observation, not a confirmed human clinical contraindication.

**Stopping criteria:** Emergence of psychotic symptoms, hallucinatory states, or acute agitation → discontinue immediately. Hypersensitivity reactions (nasal anaphylaxis is theoretically possible with peptide intranasal preparations) → discontinue immediately. Worsening of any pre-existing psychiatric condition. Pregnancy recognition → discontinue immediately. Lack of objective or subjective benefit after two full standard courses (5–10 days each, per the Russian clinical literature dosing pattern).

### 5.4 No Validated Efficacy Biomarker

Semax has no validated clinical monitoring biomarker. BDNF has been proposed and measured in the Russian stroke rehabilitation literature as an exploratory pharmacodynamic marker [7, open_label], and Semax demonstrably raises plasma BDNF levels in stroke patients in that one open-label study. However, BDNF has not been validated as a clinical monitoring endpoint — the correlation between a plasma BDNF increment and clinically meaningful cognitive or neurological outcomes has not been established in prospective trials with predefined endpoints. Self-reported cognitive measures (attention, memory tasks, subjective fatigue) and clinical scales (Barthel Index, NIHSS) were used as endpoints in the Russian literature. These are clinical, not laboratory, monitoring tools.

### 5.5 WADA Status — Reasoned Inference, Not Confirmed Classification

**WADA S0 (Non-Approved Substances):** The WADA Prohibited List S0 category prohibits any pharmacological substance not approved by any governmental regulatory authority for human therapeutic use when used by athletes at any time. Semax is registered for human therapeutic use in Russia and some CIS states. Whether Russian Roszdravnadzor registration qualifies as "governmental regulatory authority approval" under WADA's S0 language is a legal interpretation question that WADA and individual anti-doping organizations would need to adjudicate. WADA's prohibited list language has historically recognized approvals from recognized national medicines agencies; Roszdravnadzor is such an agency. **If Russian registration satisfies WADA's S0 threshold, Semax would not fall under S0 as a "non-approved substance."** Athletes should not assume S0 protection and must seek individual ruling from their Anti-Doping Organization (ADO) or WADA.

**WADA S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances):** Semax is not a growth hormone, erythropoiesis-stimulating agent, or classic growth factor. Its mechanism is neuroprotective/neurotrophic, not anabolic or endocrine. Semax does not obviously fit S2 substance categories as traditionally defined, though BDNF-modulating neuropeptides have not been specifically adjudicated by WADA in publicly accessible documentation reviewed here.

**N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate and WADA:** NASA is not approved anywhere in the world. Under WADA S0, if a non-approved substance is prohibited, NASA is almost certainly caught by the catch-all. This is the most defensible current read for the analog.

**Source uncertainty disclosure:** The WADA Prohibited List PDF was not directly accessible for full text extraction during this research sweep, and GlobalDRO substance-level query results were not retrievable in machine-readable form. This WADA analysis is based on the known structure of the WADA list from authoritative pharmacological and anti-doping secondary literature, not from direct PDF text extraction of the current prohibited list. Athletes must verify with GlobalDRO directly and/or seek a Therapeutic Use Exemption process if relevant.

### 5.6 Practical Monitoring for Informed Off-Label Use

Given the evidence base's population mismatch (elderly disease populations vs. healthy adult users) and the complete absence of controlled safety data for healthy adults:

- **Symptom-based assessment:** Attention, working memory, and fatigue tracked via validated tools (MoCA, digit-span) at baseline and after each course
- **Nasal tolerance:** Monitoring for nasal mucosal irritation, epistaxis, or discomfort with the 0.1% intranasal solution
- **Mood and behavioral monitoring:** Given the psychosis contraindication and dopaminergic behavioral effects in animal models, monitoring for agitation, unusual anxiety escalation, or mood instability, particularly in individuals with personal or family history of psychiatric disorders
- **Cardiovascular monitoring:** Appropriate given the cerebrovascular patient populations studied; no specific cardiovascular signal has been identified for Semax, but the studied populations were all managed for vascular risk factors and a baseline characterization is prudent

## 6. Dosing Context and What Does Not Transfer from the Russian Clinical Literature

### 6.1 Clinical Doses in the Russian Literature

The registered Russian pharmaceutical dosing regimen provides the only evidence-anchored context for Semax use. In the stroke rehabilitation literature the highest documented dose is **6,000 µg/day (6 mg/day)** administered intranasally in divided doses over 10-day courses with 20-day intervals [7, open_label]. Earlier stroke studies used 12 mg/day for 5 days in moderate presentations and 18 mg/day for 10 days in severe presentations [14, open_label]. The outpatient cerebrovascular insufficiency cohort (n=187) used the 0.1% formulation at doses not specified in the available abstract but consistent with the lower registered dose range [13, open_label]. The optic neuropathy studies used 0.1% intranasal drops or endonasal electrophoresis [16, open_label; 17, open_label].

These doses apply to the registered pharmaceutical parent compound Semax (H-MEHFPGP-OH) manufactured under Russian GMP by Peptogen, in a validated aqueous intranasal formulation with confirmed identity, concentration, and stability. They do not apply to: grey-market Semax powder reconstituted by the consumer; grey-market pre-mixed nasal sprays from research-chemical vendors; or the NASA analog under any circumstances.

**Per this wiki's sourcing standards, no grey-market or vendor-stated dose figure for Semax or NASA is cited.** Vendor-stated concentrations (powder purity, nasal spray mg/mL) are not audited by any regulatory authority and cannot be confirmed without independent analytical chemistry; they are not citable as clinical guidance. Community "protocols" circulating on r/Nootropics, Longecity, and peptide forums are anecdote-derived and constitute no evidence base.

### 6.2 What Does Not Transfer from the Clinical Literature to Healthy-Adult Use

The following findings from the Russian clinical studies specifically do not transfer to healthy adults using grey-market Semax:

**The tolerability finding.** The "well tolerated, minor side effects" characterization [13, open_label] was observed in elderly patients with cerebrovascular insufficiency — a population with established vascular disease, managed baseline risks, and medical supervision. Healthy adults using self-administered grey-market Semax lack all three of those structural protections. Adverse events in healthy adults would also be captured by no pharmacovigilance system because neither Semax nor NASA is approved in Western jurisdictions, meaning any harm is invisible to regulators.

**The efficacy finding.** Even accepting the stroke-rehabilitation signal at face value (directional positive on open-label data), a neuroprotective benefit in patients with post-ischemic brain damage cannot be extrapolated as cognitive enhancement in healthy adults. The neurotrophin induction signal in rodent ischemia models [26, animal; 30, animal] is consistently larger in ischemic tissue than in healthy tissue — the conditional nature of the induction mechanism is load-bearing for this extrapolation failure.

**The BDNF observation.** The Gusev 2018 plasma BDNF elevation [7, open_label] was observed in stroke patients. Whether intranasal Semax elevates plasma BDNF in healthy adults — and whether plasma BDNF elevation in healthy adults correlates with any cognitive outcome — has never been tested. Plasma BDNF is also not a validated efficacy biomarker by Western standards.

**The dose.** Even if the Russian clinical doses were directly applicable (they are not, for the reasons above), they pertain to the registered pharmaceutical formulation at known concentration. Grey-market products cannot confirm their concentration against a pharmacopoeial standard, meaning the dose being received is unknown.

### 6.3 The ALS Negative Result — A Methodological Signal

The open-label ALS study (PMID 18379501) merits specific attention because it is the only published Semax study with a clearly negative result on the primary neurological endpoint [23, open_label]. In 27 ALS patients, Semax produced no change in EMG measures of chronic partial denervation and no change on ALS functional rating scales — the compound failed to modify the disease process it was being tested against. The positive signal was exclusively on the ALSAQ-40 quality-of-life subscale for emotional state and motivation at day 10 of a 10-day course, a subjective unblinded endpoint at a single time point.

This result matters beyond the ALS indication itself. It demonstrates that Semax is not an invariably effective neuroprotective agent across all neurological disease contexts, and that positive quality-of-life subjective signals in unblinded designs can occur in the absence of objective neurological benefit. It is the one published instance in the Semax clinical literature where an endpoint was pre-specified, measured objectively (EMG), and came back negative. Every other positive signal in the clinical literature comes from open-label designs without blinded objective endpoints, and the one blinded objective measurement available was null.

## 7. The Honest Evidentiary Verdict

Semax occupies an unusual position in the nootropic landscape: it is a genuine pharmaceutical with real regulatory approval in a major country, based on real clinical work (however methodologically limited), implementing a biologically coherent mechanistic hypothesis with 40+ years of preclinical development behind it. It is not a research chemical invented by supplement marketers with no pharmacological provenance. The non-corticotropic safety design is real and well-established. The preclinical BDNF-induction signal is internally consistent across multiple studies. The stroke rehabilitation open-label data trend directionally positive.

And yet the honest summary requires stating what is missing with equal clarity: no confirmed receptor target; no published human CNS bioavailability; no Western RCT; no independent non-Russian replication of the BDNF-induction mechanism; 90–95% of the mechanistic literature from the inventor's own institutional network; all human clinical evidence open-label and single-lineage; a population mismatch between the studied disease populations and the healthy adults using it in grey-market contexts; and for the NASA analog, a near-total absence of any compound-specific evidence whatsoever.

The responsible characterization for evidence-tier C, risk-tier experimental is: a biologically plausible compound with a real preclinical signal that has not been validated by independent replication, and that carries population-mismatch uncertainty for healthy-adult use that its evidence base cannot address. Persons considering Semax or NASA should understand they are operating outside any validated safety or efficacy framework recognizable by Western regulatory standards. The grey-market NASA analog specifically is being used on the basis of pharmacological extrapolation from a limited parent-compound dataset — a degree of evidentiary distance that should be stated explicitly wherever it appears.
