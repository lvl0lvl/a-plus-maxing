# Semax — Practitioner / Clinical-Use + Grey-Market Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how Semax is accessed, dosed, and monitored in its two real-world contexts*: (1) the Russian-registered clinical prescribing pathway, and (2) the Western grey-market nootropic context including the N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate (NASA) analog. It is **not** an efficacy document — the research report covers that. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (Tier 3), and `vendor_label` / `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4) are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions. **None of these source classes grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.**
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** Semax is **registered in Russia** as a pharmaceutical nasal spray in two formulations for neurological indications. It is **NOT approved by the FDA, EMA, UK MHRA, TGA, or any Western regulatory authority**, has never undergone Western Phase II or Phase III trials, and no Investigational New Drug (IND) application exists for it in the FDA's public database. There is **no lawful Western prescribing pathway** and **no 503A compounding pathway** — Semax is a Russian-manufactured proprietary biologic (JSC Peptogen), not a bulk active pharmaceutical ingredient licensed for Western compounding. Western grey-market use — of either parent Semax or the NASA analog — is unregistered, outside any pharmacovigilance system, and purchased under "not for human use" research-chemical disclaimers.

## Regulatory State

### Russian Registration (Primary Jurisdiction)

Semax is approved for human therapeutic use in the Russian Federation in two formulations:

**Semax 0.1% intranasal drops (1 mg/mL):**
- Registered indications: cerebrovascular insufficiency (including memory impairment, attention deficit, reduced work capacity associated with CNS pathology), stroke prevention, rehabilitation after ischemic stroke in outpatient settings, optic nerve disease
- Setting: outpatient, neurologist-prescribed
- On Russia's List of Vital and Essential Medicines (ЖНВЛП — Zhiznenno Neobkhodimye i Vazhneishie Lekarstvennye Preparaty) since approximately 2011 [1, regulatory]
- Manufacturer: JSC "Innovative Research-and-Production Center 'Peptogen'" (Moscow), incorporated 2005 with direct IMG-RAS participation

**Semax 1% intranasal drops (10 mg/mL):**
- Registered indications: acute ischemic stroke (hospital inpatient acute phase), ischemic and traumatic optic neuropathy
- Setting: inpatient, acute neurology unit, neurologist- or hospitalist-prescribed

**Provenance caveat:** These registration facts are **author-reported via the Deigin et al. 2022 narrative review** [1, regulatory], not sourced from a primary GRLS filing or Ministry of Health decree. The GRLS (State Register of Medicines, grls.rosminzdrav.ru) is the primary source but was not directly machine-accessible in English during this research sweep. Regulatory facts here should be treated as well-established within the Russian literature, not independently verified against primary regulatory documents. The Deigin review authors themselves are embedded within Russian state research institutions (Shemyakin-Ovchinnikov Institute of Bioorganic Chemistry, RAS; Sechenov MMSMU) — part of the same apparatus that produced and approved the compound. The independence chain is accordingly disclosed.

**Russia's ЖНВЛП list** is maintained by the **Russian Ministry of Health** as a government health-administration body, not by an independent health-technology-assessment (HTA) agency. ЖНВЛП listing reflects administrative availability and pricing policy; it does not constitute an independent HTA efficacy verdict comparable to NICE (UK), HAS (France), or IQWiG (Germany) appraisals.

**Russia is not an ICH member.** Clinical trials underlying Semax's registration were conducted under Soviet/early-post-Soviet clinical research standards and are not harmonized with ICH E6 GCP. Approval was granted within a system where the inventing institution and the commercial manufacturer are the same legal entity.

### Western and International Regulatory Status

- **United States (FDA):** NOT approved. No NDA, BLA, or IND on record. Semax is not on FDA's 503A Category 1 authorized-for-compounding bulks list. It has not been nominated for or subjected to FDA 503A Category 2 safety review. No lawful US compounding pathway exists.
- **European Union (EMA):** NOT approved. No marketing authorization. No centralized procedure filing on record.
- **United Kingdom (MHRA):** NOT approved. No UK marketing authorization. Classified as a prescription-only medicine (POM) status unclear for import; personal importation sits in regulatory grey zone.
- **Australia (TGA):** NOT approved. No ARTG entry. Import for personal use is possible under Section 19A/personal importation scheme but constitutes unapproved use.
- **Canada (Health Canada):** NOT approved. No DIN or Natural Product Number.

### CIS State Registration

Semax registration has followed the Russian pathway in several CIS jurisdictions. This extends formal regulatory status to parts of the former Soviet Union but does not create a Western approved-drug precedent.

### Russian Pediatric / ADHD Use

No published controlled pediatric clinical trials of Semax were identified in any indexed database (PubMed search for "semax pediatric" returned zero results). The ADHD indication circulating in grey-market discussions derives entirely from a single *Medical Hypotheses* conjecture paper (PMID 16996699, Tsai 2007) [2, mechanism_review] — a published hypothesis with no empirical data, in a conjecture-only journal. There is no Russian-registered pediatric indication for Semax. Any pediatric use mentioned in community contexts is anecdote-only and carries no clinical or regulatory basis.

### N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate (NASA) — Registered Nowhere

NASA (Ac-MEHFPGP-NH₂) is **not approved anywhere in the world** — not in Russia, not in the EU, not in the US, and not in any other jurisdiction. It is a grey-market synthetic analog invented by the Western research-chemical trade, not by IMG-RAS or any regulatory-track program. NASA has no INN, no pharmacopoeial monograph, no regulatory dossier, and no IND application in any jurisdiction. It occupies a US regulatory grey zone: not a DEA-scheduled controlled substance, but not a lawfully marketed drug for human use.

## Clinical Use (Russian-Registered Pathway)

> **Source tagging:** Russian registered dosing regimens are cited as `regulatory` (Tier 2) because they derive from the approved pharmaceutical label and registered clinical study protocols, as reported in the Deigin 2022 review [1, regulatory] and the primary Gusev clinical series [5, open_label; 6, open_label; 7, open_label]. Dose figures from open-label studies are tagged `open_label`. Grey-market doses are not cited — only labeled Russian posology appears here.

### Registered Posology

**Semax 0.1% (1 mg/mL) — Outpatient / Cerebrovascular Indications:**

The outpatient 0.1% formulation is prescribed for cerebrovascular insufficiency, post-stroke rehabilitation, and optic nerve disease in ambulatory patients. Per the registered label (author-reported via Deigin review [1, regulatory]):
- Route: intranasal drops
- Typical adult dose: 2–3 drops per nostril (approximately 200–600 µg total per administration), 2–3 times daily
- Course duration: 5–14 days, repeated as indicated

The largest outpatient clinical cohort (n=187, PMID 15792140, Gusev et al. 2005) used the 0.1% formulation on an unspecified dose schedule consistent with the above range [6, open_label].

**Semax 1% (10 mg/mL) — Acute Stroke / Inpatient Indications:**

The 1% formulation is for acute ischemic stroke and ischemic optic neuropathy in hospital settings:

- Route: intranasal drops
- Dose range in stroke studies: 12–18 mg/day (1,200–1,800 µg/100 µL increments)
  - Moderate severity: 12 mg/day (1.2 mL of 1%) for 5 days [7, open_label]
  - Severe presentations: 18 mg/day for 10 days [7, open_label]
  - Stroke rehabilitation cohort (n=110, Gusev 2018): 6,000 µg/day in divided doses over 10-day courses with 20-day intervals [5, open_label]
- Setting: neurologist-prescribed, typically administered by nursing staff in inpatient neurology unit
- Pre-specified patient populations: acute hemispheric ischemic stroke within 12–72 hours of onset (in the primary registration studies)

**Optic neuropathy clinical use:**
- Semax 0.1% intranasal drops or endonasal electrophoresis (delivery via electrophoretic current through nasal applicator) [8, open_label; 9, open_label]
- Studies reported 3–10 day courses; electrophoresis route shows broader electrophysiological effects across mixed optic pathologies [8, open_label]

### Administration Setting and Prescribing Pathway (Russia)

In Russian clinical practice, Semax is:
- Prescribed by neurologists and ophthalmologists (for optic neuropathy)
- Available at Russian pharmacies by prescription; ЖНВЛП listing means it is subsidized at controlled pricing
- Self-administration of the intranasal drops is technically feasible and some outpatient regimens are self-administered, but the acute stroke and inpatient regimens are hospital-administered
- No therapeutic drug monitoring (lab tests) is required in the Russian clinical protocol; monitoring is clinical (NIHSS, Barthel Index, visual acuity)

### Non-Corticotropic Safety Advantage

Semax's most important mechanism-level safety property is design-intentional and well-established: **it lacks corticotropic activity**. The ACTH(4-7) core does not carry the sequences (ACTH 1-24 domain) responsible for adrenocortical steroidogenesis stimulation. This was confirmed across the foundational non-corticotropic ACTH-fragment literature [10, mechanism_review; 11, mechanism_review] and in Semax-specific preclinical data [12, animal].

Clinical implications:
- No adrenal suppression risk
- No HPA-axis dysregulation from use or discontinuation
- No hypercortisolemia
- No Cushingoid effects
- No rebound adrenal insufficiency on stopping

This distinguishes Semax from full-length ACTH, from corticosteroids, and from any compound whose mechanism involves HPA activation. It is the central pharmacological safety differentiation and it holds across the evidence base. Patients who have experienced adrenal issues with corticosteroids face none of the same structural risk from Semax.

## Critical Hazards (Western Grey-Market / Nootropic Context)

These hazards apply specifically to persons using Semax or NASA outside the Russian-registered clinical pathway — i.e., the actual population purchasing these compounds through grey-market channels. They must be read before the dose/monitoring sections below.

### Hazard 1 — Single-Lineage Evidence: Buyers Are Sold a Nootropic the Independent Evidence Doesn't Validate

**78–95% of all verifiable peer-reviewed publications on Semax carry authorship from the IMG-RAS / Myasoedov cluster or its direct institutional collaborators** (Zakusov Institute, Pirogov University, Lomonosov MSU). Myasoedov NF — Semax's co-inventor — appears as author or co-author on at least 20 core mechanistic studies across 40 years. JSC Peptogen, the commercial manufacturer, was incorporated in 2005 with direct IMG-RAS participation: the science-producer and the commercial beneficiary are the same legal entity [1, regulatory].

**Zero independent non-Russian replication of the BDNF-induction mechanism exists.** Zero randomized placebo-controlled trials exist in any jurisdiction. Zero Western clinical trials exist. The two genuinely independent Western papers that studied Semax directly address copper-chelation chemistry [13, in_vitro] and forensic analytical detection [14, in_vitro] — neither addresses BDNF, neuroprotection, or cognitive pharmacology.

The structural parallel to the BPC-157/Sikiric Zagreb situation is exact: directionally consistent preclinical signal from a single institutional network that has never been independently stress-tested. The preclinical science is real; the independent replication is absent. Western grey-market buyers are purchasing a Russian pharmaceutical whose core efficacy claims have never survived adversarial replication. That is not fraud — it is an unresolved evidentiary gap that the marketing does not adequately disclose.

### Hazard 2 — Population Mismatch: Healthy Adults Are Not the Studied Population

**Every human efficacy and safety data point in the Semax literature derives from elderly Russian patients with cerebrovascular disease, acute ischemic stroke, or optic neuropathy** [5, open_label; 6, open_label; 7, open_label; 8, open_label; 9, open_label]. The youngest major cohort described in available records is not specified; the cerebrovascular insufficiency cohort is described as including "older age groups" with managed vascular risk factors.

This population is categorically different from healthy adults (20s–40s) using self-administered grey-market Semax for cognitive enhancement or nootropic benefit. The differences are not minor:
- Disease-state neurochemistry (ischemia-induced BDNF suppression that Semax may reverse) vs. healthy-adult baseline neurochemistry (where the conditional induction may not operate the same way)
- Pre-existing vascular disease vs. healthy vasculature
- Medical supervision with clinical monitoring vs. self-administration with no oversight
- Russian healthcare setting with AE capture vs. no pharmacovigilance for unapproved Western use

The "well tolerated by patients, including those of older age groups" characterization [6, open_label] describes elderly vascular patients under neurological supervision. It says nothing about healthy adults who lack all three structural protections: disease-baseline context, dose titration by a clinician, and pharmacovigilance infrastructure.

**The neurotrophin induction signal in rodent ischemia models is consistently larger in ischemic tissue than in healthy tissue** [15, animal; 16, animal] — suggesting the mechanism is conditionally activated by pathological state rather than constitutively and equally active in healthy brain. The extrapolation from "helps stroke patients recover" to "enhances healthy adult cognition" rests on an unvalidated assumption that may be contradicted by the biology.

### Hazard 3 — NASA Near-Zero Evidence: A Distinct Compound With No Independent Data

**N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate is a chemically distinct entity from pharmaceutical Semax.** The N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation provide additional exopeptidase resistance at both termini but are not pharmacologically neutral modifications:

- N-terminal acetylation eliminates the free α-amine, altering receptor-binding geometry at any site that recognizes the free N-terminus
- Published investigation shows N-terminal acetylation alters copper(II) coordination geometry (CuN₄ → distorted CuN₃O) and **abolishes the neuroprotective effect against copper-induced cytotoxicity in vitro** [13, in_vitro] — structural consequences illustrating how this modification changes the molecule in pharmacologically significant ways
- Whether N-terminal acetylation analogously disrupts Semax's receptor-binding pharmacology at its (unidentified) CNS binding site is unknown and has not been studied

**Zero peer-reviewed primary studies of any kind — clinical, animal, or pharmacokinetic — exist for NASA as a distinct compound** in any indexed database. Its entire claimed evidence base is parent Semax data extrapolated by assumption of pharmacological equivalence. That assumption is unvalidated and potentially disrupted by the documented structural changes. NASA buyers are purchasing on the basis of an extrapolation that has not been tested and may not hold.

Additionally: NASA is not approved anywhere, including Russia. Under WADA S0, it is almost certainly caught by the non-approved-substance catch-all (see Hazard 5).

### Hazard 4 — Grey-Market Intranasal Hazards: Purity, Sourcing, and No Oversight

Western grey-market Semax and NASA introduce route-specific and supply-chain risks that the Russian pharmaceutical product does not carry:

**Supply-chain risks:**
- Lyophilized powder from unregulated research-chemical vendors is produced under non-GMP solid-phase peptide synthesis conditions; risks include residual coupling reagents (HOBt, DIC, HATU), incomplete deprotection products, incorrect-sequence peptides, aggregates, and microbial/endotoxin contamination
- Pre-mixed nasal sprays from off-whitelist vendors carry unknown sterility, preservative identity, and concentration accuracy; no regulatory authority audits their claims
- Vendor purity COA claims (≥99% HPLC) are self-reported; independent analytical verification does not exist

**Route-specific risks:**
- Intranasal administration delivers any contaminants directly to the olfactory epithelium and cribriform plate — the closest anatomical proximity to the CNS that any route of administration provides; there is no first-pass barrier and no hepatic detoxification before CNS exposure
- The olfactory route is specifically how nose-to-brain delivery works; this is the mechanism that makes intranasal peptides appealing and the same mechanism that makes contamination uniquely hazardous compared to oral routes

**Pharmacovigilance vacuum:**
- Neither Semax nor NASA is approved in Western jurisdictions; no FDA MedWatch or EMA adverse-event system collects reports for unapproved research chemicals
- Any harm from grey-market use is invisible to regulators and therefore does not feed back into safety signal detection
- This means the observed "clean" community reputation for Semax is partially a function of absent pharmacovigilance, not proven safety

### Hazard 5 — WADA Status: Reasoned Inference, Verification Required

**For parent Semax:**
WADA's S0 (Non-Approved Substances) category prohibits any pharmacological substance not approved by **any** governmental regulatory authority for human therapeutic use. Semax is registered in Russia and some CIS states. Whether Russian Roszdravnadzor registration satisfies the WADA S0 threshold is a legal interpretation question requiring individual anti-doping organization (ADO) adjudication — WADA has historically recognized approvals from recognized national medicines agencies, and Roszdravnadzor is such an agency. **Semax may not fall under WADA S0 as a non-approved substance given Russian registration — but athletes cannot assume this and must seek an individual ruling from their ADO or verify via GlobalDRO.com.** The WADA Prohibited List PDF was not directly retrievable for full-text extraction during this research sweep; GlobalDRO.com covers prescription medications but does not cover research chemicals or unlisted compounds specifically.

Semax does not obviously fit WADA S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances) as traditionally defined — it is neuroprotective, not anabolic/endocrine — but BDNF-modulating neuropeptides have not been adjudicated publicly by WADA in accessible documentation.

**For NASA (N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate):**
NASA is not approved anywhere in the world. Under WADA S0, a substance with zero governmental regulatory approval for human therapeutic use is almost certainly caught by the catch-all prohibition. This is the most defensible current read. Athletes subject to anti-doping rules who use NASA should treat it as prohibited in-competition until the contrary is established by a formal WADA/ADO ruling.

**Verification status of this analysis:** The WADA 2026 Prohibited List was not directly extractable during this research sweep. This analysis is based on the known structure of WADA S0 from authoritative anti-doping secondary literature and the Mavrych 2026 Front Aging review [17, mechanism_review]. Athletes must verify directly with GlobalDRO.com and/or their NADO.

## Monitoring Framework (Grey-Market / Experimental Use Context)

> **Source class:** This monitoring framework is rational inference from the mechanism and hazard profile — not a validated clinical protocol. No controlled safety study of Semax in healthy adults exists to ground validated monitoring parameters. The Russian clinical monitoring in the registered studies was clinical (neurological rating scales, visual acuity), not laboratory-based, consistent with the studied disease populations.

No validated clinical monitoring protocol exists for healthy-adult Semax use. The following framework is constructed from: (a) Semax's mechanism (BDNF/NGF induction, melanocortin receptor-mediated signaling, non-corticotropic design), (b) the hazards identified above, and (c) the absence of confirmed healthy-adult safety data.

**Baseline (before starting):**

| Domain | Assessment | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Cognitive baseline | Validated self-report or task battery (e.g., MoCA; digit-span; self-rated attention) | Population-mismatch means healthy-adult cognitive response is unknown; baseline enables individual-level change detection |
| Mood / psychiatric screen | Self-report (PHQ-9, GAD-7 or DASS-21) | Psychosis contraindication in Russian label; dopaminergic behavioral activation in rodents [18, animal]; baseline establishes pre-treatment psychiatric status |
| Nasal mucosa | Self-inspection; note any baseline epistaxis or mucosal sensitivity | Intranasal route; baseline enables attribution of any mucosal irritation to compound |
| Cardiovascular | Resting BP | Cerebrovascular patient populations in studies were all managed for vascular risk; no specific CV signal identified for Semax, but low-cost baseline is prudent |
| HRV (optional) | Wearable baseline if already tracked | Autonomic signal consistent with neuropeptide mechanism; time-series baseline enables change attribution |

**Follow-up (end of first course; days 5–14 depending on regimen):**
- Repeat cognitive task battery / self-rated function
- Repeat mood self-report (PHQ-9, GAD-7)
- Nasal mucosal inspection: irritation, epistaxis, crusting
- BP if baseline was borderline
- Clinical judgment: did subjective benefit occur? Was any adverse effect noted?

**Stopping criteria (rational, not validated):**
- Emergence of psychotic symptoms, hallucinatory states, or acute agitation → discontinue immediately (Russian label contraindication)
- Worsening of any pre-existing psychiatric condition → discontinue immediately
- Unusual anxiety escalation, panic, or sleep disruption not attributable to other causes → discontinue and reassess
- Progressive nasal mucosal irritation, epistaxis, or persistent discomfort → discontinue
- Pregnancy recognition → discontinue immediately (Russian label contraindication)
- Hypersensitivity reaction to any nasal application → discontinue immediately
- Absence of any subjective benefit after two full standard courses → reassess rationale
