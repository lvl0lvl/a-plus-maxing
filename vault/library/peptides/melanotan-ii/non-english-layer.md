# Melanotan-II — Non-English Literature Coverage

Scope: Phase 8 survey of non-English peer-reviewed literature for Melanotan-II (MT-II; Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-NH₂; cyclic melanocortin receptor agonist). Non-English sources are admissible under the same tier rules as English; language is noted on each entry. Every citation below was located via a retrievable identifier (PMID / DOI / stable journal URL). No citation is fabricated.

Key admissibility distinction applied throughout: a study **authored by researchers at a German, Spanish, or Russian institution but published in English** is NOT counted as a "genuinely non-English source" — it is already reachable by the English layer. Only articles whose **primary publication language is German, Spanish, or Russian** are counted here as genuine non-English primaries. Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas publishes bilingually (Spanish-language edition + English translation); for this survey, only the confirmed Spanish-language version (PubMed language tag: Spanish) is counted — the English translation belongs to the English layer.

---

## German (surveyed; citations or explicit "none located" + search terms/databases)

**Result: 3 genuinely German-language admissible primaries located.** All three are PubMed-indexed with confirmed "[Article in German]" language tags, published in German-language medical journals. Two are in *Hautarzt* (Springer; the primary German-language dermatology journal) and one is in *Urologe A* (Springer; German-language urology journal). Together they span the dermatologic safety signal (nevi morphology change, dermoscopic diagnostic difficulty) and the early pharmacological literature (MT-II as an erectile-dysfunction candidate in German urology).

### G1 — Mang R, Krahl D, Assmann T. 2012.

**German Title:** Dermatoskopische Veränderungen von melanozytären Nävi unter Verwendung von Melanotan II

**English translation of title:** Dermoscopic changes in melanocytic nevi during use of Melanotan II

**Journal:** Hautarzt (*Die Dermatologie*; Springer; primary German-language dermatology journal)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2012; 63(11):880–884

**PMID:** 23052015 | **DOI:** 10.1007/s00105-012-2420-1

**Language:** German (PubMed: "[Article in German]"; journal is German-language)

**Study type:** Case report (Kasuistiken)

**Study population:** 1 patient (24-year-old male) who self-administered subcutaneous MT-II obtained online for cosmetic tanning purposes.

**Key findings:** Sequential videodermoscopy was used to document pigmented lesion morphology before and during the MT-II injection course. The patient's melanocytic nevi underwent dermoscopic changes during treatment that created diagnostic difficulty distinguishing benign nevi from melanoma. The authors concluded that non-selective, UV-ungated melanocyte activation by MT-II produces dermoscopic alterations that substantially complicate the clinical differentiation of benign pigmented lesions from malignant ones — a safety-relevant finding for dermatologists encountering patients who have used grey-market tanning peptides.

**Population note:** Single patient (n=1); case report; no control group. Male; phototype not stated. No histological data reported in abstract.

**Translation provenance:** Title translated from confirmed German-language journal article. PubMed abstract rendered in English by NCBI; main text in German; language confirmed by PubMed "[Article in German]" tag and journal identity.

### G2 — Bayerl C. 2015.

**German Title:** Unerwünschte und erwünschte Pigmentierung

**English translation of title:** Undesirable [and desirable] pigmentation

**Journal:** Hautarzt (*Die Dermatologie*; Springer; primary German-language dermatology journal)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2015 Oct; 66(10):757–763

**PMID:** 26315100 | **DOI:** 10.1007/s00105-015-3671-4

**Language:** German (PubMed: "[Article in German]"; journal is German-language)

**Study type:** Review article

**Key melanotan content:** The review examines cosmetically problematic skin discolorations and undesired pigmentation states. In the section addressing pharmacological modulation of pigmentation, the author discusses case reports documenting "activation of dysplastic naevi by melanotan I" — the same dermatologic safety signal documented for MT-II (both compounds share non-selective MC1R agonism as the proposed mechanism). The review is not exclusively about melanotan; melanotan I/II is cited as a causative agent in a broader discussion of undesired melanocytic activation.

**Population note:** Review article; no experimental subjects directly studied. Underlying cited data from case reports and clinical literature.

**Translation provenance:** Title translated from confirmed German-language journal. PubMed abstract rendered in English by NCBI; language confirmed by PubMed "[Article in German]" tag and journal identity.

### G3 — Porst H. 2003.

**German Title:** Therapie der erektilen Dysfunktion im Jahr 2005

**English translation of title:** Therapy of erectile dysfunction in [the year] 2005

**Journal:** Urologe A (Springer; German-language urology journal; now *Die Urologie*)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2003 Oct; 42(10):1330–1336

**PMID:** 14569381 | **DOI:** 10.1007/s00120-003-0418-0

**Language:** German (PubMed: "[Article in German]"; journal is German-language)

**Study type:** Review / therapeutic perspective article

**Key melanotan content:** Written as a forward-looking review of ED pharmacotherapy expected to mature over the subsequent 2–5 years. The abstract explicitly names "intranasal application of the melanocortin agonist PT 141 (Melanotan II)" as a "promising" mechanism-of-action approach alongside guanylate cyclase activators and Rho-kinase inhibitors. This is the earliest German-language medical publication to name MT-II (as PT-141) in the peer-reviewed literature, providing evidence that the compound entered the German-language urology literature as a candidate therapy contemporaneous with the Wessells 1998–2000 English-language RCTs.

**Population note:** Review / perspective; no experimental subjects. Clinical data cited from English-language trial literature.

**Translation provenance:** Title and abstract content translated/summarized from PubMed English abstract (NCBI); main text in German; language confirmed by PubMed "[Article in German]" tag and journal identity.

### German landscape boundary note

Three PubMed-indexed German-language primaries were located. The full PubMed-indexed German-language corpus for "melanotan" in title/abstract is exactly these three articles (confirmed by direct PubMed language-filter query: `melanotan[tiab] AND German[la]` — 3 total results). No additional German-language Melanotan article is present in PubMed.

The grey-market MT-II tanning phenomenon is well documented in Germany and Austria (popular in gym culture under the "Barbie drug" label), but the German-language scientific literature on MT-II is thin: the primary pharmacology and nevi/melanoma case-series literature was published in English-language international journals (JAMA Dermatology, British Journal of Dermatology, JAAD — multiple English case reports by independent groups). The Mang/Krahl/Assmann 2012 *Hautarzt* case report (G1) is the single German-language primary case report on the dermoscopic safety signal; Bayerl 2015 cites the broader literature in a German-language review context (G2); and Porst 2003 captures the early pharmacological mention (G3).

### S1 — Mahiques-Santos L. 2012.

**Spanish Title:** Melanotan

**English title of English-edition parallel:** Melanotan [same title]

**Journal:** Actas Dermosifiliogr (Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas; Elsevier España)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2012 May; 103(4):257–259

**PMID:** 22051769 | **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ad.2011.08.002

**Epub:** 2011 Nov 1

**Language:** Spanish (PubMed language filter confirmed; "[Article in Spanish]")

**Author affiliation:** Servicio de Dermatología, Hospital de Manises, Valencia, Spain

**Study type:** Opinion article (artículo de opinión)

**Key content:** The author reviews the illegal market for melanotan sold via websites and distributed in gym settings under the street name "droga Barbie" (Barbie drug) due to its combined tanning + appetite suppression + sexual function effects. Safety concerns documented include: hypertension, eruptive nevi, potential melanoma risk, and infection hazards from shared syringe use. The author advises Spanish dermatologists to consider MT-II use when patients present with excessive tanning and sudden onset of atypical melanocytic lesions. This is the first Spanish-language medical opinion piece to address the illicit MT-II market for a Spanish-language dermatology readership.

**Population note:** Opinion article; no experimental subjects. Reviews published case literature.

**Translation provenance:** Title confirmed from PubMed Spanish-language record; article language confirmed by PubMed `Spanish[la]` filter and Spanish-URL form on actasdermo.org. The English parallel edition (translated) carries DOI 10.1016/j.adengl.2012.05.003; it is a different document and belongs to the English layer.

### S2 — Hueso-Gabriel L, Mahiques Santos L, Terrádez Mas L, Santonja López N. 2012.

**Spanish Title:** Nevos displásicos eruptivos tras el consumo de Melanotan

**English title of English-edition parallel:** Eruptive Dysplastic Nevi Following Melanotan Use

**Journal:** Actas Dermosifiliogr (Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas; Elsevier España)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2012 May; 103(4):329–331

**PMID:** 22425244 | **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ad.2011.10.001

**Epub:** 2012 Mar 15

**Language:** Spanish (PubMed language filter confirmed; "[Article in Spanish]")

**Study type:** Case report / research letter (Carta científica)

**Study population:** 1 primary case: 25-year-old male construction worker who self-administered subcutaneous MT-II injections for 4 weeks. Post-injection examination revealed >100 melanocytic nevi predominantly on the back, many with atypical clinical and dermoscopic features; the 10 most atypical lesions were excised. Histological diagnoses: all 10 were dysplastic melanocytic nevi (three with severe dysplastic change); one concurrent pigmented basal cell carcinoma was also excised. No histological melanoma in this specific patient; however, the case report references previously published cases of melanoma associated with MT-I/MT-II use (including a 23-year-old patient with a 1-mm Breslow melanoma after MT-I use).

**Key findings:** Sudden eruptive appearance of >100 melanocytic nevi and rapid morphological transformation of pre-existing nevi within weeks of MT-II injection. Dermoscopic atypicality was high enough to prompt surgical excision of 10 lesions; three showed severe dysplasia. The authors identify patients with pre-existing nevi and additional risk factors as particularly vulnerable to melanocytic activation by melanotan. This is the primary Spanish-language case report on the MT-II dermatologic safety signal.

**Population note:** Case report; n=1 primary case (25-year-old male). Fitzpatrick phototype not specified.

**Translation provenance:** Title and study details confirmed from actasdermo.org Spanish-URL record and PubMed Spanish-language metadata. The English parallel edition carries DOI 10.1016/j.adengl.2012.05.002; it is a different document and belongs to the English layer.

## Russian (surveyed; citations or explicit "none located" + search terms/databases)

**Result: 1 genuinely Russian-language admissible primary located.** One PubMed-indexed Russian-language article was found in which MT-II is used as a pharmacological tool compound: a 2015 in vivo mouse study examining brain melanocortin receptors and stress-induced appetite suppression, published in the Russian-language flagship physiology journal. MT-II is not the primary subject of this paper — it serves as an MC receptor agonist in the experimental design — but the article is a genuine Russian-language primary that uses and characterizes MT-II's pharmacological properties. No Russian-language clinical case report, safety report, or dermatologic primary on MT-II was located.

### R1 — Bazhan NM, Kulikova EV, Makarova EN, Yakovleva TV, Kazantseva AYu. 2015.

**Russian Title (from PubMed):** [Studying the role of brain melanocortin receptors in the suppressing of food intake under ether stress in mice]

**Journal:** Российский физиологический журнал им. И.М. Сеченова (*Ross Fiziol Zh Im I M Sechenova* / Russian Physiological Journal named after I.M. Sechenov)

**Year / Volume / Issue / Pages:** 2015; 101(12):1337–1346

**PMID:** 26987226

**Language:** Russian (PubMed language filter: Russian; English abstract available on PubMed; main text in Russian; journal is the Russian-language flagship physiology journal of the Russian Academy of Sciences)

**Study type:** Primary experimental study (in vivo animal model)

**Study population:** Mice; ether stress inhalation model; pharmacological manipulation of central MC receptor activity. Group sizes not recoverable from abstract.

**Compounds studied:** (1) SHU9119 — a synthetic non-selective MC receptor blocker; (2) MT-II — used as the MC receptor agonist comparator. MT-II was employed as an experimental tool to activate central melanocortin receptors and measure effects on stress-induced food intake suppression, not as the compound under safety or efficacy review.

**Key findings:** The central melanocortin system is involved in stress-induced anorexia in mice; pharmacological blockade and activation of brain MC receptors modulated the food intake response to ether stress. MT-II's role is as the positive agonist control, confirming its CNS MC receptor activity in the experimental context.

**MT-II pharmacological role note:** MT-II here confirms its known property as a non-selective MC receptor agonist (consistent with its MC4R-mediated appetite suppression pharmacology described in the English-layer literature, e.g., Wessells 2000 and the Hruby/Hadley mechanistic corpus). The study does not evaluate MT-II for tanning, erectile function, or dermatologic safety — it exploits an established pharmacological property for a different experimental purpose.

**Population note:** Mice; in vivo; ether stress model; no human or clinical subjects.

**Translation provenance:** Title translated from PubMed English-translation metadata; abstract content from PubMed English abstract (NCBI); main text confirmed as Russian-language by journal identity and PubMed language filter; journal *Ross Fiziol Zh Im I M Sechenova* is a Russian-language physiology journal of the Russian Academy of Sciences.

## What the non-English literature adds (honest assessment)

**German (3 admissible — 1 case report + 1 review + 1 therapeutic perspective):**

The single German-language case report (G1, Mang/Krahl/Assmann, *Hautarzt* 2012) is the most substantive contribution: it documents dermoscopic changes in melanocytic nevi during MT-II injection in a 24-year-old male, specifically characterizing the diagnostic difficulty this creates for clinicians — a finding that adds a clinically operational dimension (how do you differentiate a changing nevus from early melanoma?) that the English-layer case reports do not all address explicitly from a dermoscopy-protocol standpoint. The Bayerl 2015 review (G2) confirms that the "activation of dysplastic naevi by melanotan I" signal was incorporated into the German-language dermatology teaching literature by 2015, indicating the safety signal had penetrated the German specialist press. The Porst 2003 article (G3) establishes that MT-II (as PT-141) was under active consideration in the German urology literature as a candidate ED therapy contemporaneous with the English-language Wessells RCTs — evidence that the German medical community tracked this compound's development. Together, the German-language literature demonstrates that the dermatologic safety signal and the pharmacological profile of MT-II were both documented in the German-language specialist press, and that the grey-market penetration in German-speaking Europe was sufficient to generate clinical cases warranting publication.

**Spanish (2 admissible — 1 case report + 1 opinion article):**

Both Spanish-language primaries were published in the same issue of *Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas* (May 2012), suggesting that a cluster of MT-II cases reached Spanish dermatology services around 2011–2012. S2 (Hueso-Gabriel et al.) is the primary case report: it documents the highest absolute nevus count (>100 new or transformed melanocytic lesions) in a single MT-II-exposed patient in the non-English literature, with histological confirmation of severe dysplasia in three excised lesions. The severity of this single case adds to the body of evidence that MT-II's melanocyte-activating effect can produce clinically significant and histologically confirmed dysplastic change. S1 (Mahiques-Santos) is the first Spanish-language opinion piece to name the illicit "droga Barbie" market for a Spanish dermatology audience, establishing the regulatory and clinical context. No histological melanoma was confirmed in the single Spanish case, but the level of dysplasia and the documented >100 nevi are the most extreme clinical presentation found in the non-English literature.

**Russian (1 admissible — animal pharmacology primary):**

The single Russian-language primary (R1, Bazhan et al. 2015) does not add clinical safety or dermatologic signal evidence. Its contribution is mechanistic: it confirms, in a Russian-language peer-reviewed context, that MT-II behaves as an effective central MC receptor agonist in mammals (mice), consistent with the MC4R-mediated appetite suppression and CNS pharmacological properties documented in the English-language literature. The fact that MT-II is used as a standard pharmacological tool compound by a Russian physiology group (Institute of Cytology and Genetics, Russian Academy of Sciences, Novosibirsk) indicates that the compound's pharmacological properties are sufficiently established to serve as experimental infrastructure in non-English academic science — but this does not translate to Russian clinical or dermatologic primaries. The Russian clinical press has not engaged with the dermatologic safety signal.
