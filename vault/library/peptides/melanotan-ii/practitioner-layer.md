# Melanotan-II — Practitioner / Grey-Market Layer

> **Scope discipline.** This document records *how Melanotan-II (MT-II) is actually accessed, dosed, and circulated in practice* — the grey-market dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions that appear in dosing aggregators and research-chemical vendor listings, plus the full regulatory warning landscape. It is **not** an efficacy document. Per the source-tagging rule, `practitioner_protocol` (Tier 2.7), `compounding_data_sheet` (Tier 3), and `vendor_label` / `anecdote_aggregate` (Tier 4) sources are admissible **only** for dose/cycle/route/reconstitution conventions and (for vendor labels) purity/reconstitution math. **None** of these source classes grounds an efficacy, adverse-event-rate, or mechanism claim.
>
> **Regulatory state, read first.** Melanotan-II has **never been approved by the FDA, MHRA, TGA, or EMA for any indication**. Clinical development was abandoned circa 2000 after early human trials. The FDA issued a public warning letter against Melanocorp Inc. in August 2007 for illegal marketing of MT-II. The MHRA has issued repeated public warnings against MT-II as an unlicensed/illegal medicine, closed 72+ UK websites supplying MT-II in a three-month enforcement period, and received 22 adverse drug reaction reports (93 adverse reactions total). The TGA issued 27 infringement notices totalling $101,412 AUD to a NSW-based individual for unlawful supply of MT-II (notices paid May 2026). The two FDA/EMA-approved melanocortin descendants — **afamelanotide/Scenesse** (MC1R-selective; Clinuvel; FDA-approved October 8, 2019, for erythropoietic protoporphyria) and **bremelanotide/Vyleesi** (MC4R-targeted; Palatin; FDA-approved June 21, 2019, for HSDD in premenopausal women) — are **separate approved compounds, NOT MT-II, and are NOT interchangeable with it**. MT-II is not a compoundable 503A bulk drug substance. No legitimate clinical-grade MT-II supply chain exists.
>
> **The "prescribing practice" documented here is an exclusively grey-market, unregulated phenomenon.** No licensed clinician can legally prescribe MT-II in the US, UK, Australia, or EU. The dosing conventions below are anecdote_aggregate and vendor_label tier only; none is validated by a human dose-finding trial.

## Regulatory Landscape (read before everything else)

### FDA (United States)

MT-II is not an FDA-approved drug for any indication. On **August 30, 2007**, the FDA issued a warning letter to **Melanocorp Inc.** (Hendersonville, Tennessee; owner Brian Manookian), citing illegal marketing of MT-II as an unapproved drug and calling on consumers to stop use and contact their healthcare provider if they had experienced adverse events [1, regulatory]. A subsequent **Notice of Opportunity for Hearing** was issued against Edward Manookian on **August 5, 2016**, and a **debarment order** followed on November 14, 2016 (Federal Register Vol. 81 No. 220) [1, regulatory]. The FDA's consumer-directed warning stated: *"There is no evidence that the product is generally recognized as safe and effective."* As of June 2026, MT-II is not listed on any 503A bulk drug substances category. MT-II's clinical development was halted circa 2000 due to unresolved safety concerns; no IND was ever converted to an NDA. MT-II is therefore an **illegal drug product** if sold or marketed for human use in the United States.

**Fetch disclosure:** The FDA consumer article URL that previously hosted "Striking Back at Tanning Injections" returned HTTP 404 as of June 2026 — the content is no longer accessible at that URL. The 2007 warning letter facts are sourced instead from contemporaneous reporting (UPI, Drug Topics) indexed at the time of issuance [1, regulatory].

### MHRA (United Kingdom)

The MHRA considers MT-II a medicinal product that requires a UK marketing authorisation; no such authorisation exists. The MHRA has:

- Issued **repeated public warnings** against Melanotan products as unlicensed medicines carrying unknown health risks [2, regulatory]
- Closed **72+ UK websites** supplying MT-II within a single three-month enforcement period; 100+ websites suspended over a 12-month period in total [2, regulatory]
- Confirmed awareness of MT-II being sold through gyms, beauty salons, and the internet [2, regulatory]
- Received **22 adverse drug reaction reports** describing 93 adverse reactions associated with MT-II use as of June 2014 [2, regulatory]
- Spent, by its own account, **10 years** targeting MT-II products through enforcement [7, anecdote_aggregate]

**Fetch disclosure:** The MHRA blog URL (2012 unlicensed-medicine notice) returned HTTP 404 as of June 2026. Facts are sourced from Reactions Weekly (2008), a pharmaceutical news aggregator that documented the MHRA advisory contemporaneously, and from subsequent enforcement reporting [2, regulatory].

### TGA (Australia)

MT-II is classified as a prescription-only medicine in Australia but is **not on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG)** and is not approved for supply as a tanning agent or for any other indication. Key enforcement:

- **January 2023:** TGA issued a public consumer warning urging Australians to avoid MT-II, citing serious adverse effects and stating that "its development as a potential medicine was halted some years ago due to safety reasons" [3, regulatory]
- **May 2026:** TGA issued **27 infringement notices totalling $101,412 AUD** to a NSW-based individual for the alleged unlawful supply of MT-II to Australian consumers, in contravention of the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989. Notices were paid in May 2026 [3, regulatory]
- In 2023–24, TGA requested removal of **4,800+ unlawful advertisements** of therapeutic goods from digital platforms, including MT-II-related content [3, regulatory]

### EMA (European Union / broader)

MT-II has not received EMA marketing authorisation for any indication. MT-II is neither listed nor authorized for clinical use in any EU member state. The research programme that preceded MT-II (University of Arizona, MAC Hadley / Victor Hruby group, 1980s–1990s) generated early Phase I/II data; none was submitted for regulatory approval [12, regulatory].

### WADA (Sport / Anti-Doping)

MT-II is **not explicitly named** on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List [4, regulatory]. As a non-approved peptide that mimics α-MSH, it **may** fall under the **S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics)** catch-all — which covers substances of similar chemical structure or biological effect to named S2 compounds — but this is **not confirmed** by direct retrieval of the S2 subcategory text. Athletes subject to anti-doping testing **MUST verify MT-II's status directly with their National Anti-Doping Organization (NADO) or the WADA list** before any use; do not assume either prohibition or permission.

## The Two Approved Descendants — NOT MT-II

MT-II spawned two clinically approved compounds via separate derivative programs. These are **distinct chemical entities** with different receptor selectivity, indication, manufacturer, approval status, and safety profile from MT-II:

| Compound | Brand | Sponsor | Receptor | FDA Approval | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Afamelanotide (Melanotan I) | Scenesse | Clinuvel Pharmaceuticals | Selective MC1R agonist | October 8, 2019 | Erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP) — prevent phototoxicity |
| Bremelanotide (PT-141) | Vyleesi | Palatin Technologies | MC4R-targeted (cyclic MT-II analog) | June 21, 2019 | Hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women |
| **Melanotan-II (MT-II)** | **None** | **None (abandoned ~2000)** | **Non-selective MC1–5R** | **NEVER** | **No approved indication** |

The critical sourcing hazard: **grey-market nasal sprays sold as "MT-I," "MT-II," "Melanotan," "tan nasal spray," or "PT-141" may contain any or none of these compounds**, and the "MT-I vs MT-II vs PT-141" naming confusion is a documented safety hazard in the unregulated market (see Safety section below) [5, anecdote_aggregate].

## Grey-Market Reality

MT-II occupies a large, active unregulated market as of mid-2026:

**Products and routes in circulation:**

- **Injectable lyophilized powder** (10 mg and 5 mg vials): the original grey-market form; reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and self-injected SC. Dominant in bodybuilding and tanning subcultures since at least the early 2000s.
- **Nasal spray ("tanning nasal spray" / "Barbie drug"):** gained major traction from TikTok and Instagram promotion circa 2022–2025 despite platform bans on #melanotan, #melanotan2, and #tanningnasalspray. Bioavailability is substantially lower than SC injection (~30–40% vs ~100%); marketed doses are therefore higher on a per-spray basis. Nasal sprays from unlicensed suppliers are especially prone to identity uncertainty — tested samples have contained unidentified ingredients, variable actual MT-II content, and microbial contaminants [7, anecdote_aggregate] [8, open_label].
- **Research-chemical vendor network ("not for human use"):** operates under a legal fiction of research use only. The Channel 4 documentary *Untold: Deadly Diet Drugs* (2024) profiled active users, including a 21-year-old bodybuilder using MT-II for one year, demonstrating the disconnect between "research use only" labeling and actual consumer use [7, anecdote_aggregate].
- The "Barbie drug" label (combining MT-II's appetite-suppression + tanning + sexual effects) is a documented social media framing that conflates slimming-drug marketing with tanning-drug marketing; it is not a clinical term [7, anecdote_aggregate].

**Product identity hazard:** TGA testing found MT-II products containing "over 100 unidentified ingredients alongside melanotan II" [8, open_label]. The MT-I / MT-II / PT-141 naming confusion is active: a product labeled "MT-2 nasal spray" may contain MT-I (afamelanotide), bremelanotide (Vyleesi), PT-141, or a synthetic analog that is none of the above. No reliable chemical identity standard exists in the unregulated supply chain.

## Critical Safety and Sourcing Hazards

### 1. Dermatologic Melanoma Risk (the practitioner imperative)

The most serious grey-market safety gap is that **virtually no consumer using MT-II from grey-market sources undergoes baseline mole-mapping or dermoscopy before starting**. This is the operational equivalent of injecting a potent melanocyte stimulant with no photographic or dermoscopic baseline.

**Published case record:**

- **Sivyer GW (2012):** 16-year-old female with FAMMM (Familial Atypical Mole and Malignant Melanoma) syndrome. Self-injected MT-II 0.5 mg/day SC for 2 months (needle obtained from a local needle-exchange program). Nevus in left groin (14 × 8 mm) darkened and enlarged within 3 weeks of starting MT-II; dermoscopy showed "4 colors, black dots, and central blue-white structures." Histopathology: dysplastic compound nevus with moderate cytoarchitectural atypia; surgically removed. *Dermatology Practical & Conceptual* 2012; 2(3):57. DOI: 10.5826/dpc.0203a10. PMID: 23785612. [9, open_label]

- **Paurobally et al. (2011):** Melanotan-associated melanoma; MT-I exposure (not MT-II) but demonstrates the class signal. *British Journal of Dermatology* 2011; 164(6):1403. DOI: 10.1111/j.1365-2133.2011.10273.x. [10, open_label] *(Note: access restricted; citation from indexed abstract and search summary.)*

- **Eruptive dysplastic nevi case (Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas, 2012):** 25-year-old male; eruptive multiple melanocytic nevi and rapid nevus transformation occurring weeks after a 4-week MT-II SC course; histopathology confirmed dysplastic melanocytic nevi with severe dysplasia. *Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas* 2012; DOI: 10.1016/j.ad.2012.02.009. [10, open_label]

- **Cairns R (The Conversation, 2025):** *"Melanotan-II use has been linked with melanoma developing from existing moles"* during or shortly after use. Dr. Rose Cairns, Senior Lecturer in Pharmacy, University of Sydney; published January 16, 2025. [8, open_label] (used here to establish the established-concern framing, not as a primary source)

**The practitioner imperative** (if any clinician is ever evaluating a patient using grey-market MT-II):

1. **Full-skin exam + dermoscopy BEFORE any further MT-II use** — establish photographic baseline of all nevi
2. **Stop on any changing nevus** — rapid darkening, enlargement, irregular border, or new dermoscopic features mandate immediate cessation and dermatology referral
3. **High-risk patients are absolutely contraindicated:** personal or family history of melanoma, FAMMM syndrome, multiple atypical nevi, Fitzpatrick type I or II skin, heavy UV tanning use concurrent with MT-II (the case series includes a patient who used both MT-II SC and UV tanning bed sessions)
4. **Serial dermoscopy at minimum 4-week intervals** if a patient refuses to stop — this is NOT a validated protocol; it is a harm-reduction minimum given the zero-monitoring status of the current grey-market

### 2. MT-I vs MT-II vs PT-141: The Three-Compound Confusion

This is an active sourcing hazard that can route consumers into unexpected pharmacological territory:

| Compound | Structure | Receptor Profile | Approval Status | Primary Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MT-I (afamelanotide) | Linear | Selective MC1R | FDA-approved (Scenesse) for EPP | Pigmentation (minimal erectile effect) |
| MT-II | Cyclic | Non-selective MC1–5R | **Never approved** | Tanning + erection + nausea + autonomic |
| PT-141 (bremelanotide) | Cyclic N-acetyl MT-II analog | MC4R-targeted | FDA-approved (Vyleesi) for HSDD | Sexual desire (in premenopausal women) |

The dangers of the confusion:

- A consumer buying a "tanning nasal spray" expecting MC1R-mediated pigmentation may be receiving a non-selective MC1–5R compound producing priapism, severe hypertension, and nausea (MT-II profile)
- A consumer buying "PT-141 for libido" may receive MT-II, which differs in receptor distribution and has a materially different safety profile
- Product labels in the unregulated market are unreliable: MT-II can be sold as MT-1, MT-2, "Melanotan," "tan peptide," or (falsely) as PT-141 [5, anecdote_aggregate]
- Nasal sprays have especially poor identity reliability: one tested spray contained 100+ unidentified ingredients alongside the stated compound [8, open_label]

### 3. Idiosyncratic Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)

**Priapism:** Documented in published case reports; mechanism is MC4R agonism in the hypothalamic–spinal axis, producing reflexogenic and psychogenic erections. Sustained priapism (>4 hours) is a urological emergency requiring decompression. Anecdotally, MT-II's erectile effect is described in community sources as "spontaneous" and dose-dependent; clinical case reports confirm ischemic priapism managed with surgical decompression. [11, open_label]

**Rhabdomyolysis + renal failure:** MT-II injection resulting in systemic toxicity and rhabdomyolysis is documented in case reports in the peer-reviewed literature (Reactions Weekly 2013; see also ResearchGate: "Melanotan II injection resulting in systemic toxicity and rhabdomyolysis"). Mechanism uncertain — possibly vasoconstrictive cascade causing muscle ischemia. [11, open_label]

**Renal infarction:** Peters B, Hadimeri H, Wahlberg R, Afghahi H (2020). 45-year-old male, no prior renal history; received 27 mg MT-II SC over 6 months; presented with right-sided abdominal pain and fever; CT showed right-sided renal infarction affecting ~50% of the kidney; persistent hypertension requiring ACE-inhibitor at 2-month follow-up (eGFR 81 mL/min per 1.73 m²). *CEN Case Reports* 2020. DOI: 10.1007/s13730-020-00447-z. PMID: 31953620. Authors describe this as the **first reported case of MT-II-associated renal infarction**. [11, open_label]

**PRES (Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome):** Documented in published correspondence in *Annals of Internal Medicine* (2013; 158(9)): MT-II use associated with PRES presentation (seizures, visual disturbances, headache, bilateral posterior white-matter changes on MRI). [11, open_label] *(Direct access to the AIM article was restricted (403); citation from indexed abstract and search summary.)*

**Hypertensive crisis:** 2019 British Journal of Dermatology case: 28-year-old male; systolic BP 210 mmHg, heart rate 142 bpm, chest pain 90 minutes after 1 mg MT-II SC injection; no prior hypertension history. [11, open_label]

**Endocrine disruption / DKA precipitant:** Aung HH, Ko Ko E, Fernandez C (2025). 39-year-old male with type 1 DM; MT-II 2 mg SC once weekly for 2–3 weeks seasonally; 5 days post-last-injection: severe hyperglycemia (capillary BGL 41 mmol/L), ketones 4.2 mmol/L, BP 176/107 mmHg, cortisol >1,750 nmol/L (normalized within 3 days), hyponatremia (Na 128 mmol/L). Outcome: impending DKA averted; symptoms resolved on discontinuation. *British Journal of Diabetes* 2025; 25(1):18–21. [12, open_label] *(Directly accessed and verified.)*

**Common dose-dependent effects (not SAEs, but clinically relevant):**

- Nausea / vomiting / abdominal cramps (dominant, dose-related, peaks days 1–3 of loading)
- Facial flushing
- Appetite suppression / weight loss (the "Barbie drug" combined-effect framing)
- Spontaneous erections in males
- Darkening of nevi and skin / nails
- Yawning

### 4. Nausea Is the Primary Dose-Limiter in Grey-Market Use

The most common reason for dose modification in the grey-market population is nausea, not a safety signal. Community protocols layer "slow escalation" + "inject before bed" + "empty/light stomach" as mitigation, not as evidence-based management [5, anecdote_aggregate] [13, anecdote_aggregate].

## Grey-Market Dose / Cycle / Route Conventions

There is **no human dose-finding trial for MT-II in any tanning, sexual function, or other indication that has been published with sufficient Phase 2 granularity to generate a validated dose range**. The few early Phase I/II studies (Dorr et al., 1996, tanning in n=3; Wessells et al., 1998, erectile in n=10) were small, single-site, and abandoned. Every figure below is a grey-market convention with no clinical validation. Source tags strictly applied.

### Convention 1 — Garret Grant (peptidedosingprotocols.com), June 2026

Author: Garret Grant, Founder and Lead Researcher, peptidedosingprotocols.com. Last updated: June 2026. Explicit disclaimer: *"Not FDA-approved. Illegal to sell for human use in the US, UK, and Australia. This page is an educational research reference. It is not medical advice."* [13, anecdote_aggregate]

**Loading phase:**

| Days | Daily dose |
|------|-----------|
| 1–3 | 100–250 mcg |
| 4–14 | 250–500 mcg |
| 15–42 (Weeks 3–6) | 500–1,000 mcg |

**Tanning maintenance:** 500–1,000 mcg once or twice weekly after pigmentation goal achieved.

**Sexual function (as-needed):** 500 mcg–1 mg SC, 1–4 hours before desired activity; effects reported 1–4 hours post-injection and lasting 6–12 hours or longer.

**Reconstitution:** 10 mg vial + 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water → 5 mg/mL; 500 mcg dose = 0.10 mL = 10 units on U-100 insulin syringe.

**Nasal spray note:** intranasal route described as "less predictable"; SC injection cited as "the most studied route."

**Monitoring guidance in this source:** mole photo-documentation before starting; re-photograph every 2–4 weeks; "melanoma case reports link MT-2 use to mole changes and melanoma diagnoses."

### Convention 2 — PeptideWiki Editorial Team (peptidewiki.co), February 2026

Author: PeptideWiki Editorial Team. Last updated: February 24, 2026. [5, anecdote_aggregate]

**Loading phase:** 0.25 mg once daily (before bed) for 2–4 weeks as starting dose; standard escalation to 0.5 mg once daily for 2–3 weeks.

**Maintenance:** 0.5–1 mg once or twice per week; some users dose as-needed before UV exposure.

**Tanning protocol:** inject before bed on empty/light stomach; 10–20 minutes natural sun or low-intensity tanning within 24 hours post-injection; visible darkening within 7–14 days; full tan at 3–4 weeks.

**Sexual use:** 0.5–1 mg SC, 1–4 hours before sexual activity.

**Reconstitution:** 10 mg vial + 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water → 5 mg/mL; doses: 0.25 mg = 5 U, 0.5 mg = 10 U, 1 mg = 20 U on insulin syringe.

**Cycle:** no fixed end-date; "8 weeks on / 4 weeks off" referenced as a common cycling convention.

**Contraindications listed in this source:** melanoma history, atypical moles, pregnancy, active skin cancer, uncontrolled hypertension, Addison's disease.

### Convention convergence and divergence

Both conventions use a **loading phase + maintenance** structure. Dose units differ slightly by naming convention (Convention 1 uses mcg notation, Convention 2 uses mg notation — same numbers). They agree on:
- SC route preferred
- Reconstitution: 10 mg + 2.0 mL → 5 mg/mL
- As-needed sexual function use: 0.5–1 mg, 1–4 hours pre-activity
- Inject before bed to reduce nausea

No dose above is validated by a human dose-finding trial. Convention documents are provided for pharmacovigilance and harm-reduction awareness purposes only.

## Reconstitution / Route Notes (Admin Math Only)

Reconstitution math is **Tier-4 vendor / aggregator; grounds no efficacy or safety claim.**

| Vial | + Bac. water | Concentration | Dose examples | U-100 insulin syringe |
|------|-------------|---------------|---------------|-----------------------|
| 10 mg | 2.0 mL | 5.0 mg/mL | 0.25 mg | 5 U (0.05 mL) |
| 10 mg | 2.0 mL | 5.0 mg/mL | 0.5 mg | 10 U (0.10 mL) |
| 10 mg | 2.0 mL | 5.0 mg/mL | 1.0 mg | 20 U (0.20 mL) |

- **Reconstitution technique:** alcohol swab stopper; inject bacteriostatic water slowly down vial wall; swirl gently (do not shake)
- **Storage:** lyophilized powder: −20 °C; reconstituted: 2–8 °C, use within 7–14 days (conservative end preferred given contamination risk in unregulated supply chain)
- **Nasal spray route:** lower bioavailability (~30–40% vs ~100% SC); identity reliability is lower in nasal spray format (documented contamination / unidentified ingredients); not recommended for any rational clinical evaluation
- **Syringe:** U-100 insulin syringe (27–31G, 0.5–1.0 mL)
