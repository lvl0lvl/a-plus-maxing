# Triglycerides — Canonical Research Report

## Summary

Triglycerides (TG) are the principal dietary and stored fat, transported in plasma packaged inside triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (TRLs) — chylomicrons of intestinal origin and very-low-density lipoproteins (VLDL) of hepatic origin [1, mechanism_review]. The single most important interpretive fact about a plasma TG value is that the TG molecule itself is not directly atherogenic; it is hydrolyzed by lipoprotein lipase (LpL) and used for energy, while it is the cholesterol carried within the TRL remnants left behind that drives atherosclerosis [1, mechanism_review]. A TG measurement is therefore best read as a readily measured **marker** of the concentration of atherogenic remnant cholesterol — the cholesterol cargo **mediates** the disease, the TG number **marks** it. This single distinction governs the entire interpretation that follows.

Observationally, top-tertile TG carries an adjusted odds ratio of 1.72 (95% CI 1.56–1.90) for coronary heart disease (CHD), but the association attenuates substantially after adjustment for established risk factors because TG travels with HDL-C, LDL-C, adiposity, and insulin resistance [8, meta_analysis]. Causality is therefore established not by observation but by genetics: Mendelian randomization (MR) on the APOA5 −1131T>C variant gives a per-allele CHD odds ratio of 1.18 (95% CI 1.11–1.26) concordant with the observational estimate [7, meta_analysis], genetically elevated remnant cholesterol gives a causal risk ratio of 3.3 (95% CI 2.1–5.2) per 1 mmol/L for ischemic heart disease [2, cohort], and the mirror-image experiment — APOC3 loss-of-function carriers with 39% lower TG — gives a 40% lower CHD risk (OR 0.60, 95% CI 0.47–0.75) [9, meta_analysis]. Raising TRLs raises risk; lowering them lowers risk; the cholesterol cargo is the consistent mediator.

A second, mechanistically **separate** domain attaches to very-high TG: acute pancreatitis. This is a chylomicron-mass problem (LpL clearance saturated, chylomicrons accumulate), not a remnant-cholesterol problem, and the risk rises markedly above ~1000–2000 mg/dL [4, mechanism_review]. The two domains must not be conflated: mild-to-moderate TG elevation is a cardiovascular (remnant) signal, whereas severe and very-severe TG elevation is a pancreatitis (chylomicronemia) signal.

The fasting reference bands (NCEP ATP III) are normal <150 mg/dL, borderline-high 150–199, high 200–499, very high ≥500 mg/dL [10, mechanism_review][11, mechanism_review][18, mechanism_review]. Non-fasting sampling is increasingly the default, with abnormal non-fasting TG flagged at ≥175 mg/dL (≥2 mmol/L) by the EAS/EFLM consensus [3, mechanism_review]. mg/dL is converted to mmol/L by dividing by 88.5 [10, mechanism_review].

## Identity & Physiology

**TG-rich lipoproteins.** Triglycerides do not circulate as free molecules; they are packaged inside TRLs and ferried through plasma [1, mechanism_review]. The two TRL classes differ by origin: chylomicrons are intestinally derived and carry dietary fat into the circulation after a meal, while VLDL is hepatically derived and carries endogenously synthesized fat [1, mechanism_review]. Each TRL particle carries a single apolipoprotein B molecule (apoB-48 on chylomicrons, apoB-100 on VLDL), so TRLs are part of the larger apoB-particle population that also includes IDL, LDL, and Lp(a).

**Lipolysis and the birth of remnants.** Lipoprotein lipase, anchored at the capillary endothelium, hydrolyzes the triglyceride core of TRLs, progressively shrinking these particles into smaller, cholesterol-enriched **remnants** [1, mechanism_review]. As lipolysis proceeds, the remnant becomes proportionally depleted of TG and enriched in cholesterol — VLDL remnants in particular are "smaller and more cholesterol-rich" after lipolysis, with prolonged circulation and enhanced arterial-wall penetration [1, mechanism_review]. The remnant is thus the metabolic product of the TG-clearance machinery doing its job; it is what is left behind when the energy-bearing triglyceride has been removed.

**Why remnants are atherogenic.** Remnant particles are small enough to enter and be retained in the arterial intima, and per particle they can deliver "up to 4 times more cholesterol to the arterial wall than LDL particles" [1, mechanism_review]. This is the physical basis of the marker/mediator distinction: the cholesterol carried within the retained remnant is deposited in the arterial wall, while the triglyceride that gave the particle its name has already been hydrolyzed away. A plasma TG value indexes the size of the circulating TRL/remnant pool but is not itself the toxic species.

**The central distinction — remnant cholesterol mediates, TG marks.** The pivotal physiological point for interpreting any TG value is that the TG molecule itself is not directly atherogenic, whereas the cholesterol carried within TRL remnants drives atherogenesis through cholesterol deposition in the arterial wall [1, mechanism_review]. A plasma TG measurement is therefore best read as a marker of the concentration of atherogenic remnant cholesterol, not as a directly causal toxin. This framing — remnant cholesterol as mediator, TG as the readily measured surrogate — is the honest description of the causal chain and is preserved throughout this report.

## Causal Evidence

The causal case for TRLs/remnants rests on the contrast between weak, confounded observation and strong, unconfounded genetics.

**Observational association.** Across 29 Western prospective studies (10,158 incident CHD cases among 262,525 participants), individuals in the top third of usual log-triglycerides had an adjusted odds ratio for CHD of 1.72 (95% CI 1.56–1.90) versus the bottom third; the effect was "substantially attenuated" after adjustment for established risk factors (e.g., OR 1.76 [95% CI 1.39–2.21] in Reykjavik, 1.57 [95% CI 1.10–2.24] in EPIC-Norfolk) [8, meta_analysis]. The attenuation under adjustment is precisely why observational data alone cannot settle causality — TG travels with HDL-C, LDL-C, adiposity, and insulin resistance — and is the motivation for the genetic evidence below.

**Mendelian randomization — APOA5.** Genetic instruments break the confounding by exploiting variants that randomly perturb lifelong TRL/remnant levels. The APOA5 −1131T>C variant (rs662799) raises TG by 16.0% (95% CI 12.9–18.7), or 0.25 mmol/L (0.20–0.29), per C allele (p=4.4×10⁻²⁴), while being unassociated with LDL-C or most non-lipid risk factors [7, meta_analysis]. The per-allele CHD odds ratio was 1.18 (95% CI 1.11–1.26; p=2.6×10⁻⁷), concordant with the observational hazard ratio of 1.10 (95% CI 1.08–1.12) per 16% higher TG [7, meta_analysis] — the genetic and observational estimates agreeing, which is the signature of a causal relationship. The variant also raised VLDL particle concentration (mean difference 12.2 nmol/L per C allele, 95% CI 7.7–16.7; p=9.3×10⁻⁸) and reduced HDL particle size, a plausible mediating chain [7, meta_analysis]. The instrument drew on a genetic comparison of 20,842 CHD patients and 35,206 controls plus an observational arm of 302,430 participants with 12,785 incident CHD cases over 2.79 million person-years [7, meta_analysis].

**A broad genetic test — 185 lipid variants.** In a model accounting for each variant's effect on LDL-C and/or HDL-C, the strength of a polymorphism's effect on TG correlated with the magnitude of its effect on CHD risk, supporting that TRLs causally influence CHD independently of LDL-C and HDL-C [6, cohort]. (The paper's precise per-SD odds ratio lives in the paywalled body; the abstract supports the qualitative causal conclusion and the 185-variant design, which is what is cited here. The quantitative causal anchors are APOA5 [7] and remnant cholesterol [2].)

**Remnant cholesterol, genetically elevated.** In three Copenhagen cohorts (60,608 individuals, 10,668 IHD cases, 1977–2011), genetically elevated remnant cholesterol carried a causal risk ratio for ischemic heart disease of 3.3 (95% CI 2.1–5.2) per 1 mmol/L, alongside a causal 28% higher C-reactive protein (95% CI 10–48) [2, cohort]. The comparison is instructive: elevated LDL-C in the same study causes IHD (causal risk ratio 1.8, 95% CI 1.5–2.2 per 1 mmol/L) but **without** any causal CRP association [2, cohort]. Remnant cholesterol therefore causes both low-grade inflammation **and** IHD, whereas LDL-C causes IHD without inflammation — a dual mechanism (lipid loading plus inflammation) specific to the remnant pathway [2, cohort].

**APOC3 loss-of-function — the mirror-image experiment.** Carriers of rare APOC3 loss-of-function mutations (498 carriers vs 110,472 noncarriers, ~1 in 150 persons) had triglyceride levels 39% lower than noncarriers (p<1×10⁻²⁰) and circulating APOC3 46% lower (p=8×10⁻¹⁰), with a 40% lower CHD risk (odds ratio 0.60, 95% CI 0.47–0.75; p=4×10⁻⁶) [9, meta_analysis]. apoC-III is an LpL inhibitor, so loss-of-function carriers clear TRLs faster; genetically lowering TRLs lowers disease, exactly mirroring the APOA5 result in which raising TRLs raises disease.

**TRL cholesterol content, measured directly.** In the Women's Health Study (480 cases), top-quartile TRL-cholesterol carried a hazard ratio of 3.05 (95% CI 1.46–6.39) for myocardial infarction, independent of LDL-C and hsCRP [5, cohort] — confirming that it is the cholesterol *in* the remnant, not the triglyceride, that is the atherogenic quantity.

**Synthesis.** Raising TRLs (APOA5, remnant-cholesterol alleles) raises risk; lowering them (APOC3 loss-of-function) lowers risk; the cholesterol cargo is the consistent mediator across every experiment. The TG number is a surrogate for that pool, which is why it predicts risk and why interventions that lower it can reduce events — but the honest causal statement is about remnant cholesterol, not triglyceride per se.

**The residual-risk concept.** Even with LDL-C well controlled on statins, "significant residual risk persists," and remnant cholesterol is a leading contributor to this residual cardiovascular risk independent of LDL-C management — the rationale for measuring and addressing TRLs after LDL-C targets are met [1, mechanism_review].

## Reference Ranges & Units

**Fasting bands (NCEP ATP III).** The reference classification of fasting serum/plasma TG derives from the U.S. National Cholesterol Education Program (NCEP) Adult Treatment Panel III (ATP III) and is echoed across society guidelines. The four fasting bands are: normal <150 mg/dL; borderline-high 150–199 mg/dL; high 200–499 mg/dL; very high ≥500 mg/dL [10, mechanism_review][11, mechanism_review][18, mechanism_review]. "Desirable" fasting TG is <150 mg/dL [11, mechanism_review], and the 2011 AHA scientific statement additionally designates an **optimal** fasting band of <100 mg/dL [18, mechanism_review].

**A finer scheme (Endocrine Society 2012).** The Endocrine Society uses a finer diagnostic stratification: normal <150 mg/dL (<1.7 mmol/L); mild 150–199 (1.7–2.3); moderate 200–999 (2.3–11.2); severe 1000–1999 (11.2–22.4); very severe ≥2000 mg/dL (≥22.4 mmol/L) [4, mechanism_review]. The "moderate" upper bound (999) differs from ATP III's "high" upper bound (499); these are distinct schemes reported separately, not merged. The Endocrine Society's broader "moderate" band reflects its focus on the pancreatitis gradient, where the clinically meaningful break is at the severe/very-severe transition rather than at 500.

**Pancreatitis thresholds.** Acute-pancreatitis risk rises with TG and is documented as increased at ≥500 mg/dL, becoming clinically significant at ≥880 mg/dL (~10 mmol/L) and dramatically elevated above 1500–2000 mg/dL [10, mechanism_review][11, mechanism_review]; between 12% and 38% of acute-pancreatitis patients present with TG ≥1000 mg/dL [10, mechanism_review]. Pancreatitis can usually be prevented by keeping TG below 250–500 mg/dL [11, mechanism_review], and the Endocrine Society states it can be prevented by maintaining TG below 1000 mg/dL [4, mechanism_review].

**Non-fasting ranges.** Non-fasting sampling is increasingly the default. The AHA designates a non-fasting TG of ≥200 mg/dL as the trigger for a confirmatory fasting panel within ~2–4 weeks [18, mechanism_review], while the EAS/EFLM 2016 joint consensus flags non-fasting TG as abnormal at ≥2 mmol/L (175 mg/dL) and fasting TG as abnormal at ≥1.7 mmol/L (150 mg/dL) [3, mechanism_review]. A Women's Health Study analysis derived an optimal non-fasting cutpoint of 175 mg/dL (1.98 mmol/L), statistically superior (c-statistic 0.656) to the AHA 200 mg/dL value (0.628) [19, cohort].

**Unit conversion.** Divide mg/dL by 88.5 to obtain mmol/L; equivalently, a 1 mmol/L change in TG ≈ 88.5 mg/dL [10, mechanism_review]. Thus 150 mg/dL ≈ 1.7 mmol/L, 175 ≈ 2.0, 500 ≈ 5.6, and 880 ≈ 10 mmol/L [10, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review]. (The factor differs from the cholesterol conversion of 38.67 because triglyceride's molar mass reflects three fatty-acyl chains on a glycerol backbone.)

## Measurement

**Fasting vs non-fasting biology (postprandial lipemia).** After a habitual meal, TRLs rise as dietary chylomicrons enter the circulation — but the maximal mean change in TG at 1–6 h was judged clinically insignificant at only +0.3 mmol/L (26 mg/dL) [3, mechanism_review]. Consequently, the EAS/EFLM consensus recommends non-fasting lipid profiles for most patients, flags abnormal non-fasting TG at ≥2 mmol/L (175 mg/dL), and reserves a repeat fasting sample for non-fasting TG >5 mmol/L (440 mg/dL) [3, mechanism_review]. Non-fasting measures predict cardiovascular risk at least as well as fasting [3, mechanism_review] — biologically sensible, since postprandial remnants are precisely the atherogenic species the test aims to capture. The AHA's threshold for triggering a confirmatory fasting draw is the higher value of non-fasting ≥200 mg/dL [18, mechanism_review]; clinicians choosing between guidelines should note the EAS/EFLM (175 mg/dL) and AHA (200 mg/dL) cutpoints differ.

**Assay chemistry.** TG are quantified by an automated enzymatic (GPO-PAP) assay: lipase hydrolyzes TG to glycerol plus free fatty acids; glycerol kinase phosphorylates glycerol to glycerol-3-phosphate; glycerophosphate oxidase (GPO) oxidizes it to hydrogen peroxide; and peroxidase couples the H₂O₂ with 4-aminoantipyrine and a phenol to form a red Trinder chromogen whose absorbance is proportional to TG concentration [16, mechanism_review]. Because the assay measures the glycerol backbone, endogenous **free glycerol** overestimates TG unless removed by a blanking pre-incubation with GPO plus peroxidase [16, mechanism_review].

**High biological variability.** TG show the largest within-individual variation of the standard lipid panel — within-individual CV ≈ 25% (between-individual CV ≈ 35.9%) in healthy adults [13, cohort], with reported biological-fluctuation CV ranging ~5–25% and total intra-individual variability ~24% [13, cohort]; TG variability is at least double that of total-, HDL-, and LDL-cholesterol [13, cohort]. This high day-to-day variability — distinct from the small postprandial shift — is the practical reason a single elevated TG should be confirmed on a repeat draw before treatment decisions, and the reason trend matters more than any single value.

## Determinants

**Diet (major, modifiable).** Fructose (from sucrose and high-fructose corn syrup) and refined carbohydrate raise hepatic de-novo lipogenesis via SREBP1c/ChREBP, increase TG-rich VLDL secretion, and upregulate APOC3/apoC-III (an LpL inhibitor) — yielding hypertriglyceridemia and atherogenic remnants [17, mechanism_review]. Alcohol increases hepatic large-VLDL synthesis and acutely inhibits lipoprotein lipase, adding to the postprandial TG peak [17, mechanism_review]. Among the standard lipid markers, TG is the most diet-responsive, which makes it a sensitive (if noisy) index of recent carbohydrate, sugar, and alcohol load.

**Adiposity, insulin resistance, T2D.** Obesity, insulin resistance, and uncontrolled type 2 diabetes are major secondary drivers — increased VLDL production combined with impaired LpL-mediated clearance; hypothyroidism also raises TG [10, mechanism_review][17, mechanism_review]. The TG/HDL relationship that underlies much of the observational confounding (Causal Evidence) traces to this shared metabolic-syndrome physiology.

**Genetics.** Familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) is a rare autosomal-recessive monogenic disorder caused by biallelic variants in LPL, APOC2, APOA5, GPIHBP1, or LMF1; 50–90% of FCS cases are biallelic LPL, the remaining 10–50% in the other four genes [14, mechanism_review]. apoC-II (APOC2) and apoA-V (APOA5) are LpL cofactors, and GPIHBP1 transports and anchors LpL at the capillary endothelium [14, mechanism_review]. Far more common is multifactorial (polygenic) chylomicronemia — a cluster of minor variants exacerbated by secondary factors — affecting ~1 in 600 of the population [14, mechanism_review]. The same genes that, when mildly perturbed at the population level, drive MR causal signals (APOA5, APOC3) cause monogenic disease when biallelically disrupted.

**Drugs that raise TG.** Oral estrogens, corticosteroids, retinoids (isotretinoin), beta-blockers, thiazides, tamoxifen/raloxifene, oral contraceptives, protease inhibitors, atypical antipsychotics, bile-acid sequestrants, and immunosuppressants [10, mechanism_review][11, mechanism_review].

**TG-lowering therapies (reference magnitudes).**
- **Fibrates:** ~30–50% TG reduction [11, mechanism_review]; in cardiovascular-outcome meta-analysis, a 10% relative risk reduction for major CV events (95% CI 0–18) and 13% for coronary events (95% CI 7–19) [10, mechanism_review].
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** ~20–50% TG reduction [11, mechanism_review]; prescription omega-3-acid ethyl esters (Lovaza) are FDA-approved for very-high TG ≥500 mg/dL at 4 g/day, lowering TG ~39–45% in pivotal trials [15, regulatory].
- **Icosapent ethyl (high-purity EPA):** in REDUCE-IT (n=8,179; fasting TG 135–499 mg/dL, LDL-C 41–100 mg/dL, all on statin, 70.7% secondary prevention), 4 g/day reduced the primary composite CV endpoint from 22.0% to 17.2%, hazard ratio 0.75 (95% CI 0.68–0.83; P<0.001; ~25% relative risk reduction) [12, rct]. Notably, the benefit exceeded what the modest TG lowering alone would predict, reinforcing that the TG number is a marker and that the trial's effect operates on the underlying TRL/remnant and pleiotropic biology.
- **Statins:** modest, ~10–30% TG reduction, greater at higher baseline TG [10, mechanism_review][11, mechanism_review].
- **Niacin** ~10–30% and **ezetimibe** ~5–10% for context [11, mechanism_review].

## Clinical Significance

**ASCVD residual risk via remnants.** The clinical value of a TG measurement in cardiovascular risk is as an index of the TRL/remnant-cholesterol burden that contributes residual atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk after LDL-C is controlled [1, mechanism_review]. Because the cholesterol cargo of remnants is the mediator (Causal Evidence), TG-guided management is really remnant management: lowering the TRL pool — through diet, weight loss, glycemic control, and, where indicated, the agents above — reduces the supply of cholesterol-laden remnants to the arterial wall. The MR concordance (APOA5 raising risk, APOC3 lowering it) is the warrant that this is causal, not merely associative, and the REDUCE-IT outcome (HR 0.75) is the clinical-trial demonstration that intervening on the TRL axis on top of statin therapy reduces events [12, rct]. The honest framing remains: TG marks the burden; remnant cholesterol mediates the harm.

**Pancreatitis as a separate, very-high-TG domain.** The very-high-TG → acute pancreatitis hazard is mechanistically distinct from atherosclerosis. At very high levels the LpL clearance system is saturated and chylomicrons accumulate (chylomicronemia) [4, mechanism_review]. "The risk of pancreatitis markedly increases with very severe triglyceride levels above 2000 mg/dl, but it can be prevented by maintaining triglyceride levels below 1000 mg/dl" [4, mechanism_review]. The two domains diverge cleanly: "mild or moderate hypertriglyceridemia may be a risk factor for cardiovascular disease," whereas "severe and very severe hypertriglyceridemia increase the risk for pancreatitis" — the former a remnant/cholesterol problem, the latter a chylomicron-mass problem [4, mechanism_review]. Practically, this means a TG in the borderline-to-high band (150–499 mg/dL) is read on the cardiovascular axis (remnant burden), whereas a TG in the very-high band (≥500, and especially ≥1000 mg/dL) shifts the clinical concern to acute pancreatitis prevention, with the goal of pulling TG below ~500 mg/dL and ideally below ~250 mg/dL to remove the pancreatitis hazard [11, mechanism_review]. The same lab number thus carries two different clinical meanings depending on its magnitude, and the two must never be conflated.
