# ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)

## Metadata
- category: blood
- unit: U/L (= IU/L; 1 U/L ≈ 16.67 nkat/L)
- source: lab
- confidence: established

## Target Range
- conventional lab ULN (men): ~30–55 U/L (lab-dependent; sex-specific; do not use as health threshold)
- conventional lab ULN (women): ~30–45 U/L (lab-dependent)
- evidence-based "healthy" ULN (men): **~29–33 U/L** — Prati 2002 (30 U/L) + ACG 2017 (29–33 U/L); levels above this should be assessed
- evidence-based "healthy" ULN (women): **~19–25 U/L** — Prati 2002 (19 U/L) + ACG 2017 (19–25 U/L); levels above this should be assessed
- AASLD 2018 HBV management threshold: 35 U/L (men) / 25 U/L (women)
- interpret by ×ULN bands: <5× = mild-moderate (MASLD, ALD, DILI, viral hepatitis); 5–15× = active hepatocellular injury; >15× = acute necrosis (ischemic/toxin/viral); >10,000 U/L = almost exclusively ischemic hepatopathy or toxin
- pair with AST (De Ritis ratio): >2 → alcoholic liver disease; <1 → MASLD / acute viral hepatitis; rising >1 in chronic disease → advancing fibrosis/cirrhosis
- alert: >3× ULN (any) warrants clinical evaluation; >5× ULN triggers drug cessation under DILI guidelines; ALT ≥3× ULN + bilirubin ≥2× ULN = Hy's Law signal (~10% mortality risk)
- source of target: [[library/biomarkers/alt/research-report]] + Prati 2002 (PMID 12093239) + ACG 2017 (PMID 27995906)

## Affected By
- RAISES — mild-to-moderate (<5× ULN): MASLD/NAFLD (leading cause; >80% with steatosis have "normal" ALT by conventional ULN); alcohol-related liver disease (AST>ALT, both usually <300 U/L); chronic viral hepatitis B/C; drugs and herbs (statins [mild, transient, 1–3%], acetaminophen [supratherapeutic], amoxicillin-clavulanate, fluoroquinolones, isoniazid, methotrexate, herbal supplements); hemochromatosis; autoimmune hepatitis; Wilson disease; α1-antitrypsin deficiency; celiac disease; thyroid dysfunction (hypo- and hyperthyroid); non-hepatic: skeletal muscle injury / strenuous exercise / rhabdomyolysis (correlate with CK — markedly elevated CK establishes muscle source)
- RAISES — marked (>10–25× ULN): acute viral hepatitis A/B/E; ischemic hepatitis / shock liver (≥20× ULN, peaks 1–3 days, resolves 7–14 days); acetaminophen overdose (>100× ULN possible); acute biliary obstruction (transient spike first 24–48h)
- LOWERS: vitamin B6 (pyridoxal-5-phosphate) deficiency — suppresses enzyme activity regardless of hepatic injury (common in hemodialysis, alcoholism, IBD, chronic inflammatory states); CKD/hemodialysis (mean ~7 IU/L; conventional ULN invalid in this population); frailty/sarcopenia/aging (low ALT in elderly = reduced lean mass + hepatic reserve; independently predicts higher all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in ≥65 cohorts)
- assay variable: non-P5P reagents (>60% of labs) systematically underestimate ALT ~12% on average; larger bias in B6-deficient patients

## Why It Matters
ALT is the most liver-specific aminotransferase in routine clinical use, making it the primary screen for hepatocellular injury. It marks disruption of the hepatocyte plasma membrane — not hepatic synthetic function — so a normal ALT does NOT exclude significant liver disease including cirrhosis: in chronic HBV patients with persistently normal ALT, up to 49% of HBeAg-positive patients had significant fibrosis (≥F2) on biopsy. The standard laboratory ULN is set from unscreened populations and is too high; the evidence-based healthy threshold (ACG 2017: 29–33 U/L men / 19–25 U/L women) flags MASLD and early hepatic injury that the conventional cutpoint misses. ALT is always interpreted alongside AST via the De Ritis ratio (AST:ALT): >2 identifies alcoholic liver disease with high specificity; <1 characterizes MASLD and acute viral hepatitis; a rising ratio >1 in chronic liver disease signals advancing fibrosis. Even high-normal ALT within the conventional range carries prognostic weight: each SD increase in log-ALT in the Framingham Offspring cohort predicted 48% higher odds of incident T2D and 21% higher odds of metabolic syndrome at 20 years; in WOSCOPS, the top ALT quartile (≥29 U/L) carried HR 3.38 for new-onset T2D. ALT is therefore both a liver injury signal and an early metabolic warning.
