## Target Range
- ideal: STRONGLY age-dependent — interpret only as an age- and sex-matched **SD score (z-score)**; a raw value is meaningless without age
- acceptable: SDS −2 to +2 for the specific assay used (Bidlingmaier 2014 IDS-iSYS normative cohort, N=15,014, ages 0–94)
- representative values (IDS-iSYS platform):
  - puberty peak (~age 14–16): readily >400–600 ng/mL; 97.5th percentile male ~>700 ng/mL
  - young adult (18–20 yrs): ~155–421 ng/mL (sex-averaged range)
  - mid-adulthood (30–39 yrs): ~108–294 ng/mL
  - late adulthood (70–89 yrs): ~56–192 ng/mL
- timing: no diurnal variation; time of draw is not a material pre-analytical variable (unlike GH or cortisol)
- source of target: [[library/biomarkers/igf-1/research-report]] + Bidlingmaier 2014 (PMID 24606072) + Katznelson 2014 Endocrine Society guideline (PMID 25356808)

## Affected By
- RAISES IGF-1: acromegaly / GH-secreting pituitary adenoma (the primary clinical driver of marked elevation); exogenous GH (therapeutic or illicit — basis of WADA GH biomarkers test); puberty (peak in adolescence); adequate caloric and protein intake; insulin (permissive hepatic effect); sex steroids via non-oral routes
- LOWERS IGF-1 (GH-related): GH deficiency from hypopituitarism, pituitary adenoma, cranial irradiation, or traumatic brain injury
- LOWERS IGF-1 (non-GH causes — important: a low IGF-1 is NOT specific to GHD): malnutrition / prolonged fasting / anorexia nervosa; liver disease / cirrhosis (IGF-1 is hepatically synthesized); poorly controlled diabetes; hypothyroidism; oral oestrogen (first-pass hepatic GH resistance — effect absent with transdermal/parenteral routes); aging / somatopause (progressive decline from the 3rd decade onward); acute systemic illness / catabolism (sepsis, surgery, cancer cachexia)
- Assay artifacts: IGFBP interference (incomplete acid dissociation suppresses immunoreactivity); biotin supplementation at pharmacological doses (falsely low on IDS-iSYS and other biotin-streptavidin platforms); heterophile antibodies (falsely elevated); results NOT transferable across immunoassay platforms

## Why It Matters
IGF-1 is the stable, time-integrated read-out of GH secretory status. Because IGF-1 circulates bound to IGFBP-3 and the acid-labile subunit in a 150 kDa ternary complex, its serum half-life (~12–15 hours) is orders of magnitude longer than free GH (~10–20 minutes); the result is that IGF-1 shows no meaningful diurnal fluctuation and reflects the preceding days' integrated GH output — making a single measurement far more informative than any random GH draw. It is THE first-line screen for acromegaly: an elevated age-adjusted IGF-1 (SDS >+2) should prompt confirmatory OGTT-GH suppression testing; a normal age-adjusted IGF-1 effectively excludes active disease. On the low side, IGF-1 contributes to the GHD workup, but — critically — a low IGF-1 is not specific to GH axis pathology: malnutrition, liver disease, uncontrolled diabetes, hypothyroidism, oral oestrogen, and aging all suppress IGF-1 independently, so dynamic stimulation testing remains mandatory for GHD diagnosis. Interpretation must ALWAYS be age- and sex-normalized (SDS), and must use the reference population for the specific assay platform; numeric values from different immunoassay platforms are not directly comparable. The longevity picture is genuinely paradoxical: reduced GH/IGF-1 signaling extends lifespan in multiple animal models, and a 22-year natural experiment in ~90 GH-receptor-deficient Ecuadorian subjects with Laron syndrome showed near-zero cancer and diabetes incidence — yet population epidemiology reveals a U-shaped mortality curve in humans, with both high IGF-1 (prostate and breast cancer associations) and low IGF-1 (cardiovascular mortality in older adults) carrying elevated risk. The take-away for health tracking: this is not a parameter to manipulate naively, and the honest framing of the longevity data is species-flagged and population-specific, not an argument for suppressing IGF-1 in healthy adults.
