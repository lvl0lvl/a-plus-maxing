# Lp(a) — Lipoprotein(a)

## Metadata

## Target Range
- desirable / low concern: < 75 nmol/L (≈ < 30 mg/dL)
- intermediate / grey zone: 75 – 125 nmol/L (≈ 30 – 50 mg/dL)
- high / risk factor: > 125 nmol/L (≈ > 50 mg/dL)
- very-high / genetic (heFH-equivalent): > 430 nmol/L (≈ > 180 mg/dL)
- note: mass (mg/dL) ↔ molar (nmol/L) is NOT linearly interconvertible — apo(a) isoform size varies between people, so the parenthetical alignments are guideline approximations, never arithmetic conversions; an individual's two readings must never be derived from one another.
- source of target: [[library/biomarkers/lp-a/research-report]] + EAS 2022 consensus (Kronenberg et al., Eur Heart J 2022; PMID 36036785)

## Affected By
- genetics [[dna/lpa]] — *LPA* locus, KIV-2 copy-number variant; ~80–90% genetically determined (largely fixed at birth)
- raised by: statins (~10–20% paradoxical increase), menopause, hypothyroidism, renal / nephrotic disease
- lowered by: [[compounds/pcsk9-inhibitors]] (~20–30%), niacin (not recommended — no outcome benefit), lipoprotein apheresis (~60–70% acute)
- investigational RNA agents (not approved): pelacarsen, olpasiran, lepodisiran
- minimal effect: diet, exercise, weight loss — Lp(a) is largely refractory to lifestyle

## Why It Matters
Lp(a) is an LDL-like particle carrying apolipoprotein(a) that is an independent, causal risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and the only identified modifiable causal risk factor for calcific aortic valve stenosis; its risk is additive to LDL-C and apoB and persists after full adjustment for conventional lipids. Because the concentration is ~80–90% genetically determined and stable across the lifespan, it is a once-in-a-lifetime genetic measure — a single lab draw reclassifies lifetime cardiovascular risk for the individual and, via cascade screening, for first-degree relatives.
