# DHEA-S (Dehydroepiandrosterone Sulfate)

## Metadata

- source: lab

## Target Range
- note: DHEA-S is **strongly age- and sex-dependent** — a value without its age band is clinically meaningless
- adult men (representative, all ages): ~25–510 µg/dL; peak in 20s (~110–510 µg/dL at 18–29), declining to <204 µg/dL upper limit at 60+
- adult women (representative, all ages): ~15–325 µg/dL; peak in 20s–30s (~40–325 µg/dL at 18–39), declining to <145 µg/dL upper limit at 60+
- alert (elevated): >700 µg/dL → adrenal tumor red flag (adrenocortical carcinoma or androgen-secreting adenoma); warrants adrenal imaging regardless of sex or age
- alert (low): <25 µg/dL in adults under 60 → supports adrenal insufficiency workup; confirm with cosyntropin stimulation testing
- no diurnal variation: sampling time is NOT critical — morning or afternoon draw is equally valid (practical advantage vs cortisol)
- assay note: reference intervals are platform-specific (immunoassay vs LC-MS/MS); use the reporting laboratory's own age-stratified ranges for clinical decisions
- source of target: [[library/biomarkers/dhea-s/research-report]] + Orentreich 1984 (PMID 6235241) + Saini 2025 (PMID 40909019)

## Affected By
- RAISES: adrenal androgen-secreting tumor / adrenocortical carcinoma (>700 µg/dL red flag); congenital adrenal hyperplasia (21-hydroxylase or 3β-HSD deficiency, ACTH-driven shunting); PCOS (adrenal component, ~20–33% of affected women, typically mild-moderate elevation); premature adrenarche; exogenous DHEA supplementation (OTC, raises DHEA-S into or above young-adult range)
- LOWERS: primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison disease); secondary/central adrenal insufficiency (hypopituitarism, low ACTH); exogenous glucocorticoids (even inhaled/topical doses suppress HPA → DHEA-S); aging / adrenopause (~2–5%/yr from peak in mid-20s; 70–80% total decline by age 75); chronic illness; anorexia nervosa
- Assay artifact — RAISES falsely: biotin supplementation ≥5 mg/day (competitive immunoassay streptavidin-biotin interference; wait ≥12 h after last dose before draw); heterophile antibodies (HAMA); cross-reactivity with pregnenolone sulfate and androstenedione (immunoassay, platform-dependent)
- Assay artifact — context: DHEA-S can falsely elevate direct testosterone immunoassays in women and children (Middle 2007, PMID 17362583)

## Why It Matters
DHEA-S is the canonical marker of adrenal androgen output — produced almost exclusively by the adrenal zona reticularis, it reflects adrenal androgen secretory capacity without contamination from gonadal sources. Its key practical advantage over all other adrenal steroids is the absence of clinically significant diurnal variation: the long plasma half-life (~7–10 h) and slow metabolic clearance mean a specimen collected at any time of day reliably reflects adrenal androgen output, unlike cortisol, which requires a timed morning draw. This timing flexibility makes it the easiest adrenal androgen to measure reproducibly. In the hyperandrogenism workup, DHEA-S functions as an organ discriminator: elevated DHEA-S alongside elevated testosterone points to adrenal origin; normal DHEA-S with elevated testosterone points to the ovary — a localization step that shapes imaging and suppression test decisions. A markedly elevated DHEA-S (>700 µg/dL) is a red flag for adrenal tumor, particularly adrenocortical carcinoma. Low DHEA-S supports adrenal insufficiency and tracks adrenopause — the uniform, progressive decline in adrenal androgen reserve beginning in the 30s and reaching 70–80% loss by the 70s. Despite its age trajectory, DHEA supplementation does not have anti-aging support in healthy older adults: the best evidence (a 25-RCT meta-analysis in elderly men and the 2023 Endocrine Society Scientific Statement) finds no meaningful benefit for body composition, lipids, bone, or quality of life beyond what is explained by downstream hormonal conversions; guideline-supported use is limited to women with primary adrenal insufficiency reporting androgen-deficiency symptoms. DHEA-S must always be interpreted against an age- and sex-specific reference range.
