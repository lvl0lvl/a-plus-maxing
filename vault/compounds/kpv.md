# KPV

## Metadata
- class: peptide (Lys-Pro-Val; the C-terminal tripeptide of α-MSH, residues 11–13)
- evidence_tier: C — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]] (entirely preclinical; **zero human studies of any design**; strongest signal = rodent colitis, but ~75% of gut-IBD in-vivo efficacy primaries from a single lab)
- risk_tier: experimental — unapproved, no human safety data, immunomodulatory
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-19

> **Read this first.** KPV is the anti-inflammatory C-terminal tripeptide of α-MSH (Lys-Pro-Val), acting largely **melanocortin-receptor-INDEPENDENTLY** (intracellular NF-κB inhibition; in gut, via PepT1 transport). Evidence is **entirely PRECLINICAL — zero human clinical studies of any design** (registry + literature null; the often-cited Pawar 2017 is an *ex-vivo* skin-permeation study, not a trial). Strongest data = rodent colitis/IBD, but **~75% of the gut-IBD in-vivo efficacy primaries come from one lab** (Merlin/Georgia State). Native-KPV **antimicrobial activity is contested** (one positive single-lineage vs an independent null). Acne/gout "efficacy" belongs to the **analogues KdPT and (CKPV)₂, not the KPV monomer** — and **KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) ≠ KdPT (Lys-D-Pro-Thr)**. FDA: not approved; 503A Cat-2 (2023) → **removed ~Apr 2026** (nomination withdrawn), now unlisted pending PCAC 23–24 Jul 2026 — **removal ≠ approval; not lawfully compoundable.** WADA: not individually named but **prohibited at all times under S0** (no human approval). Vendor "no LD50 / ≥100 mg/kg tolerated" figures are **unverified**. Full depth + citations: [[library/peptides/kpv/research-report]]. Operator fields blank — goal-agnostic library entry.

## Mechanism
KPV suppresses inflammation largely independent of melanocortin receptors: it enters cells (in gut epithelium/immune cells via the PepT1/SLC15A1 oligopeptide transporter — PepT1-knockout abolishes its benefit) and inhibits NF-κB/MAPK signaling, lowering pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8). A proposed molecular step (nuclear block of p65/RelA–importin-α3) rests primarily on a single-lab study; the upstream NF-κB phenotype is multiply supported. All mechanism evidence is in-vitro/animal — no human data. See report §2.

## Evidence Summary
- Dalmasso 2008 (PMID 18061177) — mouse DSS+TNBS colitis + human cell lines; oral KPV reduces colitis via PepT1, nanomolar NF-κB inhibition. `animal`/`in_vitro`
- Viennois 2016 (PMID 27458604) — PepT1-KO **abolishes** KPV benefit (genetic necessity). `animal`
- Kannengiesser 2008 (PMID 18092346) — independent (Münster) KPV colitis protection. `animal`

Counter-evidence / caveats:
- Antimicrobial: Cutuli 2000 (PMID 10670585, positive, single-lineage) vs Songok 2018 (PMID 29953505, **null** for native KPV) → low-confidence/contested.
- Acne/gout efficacy is for **KdPT** (PMID 21010060-class analogue work) / **(CKPV)₂** dimer, not KPV monomer.
- **Zero human studies**; ~75% of gut-IBD efficacy primaries single-lab (Merlin/GSU); magnitudes single-lab.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): preclinical only; no human AE data / no human safety trial. Generally well-tolerated in rodent models (no validated human tolerability).
- adverse effects (anecdotal): injection-site reactions (`anecdote_aggregate`, qualitative).
- contraindications:    immunomodulator → caution in active infection / immunosuppression; no human safety margin; pregnancy/lactation (no data); active/history of cancer (precautionary, α-MSH lineage).
- monitoring:           **no KPV-specific validated biomarker exists**; objective monitoring would use general named assays — [[biomarkers/hs-crp]] (systemic inflammation), fecal calprotectin (gut), CBC + CMP (immunomodulator safety). These are not validated surrogates of KPV activity.
