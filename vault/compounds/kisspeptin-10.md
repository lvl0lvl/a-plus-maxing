# Kisspeptin-10

## Metadata
- class: peptide (endogenous neuropeptide; KISS1 gene product; C-terminal decapeptide YNWNSFGLRF-NH₂; MW ~1302.5 Da; KISS1R/GPR54 agonist; master HPG-axis regulator)
- evidence_tier: C — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]] (KP-10's own direct human evidence is limited to ~3 small studies, n ≤ 10 each; the substantial HPG-axis clinical literature — HA restoration, IVF triggering, pubertal diagnosis — belongs to the pharmacokinetically distinct isoform KP-54 [t½ ~28 min vs. KP-10's ~4 min]; the human sexual/limbic/HSDD evidence is 5 high-quality RCTs in tier-1 journals, but all single-center [Imperial College London, Comninos/Dhillo group] with no independent replication, and all using IV kisspeptin in hospital settings — not SC KP-10; surrogate endpoints only [fMRI activation, tumescence, psychometric items]; no validated composite sexual-function score as primary endpoint; no Phase 3 trial exists for any kisspeptin isoform)
- risk_tier: experimental — no FDA-approved product; PCAC voted against 503A inclusion October 2024 (Category 2: not eligible for compounding); WADA explicitly prohibits "kisspeptin and its agonist analogues" under S2.2.1 (Testosterone-Stimulating Peptides in Males, in- and out-of-competition); bolus-stimulates / continuous-desensitizes pharmacodynamics are documented in humans (near-complete tachyphylaxis by Day 14 of twice-daily SC KP-54, LH 24→2.5 IU/L); naive continuous dosing can paradoxically suppress the HPG axis; grey-market preparations carry unverifiable purity and C-terminal amidation status
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-21

> **Read this first.** Kisspeptin-10 is the minimal active decapeptide of the KISS1 gene product and a legitimate KISS1R/GPR54 agonist that drives pulsatile GnRH → LH/FSH → gonadal steroid secretion. The mechanism is well-established. However, four structural constraints on the evidence base must be understood before applying any claim: **(1) KP-10's own direct human evidence is thin** — only ~3 small studies (n ≤ 10) administered KP-10 specifically to humans; virtually all HPG-axis efficacy literature is KP-54 (a distinct isoform with ~7-fold longer half-life). **(2) The human sexual/HSDD evidence is 5 RCTs, all single-center (Imperial College London), unreplicated, using IV kisspeptin in hospital settings, with surrogate endpoints.** **(3) Continuous or high-frequency dosing triggers β-arrestin–mediated KISS1R desensitization that paradoxically suppresses the HPG axis** — documented in humans within 14 days. **(4) WADA explicitly prohibits kisspeptin (S2.2.1, at all times for males); FDA has no approved product and the 503A compounding pathway closed October 2024.** PT-141/bremelanotide (Vyleesi) is the FDA-approved HSDD peptide — it acts on MC4R, not KISS1R, and is not kisspeptin. Full depth + every citation: [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]].

## Mechanism
KP-10 (sequence Tyr-Asn-Trp-Asn-Ser-Phe-Gly-Leu-Arg-Phe-NH₂; C-terminally amidated) binds KISS1R (GPR54), a Gq/11-coupled seven-transmembrane receptor expressed on hypothalamic GnRH neurons. Binding activates Gαq/11 → PLC-β → IP₃/DAG → intracellular Ca²⁺ mobilization and PKC activation, driving membrane depolarization of GnRH neurons and pulsatile GnRH secretion into the hypophysial portal circulation. GnRH then stimulates pituitary gonadotrophs to release LH and FSH, driving testicular testosterone synthesis in males. This makes KP-10 the most upstream pharmacological handle on the HPG axis — acting at the hypothalamic command node rather than the pituitary or gonadal level. The GnRH relay is obligate in humans (GnRH receptor antagonists completely block the LH response to IV KP-10). KP-10 also appears to directly engage limbic circuits via nitric oxide signaling in the ventromedial hypothalamus in rodent models, independently of downstream hormonal mediation — the proposed mechanistic basis for the human fMRI sexual-processing effects. At high concentrations or continuous exposure, KISS1R undergoes β-arrestin-1/2 recruitment and clathrin-mediated internalization, producing receptor downregulation and paradoxical HPG suppression. The bolus-stimulates / continuous-desensitizes dichotomy is the single most operationally critical pharmacodynamic property for any use case. KP-10 retains IC₅₀ ~1.0 nM at human KISS1R in CHO cells, comparable to KP-54; receptor-level potency is equivalent per occupancy [Jayasena et al. 2015], but in vivo LH profiles diverge dramatically due to KP-10's ~4-min plasma half-life vs. KP-54's ~28 min. See [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]] §1.

## Evidence Summary
Strongest sources (weight order):

- [George et al. 2011] — n=6 healthy men (IV bolus) / n=4 (infusion phases); within-subject crossover; **KP-10-specific**; IV bolus 0.01–3.0 µg/kg: peak LH 12.4 ± 1.7 IU/L at 1 µg/kg; blunted LH response at 3 µg/kg (dose-dependent receptor saturation signal); continuous IV infusion 1.5 µg/kg·h raised LH pulse frequency 0.7 → 1.0 pulses/h; 4 µg/kg·h × 22.5 h raised testosterone 16.6 → 24.0 nmol/L without tachyphylaxis over that interval. The primary KP-10-specific human HPG evidence. `open_label`; Edinburgh/MRC + Mayo Clinic; JCEM Tier 1. PMID 21632807. [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]]

- [Mills et al. 2023] — n=32 men with physician-confirmed HSDD (37 randomized); double-blind 2-way crossover RCT; IV kisspeptin; left middle frontal gyrus and anterior cingulate activation vs. placebo; penile tumescence 56% greater vs. placebo at 8 min (p=.02); self-reported "happiness about sex" p=.02. **KP-54 or unspecified isoform; single-center; no validated composite sexual-function score as primary endpoint; no independent replication.** `rct`; Imperial College London exclusively; JAMA Network Open. PMID 36735255. [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]]

- [Thurston et al. 2022] — n=32 premenopausal women with HSDD (40 randomized; ~20% dropout due to IV burden); double-masked placebo-controlled 2-way crossover RCT; IV kisspeptin; left inferior/middle frontal gyrus deactivation during erotic stimuli (mechanism-dependent interpretation); hippocampal activation correlated with baseline sexual distress (r=0.469, p=.007); self-reported sexiness increased (p=.04); no AEs. **KP-54/unspecified isoform; single-center; surrogate endpoints.** `rct`; Imperial exclusively; JAMA Network Open. PMID 36287566. [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]]

- [Abbara et al. 2017] — n=62 women at high OHSS risk; Phase 2 double-blind RCT; single vs. double-dose SC KP-54 as IVF oocyte-maturation trigger; double-dose: oocyte yield ≥60% achieved in 71% vs. 45% (p=0.042); live birth rate per cycle 39% vs. 19.4%; zero moderate/severe/critical OHSS. Most mature therapeutic kisspeptin evidence, but **KP-54 exclusively, single-center (Hammersmith), no Phase 3 trial.** `rct`; Imperial; Human Reproduction. PMID 28854728. [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]]

- [Jayasena et al. 2011] — n=~12 healthy men and women; open-label; **KP-10-specific**; IV bolus 0.3–10 nmol/kg (men and women) + SC bolus 2–32 nmol/kg (women); measured t½ = 3.8 ± 0.3 min men / 4.1 ± 0.4 min women; documented sexual dimorphism in LH response (preovulatory > follicular > men at equivalent molar doses). `open_label`; Imperial; JCEM Tier 1. PMID 21976724. [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]]

Counter-evidence and limits:
- [Jayasena et al. 2009] — n=10 women with hypothalamic amenorrhea; twice-daily SC KP-54 × 14 days: Day 1 peak LH 24.0 ± 3.5 IU/L → Day 14 LH 2.5 ± 2.2 IU/L. **Near-complete tachyphylaxis within 2 weeks.** GnRH challenge confirmed preserved pituitary responsiveness — desensitization is at KISS1R level. Canonical demonstration of the continuous-dosing risk. `open_label`; JCEM Tier 1. PMID 19820030.
- [George et al. 2011] — 3 µg/kg IV bolus produced **blunted** LH vs. 1 µg/kg — in-human signal of dose-dependent receptor saturation. `open_label`.
- All five human sexual/limbic RCTs (Comninos 2017, 2018; Yang 2020; Mills 2023; Thurston 2022) are Imperial College London exclusively — no independent group has replicated the central finding that peripherally administered kisspeptin enhances human sexual brain processing. `rct` caveat.
- KP-10 has no independent human sexual-brain or HSDD study; the sexual evidence extrapolates from KP-54 / unspecified IV isoform administered in hospital settings.
- No Phase 3 trial exists for any kisspeptin isoform or indication.

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
See [[library/peptides/kisspeptin-10/research-report]] §5 for full detail. No `compounding_data_sheet` sources are admissible (kisspeptin-10 is not lawfully compoundable under 503A as of October 2024 PCAC ruling; no licensed pharmacy data sheets located).

- **Compounding pharmacy data sheets:** none admissible. The PCAC voted against 503A inclusion for kisspeptin-10 at its October 29, 2024 meeting, citing physicochemical characterization gaps, parenteral immunogenicity risk, insufficient effectiveness evidence, and available approved alternatives [FDA PCAC briefing, `regulatory`]. Compounding pharmacies (Empower, Tailormade, Hallandale, etc.) may not lawfully prepare kisspeptin-10 under current US rules.
- **Practitioner reference texts:** no A4M/IFM/Seeds Peptide Protocols monograph specifically for KP-10 at a currently compoundable dose has been identified consistent with legal status. IPS / AAOPM materials referencing kisspeptin predate the October 2024 PCAC ruling and reflect an access pathway that no longer exists.
- **Consensus practitioner convention (NOT efficacy-grounded):** Grey-market vendor labeling circulates 100–500 µg SC daily or every-other-day `[vendor_label]`; community forums echo these figures `[anecdote_aggregate]`. No basis in published human PK or any controlled SC dosing study in the HSDD or testosterone-optimization indication; dosing intervals consistent with documented tachyphylaxis risk.
- **Originator-group dose (for reference only):** George et al. 2011 (Edinburgh/MRC) — the KP-10-specific human trial used IV bolus 1 µg/kg for the best LH stimulation signal in healthy men, and continuous IV infusion at 4 µg/kg·h × 22.5 h for testosterone elevation. IV route; hospital setting; not translatable to SC self-injection by simple conversion.
- **Gap:** KP-10 has no validated SC self-injection dosing protocol from a controlled human trial in any therapeutic indication. The IVF, HSDD, and HA literature is KP-54 by IV or SC under clinical supervision. Grey-market figures are entirely ungrounded in human PK data for KP-10 by the subcutaneous route in any indication.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): no serious adverse events attributed to KP-10 or KP-54 in academic trials (n=4–62 per trial; blood pressure, heart rate, standard labs stable); n=60 IVF women (KP-54): 3/60 mild early OHSS only, zero moderate/severe/critical; n=32 men and 32 women in HSDD RCTs: no AEs reported among completers; 8/40 women did not complete the HSDD crossover (~20% dropout, IV-visit burden as primary driver). No long-term chronic-dosing safety data in humans.
- adverse effects (anecdotal): injection-site reactions, headache, flushing reported on forum-level (`anecdote_aggregate`; qualitative only; no validated prevalence); HPG suppression from naive continuous dosing is a pharmacodynamic risk, not a side-effect category
- contraindications:    hormone-sensitive conditions (hormone-receptor-positive malignancies, sex-steroid-dependent disorders) — KP-10 raises LH/FSH and downstream gonadal steroids; theoretical risk; naive continuous/high-frequency dosing (paradoxical HPG suppression via KISS1R desensitization — the operationally dominant risk for grey-market self-injection); WADA-tested athletes (prohibited S2.2.1, out-of-competition, males; non-specified-substance tier — no contamination-defense reduction available); pregnancy/lactation (theoretical risk from premature LH surge or luteal disruption; no safety data); precocious puberty risk in pediatric or adolescent context
- monitoring:           [[biomarkers/total-testosterone]] (primary downstream HPG endpoint; confirm axis engagement or detect tachyphylaxis-driven suppression; baseline + 24–48 h post-bolus, or at end of multi-day infusion period); [[biomarkers/free-testosterone]] (free fraction tracks total; relevant at baseline and follow-up); [[biomarkers/estradiol]] (female context: cycle-phase gating; baseline and post-dose for IVF-trigger or HA context); LH and FSH (direct pharmacodynamic targets; baseline + 30–60 min post-dose; suppression below baseline on repeat dosing = tachyphylaxis signal requiring dosing-interval reassessment); vitals (BP, HR — monitored and stable in all academic trials; each administration visit); injection-site assessment (theoretical aggregation/immunogenicity concern; unverified purity in grey-market preparations adds uncertainty)
- known interactions:   no characterized interactions; theoretical: additive HPG stimulation with GnRH agonists (same downstream pathway, distinct receptor targets); theoretical antagonism with GnRH receptor antagonists (documented mechanistically — ganirelix/cetrorelix completely block LH response to KP-10 in humans); sex-steroid feedback: estrogen priming is required for full LH responsiveness (cycle-phase gating documented in women; Dhillo 2007)

## Trial Status
- linked experiment:    none
- baseline biomarkers:  n/a (library entry)
- expected response window: IV bolus: LH peak within 30 min, testosterone response within 24–48 h (KP-10-specific data, George 2011); tachyphylaxis risk window begins ~Day 7–14 of continuous/daily SC use (KP-54 HA data — KP-10 interval equivalent uncharacterized but pharmacokinetically at least as susceptible given shorter t½)
- stopping criteria:    stop if LH and/or FSH suppressed below baseline on repeat dosing (tachyphylaxis signal — paradoxical HPG suppression is the primary pharmacodynamic risk for grey-market dosing); total testosterone declining on a schedule expected to produce elevation (axis suppression, not enhancement); any systemic allergic or anaphylactoid response; unanticipated HPG stimulation in hormone-sensitive conditions (hormone-receptor-positive malignancy, precocious puberty risk); WADA-prohibited in competitive athletes — stop immediately on entry into any WADA-governed competition period

## Decision Notes
Per [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]], Kisspeptin-10 at evidence_tier=C / risk_tier=experimental → **doctor-required / avoid for performance or sexual-health use outside supervised research**: KP-10's own direct human evidence is thin (~3 small open-label studies, all IV, n ≤ 10); the substantial HPG-axis efficacy literature is KP-54 (a pharmacokinetically distinct isoform at ~7-fold longer half-life); the human sexual/HSDD literature is 5 genuine double-blind RCTs but all single-center (Imperial) with no independent replication, using IV administration and surrogate endpoints, not the SC-self-injection use case. The bolus/desensitization pharmacodynamics mean that naive grey-market dosing risks converting a hypothalamic stimulant into an HPG suppressant within ~14 days — documented in humans, not speculative. The compound is WADA-prohibited (S2.2.1, males, at all times) and not lawfully compoundable in the US. The FDA-approved HSDD peptide is bremelanotide (Vyleesi/PT-141), a MC4R agonist with a completely distinct mechanism and a Phase 3 evidence base. KP-10's biological mechanism is coherent and its HPG-axis pharmacology is established at the proof-of-concept level, but the evidence is not at a stage that supports unsupervised use.
