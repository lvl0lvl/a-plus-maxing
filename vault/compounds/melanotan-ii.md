# Melanotan-II

## Metadata
- class: peptide (synthetic cyclic melanocortin-receptor agonist; non-selective; Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-NH₂; MW ~1024.2 Da; University of Arizona, Hadley/Hruby, late 1980s)
- evidence_tier: C — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]] (tanning evidence = 1 open-label pilot, n=3; erectile evidence = 3 double-blind placebo-controlled crossover RCTs, n=10/20/10 — all single PI, single site [University of Arizona], never independently replicated; MT-II abandoned ~2000; no Phase 3 ever conducted; approved descendants are structurally distinct compounds whose evidence belongs to them, not MT-II)
- risk_tier: experimental — never approved anywhere (FDA/MHRA/TGA/EMA all issued explicit warnings); FDA Category 2 bulk compounding (not lawfully compoundable under 503A/503B); multi-source dermatologic signal (eruptive/dysplastic nevi + temporally-associated melanoma case reports, biologically coherent, regulator-acknowledged); idiosyncratic SAE case reports (rhabdomyolysis, renal infarction, PRES, hypertensive crisis in healthy young adults, T1DM-associated impending DKA); common dose-dependent nausea/vomiting/flushing; spontaneous priapism risk (MC4R); almost certainly prohibited under WADA S2 non-approved-substances clause — NADO verification confirmatory; ~90% single-origin primary pharmacology (University of Arizona), above 70% flag
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-21

> **Read this first.** Melanotan-II is a non-selective melanocortin-receptor agonist (MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, MC5R) with real human pharmacological evidence — tanning in an n=3 open-label pilot and erectile function in three small double-blind crossover RCTs — but with four structural constraints that must be front of mind: (1) all formal human evidence originates from a single PI group at a single institution (University of Arizona), constituting proof-of-concept only; (2) MT-II was **never approved** and was abandoned ~2000; its two licensed descendants are **afamelanotide/Scenesse** (MC1R-selective, EPP) and **bremelanotide/Vyleesi** (des-amide MT-II analog, HSDD) — their regulatory approvals are **not MT-II's**; (3) there is a **biologically coherent, multi-source, regulator-acknowledged dermatologic safety signal** (eruptive/dysplastic nevi, temporally-associated melanoma in peer-reviewed case reports from independent centers across UK, Europe, Australia) from non-selective UV-ungated melanocyte activation — not proof of causation, but a signal that must be weighted heavily given grey-market use with no baseline dermoscopic mapping; (4) idiosyncratic serious adverse events spanning rhabdomyolysis, renal infarction, and PRES have been reported. The MT-I / MT-II / PT-141 consumer-label conflation is a primary sourcing hazard. Full depth, citations, and mechanism: [[library/peptides/melanotan-ii/research-report]].

## Mechanism
Melanotan-II activates all four of the pharmacologically distinct melanocortin receptor subtypes (MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, MC5R) with high potency simultaneously [Novoselova 2018, mechanism_review]. This non-selectivity defines both the pharmacological effects and the safety profile. MC1R activation on epidermal melanocytes drives the cAMP/PKA/MITF cascade → transcriptional upregulation of tyrosinase, TRP-1, and TRP-2 → eumelanin synthesis — producing tanning without UV gating and without the homeostatic keratinocyte-paracrine feedback that normally constrains melanocyte activation [Herraiz 2021, mechanism_review]. MC4R activation in hypothalamic paraventricular and limbic nuclei is the mechanistic basis of centrally initiated penile erection independent of nitric-oxide-mediated peripheral vascular pathways, as well as the appetite suppression and nausea/yawning/stretching cluster that are pharmacodynamically inseparable from the erectogenic effect [Baldini 2019, mechanism_review]. MC3R contributes to nausea and autonomic signaling. The absence of UV gating in systemic MC1R activation means pharmacological stimulation reaches all melanocytes simultaneously — including dysplastic clones — without the paracrine constraints that govern physiological tanning, which is the mechanistic basis of the dermatologic safety signal [Herraiz 2021, mechanism_review]. The lactam cyclization between Asp and Lys side chains constrains the bioactive β-turn conformation of the natural, linear α-MSH peptide, conferring substantially greater potency and proteolytic resistance [Dorr 1996, open_label].

## Evidence Summary
Strongest sources (weight order):

- [Wessells et al. 1998, J Urol; PMID 9679884] — n=10 men with psychogenic ED; double-blind placebo-controlled crossover; MT-II 0.025 mg/kg SC; 80% achieved erections (>80% tip rigidity); mean rigidity duration 38 min (MT-II) vs 3 min (placebo; p=0.0045); nausea, yawning common. `rct` — University of Arizona; single site; no independent replication. [[library/peptides/melanotan-ii/research-report]]
- [Wessells et al. 2000, Int J Impot Res; PMID 11035391] — n=20 men with mixed-etiology ED; double-blind crossover; 17/20 (85%) achieved erections; mean rigidity 41 min; enhanced sexual desire after 68% of MT-II doses vs 19% placebo (P<0.01); severe nausea in ~12.9% of active doses. `rct` — University of Arizona; overlapping investigator roster.
- [Wessells et al. 2000, Urology; PMID 11018622] — n=10 men with organic ED; double-blind crossover; MT-II 0.025 mg/kg SC; erections after 12/19 active doses vs 1/21 placebo; mean rigidity 45.3 min; demonstrates central MC4R erection even in vasculogenic/neurogenic ED. `rct` — University of Arizona.
- [Dorr et al. 1996, Life Sci; PMID 8637402] — n=3 healthy males; single-blind alternating-day dose escalation 0.01–0.025 mg/kg SC; increased pigmentation in 2/3 subjects after 5 doses; dose-limiting nausea, somnolence, and spontaneous erections (1–5 hr) at all dose levels. `open_label` — the sole human tanning trial; University of Arizona; **no powered RCT of MT-II for tanning has ever been published**.

Counter-evidence and structural limits:
- **Single PI / single site:** all three erectile RCTs (PMID 9679884, 11035391, 11018622) share overlapping investigators (Wessells, Hadley, Hruby, Dorr, Levine) at the University of Arizona. No independent replication exists anywhere. These constitute single-group proof-of-concept, not an independently corroborated literature.
- **Tanning n=3:** the only human tanning evidence is a pilot with n=3 — categorically below the threshold for any dose or efficacy inference.
- **Dermatologic signal (multi-source case reports):** eruptive/dysplastic nevi and temporally associated melanoma from independent UK, Belgian, Spanish, German, Danish, Australian, and US dermatology centers [refs 12–20 in research-report]; Habbema et al. 2017 (Leiden University, independent; Int J Dermatol) concluded unregulated α-MSH analog use carries melanocytic change and melanoma association risk. `open_label` case reports — causal inference confounded by concurrent UV exposure, but multi-sourced and regulator-acknowledged.
- **Serious AEs (case reports):** rhabdomyolysis with ICU admission (Nelson 2012, Clin Toxicol; dose ~6× research starting dose); renal infarction affecting ~50% kidney (Peters 2020, CEN Case Rep; 6-month cumulative exposure); PRES with seizures (Ann Intern Med 2013; letter). Idiosyncratic, likely under-reported.

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
See [[library/peptides/melanotan-ii/research-report]] §5 for full detail. MT-II is **not lawfully compoundable** in the US (FDA Category 2 bulk compounding list — 503A and 503B pharmacies may not compound it). No `compounding_data_sheet` sources are admissible.

- **Compounding pharmacy data sheets:** none admissible. MT-II is Category 2 prohibited bulk for compounding (FDA 2014 bulk nominations). No licensed US 503A/503B pharmacy may legally prepare it.
- **Practitioner reference texts:** no A4M/IFM/Seeds peptide monograph for MT-II as a licensed compoundable entity; the Arizona research-group's own historical review (Hadley & Dorr 2006, Peptides) documents that MT-II was abandoned as a development candidate and superseded by its descendants. Not a prescribing-practice source.
- **Academic trial doses (citable; no alternatives exist):** Dorr 1996 starting dose 0.01 mg/kg SC, escalated to 0.025–0.03 mg/kg SC, alternating days over two weeks — the only peer-reviewed SC dose data in humans. Wessells RCTs used 0.025 mg/kg SC as a single injection per study visit. **There is no approved therapeutic dose** because MT-II has never received marketing authorization for any indication.
- **Grey-market vendor conventions** `[vendor_label; anecdote_aggregate]`: community norms circulate loading protocols of 0.5–1 mg SC daily and maintenance of 0.25–0.5 mg SC "as needed" — derived entirely from anecdote, not clinical data. The Nelson rhabdomyolysis case involved a dose approximately 6× the Dorr starting dose; community escalation norms are specifically hazardous.
- **Gap:** there is no prescribing-practice layer from licensed compounders because the compound cannot be legally compounded. Grey-market figures are ungrounded in human PK data and have produced documented multi-organ toxicity at escalated doses.

## Protocol
- dose:               no established therapeutic dose — no marketing authorization exists; academic trials used 0.025 mg/kg SC as single-visit injection (Wessells erectile RCTs) or 0.01→0.025 mg/kg SC alternating-day dose escalation (Dorr tanning pilot); grey-market community doses frequently exceed these
- route:              subQ (research and grey-market convention); nasal spray available grey-market ("Barbie drug") but zero human PK or efficacy data; oral not bioavailable (peptide, GI proteolysis); IV occasionally reported in pharmacovigilance — embolic and infectious risk
- timing:             n/a (library entry)
- cycle:              no validated cycle; grey-market convention of loading phase (daily) followed by "maintenance" unsupported by any clinical data
- stack:              n/a

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): **nausea/vomiting** — very common at all dose levels (Dorr 1996, all subjects); mechanism MC4R/area postrema; severe in ~12.9% of active doses (Wessells 2000, Int J Impot Res); **facial flushing** — concurrent with nausea (MC3R/MC4R vasodilation); **spontaneous penile erection 1–5 hours** — documented in Dorr 1996 in all male subjects receiving effective doses; **priapism** (>4 hours) is a documented risk at supra-threshold doses — medical emergency with permanent erectile dysfunction risk if untreated; **fatigue/somnolence** at 0.03 mg/kg (Dorr 1996 grade II event); **appetite suppression** (MC4R/hypothalamic; secondary to nausea in human dose range); **diffuse melanin darkening including mucosal melanocytes** (Bonchev 2026, buccal/gingival pigmentation)
- adverse effects (anecdotal): eruptive nevi within 24 hours of single dose (Schulze 2014); rapid pre-existing nevus darkening/enlargement (multiple case reports); nausea pre-treated with antiemetics in grey-market practice (not studied); phototype amplification (grey-market users commonly combine with sunbeds — documented highest-risk combination)
- contraindications:  **personal or family history of melanoma or dysplastic nevus syndrome (FAMMM)** — Sivyer 2012 demonstrates accelerated nevus atypia with histologically confirmed dysplastic compound nevus in a 16yo with FAMMM after daily 0.5 mg + UV × 3 weeks; **multiple (>50) atypical nevi** — amplifies dermatologic monitoring burden and absolute transformation risk under pharmacological melanocyte stimulation; **Type 1 diabetes mellitus / insulin-dependent diabetes** — documented impending DKA + acute cortisol/stress-axis dysregulation (BGL 41 mmol/L, ketones 4.2 mmol/L, cortisol >1,750 nmol/L; Aung 2025); a life-threatening contraindication class for this population; **cardiovascular disease or hypertension — AND any individual without a recent cardiovascular baseline assessment** — severe acute hypertensive/sympathomimetic events have been documented at single community doses in apparently healthy young adults with no prior CVD: Nelson 2012 (39yo, BP 151/85, HR 130–146); BJD 2019 (28yo, no prior HTN, BP 210 mmHg systolic, HR 142, chest pain 90 min post-injection); the cardiovascular risk is NOT limited to those with pre-existing disease; **renal impairment** — renal infarction case (Peters 2020) and nephrotoxic component of rhabdomyolysis case (Cr 2.25 mg/dL, Peters cells, proteinuria; Nelson 2012); **history of seizures or neurological conditions** — PRES with MRI white-matter abnormalities and seizures (Ann Intern Med 2013); **sickle cell trait, PDE5 inhibitor use, anticoagulant use** (additive priapism risk); **pregnancy/breastfeeding** (no data; MC4R effects on reproductive axis; theoretical risk); **Fitzpatrick skin type I or II with concurrent UV tanning practices** (highest-risk dermatologic profile across nevi/melanoma case series)
- monitoring:         PRIMARY monitoring obligation — **full-body skin examination with total-body photography (baseline before any exposure)** + **dermoscopy of all nevi (baseline + q3–4 weeks during use)** + **baseline mole-mapping** — without pre-use dermoscopic documentation, change detection is impossible; the pharmacological diffuse melanin darkening darkens all nevi simultaneously, obscuring single-lesion change against a uniformly darkened background (Sivyer 2012); **oral mucosal inspection** for gingival/buccal pigmentation (Bonchev 2026); [[biomarkers/egfr]] (renal function baseline + monitoring for rhabdomyolysis and renal infarction signal; creatinine, urinalysis); **CK** (creatinine kinase — rhabdomyolysis screen; baseline + on muscle pain/dark urine — no dedicated biomarker page yet — REQUIRED); **blood pressure and heart rate** (cardiovascular monitoring; baseline before use — no dedicated biomarker page yet — REQUIRED). **IMPORTANT CAVEAT: No serum or laboratory biomarker detects the dermatologic (melanocyte-transformation) safety signal — clinician dermoscopy + total-body photography is the only surveillance modality for the melanoma signal; the monitoring panel above is for the systemic SAEs (rhabdomyolysis, renal infarction, hypertensive crisis), NOT the dermatologic melanoma risk.**
- known interactions: no characterized pharmacokinetic interactions; theoretical: additive erectogenic effect with PDE5 inhibitors; additive hypertensive burden with sympathomimetics; no pharmacokinetic interaction data for any combination

## Trial Status
- linked experiment:    none
- baseline biomarkers:  n/a (library entry)
- expected response window: tanning onset within 5 alternating-day doses in 2/3 subjects (Dorr 1996); erectile effect within 1–5 hours of single SC injection (Wessells RCTs); human timeline for chronic use endpoints unknown
- stopping criteria:    **PRIMARY — ANY new, changing, or darkening nevus → stop immediately + dermatology referral before considering any resumption** (dermoscopic and histological evaluation of lesion(s) required); **prolonged erection (>4 hours)** — emergency evaluation for priapism; **significant nausea or vomiting impairing function**; **BP >150/95 mmHg or HR >100 bpm not otherwise explained** (cardiovascular stopping criterion); **muscle pain with dark urine** — rhabdomyolysis screen (CK, creatinine, UA; Nelson 2012); **neurological symptoms: headache with visual changes, seizure, or confusion** — PRES screen (Ann Intern Med 2013); **unilateral flank pain** — renal infarction screen (Peters 2020); **in T1DM/insulin-dependent diabetes: sudden severe hyperglycemia, ketosis, or DKA symptoms** — stop immediately, emergency medical evaluation (Aung 2025)

## Decision Notes
Per [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]], MT-II at evidence_tier=C / risk_tier=experimental → **doctor-required / avoid** for any use context. The honest picture: genuine human melanocortin pharmacology, proof-of-concept erectile and tanning evidence from a single academic center, abandoned before regulatory approval, superseded by licensed descendants whose evidence cannot be reverse-attributed to MT-II, and carrying a biologically coherent multi-source dermatologic safety signal in an unmonitored grey-market use context. The evidence ceiling is low and concentration-flagged (~90% single-origin, University of Arizona). The approved descendants (afamelanotide/Scenesse for EPP; bremelanotide/Vyleesi for HSDD) carry the legitimate evidence and regulatory oversight; neither is MT-II. MT-II almost certainly falls under WADA S2 (non-approved peptide hormone); NADO verification of by-name status is confirmatory, not permissive.
