# Epitalon

## Metadata
- class: peptide (synthetic pineal-derived tetrapeptide, sequence Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly [AEDG], ~390 Da; synthetic analog — not equivalent to — the crude bovine pineal extract Epithalamin; developed by Vladimir Khavinson at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology; founding compound of the "peptide bioregulator" / cytomedine school, a Soviet-era framework not adopted by Western gerontology)
- evidence_tier: D — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]] (all human clinical outcome data was generated using the crude bovine extract Epithalamin, not the synthetic AEDG tetrapeptide sold commercially; the telomerase/telomere-elongation mechanism has genuine in-vitro support in two independent studies [Khavinson 2003; Brunel 2025], but those studies partially disagree on mechanism in cancer cell lines; no controlled in-vivo or human telomere measurement exists; human mortality reduction claims [1.6–4.1×] rest entirely on single-group Khavinson open-label/non-randomized cohorts using historical controls; ≥80% of primary efficacy literature originates from a single tightly connected St. Petersburg research ecosystem — above the 70% single-lab concentration flag; zero Phase 1/2/3 trials registered for synthetic Epitalon in any jurisdiction)
- risk_tier: experimental — FDA-unapproved; removed from 503A Category 2 effective April 22, 2026 (notice published April 16, 2026, Fed. > **Read this first — three facts that cannot be buried.** (1) **The human longevity data used the wrong compound.** Every major human outcome claimed for this compound class — 1.6–4.1× mortality reduction, melatonin restoration, 15-year cardiac survival benefit — was generated using the crude bovine pineal extract Epithalamin, not the synthetic AEDG tetrapeptide circulating on the grey market as "epitalon." No controlled efficacy trial of synthetic Epitalon (AEDG) exists in any clinical trial registry. (2) **The telomerase claim is in-vitro only, and the replication partially disagrees on mechanism.** Telomere elongation in normal human cell lines has two supporting studies; but the only independent Western replication (Brunel 2025) found hTERT downregulated — not upregulated — in cancer cell lines, with ALT-pathway activation instead. ALT is associated with aggressive tumor biology. No controlled animal experiment by an independent group has measured telomere length change. No human trial has measured blood or tissue telomere length. (3) **≥80% single-group + telomerase-oncogenic tension.** An estimated ≥80% of primary efficacy papers originate from Khavinson's St. Petersburg Institute and its direct collaborators. Telomerase reactivation is a hallmark of malignancy; the oncogenic safety of chronic epitalon use in humans is uncharacterized in any independent study. Active or prior malignancy is a precautionary contraindication. Full depth + citations: [[library/peptides/epitalon/research-report]].

## Mechanism

Epitalon (AEDG) is a synthetic tetrapeptide whose proposed mechanisms center on the pineal gland and telomere biology. The compound was derived by Khavinson's group from sequence analysis of Epithalamin — the heterogeneous bovine pineal extract — as the putative minimal active fragment; this derivation claim has not been validated by controlled head-to-head comparison of equivalent doses.

**Telomerase / telomere elongation:** Khavinson et al. (2003) reported induction of hTERT expression, telomerase enzymatic activity, and telomere elongation in telomerase-negative human fetal fibroblast cultures [Khavinson 2003, in_vitro; tier 3]. The founding claim went unreplicated for over two decades. The first independent replication arrived in September 2025, when Al-Dulaimi et al. at Brunel University London (with Royal Brompton Hospital) confirmed dose-dependent telomere length extension across six human cell lines treated at 0.5–1 µg/mL for four days [Al-Dulaimi 2025, in_vitro; tier 2]. In normal fibroblasts and epithelial cells, elongation proceeded via hTERT upregulation — consistent with Khavinson. In cancer cell lines, however, hTERT was downregulated and elongation proceeded via Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres (ALT) instead — a mechanistic discrepancy that is not a detail. ALT is the telomerase-independent pathway by which cancer cells lacking canonical telomerase reactivation achieve replicative immortality, and its activation by epitalon in cancer cell lines is a biologically significant signal demanding independent long-term carcinogenicity study. A companion 2004 Khavinson study reported that epitalon enabled human fetal fibroblasts to surpass the Hayflick replicative limit [Khavinson 2004, in_vitro; tier 3]. Both studies use fetal fibroblast or cancer cell line models; the cell biology has not been demonstrated in aged somatic tissue in a living organism by any group.

**Pineal peptide / melatonin restoration:** AEDG's derivation from pineal tissue motivates the claim that it restores melatonin biosynthesis lost in aging. The primary human/primate evidence is a Korkushko et al. (2007) open-label study in aged rhesus monkeys and elderly humans reporting recovery of the nocturnal melatonin peak and circadian melatonin normalization, plus reduced glucose and insulin in treated old monkeys [Korkushko 2007, open_label; tier 3]. No placebo control; blinding details absent; human sample size not reported. No independent blinded controlled trial has replicated circadian melatonin normalization with either Epithalamin or synthetic Epitalon.

**Gene expression / histone binding:** A 2020 in-vitro study in human gingival mesenchymal stem cells (partial Italian co-authorship; Khavinson-led) proposed AEDG binds histone variants H1/3 and H1/6 at DNA-interacting sites via in-silico modeling, with neuronal differentiation gene mRNAs reportedly increased 1.6–1.8 fold. The histone-binding mechanism is computationally inferred; crystallographic confirmation has not been published.

**Antioxidant activity:** The most clearly independent mechanistic study is Yue et al. (2022) from Shanxi Medical University / Chinese Academy of Sciences / University of Missouri, showing significant ROS reduction, preserved mitochondrial membrane potential, and reduced early apoptosis in post-ovulatory aging mouse oocytes at 0.1 mM — no Khavinson co-authorship [Yue 2022, in_vitro; tier 2]. Antioxidant activity in a mouse oocyte model does not establish systemic antioxidant activity in humans.

**All mechanistic and efficacy evidence is preclinical (in-vitro or animal).** No human trial of any design has measured pharmacodynamic endpoints for synthetic Epitalon.

## Evidence Summary

Strongest sources (weight order):

- Al-Dulaimi et al. 2025 (*Biogerontology*, PMID 40908429) — n=6 human cell lines (2 breast cancer, 2 normal, 1 ALT+ osteosarcoma, 1 telomerase+ prostate cancer); 0.5–1 µg/mL × 4 days; telomere elongation confirmed across all cell types; normal cells: hTERT upregulation; cancer cell lines: hTERT downregulated, ALT activated; 3-week exposure required for significant elongation in normal cells vs. 4-day for cancer cells. First independent Western replication; Brunel University London + Royal Brompton Hospital; no Khavinson affiliation. [in_vitro, tier 2] [[library/peptides/epitalon/research-report]]
- Anisimov et al. 2003 (*Biogerontology*, PMID 14501183) — female Swiss-derived SHR cancer-prone mice (a cancer-susceptible Swiss-derived strain; "SHR" here is a mouse strain label, not the spontaneously hypertensive rat line), n=54/group; 1.0 µg/mouse SC monthly from age 3 months to death; mean lifespan NOT significantly extended; maximum lifespan +12.3%, last-decile survival +13.3%; spontaneous leukemia inhibited ~6-fold; total tumor incidence not reduced. Dual St. Petersburg institution (Khavinson + Anisimov groups); not independently replicated. [animal, tier 2] [[library/peptides/epitalon/research-report]]
- Korkushko et al. 2011 (*Bull Exp Biol Med*, PMID 22451889) — most methodologically developed human study; n=79 elderly coronary patients; "randomized comparative study"; Epithalamin (NOT synthetic AEDG) IM courses × 3 years; 15-year follow-up; 66.7% survival treated vs. 40% control; Kaplan-Meier + log-rank analysis. Single-site, Khavinson-affiliated, Russian-translated journal, unreplicated, small n; used Epithalamin only. [claimed rct, tier 3] [[library/peptides/epitalon/research-report]]
- Yue et al. 2022 (*Aging Albany NY*, PMID 35413689) — mouse post-ovulatory oocytes, 0.1 mM epitalon; ROS: 4.4 ± 1.0 vs. 32.8 ± 5.8 (relative fluorescence, p <0.001); preserved mitochondrial membrane potential and spindle integrity; reduced early apoptosis. Shanxi Medical University / CAS / University of Missouri; genuinely independent. [in_vitro, tier 2] [[library/peptides/epitalon/research-report]]
- Araj et al. 2025 (*Int J Mol Sci*, PMID 40141333) — comprehensive independent secondary synthesis by Polish authors (Medical University of Warsaw + Nicolaus Copernicus University, Torun); no Khavinson co-authorship; endorses antioxidant, neuroprotective, anti-aging, and antimutagenic properties while explicitly noting limited physico-chemical structural investigations and unresolved mechanistic uncertainty. [mechanism_review, tier 2] [[library/peptides/epitalon/research-report]]

Counter-evidence and limits:
- **EXTRACT-vs-SYNTHETIC CONFOUND (most consequential).** Human longevity/mortality claims (Khavinson and Morozov 2003, n=266, 1.6–4.1× mortality reduction; Korkushko et al. 2011, 15-year cardiac survival) used Epithalamin (crude bovine pineal extract), not synthetic AEDG. The claim that synthetic Epitalon shares Epithalamin's clinical effects is an inferential assumption that has not been validated by any controlled head-to-head comparison. [open_label, tier 3]
- **ALT-pathway activation in cancer cell lines** (Al-Dulaimi 2025): hTERT downregulated in cancer lines while ALT is activated — the two independent laboratories disagree on mechanism in cancer cells. ALT is associated with aggressive, treatment-resistant tumor phenotypes. This does not resolve the telomerase-oncogenic tension; it deepens it.
- **No independent human outcome replication.** Zero independent Western or Asian groups have replicated any human mortality, longevity, or circadian outcome from the Khavinson corpus. The evidence base is entirely Khavinson-institute-generated for all human clinical claims.
- **No controlled in-vivo telomere measurement** in any species by any independent group. The translation from cell-culture telomerase activation to meaningful telomere extension in aging organismal tissues is undemonstrated.
- **ADDF Cognitive Vitality Review (2020):** ADDF's independent secondary synthesis characterized the extraordinary claims from a single group over four decades without independent confirmation as a key concern and a signal for skepticism, not merely an evidence gap. [mechanism_review, tier 2]
- **No Phase 1/2/3 trials** of synthetic Epitalon in any jurisdiction. No registered trials on ClinicalTrials.gov as of mid-2026.

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
See [[library/peptides/epitalon/research-report]] §§5.3, 8 for full detail. No admissible prescribing-practice sources exist for synthetic Epitalon in any Western jurisdiction — as an unlawfully compoundable and FDA-unapproved substance, there is no licensed clinical framework for its administration.

- **Compounding pharmacy data sheets:** none admissible. Epitalon was removed from the FDA 503A Category 2 list effective April 22, 2026 (notice published April 16, 2026; Fed. Reg. 91 FR 20465); compounding is unlawful as of June 2026 regardless of PCAC outcome timeline. No Empower, Tailormade, Hallandale, Belmar, or other licensed U.S. pharmacy data sheet exists or could legally exist at present.
- **Practitioner reference texts:** no A4M/IFM/Seeds/International Peptide Society monograph for synthetic Epitalon located. The Russian bioregulator clinical literature (Khavinson-group) is the only practitioner-facing text and it covers Epithalamin, not the synthetic tetrapeptide.
- **Originator-group recommended dose:** Khavinson-group human trials employed Epithalamin at approximately 10 mg IM per day in courses of 10–20 consecutive days, administered one to three times per year in elderly cohorts. A circadian-rhythm study employed 0.5 mg/day sublingually for 20 days (the only published sublingual human dose on record). A retinal study used 5.0 µg/eye parabulbar for 10 days. These are Epithalamin doses; there is no published dose-finding or dose-escalation study for synthetic AEDG.
- **Grey-market convention (anecdote_aggregate only, not a clinical protocol):** community sources cite 5–10 mg/day SC for 10–20 days, two to three times per year. These figures have no regulatory grounding, no dose-response characterization for synthetic Epitalon, no established safety database, and are substantially above the per-injection-event doses documented in Epithalamin human research.
- **Consensus practitioner dose:** none exists in Western clinical practice. The compound lacks any licensed clinical pathway.
- **Gap:** The entire prescribing-practice layer for synthetic Epitalon is absent in Western medicine. No dose to recommend. Originator doses apply to a different preparation.

## Risk Profile

> **WARNING — TELOMERASE-ONCOGENIC TENSION + UNCHARACTERIZED LONG-TERM CARCINOGENICITY.** Epitalon induces hTERT expression and telomerase activity in normal human somatic cells in vitro — the same mechanism that enables cancer cell immortality. The 2025 Brunel replication found ALT-pathway activation (not telomerase upregulation) in cancer cell lines — a pathway associated with aggressive tumor phenotypes. The Khavinson group's anti-tumor animal data (single group, short-term, SHR cancer-susceptible strain) does not resolve this concern. No independent long-term carcinogenicity study of synthetic Epitalon exists in any species. Active or prior malignancy is a precautionary contraindication. Malignancy surveillance (PSA/CEA/age-appropriate screening) + CBC are the primary safety monitoring tools before and during any experimental use. For general health/inflammatory monitoring during experimental use, see [[biomarkers/hs-crp]].

- adverse effects (literature): no independent human safety database exists for synthetic Epitalon; Khavinson-group open-label studies report Epithalamin well-tolerated in elderly cohorts and zero adverse events in retinitis pigmentosa patients (n=162); no formal repeat-dose toxicology, carcinogenicity, reproductive/developmental toxicity, or dose-escalation study published for synthetic Epitalon; rapid transplacental transfer demonstrated in rabbits
- adverse effects (anecdotal): injection-site reactions (redness, bruising, transient swelling) and brief fatigue in early doses reported in peptide community forums (`anecdote_aggregate`; unvalidated, self-reported, dose-uncharacterized)
- contraindications:
  - Active malignancy of any type — telomerase reactivation is a hallmark of malignant transformation; on-target hTERT activation + ALT activation in cancer cell lines creates uncharacterized risk in any patient with active tumor burden
  - Prior malignancy with active recurrence risk — same mechanism-grounded concern; telomerase could support occult residual tumor cell immortalization
  - Pregnancy and breastfeeding — absolute contraindication; rapid transplacental transfer documented in rabbits; no reproductive or developmental toxicity data exist; mechanism would affect dividing cells
  - Autoimmune conditions — immune modulation reported in Khavinson animal work; no human safety data in immunologically vulnerable populations
  - WADA-tested athletes — S0 prohibited substance, in- and out-of-competition; no individual listing required
- monitoring:
  - **Pre-use malignancy screening:** PSA (men), CEA (where clinically indicated), dermatologic review, CBC with differential, and age-appropriate cancer screening — driven entirely by the telomerase-oncogenic mechanism; required before any experimental use
  - **Periodic re-screening:** at each proposed cycle given complete absence of long-term carcinogenicity data
  - [[biomarkers/hs-crp]] — general health / inflammatory monitoring during experimental use; CRP provides a non-specific signal for systemic stress or immune activation
  - CBC with differential — baseline + monitoring; p53/cell-cycle effects on hematopoietic progenitors uncharacterized
  - Hepatic function (ALT, AST) — baseline + monitoring; no hepatotoxicity data but standard for uncharacterized peptides
  - **No validated pharmacodynamic endpoint** exists for dose adjustment: telomere length assays are not clinically standardized; no surrogate biomarker for epitalon response is established
- known interactions:   no characterized human interactions; theoretical: concurrent telomerase-modifying or immunosuppressive agents; concurrent cancer therapy (interaction with telomerase-activation mechanism unpredictable)

## Decision Notes
Per [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]], Epitalon at evidence_tier=D / risk_tier=experimental → **avoid / do not self-experiment outside a clinical trial framework**: this is the lowest evidence tier, with no controlled efficacy trial of synthetic Epitalon (AEDG) in any jurisdiction, no Phase 1 safety data for the synthetic tetrapeptide, and human outcome claims resting entirely on Epithalamin (a different preparation) in single-group Khavinson open-label studies with historical controls and no independent replication over four decades.

The honest summary of the evidence: the in-vitro telomerase/telomere-elongation biology is real in cell culture — with genuine independent replication at the Brunel level. Everything above the cell level in humans is either untested (no human telomere measurement) or supported only by an unconflicted single research group whose extraordinary claims have gone unverified since the 1980s. The cytomedine/peptide-bioregulator theoretical framework from which epitalon is derived is not adopted, validated, or peer-reviewed as a recognized theory by Western gerontology bodies. The oncogenic tension is biologically grounded in the proposed mechanism and the 2025 Brunel ALT finding, not a theoretical footnote. The Russian MoH registration for Epithalamin is not a safety or efficacy endorsement for the synthetic tetrapeptide; the FDA's April 2026 Category 2 removal does not authorize compounding. Grey-market sourcing carries Epithalamin/Epitalon nomenclature confusion and unverifiable purity.

Khavinson's structural COI (inventor + director + commercializer) is not adjudicated as fraud but is load-bearing: Western journal COI policies would require mandatory disclosure of this triple-conflict; Khavinson-group papers routinely state "no conflict of interest."
