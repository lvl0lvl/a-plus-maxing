# Tirzepatide

## Metadata
- class: peptide — a **dual GIP + GLP-1 receptor agonist** ("twincretin"); a single engineered 39-amino-acid linear peptide (not a co-formulation) that activates both incretin receptors. This added GIP-receptor arm is what mechanistically distinguishes it from semaglutide, which is GLP-1-only.
- brands / indications: **Mounjaro** (type 2 diabetes) and **Zepbound** (chronic weight management; moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in obesity) — the same molecule marketed by **Eli Lilly** under two trade names with separately approved labels. Administered by **once-weekly subcutaneous injection**.
- manufacturer: Eli Lilly and Company
- evidence_tier: A — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]]. Multiple Phase-3 RCTs across two programs (the SURPASS glycemic program and the SURMOUNT obesity program), an approved outcome trial on a hard objective endpoint (SURMOUNT-OSA, apnea-hypopnea index), a dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial (SURPASS-CVOT, N=13,299), plus FDA + EMA approval. The single material caveat is **single-sponsor concentration**: essentially every pivotal trial is Eli Lilly-funded (~95% of the pivotal-primary evidence), with no independent large randomized outcomes replication yet — a concentration risk, not an evidence-quality defect (the trials are tier-1, registered, double-blind, adjudicated).
- risk_tier: medium — approved and unusually well-characterized, but with **real adverse effects** (GI-dominant intolerance; gallbladder disease; pancreatitis; gastroparesis/peri-operative aspiration), **predictable weight regain on discontinuation** (SURMOUNT-4, ~+14%), **lean-mass loss** (~25% of weight lost), and a **rodent-based thyroid C-cell boxed warning** (not human-demonstrated) that together keep it firmly doctor-required despite its approval status.
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-20

> **Read this first.** Tirzepatide is, on the evidence, a **genuinely approved and genuinely (very) efficacious dual GIP + GLP-1 receptor agonist** — and that dual mechanism is what distinguishes it from semaglutide, which is GLP-1-only. The **real benefits** are large and well-documented, but each must be read against its **population**, because the magnitudes differ substantially: in **type 2 diabetes** the SURPASS program produced HbA1c reductions of roughly 2.0–2.6%, and in the head-to-head **SURPASS-2** trial tirzepatide was statistically **superior to semaglutide 1 mg** (the highest then-approved T2D dose — so superiority is over semaglutide 1 mg specifically, not all regimens); in **non-diabetic obesity**, **SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff 2022)** produced a mean **~20.9% body-weight reduction at 15 mg** — larger than the figures reported for semaglutide, though the cross-drug obesity comparison is partly indirect; **obstructive sleep apnea is an APPROVED indication** on the strength of **SURMOUNT-OSA (Malhotra 2024)**, which reduced the apnea-hypopnea index by roughly 20–24 events/hr more than placebo and made tirzepatide the **first-ever FDA-approved drug for OSA**; and on cardiovascular safety, **SURPASS-CVOT was non-inferior** to dulaglutide on hard MACE — a **safety** result, **NOT** a superiority/CV-risk-reduction label. The **real caveats** are equally concrete: **gastrointestinal adverse events dominate** and drive most discontinuations; **weight is largely REGAINED on discontinuation** (SURMOUNT-4: +14% regained after switching to placebo) because this is a chronic, maintenance-dependent therapy; some of the loss is **lean (fat-free) mass**, roughly proportional to the much larger total loss at about a quarter of weight lost; gallbladder disease and pancreatitis are labeled concerns; delayed gastric emptying raises a **peri-operative aspiration** concern; and the **thyroid C-cell / medullary-thyroid-carcinoma BOXED WARNING is RODENT-based — the label itself says the human relevance "has not been determined," so it is NOT a demonstrated human carcinogenicity finding**. **Population matters everywhere**: type 2 diabetes, non-diabetic obesity, OSA, and HFpEF are distinct settings with distinct effect sizes and distinct approval status — figures are NOT interchangeable across them. Regulatory: FDA-approved as **Mounjaro (May 2022)**, **Zepbound (Nov 2023)**, and **Zepbound for OSA (Dec 2024 — first FDA OSA drug)**; EMA-approved. In sport, tirzepatide is **NOT prohibited** by WADA — but from 1 January 2026 it sits on WADA's **Monitoring Program**, which is surveillance, not a ban. The investigational frontier (HFpEF/SUMMIT, MASH/SYNERGY-NASH, the SURMOUNT-MMO morbidity/mortality trial) is promising but **not approved**. ## Mechanism
Tirzepatide (LY3298176) is a single synthetic 39-amino-acid linear peptide that acts as a **dual agonist at both the GIP receptor (GIPR) and the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R)** — the "twincretin" mechanism, mechanistically separate from GLP-1-only agonists precisely because of the added GIP arm. It is acylated at Lys20 with a C20 fatty diacid (driving reversible albumin binding and a ~5-day half-life that enables once-weekly SC dosing) and incorporates an Aib substitution to resist DPP-4 degradation. It is an **imbalanced, biased** agonist — GIPR affinity comparable to native GIP but ~5-fold lower GLP-1R affinity, with cAMP-biased GLP-1R signaling. By co-activating both receptors it amplifies the incretin program: glucose-dependent insulin secretion, context-dependent glucagon modulation, slowed gastric emptying (GLP-1 arm), central satiety/reduced food intake, and GIP-mediated adipose/lipid handling — the proposed source of its larger metabolic and weight effects versus GLP-1 monotherapy. See report §1.

## Evidence Summary
- Jastreboff 2022 (SURMOUNT-1, PMID 35658024) — n=2,539 adults with obesity **WITHOUT** diabetes, RCT, 72 wk, tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg vs placebo; **mean −20.9% body weight at 15 mg** (vs −3.1%). `rct` — the defining non-diabetic-obesity headline. [[library/peptides/tirzepatide/research-report]]
- Frías 2021 (SURPASS-2, PMID 34170647) — n=1,879 **T2D** on metformin, RCT, 40 wk, vs **subcutaneous semaglutide 1 mg**; HbA1c −2.01/−2.24/−2.30% vs −1.86%; **all three doses noninferior AND statistically superior to semaglutide 1 mg**. `rct`
- Malhotra 2024 (SURMOUNT-OSA, PMID 38912654) — adults with moderate-to-severe OSA + obesity, two parallel RCTs, 52 wk; AHI reduced ~20–24 events/hr more than placebo (with and without PAP). `rct` — basis for the **Dec 2024 OSA approval** (first drug ever approved for OSA).
- Aronne 2024 (SURMOUNT-4, PMID 38078870) — n=670 re-randomized after lead-in, RCT withdrawal; continuing lost a further −5.5% while those **switched to placebo REGAINED +14.0%** (between-group −19.4 pts). `rct` — durability/maintenance-dependence.

Notes / counterweights:
- Magnitude is population-specific: ~20.9% (non-diabetic obesity, SURMOUNT-1) vs ~14.7% (obesity WITH T2D, SURMOUNT-2, PMID 37385275) at the same 15 mg dose — a population effect, not a dosing difference.
- Nicholls 2025 (SURPASS-CVOT, PMID 41406444) — N=13,299, vs dulaglutide; 3-point MACE HR 0.92 (95.3% CI 0.83–1.01), **non-inferior (P=0.003) but NOT superior (P=0.09)** — establishes CV safety, not a CV-benefit label. `rct`
- The cross-drug obesity comparison vs semaglutide 2.4 mg is **indirect**; the head-to-head (SURMOUNT-5) is deferred elsewhere, not adjudicated here.
- Evidence base is single-sponsor-concentrated (~95% Eli Lilly); no independent large RCT replication exists. HFpEF (SUMMIT), MASH (SYNERGY-NASH Phase 2b), and SURMOUNT-MMO are INVESTIGATIONAL.

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
See [[library/peptides/tirzepatide/practitioner-layer]] (label titration is `regulatory`/authoritative; compounding/adjunct items are practice/regulatory context — never efficacy or AE-rate).
- **Label titration (authoritative):** SC once-weekly **2.5 mg ×4 wk → 5 → 7.5 → 10 → 12.5 → 15 mg**, in 2.5-mg steps after ≥4 weeks at each dose, max 15 mg. The **2.5 mg rung is a non-therapeutic initiation step** for GI acclimation. Slow titration to mitigate GI. Zepbound maintenance 5/10/15 mg for weight; **10 or 15 mg for OSA**.
- **Compounded landscape:** shortage-era 503A/503B compounded tirzepatide + telehealth; FDA declared the shortage **resolved (reaffirmed Dec 19, 2024)** with phased wind-down (503A grace to ~Feb 18, 2025; 503B to ~Mar 19, 2025), OFA litigation/remand, and salt-form (acetate/sodium) + counterfeit cautions. Reported as practice/regulatory context, not endorsing current legality or grounding efficacy.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): **GI dominant** and the main discontinuation driver (Zepbound obesity trials: nausea ~25–29%, diarrhea ~19–23%, vomiting ~8–13%, constipation ~11–17%; dose-related, concentrated in titration window; Mounjaro T2D rates lower); **gallbladder disease** (cholecystitis ~0.7% vs 0.2%); **pancreatitis** (labeled, precautionary; prior-pancreatitis patients excluded from trials); **gastroparesis/ileus + peri-operative aspiration** (delayed gastric emptying; multi-society guidance favors shared decision-making, ~1-wk hold for weekly formulations if holding chosen); **weight regain on stop** (~+14%, SURMOUNT-4); **lean-mass loss** (~25% of weight lost, roughly proportional, not selectively muscle-wasting); hypoglycemia mainly with insulin/secretagogues; injection-site reactions, AKI from volume depletion, retinopathy in at-risk diabetics.
- adverse effects (boxed/qualified): **thyroid C-cell / MTC BOXED WARNING is RODENT-based, NOT human-demonstrated** — label states the "human relevance … has not been determined"; rare postmarketing suicidal-ideation reports across the GLP-1 class (causality not established).
- contraindications:    personal/family history of **medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)** or **MEN 2**; known serious hypersensitivity to tirzepatide/excipients; **pregnancy** (not recommended — discontinue when recognized; may reduce oral-contraceptive efficacy via delayed gastric emptying).
- monitoring:           [[biomarkers/hba1c]] (glycemic response/safety), [[biomarkers/fasting-glucose]], renal function/hydration during persistent GI symptoms, weight, gallbladder + pancreatitis symptoms (lipase if suspected), retinal status in prior diabetic retinopathy, hypoglycemia if co-given with insulin/sulfonylureas.
- known interactions:   delayed gastric emptying may affect concomitant oral-drug (incl. oral-contraceptive) absorption; additive hypoglycemia with insulin/sulfonylureas.
