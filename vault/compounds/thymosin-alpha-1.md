# Thymosin Alpha-1

## Metadata
- class: peptide (endogenous thymic 28-aa N-acetylated prothymosin-α fragment; MW ~3108 Da; INN: thymalfasin; brand: Zadaxin; N-terminal acetylation required for full activity)
- evidence_tier: B — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]] (genuine RCT-grade + multi-country marketing approval: HBV registrational dossier with multiple RCTs, ~40% virological response vs ~9% control; vaccine-adjuvant signal replicated across independent US academic + Italian groups; >4,400 patients in controlled trials; post-market pharmacovigilance >25 years in 35+ countries; sepsis signal extinguished by TESTS Phase 3 2025; COVID/cancer evidence weaker; no longevity-endpoint RCT; concentration ~15–20%, below 70% flag)
- risk_tier: medium — characterized approved-drug safety record (zero drug-related SAEs across ETASS + 19-RCT meta) applies specifically to **GMP-manufactured Zadaxin/thymalfasin**, NOT to grey-market "Tα1" of unverified identity and purity (which may be the WADA-prohibited Tβ4/TB-500); injection-site reactions dominant AE; NOT FDA-approved; PCAC voted against 503A compounding Dec 2024; immune-modulation warrants caution in autoimmune disease and transplant recipients; grey-market US product carries purity and Tα1/Tβ4 identity risks; WADA status requires NADO verification for athletes
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-21

> **Read this first — honest framing.** Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1; thymalfasin; Zadaxin) sits in a fundamentally different category from BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c, or the Khavinson series. It has an INN, marketing approval in >35 countries, and a >25-year post-market pharmacovigilance record with zero drug-related SAEs in controlled trials. **What is genuinely established:** HBV virological response (~40% vs ~9% in registrational RCT), multi-country regulatory endorsement, and vaccine-adjuvant immunogenicity in elderly and dialysis populations across independent US and Italian research groups. **What is not established:** the sepsis mortality signal was definitively extinguished by the large 2025 TESTS Phase 3 trial (n~1,100; HR 0.99; p=0.93); COVID-19 evidence is not only observational — the largest cohort (Liu 2021, PMID 34408744, n=2,282, ~306 Tα1 recipients) found Tα1 associated with **worse outcomes** (adjusted OR 1.5 for non-recovery; ~20.2% vs ~13.7% in-hospital mortality); cancer evidence is exploratory meta-analysis; anti-aging/longevity use is an extrapolation beyond approved indications with no lifespan-endpoint RCT. **The exceptional safety record of Zadaxin applies to GMP-manufactured product only, not to grey-market product of unverified purity and identity.** **US-specific:** NOT FDA-approved; PCAC voted against 503A compounding in December 2024 — effectively off-limits for US compounders. Grey-market "Tα1" carries purity shortfalls (~6–15% below claimed) and a documented identity-confusion risk: Thymosin Beta-4 (TB-500/Tβ4) is a completely different peptide prohibited under WADA S2.3; Tα1 is NOT explicitly named. Full depth + all 39 citations: [[library/peptides/thymosin-alpha-1/research-report]].

## Mechanism
Tα1 is translated from the prothymosin-α (ProTα) precursor via lysosomal asparaginyl endopeptidase cleavage at Asn28-Gly29, confirming its status as a genuine endogenous peptide. Its primary mechanism is bidirectional immunomodulation operating through three overlapping nodes: (1) **TLR2 and TLR9 agonism on dendritic cells** — Romani et al. (2006) demonstrated TLR9-dependent IDO induction requiring type I IFN receptor co-signaling; Bozza et al. (2007) confirmed the TLR9/MyD88/IRF7 axis drives IFN-α and IFN-γ production in vivo, with protection abolished in TLR9-KO and MyD88-KO mice; (2) **Th1 polarization and CTL priming** — Tα1-stimulated DCs upregulate MHC class II, CD80, CD86, and IL-12 p70, biasing naive T cells toward Th1 and CD8+ cytotoxic phenotypes; and (3) **tolerogenic IDO/Treg tuning** — the same DCs that drive Th1 responses simultaneously generate Tregs and IL-10 via IDO, a bidirectionality that is mechanistically real and context-dependent. In immunodeficient or infection states the TLR9/Th1/CTL arm predominates; in hyperinflammatory states the IDO/Treg arm provides a brake. This context-dependency is hypothesized to underlie both Tα1's versatility and its exceptional safety profile. Endogenous Tα1 declines with age in parallel with thymic involution, providing the biological substrate for its proposed immunosenescence relevance. Antioxidant effects (reduced ROS, enhanced SOD/catalase/GPx in A549 cells) are documented in vitro but have not been isolated as a clinical endpoint.

## Evidence Summary
Strongest sources in weight order:

- [Chien et al. 1998] — n≈98 HBeAg-positive CHB patients, Taiwan; 3-arm RCT; 26-week Tα1 vs 52-week Tα1 vs observation; 18-month follow-up; complete virological response (HBV DNA + HBeAg clearance) 40.6% (26-week arm) vs 9.4% control; p=0.004; government-funded (Taiwan); no SciClone COI. `rct` [[library/peptides/thymosin-alpha-1/research-report]]
- [Gravenstein et al. 1989] — n=90 enrolled, n=85 analyzed (5 excluded due to unacceptable sera); mean age 77.3 yrs, long-term care; double-blind RCT; Tα1 900 µg/m² SC ×8 doses + influenza vaccine vs vaccine + placebo; significantly greater antibody titers p=0.023 in the 85-subject analyzed cohort (oldest subjects p=0.039); conducted at Medical College of Wisconsin/Brown University; no commercial sponsor; predates SciClone market entry. **Surrogate caveat:** antibody-titer endpoint, not infection-incidence. `rct` [[library/peptides/thymosin-alpha-1/research-report]]
- [Mutchnick et al. 1999] — n=97, Wayne State University; Phase III multicenter double-blind placebo-controlled US CHB RCT; 1.6 mg SC twice weekly ×6 months; 25% vs 13% sustained HBV DNA loss — numerically positive but NOT statistically significant (p<0.11); government/academic funding. Critical cautionary datum: not all CHB trials hit primary endpoint. `rct` [[library/peptides/thymosin-alpha-1/research-report]]
- [Chan et al. 2001] — 5-trial HBV meta-analysis, 353 patients; OR 2.67 (95% CI 1.25–5.68) for delayed virological response at 12 months; Chinese University of Hong Kong; independent of SciClone clinical program. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/thymosin-alpha-1/research-report]]
- [Carraro et al. 2012] — n=94 hemodialysis patients; open-label; Tα1 3.2 mg and 6.4 mg alongside adjuvanted pandemic H1N1 vaccine; both Tα1 doses achieved CHMP seroconversion/seroprotection criteria that vaccine-alone arm failed; no hematological/biochemical AEs; Italian academic group independent of Ershler/Gravenstein collaboration. `open_label` [[library/peptides/thymosin-alpha-1/research-report]]

Counter-evidence (load-bearing):
- [Wu et al./TESTS 2025] — **DEFINITIVE SEPSIS DEFLATION.** n=1,089 mITT; 22-centre double-blind Phase 3 RCT; primary outcome 28-day all-cause mortality: 23.4% Tα1 vs 24.1% placebo; HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.77–1.27); p=0.93. Null on ALL outcomes. SciClone co-funded; the sponsor-funded null trial cuts against commercial interest. A pre-specified age subgroup (≥60 yrs; HR 0.81; 95% CI 0.61–1.09) is hypothesis-generating only (CI crosses 1.0). **ETASS (2013):** n=361; **primary endpoint** 28-day mortality 26.0% vs 35.0% (RR 0.74, p=0.062 — did not reach significance); in-hospital mortality 28.7% vs 39.4% (p=0.032) is a **secondary endpoint from the full text**, not the primary result — marginal, not confirmatory. After TESTS, the prior positive sepsis meta-analyses (Liu 2016: 19 RCTs, RR 0.59; Gu 2025: 11 RCTs, OR 0.73) are largely neutralized by the Phase 3 null result. Current evidence does NOT support Tα1 for unselected sepsis. `rct`
- [Liu et al./COVID-19 2020] — n=9 treated patients; retrospective cohort; 30-day mortality 11.1% vs 30.0%; p=0.044. Small, non-randomized, severe confounding risk. Cannot be cited as efficacy evidence. `cohort`
- [Liu et al./COVID-19 2021, PMID 34408744] — **THE LARGEST COVID-19 COHORT AND ITS DIRECTIONAL-HARM FINDING.** n=2,282 (Tα1 n=306; controls n=1,976); multicenter retrospective; Hubei, China; China National Natural Science Foundation funding; no COI declared. **Tα1 was associated with WORSE outcomes:** adjusted OR 1.5 (95% CI 1.1–2.1, p=0.028) for non-recovery; in-hospital mortality ~20.2% (Tα1) vs. ~13.7% (control); ARDS ~33.9% vs. ~25.2%. The harm association persisted after multivariable adjustment; severity-channeling bias is likely but not eliminable. The COVID evidence is not merely "contradictory" — the single largest cohort showed an adjusted harm signal. **Tα1 cannot be recommended for COVID-19 or acute respiratory illness on existing data.** `cohort`

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
See [[library/peptides/thymosin-alpha-1/research-report]] §5 for full detail. Tα1 is an approved pharmaceutical outside the US with an established prescribing record; the prescribing-practice layer draws from label, RCT protocols, and the Dinetz & Lee 2024 practitioner review.

- **Compounding pharmacy data sheets:** Not applicable for US 503A pharmacies — PCAC voted against 503A Bulks List inclusion in December 2024; 503B outsourcing facilities may retain separate pathways (verify against current FDA guidance). Outside the US, Zadaxin is standard Rx.
- **Approved label dose (Zadaxin/SciClone [regulatory]):** 1.6 mg (equivalent to 900 µg/m²) SC twice weekly for 6–12 months (hepatitis B and C). Patients <40 kg: 40 µg/kg SC twice weekly. This is the only fully evidence-grounded dose.
- **Trial-derived alternative doses:** Vaccine adjuvant — 900 µg/m² SC twice weekly ×8 doses (Gravenstein 1989); 3.2–6.4 mg SC (Carraro 2012, hemodialysis). Sepsis — 1.6 mg SC twice daily ×5 days then once daily ×2 days (ETASS protocol). All from peer-reviewed sources.
- **Practitioner reference texts [practitioner_protocol]:** Dinetz & Lee 2024 (*Alternative Therapies in Health and Medicine*; tier-3 journal — contextual framing only, not primary evidence) summarizes safety and clinical usage across trial literature; dose conventions in longevity-context clinical practice align with 1.6 mg SC twice weekly. A4M/IFM-specific monograph not independently located.
- **Grey-market longevity convention [anecdote_aggregate]:** 0.5–1.6 mg SC twice weekly; no evidence base grounds these as distinct from the approved label dose — they converge on label dosing for uncertain reasons.
- **Gap between literature and practice:** For hepatitis indications, the approved label dose is directly evidence-grounded. For longevity/immunosenescence use, practitioners extrapolate from the approved hepatitis dose without a dedicated dosing RCT for that indication.

## Protocol
- dose: 1.6 mg SC twice weekly — derived from approved Zadaxin label (hepatitis B/C) and consistent with vaccine-adjuvant trial protocols; no separately validated longevity/immunosenescence-specific dose
- route: subcutaneous (SC) injection only; IV and oral routes not established; oral bioavailability negligible (peptide, GI proteolysis)
- timing: label does not specify time of day; twice-weekly SC injection at consistent intervals
- cycle: 6–12 months per approved label (hepatitis); for vaccine-adjuvant use, biweekly injections surrounding vaccination; longevity/maintenance cycle is extrapolation beyond approved label
- stack: n/a (library entry)
- source: Zadaxin (SciClone, approved prescription product) in 35+ approved markets; Chinese generic thymalfasin (post-patent, GMP-regulated); US: not 503A-compoundable as of 2026; grey-market research chemical (identity/purity risks — see Risk Profile)
- regulated status: approved Rx pharmaceutical in >35 countries (China, Italy, India, Philippines, Singapore, Russia, Argentina, Peru, others); NOT FDA-approved in the US; not 503A-compoundable after December 2024 PCAC vote; WADA: Tα1 NOT explicitly named (Tβ4/TB-500 IS prohibited S2.3 — these are different peptides; S0/S2 catch-all ambiguity requires NADO verification)
- cost: not assessed (Zadaxin prescription pricing varies by market; grey-market research-chemical pricing not assessed)

## Reconstitution (peptides only)
- supplied as: 1.6 mg lyophilized powder in single-dose vial (approved Zadaxin); grey-market product identity and purity unverified (no FDA manufacturing oversight; purity shortfalls ~6–15% below label reported in vendor ecosystem surveys — no independent analytical-lab corroboration)
- diluent: 1 mL sterile water for injection (Zadaxin label); reconstitute immediately before use; do not mix with other drugs in same syringe
- final concentration: 1.6 mg/mL (approved product); dose volume: 1 mL per dose
- storage: lyophilized vials: room temperature or refrigerated per label; reconstituted solution: use immediately; Tmax 1–2 hrs post-SC injection; t½ ~2 hrs; no accumulation on repeat dosing

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): injection-site erythema, mild discomfort, local tenderness (dominant AE class; mild, infrequent); transient injection-site atrophy (rare, repeated dosing); polyarthralgia with hand edema (rare; Zadaxin label); rash (rare; Zadaxin label); combination-therapy AEs (fever, fatigue, myalgia, neutropenia) attributed to interferon co-administration, not Tα1 monotherapy; zero drug-related SAEs in ETASS (n=361) and across 19-RCT sepsis meta-analysis (Liu 2016; n=1,354); TESTS Phase 3 (n=1,089) confirmed no significant Tα1–placebo safety difference
- adverse effects (anecdotal): injection-site reactions (consistent with literature; no novel grey-market-specific signal characterized)
- contraindications:
  - **Absolute:** hypersensitivity to thymalfasin or excipients; organ transplant recipients under deliberate immunosuppression (may worsen GVHD or cause engraftment failure in HSCT recipients)
  - **Relative:** active autoimmune disease (immune activation may exacerbate; monitor); pregnancy Category C (no adequate human data; use only if clearly needed); nursing mothers (excretion in breast milk unknown); pediatric use <18 years (safety/efficacy not established); co-administered immunosuppressants (effect may be attenuated or conflicting; avoid unless risk/benefit clearly favors)
- monitoring: see Risk/Monitoring section below
- known interactions: no established PK interactions; no CYP450 involvement; do not mix Tα1 with other drugs in the same syringe; no additive glucose-lowering or hemodynamic drug interactions characterized (distinct from GLP-1/AMPK-class compounds)

## Risk/Monitoring

**Monitoring rationale:** Tα1 is a bidirectional immunomodulator; in the context of anti-aging / immune-restoration use (the most plausible off-label application), the primary monitoring question is whether the immune balance shifts toward sustained inflammation or remains appropriately regulated. The following monitoring framework draws from ETASS biomarker endpoints and the practitioner-protocol literature:

| Parameter | Rationale | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| [[biomarkers/hs-crp]] | General systemic-inflammation safety screen; a rising hs-CRP warrants reassessment of any inflammatory process; **not a validated Tα1-specific pharmacodynamic marker** — the mechanistically-relevant ETASS biomarker for immune reconstitution was monocyte HLA-DR, not hs-CRP | Baseline; 6–8 weeks; end of cycle |
| Lymphocyte subsets (CD4:CD8 ratio, NK cell activity) | ETASS demonstrated Tα1-driven monocyte HLA-DR reconstitution; monitoring CD4/CD8 during use detects unexpected immunological shifts; meaningful for immunocompromised users | Baseline; every 3 months on-cycle |
| CBC with differential | Detect lymphocytopenia or unexpected shifts in leukocyte balance; combination-therapy AEs include neutropenia (attributed to interferon, but white-cell baseline is useful) | Baseline; every 3 months on-cycle |
| Injection-site inspection | Erythema, induration, atrophy — the dominant AE class; clinically monitor at each dose | Every injection |
| Liver function (if hepatitis indication) | Per co-managing hepatologist's protocol for HBV/HCV; Tα1 monotherapy is not hepatotoxic per trial record | Per hepatitis protocol |

**Stopping criteria:** Discontinue immediately on hypersensitivity reaction; discontinue on signs of hepatic decompensation (jaundice, encephalopathy, coagulopathy, ascites) — per Zadaxin label, a transient ALT flare in HBV patients is expected and does not require stopping, but frank decompensation does; reassess on signs of graft rejection or GVHD in transplant-adjacent use; reassess if hs-CRP rises unexpectedly above pre-treatment baseline in an otherwise stable patient; reassess on new signs of autoimmune flare.

## Decision Notes
Per [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]], Tα1 at evidence_tier=B / risk_tier=medium falls in a cell distinct from the experimental peptides — this compound has a genuine regulatory and clinical evidence pedigree. For the vaccine-adjuvant / immunosenescence use case (the most plausible healthy-aging application): the evidence base is genuine, replication is multi-group and multi-national, and the safety record is exceptional. The constraint is not evidence quality but access: in the US, obtaining it legally requires navigating a landscape where the approved Zadaxin product requires international sourcing and 503A compounding is off-limits, making the grey-market the practical default despite its purity and identity risks. For any hepatitis indication, this is a doctor-required decision in collaboration with a hepatologist; modern nucleos(t)ide analogues (entecavir, tenofovir) are first-line and the HBV evidence base was generated pre-modern-era. Sepsis use is not supported by current evidence (TESTS 2025 null). Anti-aging/longevity use is biologically plausible extrapolation from vaccine-adjuvant data, not a directly tested clinical application. **Critical disambiguation: Tα1 ≠ Tβ4.** Thymosin Alpha-1 and Thymosin Beta-4 (TB-500) share a name prefix and nothing else — different sequences, mechanisms, receptor targets, molecular weights, evidence bases, and regulatory statuses. Tβ4/TB-500 is explicitly WADA-prohibited (S2.3); Tα1 is not. Grey-market vendors and some community sources confuse these. Confirm the identity of any sourced product before use.
