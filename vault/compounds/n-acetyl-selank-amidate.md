# N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate

## Metadata
- class: peptide (grey-market synthetic modified analog; terminally modified derivative of Selank, the Russian-registered synthetic heptapeptide Tuftsin analog; sequence TKPRPGP with N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation)
- evidence_tier: D — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]]. > **Read this first — the thinnest-evidence entry in the peptide library.** N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is the N-terminally acetylated, C-terminally amidated grey-market analog of Selank (Russian-registered synthetic heptapeptide TKPRPGP, a stabilized Tuftsin analog developed at IMG RAS under Myasoedov). **This compound has zero analog-specific primary evidence.** Its entire putative evidence base is the Selank parent's literature — itself ~87–93% single-lineage (co-developer institutions only), small, Russian, open-label, and without any Western RCT — extrapolated by an assumption of pharmacological equivalence that has never been validated. The N-terminal acetylation that is supposed to improve stability simultaneously eliminates the free α-amine identified as part of the Tuftsin receptor-binding epitope, making retention of Selank's pharmacological activity mechanistically uncertain. Selank is Russia-registered but NOT FDA/EMA-approved; the analog is approved **nowhere** — grey-market research chemical only. WADA status: a 2026 MDPI review asserts prohibition under S2 or as an unapproved substance, but this was not independently confirmed against the primary WADA Prohibited List; S0 (Non-Approved Substances) is the more clearly applicable category — verify with NADO/GlobalDRO before any use by athletes. Full depth + every citation: [[library/peptides/n-acetyl-selank-amidate/research-report]].

## Mechanism
Selank's proposed mechanisms — the only mechanistic basis attributable to this analog by extrapolation — operate via four partially overlapping pathways. First, enkephalin-degrading enzyme inhibition: Selank inhibits aminopeptidase N and dipeptidyl peptidase IV (DPP-IV) in a dose-dependent manner (IC₅₀ ~15 µM in vitro), prolonging the plasma half-life of endogenous anxiolytic opioid peptides (leu- and met-enkephalin) without direct opioid receptor binding. Second, GABAergic modulation: rat frontal-cortex transcriptomics show Selank alters expression of 45 GABA-related genes at 1 hour (r = 0.86 vs. GABA effects), including upregulation of GABA-A ε and θ subunit genes and *Hcrt*; ex vivo hippocampal slice recordings show increased inhibitory postsynaptic current amplitude; notably, Selank does not produce the sedation or amnesia of direct GABA-A agonists, suggesting an indirect or allosteric mechanism. Third, serotonergic/monoaminergic effects: Selank enhances 5-HT metabolism in rat brainstem (an effect absent with Tuftsin alone, establishing the C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro extension as a required pharmacophore element for this pathway); monoamine effects are strain-dependent in mice. Fourth, BDNF upregulation: intranasal Selank increases hippocampal BDNF mRNA and protein in rats, and protects against ethanol-induced BDNF dysregulation in hippocampus and prefrontal cortex.

**Critical caveat — the N-acetyl modification and binding-epitope risk:** The free α-amine of the N-terminal Thr residue is identified in the Tuftsin receptor-binding literature as a key recognition element for Tuftsin-family receptor engagement. N-terminal acetylation eliminates this free amine. The stabilization benefit of the N-acetyl modification and the pharmacological mechanism of Tuftsin-family receptor engagement are therefore in direct structural conflict: the acetyl modification simultaneously protects against aminopeptidase attack AND may disrupt the very binding epitope that mediates the parent compound's activity. No analog-specific binding assay, functional assay, or in vivo pharmacological comparison between Selank and N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has been published. The equivalence assumption is not validated and may be mechanistically incorrect.

None of the above mechanism is demonstrated for the analog — it is extrapolated from the Selank parent's literature by assumption.

## Evidence Summary
Strongest sources for the extrapolated Selank parent (weight order); no analog-specific primary sources exist:

- Zozulia et al. 2008 (Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr, PMID 18454096) — n=62 (30 Selank vs 32 medazepam), randomized comparator, GAD + neurasthenia; Selank ≈ medazepam on HARS/CGI/Zung; additionally showed antiasthenic/psychostimulant effects and enkephalin half-life normalization; no sedation. Limitations: small n, Russian-language, no placebo arm, inventor co-authorship (Miasoedov NF + Seredenin SB listed), blinding not described in abstract, Russian regulatory context. `rct` tier-2. [[library/peptides/n-acetyl-selank-amidate/research-report]]
- Medvedev et al. 2014 (Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr, PMID 25176261) — n=60, phobic-anxiety + somatoform disorders, Selank vs phenazepam; Selank anxiolytic + mild nootropic; benefit persisted ~1 week post-dose (neuroadaptation hypothesis; expectation bias equally plausible in open-label design). Only independent (non-developer) clinical lineage. `open_label` tier-2.
- Siuniakov et al. 2015 (Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr, PMID 26356395) — n=70, Selank monotherapy vs Selank + phenazepam; Selank attenuated multiple phenazepam adverse effects (sedation, memory impairment, asthenia, sexual disturbance, orthostatic effects) without adding its own; UKU scale, SF-36, Stroop. Best tolerability data for the parent. `rct` tier-2 (blinding limited/unclear).
- Syunyakov et al. 2012 (Eur Psychiatry conf abstract, DOI 10.1016/S0924-9338(12)75281-1) — n=20, GAD, pharmaco-EEG; 40% rapid responders (HARS ~20→7 within 1–3 days), 60% gradual; rapid responders had higher baseline asthenic/cognitive burden and stronger acute-dose EEG reactivity. `open_label` tier-2; conference abstract; full text paywalled.

Counter-evidence and structural limits:
- **N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate — any study — n=0. No direct evidence exists.** Vendors state explicitly: "Studies on N-Acetyl Selank are lacking" and "no human studies have been performed on NA-Selank."
- Single-lineage concentration: ~87–93% of identifiable Selank primary literature carries IMG RAS and/or Zakusov Institute authorship — both co-developer institutions. This is not independent science.
- No Western RCT for Selank exists. No FDA/EMA safety or efficacy package.
- No analog-specific PK data (half-life, bioavailability, BBB penetration) for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate in any published source.
- Acute IV cardiovascular study in anaesthetized cats: Selank 300 µg/kg produced transient ~32% BP decrease in first 1–3 minutes post-injection. Route-specific and not directly translatable to intranasal/SC use but constitutes the only formal cardiovascular signal data.
- Route-pharmacodynamic interaction: intranasal Selank preferentially affects NMDA receptor density in frontal cortex (+23%); IP administration preferentially affects GABAergic binding (+38%); these are pharmacodynamically non-equivalent routes for the parent compound, rendering SC injection of the analog pharmacologically uncharacterized by any measure.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): **no direct safety data for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate**. Extrapolated from Selank parent (three small Russian trials totaling ~192 patients): Selank appears non-sedative and non-addictive in those trials; Siuniakov 2015 found no Selank-specific adverse effects when added to phenazepam. Acute IV cardiovascular study in anaesthetized cats (not directly translatable): transient ~32% BP decrease in first 1–3 minutes. No long-term safety data for the parent; no placebo-controlled AE reporting; all trials conducted in clinical anxiety patients not representative of healthy users.
- adverse effects (anecdotal): injection-site reactions, anxiety/agitation in some healthy users, sleep disruption (forum-level; qualitative only)
- contraindications:    pregnancy and lactation (no safety data; contraindicated by default for any unregistered investigational compound); known hypersensitivity to Selank, Tuftsin, or any component of grey-market formulations; concurrent benzodiazepines or other GABAergic CNS depressants without medical supervision (pharmacodynamic interaction plausible — Selank potentiates GABAergic signaling); active psychiatric conditions requiring monitored pharmacotherapy (self-administration of a research chemical in lieu of evidence-based care carries independent risk); baseline hypotension or concurrent antihypertensives (acute hypotensive signal in cardiovascular animal study)
- monitoring:           [[biomarkers/hs-crp]] — primary immune-sensitization/inflammation watch for the repeated-SC-injection immunogenicity risk: a rising hs-CRP trend on the injection schedule (absent intercurrent infection) is the earliest laboratory signal of an immune response to the terminally modified peptide; baseline before first injection, recheck at 4–6 weeks of repeated dosing. [[biomarkers/cortisol]] — HPA/stress-axis surveillance: morning serum or salivary cortisol provides a mechanism-adjacent objective correlate of HPA state; significant cortisol suppression or elevation would be a clinically relevant observation given the GABAergic/enkephalinergic mechanism axis, particularly in healthy users at risk for population-direction-reversal. [[biomarkers/hrv]] — autonomic/anxiety surveillance: resting HRV via consumer wearable tracks autonomic balance correlated with GABAergic tone and anxiety state; HRV deterioration in the context of use is an early objective complement to the subjective symptom diary. Also: GAD-7 symptom diary weekly; blood pressure checks (hypotensive signal); injection-site assessment at each use (signs of infection or immune reaction); CBC/CMP at baseline (reference values); worsening anxiety or panic symptoms.
- known interactions:   no characterized interactions; theoretical: pharmacodynamic addition with benzodiazepines and GABAergic agents; DPP-IV inhibition may have theoretical overlap with glucose metabolism effects (DPP-IV inhibitors are an antidiabetic drug class); monoamine effects are strain/individual-dependent in preclinical models
