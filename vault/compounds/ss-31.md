# SS-31

## Metadata
- class: peptide (Szeto-Schiller aromatic-cationic tetrapeptide; sequence D-Arg–2′,6′-dimethylTyr–Lys–Phe–NH₂; MW 639.8 g/mol; CAS 736992-21-5; also known as elamipretide / MTP-131 / Bendavia / Forzinity; FDA-approved brand Forzinity, 2025)

- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-20

> **Read this first — approved-for-Barth, experimental for everything else.** SS-31 (elamipretide / Forzinity) received FDA accelerated approval on October 30, 2025 (NDA 215244) as the **first approved treatment for Barth syndrome and the first FDA-approved mitochondria-targeting drug** — a genuine milestone. That approval rested on an **uncontrolled open-label extension in 8 completers** (median knee-extensor strength +63 N at week 168); the controlled TAZPOWER crossover missed both primary endpoints identically to placebo. Before and after that approval, **every randomized controlled trial of elamipretide missed its prespecified primary endpoint**: MMPOWER-3 (n=218, PMM), TAZPOWER crossover (n=12, BTHS), EMBRACE-STEMI (n=300), PROGRESS-HF/Daubert (n=71 each, HFrEF), and ReCLAIM-2 (n=176, geographic atrophy). The preclinical base is strong and multi-lab replicated. The off-label case for anti-aging, performance, or general mitochondrial enhancement is **biologically coherent but empirically untested in controlled human trials**. Grey-market "SS-31" is unverified as to identity, purity, and sterility. Full depth + every citation: [[library/peptides/ss-31/research-report]].

## Mechanism
SS-31 is a synthetic cell-permeable tetrapeptide that accumulates within the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) via a thermodynamic driving force generated by the large electrochemical gradient (−180 mV) and the IMM's cardiolipin (CL) enrichment (~20% of IMM phospholipids). The load-bearing mechanism — established by a 2022 eLife structure-activity relationship study — is **cardiolipin binding**, not the antioxidant properties of its 2′,6′-dimethyltyrosine (Dmt) residue; SS-20, which lacks Dmt and has no free-radical scavenging capacity, retains equivalent CL-binding and mitochondrial efficacy. SS-31 binds CL with a K~D~ of ~1.87 µM (low-µM affinity appropriate for a structural membrane interaction), modulating membrane surface electrostatics and preserving CL's physicochemical role at the IMM. The downstream consequences are: (1) maintenance of cristae architecture and ETC supercomplex integrity, (2) stabilization of cytochrome c in its electron-carrier conformation (preventing conversion to a CL-peroxidase mode that triggers lipid oxidation and apoptotic release), and (3) reduced electron leak at Complexes I and III with improved oxidative phosphorylation efficiency (P/O ratio). A key context-dependency rule established in the Chavez 2020 PNAS study: SS-31 improved respiration and reduced H₂O₂ in aged mouse heart mitochondria but **not** in young controls — benefit requires dysfunctional CL as substrate. All mechanistic measurements are from cell and animal models; direct intramitochondrial measurements in living humans have not been made. SS-31 does not restore CL content or alter the CL-to-monolysocardiolipin ratio; it preserves the structural/functional role of existing CL. See [[library/peptides/ss-31/research-report]] §1.

## Evidence Summary
Strongest sources (weight order):

- Thompson et al. 2024 (Genet Med, PMID 38602181) — TAZPOWER OLE; n=10 entrants, 8 completers at week 168; 40 mg SC QD elamipretide; median knee extensor strength +63 N (range 38–78 N) from OLE baseline of 124 N; 6MWT +96.1 m cumulative (P=0.003); cardiolipin ratio (MLCL/CL) improved and correlated with functional outcomes. **Basis for FDA accelerated approval.** `open_label` — uncontrolled OLE; practice effect, regression to mean, and survivor bias cannot be excluded; n=8 completing Barth syndrome patients.
- Thompson et al. 2021 (Genet Med, PMID 33077895) — TAZPOWER controlled crossover; n=12 males, BTHS; 40 mg SC QD × 12 weeks; co-primary endpoints: 6MWT +43 m and BTHS-SA fatigue −1.4 points **in both arms identically**; no between-group difference. `rct` — primary Phase 2/3 controlled result: BOTH ENDPOINTS MISSED.
- Siegel, Kruse, Percival et al. 2013 (Aging Cell, PMID 23692570) — 27-month-old C57BL/6 male mice; n=5–9/group; single SS-31 injection; fully restored in vivo mitochondrial ATP synthesis coupling (P/O ratio), reduced H₂O₂ emission, improved fatigue resistance within 1–8 days. `animal` — independent group (Rabinovitch/Marcinek, University of Washington; no Szeto affiliation).
- Chiao et al. 2020 (eLife, PMID 32648542) — 24-month-old C57BL/6 mice; n=7–10; SS-31 3 µg/g/day osmotic minipump × 8 weeks; significant reversal of diastolic dysfunction (E/A ratio restored), reduced mitochondrial superoxide, improved exercise capacity; systolic function unchanged (selective diastolic benefit). `animal` — independent group (Rabinovitch laboratory, Seattle).

Counter-evidence and limits:
- Karaa et al. 2023 (Neurology, PMID 37268435) — MMPOWER-3 Phase 3; n=218 adults, PMM; 40 mg SC QD × 24 weeks; 6MWT: elamipretide +15.3 m vs **placebo +17.4 m (placebo numerically superior)**; PMMSA fatigue: near-identical in both arms. Trial completed its 24-week blinded phase; the program was then terminated (no open-label extension) after the primary-endpoint failure. `rct` — BOTH CO-PRIMARY ENDPOINTS MISSED.
- Gibson et al. 2016 (Eur Heart J, PMID 26586786) — EMBRACE-STEMI; n=300, STEMI; IV elamipretide vs placebo; CK-MB AUC primary endpoint: NS; MRI infarct size numerically larger in active arm. `rct` — MISSED.
- Butler et al. 2020 (J Card Fail, PMID 32068002) — PROGRESS-HF; n=71, HFrEF; 40 mg SC × 28 days; LVESV change not significantly different between arms; no dose-response. `rct` — NOT MET.
- Cousins et al. 2024 (Ophthalmol Sci, PMID 39605874) — ReCLAIM-2; n=176, geographic atrophy; 40 mg SC QD × 48 weeks; both co-primary endpoints (LL BCVA, GA area) missed. `rct` — BOTH MISSED.
- IJMS 2026 rat HFpEF model: bioenergetic improvement (complex I/II respiration) did not translate into diastolic performance improvement when fibrosis and hypertrophy were co-dominant — establishing a functional boundary condition for the mechanism.
- Chavez 2020 (PNAS, PMID 32554501) — aged vs young mouse heart mitochondria: SS-31 improved aged mitochondria only; no effect in young/healthy mitochondria. Direct evidence that benefit requires dysfunctional CL as substrate.

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
See [[library/peptides/ss-31/research-report]] §4–§5 for full detail. Forzinity (elamipretide 80 mg/mL SC injectable, 280 mg/3.5 mL single-patient-use vial; 40 mg dose = 0.5 mL) is a licensed pharmaceutical available by specialty-pharmacy prescription **for Barth syndrome only**. Off-label compounding is in principle permissible under 503A since FDA approval, subject to state law and clinical need; no anti-aging or performance compounding protocols from licensed providers have been located.

- **Compounding pharmacy data sheets (off-label):** none located. The Forzinity prescribing information (NDA 215244, DailyMed) is the only verified pharmaceutical-grade clinical protocol and is BTHS-specific `[regulatory]`.
- **Practitioner reference texts:** no A4M/IFM/Seeds monograph for SS-31 in the anti-aging or performance context identified; the FDA prescribing information covers the approved indication only.
- **Consensus practitioner dose (BTHS, approved):** 40 mg SC once daily, weight ≥30 kg; 50% dose reduction (20 mg SC QD) for severe renal impairment (eGFR <30, not on dialysis) `[regulatory]`.
- **Off-label / grey-market convention:** no admissible consensus practitioner dose for off-label use exists. Grey-market vendor labeling circulates ungrounded dose numbers with no basis in human PK for non-BTHS populations `[anecdote_aggregate]`.
- **Gap:** there is no human trial-derived dose-response relationship for any off-label indication; the approved 40 mg dose was selected for BTHS patients with confirmed CL dysfunction and is not extrapolable to healthy aging adults without that defect.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): injection-site reactions (ISRs) are the dominant adverse effect class across all programs. In TAZPOWER controlled phase (n=12): 100% any ISR (vs 67% placebo); erythema 100% (vs 25%); pain 75% (vs 33%); induration 67% (vs 17%); pruritus 67% (vs 17%). In ReCLAIM-2 (n=117 elamipretide): any ISR 60% (vs 27% placebo); increased eosinophil counts 6% (vs 0%) — transient, mean peak at ~90 days, resolving within 6–12 months. 2 discontinuations in 168-week BTHS OLE due to ISRs. No treatment-related serious adverse events across any program. Two deaths in ReCLAIM-2 elamipretide arm (COVID-19, respiratory failure; assessed unrelated). No cardiovascular safety signal above placebo rates in cardiac trials.
- adverse effects (anecdotal): ISR complaints (injection-site discomfort, bruising, nodules) at community-forum level (`anecdote_aggregate`); qualitative only
- contraindications:    serious hypersensitivity to elamipretide or any Forzinity excipient (label contraindication); hypersensitivity reactions including serious allergic responses (rash, papular lesions, respiratory symptoms) may occur minutes to months after initiation; **reproductive safety:** no human pregnancy or lactation data exist; use not recommended in pregnancy; counsel women of childbearing potential accordingly; off-label use in reproductive-age women warrants explicit discussion of this gap
- monitoring:           [[biomarkers/egfr]] — baseline and periodically during treatment; dose reduce by 50% if eGFR falls below 30 mL/min (renal elimination pathway, AUC increased 125% at eGFR <30); injection site assessment at each visit (ISR severity and type); absolute eosinophil count (AEC) — PI-mandated, no dedicated biomarker page yet — baseline and at ≥30-day intervals (transient increase expected, peak ~day 90, resolves by 6–12 months); signs of hypersensitivity especially in first weeks
- known interactions:   no characterized drug-drug interactions; no hepatic CYP metabolism — CYP-mediated interactions not expected; renal impairment increases exposure without accumulation effect; theoretical interactions with other SC injectables at shared injection sites (rotation required)

## Trial Status
- linked experiment:    none
- baseline biomarkers:  n/a (library entry)
- expected response window: unknown for off-label indications. BTHS OLE signal emerged between weeks 52 and 168 of continuous treatment; MMPOWER-3 showed no signal at 24 weeks. ## Decision Notes
Per [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]], SS-31 at evidence_tier=C / risk_tier=experimental → **doctor-required / avoid for off-label performance, anti-aging, or cardiac protection use**: the FDA accelerated approval is real and clinically significant for Barth syndrome, where mechanistic target engagement (confirmed tafazzin → CL remodeling defect) is established at enrollment; the approval is specifically and exclusively for BTHS. For every other indication, every randomized controlled trial failed its prespecified primary endpoint, including two Phase 3-scale trials (MMPOWER-3 n=218, ReCLAIM-2 n=176). The "no effect in young/healthy mitochondria" animal finding (Chavez 2020) is a direct mechanistic prediction that benefit scales with CL dysfunction at baseline — making healthy aging adults the population least likely to respond. Five mechanistic explanations for the trial/preclinical translation gap exist (duration mismatch, population heterogeneity, pharmacological masking, bioenergetic rescue insufficient when structural disease dominates, absent CL biomarker) — they are coherent but not validated, and none constitutes a basis for off-label use decisions. The preclinical base (Rabinovitch/Marcinek aging muscle and cardiac, Allen cristae imaging, Patel swine, Russo TazKD, Zhao/Li neuroinflammation) is notably robust and multi-lab replicated — the compound warrants monitoring as NuPOWER and the BTHS confirmatory trial results emerge, but no current controlled human evidence supports off-label use.
