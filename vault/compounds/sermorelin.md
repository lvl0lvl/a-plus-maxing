# Sermorelin

## Metadata
- class: peptide — **GRF(1-29)NH2** (equivalently GHRH(1-29)NH2), the amidated synthetic 29-amino-acid peptide reproducing the first 29 N-terminal residues of human GHRH; the **prototype of the GRF(1-29) class** of GHRH-receptor agonists. Brand **Geref / Geref Diagnostic** (Serono / EMD Serono); **withdrawn from the US market ~2008.**
- evidence_tier: B — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]]. Lands at B because sermorelin has a **genuine FDA-approval history** (Geref) with **pediatric idiopathic-GHD growth-velocity efficacy** (pivotal Thorner 1996) plus a **validated diagnostic use** (GHRH-stimulation testing), lifting it above the unapproved C-tier peptides — but is **not** S/A because (i) the approval is **withdrawn**, (ii) the evidenced population is **pediatric GHD**, and (iii) the popular **adult anti-aging / body-composition** use rests only on four small old (1992–2006) aging-adult GHRH(1-29) studies at the **biomarker level** (GH/IGF-1), not robust clinical outcomes.
- risk_tier: medium — favorable, monitorable short-term safety (it augments endogenous pulsatile GH under intact feedback rather than imposing flat exogenous GH; dominant AE = minor injection-site reaction), held above "low" by GH-axis / IGF-1 class considerations and the **absence of any long-term controlled adult safety dataset**. Doctor-required.

> **Read this first.** Sermorelin is **GRF(1-29)NH2** — the **single shortest synthetic GHRH fragment that retains the full biological activity of the parent hormone**, and therefore the **prototype of the GRF(1-29) class**. (The popular "CJC-1295 without DAC / Mod GRF 1-29" is a *stabilized sermorelin analogue* on this same 29-residue backbone — keep the family straight.) Sermorelin **was an FDA-approved drug, marketed as Geref** by Serono/EMD Serono, for **(a) diagnostic testing of pituitary GH-secretory capacity** (Geref Diagnostic, 1990) and **(b) treatment of idiopathic GH deficiency in children with growth failure** (Geref, 1997). It was then **WITHDRAWN from sale ~2008 for COMMERCIAL reasons** — and the FDA's **March 4, 2013 Federal Register determination (Doc. 2013-04827) explicitly states it was NOT withdrawn for reasons of safety or effectiveness.** "Withdrawn" here means *the sponsor stopped selling it* — it does **not** mean banned, and it does **not** mean found unsafe. **CRITICAL DEMARCATION:** the evidenced and approved use is **pediatric GHD + diagnostic**. The popular **adult anti-aging / body-composition / sleep / "GH-optimization" / athletic** use is **OFF-LABEL** and rests only on a small cluster of old aging-adult GHRH(1-29) studies (Corpas 1992, Vittone 1997, Khorram 1997, Vitiello 2006) showing **biomarker change (GH/IGF-1)** plus, in aging adults only, modest skin-thickness / sex-restricted lean-mass / cognition signals — **not** robust clinical anti-aging outcomes. Do **NOT** credit tesamorelin's body-composition/cognition data (Baker 2012) or recombinant GHRH-1,44 walking/visceral-fat results to sermorelin — different molecules. Geref is now off-market, but sermorelin is **heavily §503A-compounded** (it qualifies via its prior-approval history — categorically different from the never-approved peptides), and it was **NOT** among the April 2026 FDA peptide actions (the removed-12 / PCAC-seven). It is **prohibited in sport at all times** under WADA **S2.2.4 (named)**. Full depth + citations: [[library/peptides/sermorelin/research-report]]. ## Mechanism
Sermorelin engages the **growth-hormone-releasing-hormone receptor (GHRH-R)** — a class-B (secretin-family) Gs-coupled GPCR on pituitary somatotrophs — and drives the canonical cAMP → PKA → Ca²⁺ cascade, evoking both acute exocytosis of stored GH and sustained GH gene transcription/synthesis. Released GH then drives hepatic IGF-1 production. Because sermorelin acts one step upstream at the pituitary (rather than supplying hormone directly, like recombinant GH), it **augments the body's own physiologic, pulsatile GH secretion under intact negative feedback** and depends on a responsive somatotroph population. Its defining PK limitation is a **very short half-life (~11–12 min)** driven by DPP-IV degradation (cleavage to the inactive GHRH(3-44)-NH2 metabolite) — exactly what the stabilized analogues (CJC-1295 / Mod GRF 1-29) and the (1-44)-based tesamorelin were engineered to overcome, and the rationale for stacking with a mechanistically distinct GHRP/ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist. See report §1.

## Evidence Summary
- **Thorner et al. (Geref International Study Group) 1996** (PMID 8772599) — n=110 prepubertal **GH-deficient children**, GHRH-(1-29) 30 µg/kg SC nightly, open-label; height velocity 4.1 → 8.0 cm/yr at 6 mo (74% "good response"). **Pivotal pediatric-GHD efficacy.** `open_label`
- **Prakash & Goa 1999** (PMID 18031173) — principal review of Geref's clinical dossier; once-daily SC 30 µg/kg effective in some prepubertal idiopathic-GHD children, sustained ≥12 mo, but **less than recombinant somatropin**; diagnostic use validated. `mechanism_review`
- **Ranke et al. 1986** (PMID 2880720) — GRF(1-29)NH2 1 µg/kg IV in 131 children (45 idiopathic GHD); validates the **diagnostic** GHRH-stimulation test. `cohort`
- **Aging-adult GHRH(1-29) cluster (INVESTIGATIONAL, off-label):** Corpas 1992 (PMID 1379256, biomarker reversal), Vittone 1997 (GH↑ but DEXA/IGF-1 flat), Khorram 1997 ([Nle27] variant; skin↑ both sexes, lean-mass/insulin/libido↑ **men only**), Vitiello 2006 (PMID 16399214, n=89 aging-adult **cognition** RCT). Biomarker-level + modest aging-adult signals only. `rct`

Counter-evidence / absent:
- **No large modern RCT of sermorelin in HEALTHY adults** for anti-aging, hypertrophy, fat loss, or athletic performance. Adult evidence is entirely old, small (n=9–89), in aging adults with low baseline IGF-1.
- **Do NOT credit to sermorelin:** Baker 2012 (PMID 22869065) cognition + body-comp (fat −7.4%/lean +3.7%) = **tesamorelin**; the walking/stair-climb/visceral-fat results = **recombinant GHRH-1,44** (full-length). Different molecules (report §3.2).

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): Geref-label / trial-derived (pediatric GHD + diagnostic): **local injection-site reaction** (≈1 in 6 children; 3/350 discontinued); transient facial flushing and injection-site pain most common (Prakash & Goa); other systemic AEs <1% each — headache, flushing, dysphagia, dizziness, hyperactivity, somnolence, urticaria. IV diagnostic bolus: facial flushing, injection-site pain, nausea, headache, vomiting, dysgeusia, pallor, chest tightness (transient). Hypothyroidism 6.5% on label. Anti-GRF antibodies common but clinically inconsequential (often revert; no effect on growth; no anaphylaxis reported).
- adverse effects (anecdotal): not separately grounded — no efficacy/AE-rate claims from practice/vendor sources.
- contraindications:    known hypersensitivity to sermorelin/excipients; pregnancy & lactation (Category C, no human data); active malignancy (class precaution — GH/IGF-1 trophic effects; tesamorelin label contraindicates active malignancy + HPA-axis disruption); GH-axis cautions (glucocorticoids/somatostatin/dopamine/antithyroid agents blunt response). Class-extrapolated AEs at supraphysiologic dosing: fluid retention (edema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel), glucose intolerance, IGF-1 elevation/neoplasm precaution.
- monitoring:           [[biomarkers/igf-1]] (and IGFBP-3) — keep IGF-1 **within** the age/sex reference range, not above; fasting glucose / HbA1c; thyroid function (6.5% hypothyroidism); injection-site tolerability.
- known interactions:   glucocorticoids, somatostatin/analogs, dopamine agents, antithyroid drugs, cyclooxygenase inhibitors can blunt the GH response / confound diagnostic testing.
