# Ipamorelin

## Metadata
- class: peptide (synthetic pentapeptide; NNC 26-0161; selective ghrelin-receptor [GHSR-1a] agonist / GH-secretagogue; Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2)
- evidence_tier: C — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]] (clean acute human pharmacology, but the only human EFFICACY program failed Phase 2 and was discontinued; preclinical-dominant; ~80% single-lineage)
- risk_tier: experimental — unapproved (development discontinued), GH-axis risks, no controlled chronic human safety data
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-20

> **Read this first.** Ipamorelin is a synthetic pentapeptide ghrelin-receptor (GHSR-1a) agonist — "the first **selective** GH secretagogue," raising GH **without** meaningfully raising ACTH/cortisol/prolactin (a claim grounded primarily in **ANIMAL** data, Raun 1998). It was developed by **Novo Nordisk → Helsinn**; the only clinical-efficacy program (**postoperative ileus**) **FAILED its Phase 2 primary endpoint** (Beck 2014, n≈114, 25.3 vs 32.6 h, p=0.15) → **development discontinued**. There is **no approved indication, no Phase 3, and zero human efficacy data** for the muscle/fat-loss/anti-aging uses it's marketed for. Human PK is **IV-only** (t½ ~2 h); SC (the real-world route) has **no human PK**. Concentration/provenance is **~80% Novo-Nordisk-lineage** (≥70% single-lineage flag). FDA: **never approved**; removed from 503A interim Category 2 ~Sept 2024; **PCAC voted AGAINST** adding it to the 503A bulks list (Oct 29 2024; fluid-retention/hyperglycemia/CHF concerns); **NOT** part of the Apr-2026 FR action. WADA: **prohibited at all times, S2.2** (GH secretagogues — named on the list). Full depth + citations: [[library/peptides/ipamorelin/research-report]]. Operator fields blank — goal-agnostic library entry.

## Mechanism
Selective GHSR-1a (ghrelin receptor) agonist → Gαq/11–PLC–Ca²⁺ → pulsatile GH release from somatotrophs, with downstream IGF-1. Its defining feature is **selectivity**: in animals it released GH without significantly raising ACTH/cortisol/prolactin/FSH/LH/TSH (Raun 1998), unlike GHRP-6/GHRP-2 — but this selectivity is **animal-grounded, not human-proven**. See report §2.

## Evidence Summary
- Raun 1998 (PMID 9849822) — discovery/selectivity; rat + swine; EC50 1.3 nM, no ACTH/cortisol at >200× GH ED50. `animal`
- Gobburu 1999 (PMID 10496658) — human Phase 1 PK/PD (n=40, IV); single GH pulse; t½ ~2 h. `open_label`
- Svensson 2000 (PMID 10828840) — rat bone: increased bone CONTENT/area, volumetric BMD UNCHANGED (NOT "bone density"). `animal`

Counter-evidence / honest negatives:
- Beck 2014 (PMID 25331030) — postoperative-ileus Phase 2 RCT, n≈114, **primary endpoint FAILED** (p=0.15); program discontinued (also NCT00672074, NCT01280344, no positive results). `rct`
- Johansen 1999 (PMID 10373343) — intermittent dosing did NOT raise total IGF-1 in rats (contradicts vendor IGF-1 claims). `animal`
- No human efficacy for athletic/anti-aging use; ~80% of efficacy primaries are Novo-Nordisk-lineage.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): Beck 2014 RCT — generally well tolerated short-course IV (TEAE 87.5% drug vs 94.8% placebo, no serious compound-related AEs), but only one short human RCT; no chronic-SC human safety data. GH-axis class risks: edema, arthralgia, carpal-tunnel symptoms, impaired glucose tolerance/insulin resistance; FDA (PCAC) flagged fluid retention, hyperglycemia, CHF concern.
- adverse effects (anecdotal): injection-site reactions, transient flushing/lightheadedness, increased hunger (`anecdote_aggregate`, qualitative).
- contraindications:    active/uncontrolled diabetes or glucose intolerance; active or recent malignancy (sustained-IGF-1 association — associational, not shown for exogenous ipamorelin); heart failure / fluid-retention states; pregnancy/lactation (no data).
- monitoring:           **IGF-1** [[biomarkers/igf-1]] — keep within age/sex reference range (NOT minimized; U-shaped IGF-1–mortality); fasting glucose + HbA1c; clinical edema/carpal-tunnel surveillance. (No ipamorelin-specific validated threshold.)
- known interactions:   other GH secretagogues/GHRH analogues (additive GH/IGF-1); glucose-lowering drugs (GH counter-regulation).
