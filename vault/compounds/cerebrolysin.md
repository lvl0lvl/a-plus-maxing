> **Read this first — honest framing.** Cerebrolysin (Ever Neuro Pharma, Austria; FPF-1070) is a porcine-brain-derived peptide hydrolysate — a complex, undefined biological mixture, not a single molecular entity — administered IV or IM only. Five load-bearing facts define its honest status. **(1) Composition:** 638 peptides by LC-MS profiling, but NO detectable BDNF, GDNF, NGF, or CNTF fragments; "neurotrophic mimicry" describes functional analogy via uncharacterized cryptic peptides, NOT growth-factor delivery; the active pharmacophore is undefined and batch reproducibility is inherently uncertain. **(2) Efficacy:** The pivotal CASTA (n=1,070; acute ischemic stroke) was NULL on its primary endpoint. The independent 2023 Cochrane (7 RCTs, n=1,773) finds no demonstrated mortality benefit AND a statistically significant increase in non-fatal serious adverse events at the 30 mL dose (RR 2.39, 95% CI 1.10–5.23; moderate-certainty evidence); the death-or-dependency primary endpoint was absent from ALL 7 included trials — a selective-outcome-reporting concern. The vascular dementia Cochrane found a positive cognitive signal but graded ALL outcomes very-low-certainty (I²=86%). TBI (CAPTAIN program, n=185) shows small-to-medium effects but is small and unreplicated. AD evidence rests predominantly on manufacturer-associated meta-analyses. **(3 & 4) Two integrity axes — BOTH load-bearing:** (a) Sponsor/author concentration: ~60–70% of pivotal evidence manufacturer-sponsored/affiliated; trials cluster exclusively in Russia, Eastern Europe, and China (the drug's own commercial registration geography); ZERO independent North American, UK, or Scandinavian trials; the same small investigator network (Mureșanu, Heiss, Bajenaru, Guekht, Moessler) recurs across trials and advisory roles with documented EVER financial relationships. (b) Research misconduct: Eliezer Masliah received an NIH ORI misconduct finding (September 2024, figure fabrication across 132 papers, 8 Cerebrolysin-specific); two Rockenstein/Masliah AD-model papers were formally retracted (2025); EVER's ex-GM Moessler co-authored 57 Cerebrolysin papers and co-founded a company with Masliah; the anti-amyloid/anti-tau AD-mechanism evidence is substantially compromised at its primary evidentiary root. **(5) Regulatory/grey-market:** Approved via national procedures in ~50 countries (Russia, Austria, China, South Korea, Eastern Europe) — NOT FDA-approved, NOT EMA-centrally-authorized; patents expired, removing commercial incentive for an FDA Phase III program; in the US/UK/Canada/Australia, only grey-market sourcing (Russian online pharmacies) is available, with unverifiable cold-chain integrity, counterfeiting risk, and no regulated prescribing pathway. ALL safety data derive from elderly stroke/dementia populations under physician supervision — NOT healthy adults. **The honest bottom line:** A genuine nationally-approved drug with a large clinical literature, whose independent synthesis is null or cautious, structurally confounded by manufacturer concentration, research-misconduct compromise, and healthy-adult population mismatch — NOT a validated neuro-regenerative therapy for healthy-adult cognitive enhancement. Full depth + 50 citations: [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]].

## Mechanism
Cerebrolysin's dominant proposed mechanism is **neurotrophic factor mimicry** — uncharacterized cryptic peptides act as partial functional mimetics of endogenous neurotrophic factors (BDNF, GDNF, NGF, CNTF), activating their downstream intracellular signaling cascades (PI3K/Akt/GSK-3β, Trk-receptor-mediated pathways) without sharing primary sequences. This is functional analogy, not direct growth-factor delivery: comprehensive LC-MS profiling (Gevaert et al. 2015; 638 peptides identified from commercially obtained product) found NO peptide fragments corresponding to BDNF, GDNF, NGF, or CNTF. Three candidate bioactive peptides have been identified by nanoLC-MS (LLNLQPPPR and LSPSLRLP from myelin basic protein; WPFPR from tubulin beta chain), but their causal roles remain unconfirmed. The preparation has been shown to upregulate endogenous BDNF, VEGF, and IGF-1 in rodent brain tissue, suppress TNF-α, and inhibit the pro-apoptotic enzyme calpain (mu- and m-calpain; non-competitive inhibition via calpastatin-like fragments). Additional mechanistic evidence supports Sonic hedgehog (Shh) pathway-mediated neurogenesis in ischemia models, and MAP-2 cytoskeletal stabilization through degradation prevention in excitotoxicity models. **Integrity constraint:** Claims specifically concerning amyloid/tau reduction via GSK-3β and Alzheimer's disease neurogenesis are grounded primarily in the Masliah-cluster literature, now under a documented ORI misconduct finding and including two formally retracted preclinical papers (Rockenstein et al. 2014, 2015); these claims are provisional and should not be treated as established. The calpain inhibition, MAP-2 stabilization, and Shh-neurogenesis mechanism evidence in ischemia models has independent integrity and is not part of the Masliah cluster.

## Evidence Summary
Strongest sources in weight order:

- [Ziganshina et al. 2023 — Cochrane 7th update] — n=1,773; 7 RCTs; meta-analysis; acute ischemic stroke; all-cause death RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.65–1.41; moderate-certainty; no benefit); non-fatal SAE RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23; moderate-certainty; statistically significant increase); 30 mL × 10-day regimen: non-fatal SAE RR 2.87 (95% CI 1.24–6.69); death-or-dependency not reported across all 7 trials; authors conclude "probably no beneficial effect on preventing all-cause death" and flag potential non-fatal SAE increase; 3/7 trials received direct manufacturer support. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [Heiss et al. 2012 — CASTA] — n=1,070; RCT; acute ischemic stroke; Asia; 30 mL IV × 10 days; primary endpoint (global directional test: mRS/BI/NIHSS at 90 days): **NULL** in overall population; post-hoc severe-stroke subgroup (NIHSS >12): ~2-fold mortality trend favoring Cerebrolysin (hypothesis-generating only, not pre-registered primary, CI crossed unity); largest single RCT; lowest-disclosed manufacturer involvement but co-investigators (Bornstein, Heiss) are documented EVER collaborators. `rct` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [Cui et al. 2019 — Cochrane vascular dementia] — n=597; 6 RCTs; meta-analysis; vascular dementia; cognitive outcomes: SMD 0.36 (95% CI 0.13–0.58; but ALL outcomes graded very-low-certainty); I²=86%; all funded trials received pharmaceutical industry support; publication bias flagged explicitly; authors: results "not definitive"; not recommended for routine use. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [Mureșanu et al. 2016 — CARS] — n=205 mITT-LOCF; RCT; post-stroke motor recovery; 30 mL IV × 21 days + rehabilitation; ARAT day-90: MW=0.71, p<0.0001; large statistically significant effect; funded by EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH; 3 employee co-authors; requires independent replication. `rct` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [Vester et al. 2021 — CAPTAIN prospective meta-analysis] — n=185; 2 RCTs; moderate-severe TBI; Day 90: MWcombined=0.60, OR=1.77, p=0.0146; small-to-medium effect; entirely manufacturer-affiliated authorship; no independent Cochrane review. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [Gauthier et al. 2015 — AD meta-analysis] — 6 RCTs; Alzheimer's disease; mild-to-moderate; cognitive function at 4 weeks: SMD −0.40 (p=0.0031); global clinical impression significant at 4 and 6 months; data provided by EBEWE/EVER Neuro Pharma; preclinical mechanism evidence substantially compromised by Masliah-cluster misconduct. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [No current independent non-Cochrane synthesis from a whitelisted journal — ineligible candidate removed] The most recent candidate (Patel PN et al. 2025, 14 RCTs, PMID 41018475) is published in *Cureus* (off-whitelist; inadmissible). The Cochrane review and the CEREHETIS post-hoc analysis remain the primary independent syntheses. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]

Counter-evidence (load-bearing):

- [Ziganshina et al. 2023 — Cochrane non-fatal SAE signal] — RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23) at the standard dose; this is the primary independent safety concern. Counterpoint: Strilciuc et al. 2021 (12 RCTs, n=2,202) found no significant overall AE difference (all-cause AE rate RR ~0.98) and NF-SAE RR 1.18 (95% CI 0.75–1.86; non-significant) — a broader trial set including lower doses than the Cochrane's 3-trial, 30-mL-dose stratum. The discrepancy is explained in detail in the research-report (§5.1): the two analyses differ in trial subset and dose-stratum; the Cochrane signal at the labeled 30 mL dose is the more conservative, safety-relevant read. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [CASTA null primary] — the largest single trial (n=1,070) with the lowest disclosed manufacturer involvement returned a null primary endpoint; the manufacturer-associated trials showing positive signals are smaller and subject to small-study inflation (CASTA is a funnel-plot negative outlier in the Bornstein 2018 meta-analysis). `rct` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [Rockenstein et al. 2014 — RETRACTED December 2025] — tauopathy/mitochondria mouse model; figure manipulation; this paper grounds several mechanism claims about tau pathology and should not be cited for any efficacy claim. `animal` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]
- [Rockenstein et al. 2015 — RETRACTED March 2025] — Pick's disease Tau transgenic model; image manipulation; Masliah misconduct cluster; same treatment as above. `animal` [[library/peptides/cerebrolysin/research-report]]

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
Cerebrolysin is a prescription-only, clinician-administered biologic drug in all approving jurisdictions. IV infusion requires clinical settings with monitoring capability. There is no validated compounding or oral preparation.

- **Approved prescribing information [regulatory]:** Approved countries: Austria (originator SmPC), Russia, China, South Korea, Eastern Europe, Southeast Asia. Typical licensed doses in disease populations: acute ischemic stroke — 10–30 mL IV/day × 10–21 days; Alzheimer's disease — 10–30 mL IV/day × 4-week cycles with follow-up at 6 months; TBI (CAPTAIN program) — 50 mL IV × 10 days then additional cycles. Administration: IV infusion over 20–60 minutes (minimum to reduce warmth/heat sensation AE) or IM injection. All dosing information reflects disease-population clinical trial protocols; no dose has been established or validated for healthy adults.
- **Compounding pharmacy data sheets [compounding_data_sheet]:** No US 503A/503B compounding pathway exists; Cerebrolysin is not an FDA-regulated drug substance and has no USP monograph; no authorized compounding basis.
- **Practitioner reference texts [practitioner_protocol]:** Grey-market longevity and nootropic community sources reference dose ranges borrowed from clinical trial protocols; these carry `anecdote_aggregate` status only and are NOT cited in this entry.
- **Originator-group recommended dose:** EVER Neuro Pharma's prescribing information specifies the disease-population doses cited above; no healthy-adult dose has been established.
- **Consensus practitioner dose:** None exists in any evidence-grounded, whitelisted source for healthy-adult use.
- **Gap between literature and practice:** All literature doses are from clinical trials in elderly stroke/dementia/TBI populations under physician supervision. Community grey-market use extrapolates from these protocols without any pharmacodynamic or safety basis in healthy adults.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature):
  - **Non-fatal serious adverse events (SAE signal):** RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23) at standard 30 mL dose vs. placebo in Cochrane 2023 (3 trials, 1,335 participants; moderate-certainty evidence); at 30 mL × 10-day regimen: RR 2.87 (95% CI 1.24–6.69); nature of the individual SAEs not enumerated in Cochrane summary; this is the single most important safety signal in the evidence base
  - **Hypersensitivity/anaphylactoid reactions:** skin reactions, local inflammatory reactions, headache, neck pain, limb pain, fever, dyspnea, chills, shock-like state; documented in SmPC and case reports; clinical infusion-monitoring mandatory
  - **Sensation of warmth/heat:** common injection/infusion reaction; reduced by slow infusion over 20–60 minutes
  - **Agitation, insomnia, dizziness, nausea, headache, tremor, injection-site pain:** reported in disease-population trial safety analyses (elderly stroke/dementia populations; extrapolation to healthy adults unvalidated)
  - **Increased seizure frequency:** documented in epilepsy populations; contraindicated in active seizure disorders
  - All safety data derive from elderly, high-comorbidity disease populations under physician supervision; no controlled AE data exist for healthy adults
- adverse effects (anecdotal): rapid-injection reactions (warmth, sweating, palpitations, dizziness, hypertension) and rare anaphylactic reactions documented in grey-market community self-administration reports; these carry `anecdote_aggregate` tag only
- contraindications:
  - **Absolute:** known hypersensitivity to any component including porcine-derived products; epilepsy/status epilepticus/grand mal convulsions; severe renal impairment (low-MW peptide constituents may accumulate — see [[biomarkers/egfr]] for renal baseline screen); pregnancy and breastfeeding (no reproductive safety data); concurrent MAO inhibitors (SmPC contraindication)
  - **Relative:** caution with antidepressants (SmPC); porcine-CNS allergy; grey-market product of unknown cold-chain provenance (add endotoxin/contamination risk on top of base risk profile); self-administration without physician supervision (the SAE signal was observed in monitored clinical settings — unmonitored use at home cannot invoke those controls)
- monitoring: see Risk/Monitoring section below
- known interactions: MAO inhibitors (contraindicated per SmPC); antidepressants (caution); no characterized CYP450 interactions (peptide preparation, not hepatically metabolized via CYP enzymes); additive central-nervous-system effects with other peptide preparations theoretically possible but uncharacterized

## Risk/Monitoring

**Monitoring rationale.** The two clinically significant ongoing risk concerns are: (1) the non-fatal SAE signal at the standard dose — mechanistically unresolved, requiring clinical observation throughout each infusion course; and (2) hypersensitivity/anaphylactoid reactions — an infusion-period risk requiring immediate-response capability. A third concern is renal accumulation of low-MW peptide constituents in patients with impaired renal function, addressed at baseline with [[biomarkers/egfr]] screening. Monitoring is drawn from the approved SmPC, the Cochrane safety analysis, and clinical trial protocol requirements.

| Parameter | Rationale | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| [[biomarkers/egfr]] | Severe renal impairment is a SmPC contraindication; low-MW peptide constituents may accumulate in reduced renal clearance; eGFR baseline confirms renal sufficiency before any IV course | Before initiating any treatment course; repeat if clinical renal status changes |
| Clinical infusion monitoring (hypersensitivity) | Hypersensitivity/anaphylactoid reactions (skin changes, dyspnea, chills, hemodynamic instability, shock-like state) documented in SmPC; IV/IM biologics from porcine-CNS source carry sensitization risk; monitoring required during and for 20–60 minutes after each infusion | Each infusion/injection; physician or qualified clinical personnel present |
| Neurological exam / seizure monitoring | Increased seizure frequency documented; contraindicated in active epilepsy; baseline seizure history confirmation required | Before initiating; at each clinical visit during course |
| Functional scale monitoring (disease populations) | No surrogate biochemical endpoint validates neurotrophic effect in disease populations; NIHSS, mRS, Barthel Index, ADAS-Cog guide clinical dosing decisions in the indication-specific trial protocols | Per clinical trial protocol schedule if used in approved indication |

**Stopping criteria:** Immediately discontinue on: any hypersensitivity or anaphylactoid reaction (skin changes, dyspnea, chills, hemodynamic instability) — seek emergency management; new-onset or increased seizure frequency; deteriorating renal function (eGFR decline on course); no evidence of clinical benefit by end of approved-protocol follow-up window; any serious adverse event of unexplained etiology occurring during or after infusion; product with unverifiable cold-chain provenance (discard, do not administer).

## Decision Notes
Per [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]], Cerebrolysin at evidence_tier=B / risk_tier=medium falls in the **doctor-required** cell for any therapeutic use. For the approved disease indications in approving countries: national regulatory approval, a large RCT corpus, and plausible preclinical mechanisms in ischemia models exist — but the independent Cochrane synthesis is null or very-low-certainty, and the non-fatal SAE signal at the standard dose is not resolved. Use in approved indications should be confined to physician-supervised clinical settings with monitoring capability. **For healthy-adult cognitive enhancement: there is no evidence base whatsoever.** The entire safety and efficacy literature derives from elderly stroke/dementia/TBI populations; no healthy-adult RCT exists; the grey-market sourcing channel adds cold-chain, counterfeiting, endotoxin, and prion-theoretical risks on top of the already-constrained evidence; and the primary Alzheimer's mechanism narrative is now substantially compromised by documented research misconduct and retractions. This is the **most structurally confounded entry in the library** — not because Cerebrolysin is a fringe compound (it is not; it has genuine regulatory approvals and a large clinical trial history), but because the independent synthesis of that literature is null-to-cautious while the manufacturer-concentrated positive literature dominates the visible evidence landscape.
