# Hexarelin

## Metadata
- class: peptide — synthetic **hexapeptide growth-hormone-releasing peptide (GHRP)**; an agonist of the **ghrelin receptor (growth hormone secretagogue receptor type 1a, GHS-R1a)** and a binder of the cardiac scavenger receptor **CD36**. Sequence His-D-2-methyl-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂; CAS 140703-51-1; INN **examorelin**; codes EP-23905 / MF-6003. Developed early 1990s by **Romano Deghenghi** and colleagues (Europeptides / Italian endocrine lineage) and advanced clinically by **Mediolanum Farmaceutici**.
- evidence_tier: C — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]]. Unapproved, never reached Phase III, no registrational program. Human evidence = acute pharmacodynamic provocation plus **two small short studies** (8 prepubertal short children; 12 healthy elderly) and a **preclinical-dominant cardiac line** (human cardiac data = acute inotropy only). The only multi-week human course (16 wk, twice-daily SC) showed GH desensitization with **no sustained IGF-1, IGFBP-3, or body-composition change**. No Phase 3 efficacy trial for any indication.
- risk_tier: experimental — unapproved, no product label, no long-term human safety dataset; **non-selective** (co-stimulates cortisol, ACTH, prolactin, unlike ipamorelin); measurable **acute cardiac/hemodynamic effects** (including a mean-arterial-pressure rise); GH effect **self-desensitizes within weeks**; GH→IGF-1 axis carries class-level theoretical risks; real-world supply is gray-market only.
- status: researching
- last_verified: 2026-06-20

> **Read this first.** Hexarelin is a **GHRP — a GHS-R1a (ghrelin-receptor) agonist and a CD36 binder, NOT a GHRH analogue.** It belongs to the same receptor class as GHRP-6, GHRP-2, and ipamorelin; it must **not** be confused with sermorelin, CJC-1295, or tesamorelin (GHRH-receptor agonists acting through an entirely separate receptor and cascade). Its **defining limitation is TACHYPHYLAXIS**: in the longest human study (1.5 µg/kg SC **twice daily for 16 weeks**, healthy elderly), GH AUC fell from 19.1 ± 2.4 to 10.5 ± 1.8 µg/L·h — an **~45% decline** — while **serum IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 did NOT change** (P = 0.24 and P = 0.74); the attenuation was reversible (recovered ~19.4 µg/L·h four weeks after stopping). The practical consequence is decisive: **acute GH spikes do not equal durable effect.** Hexarelin is also **less selective than ipamorelin** — it raises **cortisol, ACTH (via arginine-vasopressin), and prolactin** to clinically relevant magnitudes. Its evidenced human data are acute pharmacodynamics plus two small short studies; a distinct **CD36-mediated cardiac** line is GH-independent but **mostly preclinical** (the only human cardiac data are acute inotropy, and the one diseased-heart human population — dilated cardiomyopathy — did **not** respond acutely). **Body-composition, anti-aging, and athletic efficacy have ZERO robust human data.** Hexarelin was **never approved** (Mediolanum discontinued at Phase II); it is **NOT among the April-2026 removed-12** §503A peptides; and it is **WADA-prohibited at all times** under S2.2.4, where "examorelin (hexarelin)" is named explicitly. Full depth + citations: [[library/peptides/hexarelin/research-report]]. ## Mechanism
Hexarelin stimulates endogenous pulsatile GH release by agonizing **GHS-R1a** (the ghrelin receptor) on anterior-pituitary somatotrophs and hypothalamic neurons, via the classic **Gq/11 → phospholipase C → IP₃/DAG → intracellular Ca²⁺ → GH-vesicle exocytosis** cascade (with a secondary NO/cGMP arm). It also acts as a functional antagonist of somatostatin and facilitates endogenous GHRH tone, so it synergizes with — rather than competes against — GHRH-receptor agonists (two-receptor convergence: cAMP/PKA vs IP₃/Ca²⁺). A **second, GH-independent target is CD36**, a cardiac scavenger-receptor glycoprotein on cardiomyocytes and microvascular endothelium, the basis for the cardiac line (§3 of report); affinity is preferential for GHS-R1a over CD36. SC bioavailability ≈ 77%; oral negligible. See report §1.

## Evidence Summary
- Ghigo 1994, JCEM (PMID 8126144) — n=12 healthy adults, multiroute PK/PD; potent dose-dependent GH release all routes; SC bioavailability ~77%; IV ~2× GHRH. `rct`
- Rahim/O'Neill/Shalet 1998, JCEM — n=12 healthy elderly, 1.5 µg/kg SC BID × 16 wk; GH AUC 19.1→10.5 µg/L·h (~45% ↓, P<0.01); **IGF-1/IGFBP-3 unchanged (P=0.24/0.74); body comp + BMD unchanged; reversible.** `prospective_trial`
- Bodart 2002, Circ Res (PMID 11988484) — rat cardiac membranes; photoaffinity identification of **CD36** as the hexarelin cardiac target; dose-dependent coronary perfusion pressure. `animal`

Cardiac (human, acute / preclinical):
- Broglio 2001, Endocrine (PMID 11322491) — n=7 normal + 7 GHD + 12 dilated cardiomyopathy; acute IV ↑LVEF in normal & GHD but **NOT in DCM**, no BP/HR change. `human_acute`
- Bisi 1999, Eur J Pharmacol — n=7 GHD + 9 controls; acute IV ↑LVEF, GH-independent. `human_acute`

Absent domains (state plainly): **body composition / fat loss / muscle gain = ABSENT; anti-aging / IGF-1-elevation-as-benefit = ABSENT (chronic dosing did not raise IGF-1 and self-desensitizes); athletic/ergogenic = ABSENT; Phase 3 / approval, any indication = ABSENT.** See report §3.3.

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): transient facial flushing/warmth (class effect); **cortisol/ACTH/prolactin elevation** (non-selectivity — ACTH/cortisol activity ≈ hCRH, prolactin rise) — the distinctive liability vs selective GHRPs; **acute cardiac/hemodynamic effects** — improved LV performance but with a measurable **mean-arterial-pressure rise (P<0.05)**, HR unchanged (double-edged); decreased slow-wave sleep. **Glucose/insulin unchanged acutely** (unlike ghrelin). Chronic 16-wk dosing showed no sustained HPA/prolactin over-stimulation (trial dose, elderly). **No human AE rate exists** — all human AE data are existence-of-AE in small short studies.
- adverse effects (class / extrapolated — NOT demonstrated for hexarelin): GH/IGF-1-axis class risks — glucose intolerance/insulin resistance, fluid retention, arthralgia/myalgia, carpal tunnel, paresthesia (GH-replacement literature); IGF-1 elevation with an **associational** (not causal) IGF-1–cancer link. `dose-extrapolation`
- contraindications:    active/recent malignancy (precautionary, IGF-1–cancer association); uncontrolled diabetes / significant glucose intolerance; cardiac conditions / hemodynamically fragile patients (acute MAP rise, no long-term CV data); hyperprolactinemia; pregnancy/lactation (no human data); known hypersensitivity.
- monitoring:           [[biomarkers/igf-1]] (age/sex-specific range; central marker of cumulative GH-axis exposure); fasting glucose ± HbA1c; prolactin + morning cortisol if chronic; blood pressure / heart rate. No validated hexarelin-specific biomarker of effect exists.
- known interactions:   GHRP+GHRH synergy is a two-receptor pharmacology, not a hexarelin-intrinsic property.
