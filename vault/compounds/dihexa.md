# Dihexa

- class: peptide (oral-active angiotensin IV-derived peptidomimetic; N-hexanoyl-Tyr-Ile-(6)-aminohexanoic acid amide; also designated PNB-0408; PubChem CID 129010512; C₂₇H₄₄N₄O₅; MW 504.7 g/mol; developed at Washington State University by Harding/Wright)

> **Read this first — the most integrity-compromised entry in this library.** Dihexa is marketed in grey-market nootropic circles as an oral synaptogen "seven orders of magnitude more potent than BDNF." That claim traces to the now-retracted Benoist 2014 paper (figures found to contain fabricated Western blot data per WSU investigation). The HGF/c-Met synaptogenesis mechanism it proposed has zero non-retracted, non-WSU primary evidence supporting it. The sole independent cognitive-rescue replication (Sun 2021) found PI3K/AKT — not HGF/c-Met. The most recent independent animal study found a null result. No human has been studied under this compound. The only clinical program in this mechanistic class (fosgonimeton/ATH-1017, Athira→LeonaBio) used a different, structurally distinct compound and failed every efficacy trial (LIFT-AD n=554, ACT-AD, SHAPE halted). The foundational researcher (Kawas) had her WSU PhD revoked; Athira paid a $4M DOJ False-Claims settlement in January 2025 over NIH grants supported by manipulated figures. The theoretical oncogenic risk — HGF/c-Met potentiation drives tumor growth in multiple cancer types; its inhibitors are FDA-approved anticancer drugs — is unresolved by any carcinogenicity data. Full depth: [[library/peptides/dihexa/research-report]].

## Mechanism

Dihexa belongs to the AngIV-derived peptidomimetic lineage developed by Harding and Wright at WSU. The N-hexanoyl cap at the N-terminus and the 6-aminohexanoic acid amide at the C-terminus confer protease resistance and improve oral bioavailability and putative blood-brain barrier permeability relative to the parent AngIV hexapeptide. **Two competing mechanistic accounts exist, with sharply different evidentiary standing:**

**Account 1 — IRAP/AT4 inhibition (independent, unretracted):** The AT4 receptor — highly expressed in hippocampal CA1–CA3 and memory-related structures — was identified as insulin-regulated aminopeptidase (IRAP) by Albiston et al. (2001), an independent group with no integrity concerns. AT4 ligands inhibit IRAP's catalytic activity, potentially prolonging the half-lives of cognition-relevant neuropeptide substrates in synaptic clefts. This mechanism is corroborated by independent European medicinal chemistry programs (Uppsala University) that treat AngIV analogs as IRAP inhibitors without any reference to HGF or c-Met. The IRAP/AT4 account does NOT establish Dihexa's claimed synaptogenic potency and does NOT rescue the retracted HGF/c-Met evidence.

**Account 2 — HGF/c-Met potentiation (WSU-only, retracted):** The Harding/Wright group proposed beginning ~2012 that Dihexa and related analogs potentiate hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) at sub-threshold concentrations, driving c-Met phosphorylation → PI3K/Akt and MAPK/ERK → dendritic spine formation and de novo synaptogenesis. Both foundational papers establishing this mechanism (Kawas 2012 PMID 22129598; Benoist 2014 PMID 25187433) were formally retracted in April 2025 following a WSU investigation that found specific figures contained falsified or fabricated elements. The HGF/c-Met mechanism must be treated as **unconfirmed by any non-retracted primary literature** from any laboratory. The Sun 2021 independent replication found PI3K/AKT activation, explicitly not HGF/c-Met. The BDNF potency comparison ("seven orders of magnitude," "100 million times") appeared in the retracted Benoist 2014 paper and cannot be presented as an established fact.

The oncogenic implication of Account 2 — if it were true — is direct and load-bearing: HGF/c-Met potentiation is the pharmacological inverse of capmatinib and tepotinib, FDA-approved drugs that block this pathway to suppress cancer growth. No carcinogenicity study exists for Dihexa. See Risk Profile.

## Evidence Summary

Strongest sources by weight (post-retraction corpus):

- Sun et al. 2021 (Brain Sci, PMID 34827486) — APP/PS1 double-transgenic mice, n=12/group MWM, n=6 molecular; intragastric Dihexa 1.44 or 2.88 mg/kg/day × 3 months; cognitive rescue confirmed (reduced escape latency, increased platform crossings); mechanism: PI3K/AKT (wortmannin reversal + elevated p-AKT); neuroinflammatory markers (IL-1β, TNF-α) reduced; Nissl/synaptophysin preserved. **Sole independent non-WSU in-vivo cognitive replication; explicitly NOT HGF/c-Met; tier-2 open-access journal; no EoC or retraction.** `animal` — China Pharmaceutical University / Nanjing Medical University. [[library/peptides/dihexa/research-report]]
- Ho & Nation 2018 (Neurosci Biobehav Rev, PMID 29733881) — systematic review, 32 experimental studies of AngIV and Ang-(1-7) in cognition; 8/9 cognitive-deficit model studies found benefit; Dihexa grouped among effective compounds; does not adjudicate IRAP vs. HGF/c-Met; published before 2025 retractions. `meta_analysis` — University of Southern California, independent. [[library/peptides/dihexa/research-report]]
- McCoy et al. 2013 (JPET, PMID 23055539) — oral Dihexa 2 mg/kg/day reversed scopolamine-induced MWM deficits in male Sprague-Dawley rats (n=8/group); aged-rat cohort also showed benefit; brain penetration confirmed via radiolabeling. **⚠ Expression of Concern September 2021 (PMID 34551989); not retracted but under active integrity scrutiny; all dose figures carry this flag.** `animal` — WSU/Harding, originating group.

Counter-evidence and critical limits:
- Wells et al. 2024 (J Huntingtons Dis, PMID 38489193) — male Wistar rats, 3-NP Huntington's-like model, n≈13–14/group, 5-week SC dosing; Dihexa produced **no cognitive or motor benefit**; null result; no integrity concerns; most recent published in-vivo Dihexa behavioral study. `animal` — Whitworth University / OHSU / University of Washington.
- Benoist 2014 (JPET, PMID 25187433) — **RETRACTED 29 April 2025 (PMID 40312093)**; figures 1B and 2A/C found falsified; was the primary HGF/c-Met mechanism paper and the source of the BDNF potency figure.
- Kawas 2012 (JPET, PMID 22129598) — **RETRACTED April 2025 (PMID 40312092)**; figures 3A and 4 found falsified; was the first paper proposing AngIV analogs as HGF/c-Met modifiers.
- Fosgonimeton (ATH-1017) LIFT-AD Phase 2/3 (NCT04488419, n=554): primary GST endpoint missed, results posted April 2025. ACT-AD Phase 2: primary ERP endpoint not met. SHAPE Parkinson's trial: halted early. **Fosgonimeton is a structurally distinct compound from Dihexa; these failures do not directly apply to Dihexa — but they are the only human data from this mechanistic class, and they all failed.**

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature): no published human safety data of any kind exist for Dihexa. Preclinical rodent studies (primarily 4–12 weeks) reported no adverse findings but were not designed as toxicology studies. No systematic organ histopathology or oncogenicity evaluation has been published for any duration in any species.
- adverse effects (anecdotal): forum-level reports of variable subjective effects; qualitative only; no validated prevalence; `anecdote_aggregate`
- contraindications:
  - **Concurrent approved or investigational c-Met/MET inhibitor use (ABSOLUTE):** capmatinib (Tabrecta), tepotinib (Tepmetko), or any MET kinase inhibitor — direct pharmacodynamic opposition; combination is mechanistically irrational and contraindicated
  - **Personal or first-degree family history of cancer (especially c-Met-implicated cancers: NSCLC, hepatocellular carcinoma, gastric, breast, glioblastoma, pancreatic):** given the unresolved HGF/c-Met oncogenic theoretical risk and the complete absence of carcinogenicity data, any such history constitutes a serious precautionary hard stop
  - **Immunocompromised individuals:** HGF/c-Met has documented roles in immune cell migration; net effect of potentiation in immunocompromised states is unknown
  - **Pregnancy and lactation:** no safety data; contraindicated by default for any unregistered investigational compound
  - **WADA-tested athletes:** S0 Non-Approved Substances — prohibited at all times; no contamination defense available; consult NADO before any exposure
- monitoring:
  - **Clinical oncologic surveillance (PRIMARY):** routine cancer surveillance appropriate to age and family history; heightened attention to any unexplained mass, lymphadenopathy, systemic symptoms, or laboratory changes warranting earlier oncological evaluation — this is the load-bearing monitoring priority given the unresolved c-Met oncogenic tension and the absence of any carcinogenicity data
  - [[biomarkers/igf-1]]: growth-signaling-axis proxy for c-Met/HGF pathway activation; baseline and periodic monitoring; elevation or out-of-range trends warrant clinical evaluation
  - [[biomarkers/hs-crp]]: inflammatory surveillance marker; rising hs-CRP trend during use absent identifiable cause warrants clinical evaluation
  - [[biomarkers/alt]] / [[biomarkers/ast]]: baseline and periodic liver function monitoring given c-Met's central role in hepatocyte biology; not because hepatotoxicity has been reported for Dihexa, but because the mechanistic class warrants the precaution in the absence of formal safety characterization
  - No biomarker validated as a Dihexa-specific safety or efficacy signal exists; no established therapeutic window or pharmacodynamic marker for on-target effect in humans
- known interactions:
  - c-Met/MET inhibitors (capmatinib, tepotinib): absolute pharmacodynamic opposition — see Contraindications
  - other HGF/c-Met pathway activators: additive theoretical risk; not characterized
  - no further interaction data exist — compound has never been evaluated in humans
