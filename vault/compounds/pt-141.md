# PT-141

- evidence_tier: B — see [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]] (genuinely FDA-approved drug resting on two replicated Phase 3 RECONNECT RCTs, n=1,247 premenopausal women; both co-primary endpoints met p<.001; 100% Palatin/AMAG Phase 3 sponsor-concentration; no independent Phase 3 replication; effect is real but honestly modest, Cohen's d ~0.27–0.39; no Phase 3 in men; the sole large male RCT carries an active Expression of Concern; off-label male use is unsupported by registration-quality evidence; evidence_tier B not A because: single-sponsor, narrow-population, no independent Phase 3 corroboration, modest effect, and a male evidence base that is Phase I/II only with integrity-flagged large RCT)

> **Read this first — honest framing.** PT-141 (bremelanotide; Vyleesi) is the only FDA-approved compound in the melanocortin sexual class — a genuine regulatory approval resting on two replicated Phase 3 RCTs for a narrow, well-defined indication. The honest picture has three inseparable layers: (1) **The approval is real and the effect is real, but both are narrow and modest.** Vyleesi was approved June 21, 2019 for acquired, generalized HSDD in premenopausal women only (FDA label: "Not for postmenopausal women, men, or to enhance sexual performance"). The placebo-adjusted FSFI desire improvement was +0.35 on a 6-point scale (Cohen's d ~0.27–0.39); nausea affected ~40% of patients; ~20% discontinued in the 52-week extension. (2) **PT-141 is NOT cleanly MC4R-selective — the grey-market "100-fold MC4R selective" claim is false.** The FDA-approved prescribing information documents the in-vitro potency order as MC1R > MC4R > MC3R > MC5R. Residual MC1R agonism is the mechanistic basis for both the hyperpigmentation AE (potentially irreversible) and the nausea/flushing cluster. (3) **Off-label male use is unsupported by Phase 3 evidence.** The only large male RCT (Safarinejad 2008, n=342) carries an active Expression of Concern from the Journal of Urology (January 2023) for data-integrity concerns; a companion paper was fully retracted. The entire FDA-grade evidence base is 100% Palatin/AMAG-sponsored, with no independent Phase 3 replication. Vyleesi's commercial arc (approved 2019, AMAG exited within 13 months, Palatin sold to Cosette for $12M in 2023 implying <$15M annual sales) is the empirical signal about the gap between projected and realized market — the drug works narrowly; the grey-market "PT-141-for-everyone" narrative is not grounded in the evidence. **Critical disambiguation: MT-II ≠ PT-141 ≠ afamelanotide** — three distinct compounds, different structures, different approvals, and evidence from one does NOT generalize to another. Full depth + 32 citations: [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]].

## Mechanism
PT-141/bremelanotide is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide lactam melanocortin receptor (MCR) agonist derived from the MT-II scaffold via C-terminal des-amidation — replacing the parent compound's C-terminal primary amide (–NH₂) with a free carboxylic acid (–OH). The compound engages all four pharmacologically active MCR subtypes, with an in-vitro potency order at human cloned receptors of **MC1R > MC4R > MC3R > MC5R > MC2R** per the FDA prescribing information — not the "100-fold MC4R selective" claim circulating in grey-market literature. The clinically targeted pharmacodynamic effect is attributed primarily to **MC4R agonism** in hypothalamic and limbic nuclei (paraventricular nucleus, arcuate nucleus, medial preoptic area, nucleus accumbens, amygdala, ventral tegmental area), where MC4R activation modulates dopaminergic tone to the nucleus accumbens, increasing sexual desire and arousal motivation — a CNS mechanism mechanistically distinct from PDE5 inhibitor vascular pharmacology. The FDA label explicitly states the mechanism by which Vyleesi improves HSDD "is unknown," reflecting that the MC4R hypothesis is supported by animal and in-vitro data but not directly demonstrated in human mechanistic studies. **Residual MC1R agonism** (the highest-affinity receptor subtype) simultaneously activates melanocytes via cAMP/PKA/MITF/tyrosinase signaling, bypassing the normal UV-gated keratinocyte paracrine regulation — the mechanistic basis for focal hyperpigmentation and the nausea/flushing cluster. The lactam cyclization (Asp side-chain carboxyl → Lys side-chain amine bridge) constrains the peptide into a bioactive β-turn conformation with substantially greater potency and proteolytic resistance than linear endogenous α-MSH. Pharmacokinetics: SC bioavailability ~100%; T_max ~1.0 h; t½ ~2.7 h (range 1.9–4.0 h); protein binding 21%; metabolism by peptide hydrolysis; excretion ~65% urine, ~23% feces.

## Evidence Summary
Strongest sources in weight order:

- [Kingsberg et al. 2019] — RECONNECT Studies 301 and 302; n=1,247 premenopausal women with acquired generalized HSDD (efficacy-evaluable n=1,202); two identically designed Phase 3 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled multicenter trials; 24 weeks; bremelanotide 1.75 mg SC on-demand; co-primary endpoints: FSFI desire domain change (+0.30 Study 301, +0.42 Study 302, integrated +0.35 — all p<.001) and FSDS-DAO item 13 distress change (−0.37 and −0.29, both p≤.005); Cohen's d ~0.39 (desire) and ~0.27 (distress); nausea 40.0% vs 1.3% placebo; responder rate ~58% vs ~36% placebo; 100% Palatin/AMAG-sponsored; AMAG employee co-authors (Williams, Krop) and Palatin VP co-author (Jordan) on the byline. `rct` [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]]
- [Simon et al. 2019] — 52-week open-label extension of RECONNECT (n from blinded phase); FSFI-D improvements +1.25 to +1.30 (desire subscale); discontinuation due to AEs 18.7–23.4% across extension arms; nausea 40.4% — persistent, not self-limiting; open-label design, no placebo control, survivor-bias caution. `open_label` [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]]
- [White et al. 2017] — n=397 premenopausal women with FSD; dedicated ambulatory BP monitoring substudy; mean systolic increase ~3 mmHg at approved 1.75 mg SC dose, peaking 2–4 hours, returning to baseline within 12 hours; compensatory HR decrease ~5 bpm; net myocardial workload unchanged; provides the cardiovascular characterization underlying the CV contraindication. `rct` [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]]
- [Clayton et al. 2022] — safety meta-analysis; 43 studies, ~3,500 subjects across full clinical development program; confirms BP increase pattern characterizing it as "not deemed clinically important" in screened normotensive trial population while explicitly noting caution in patients at CV risk. `meta_analysis` [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]]
- [Diamond et al. 2004; Rosen et al. 2004; Diamond et al. 2005] — three small Phase I/II male ED trials (intranasal and SC PT-141); statistically significant RigiScan-measured erectile responses vs placebo at ≥7 mg intranasal and ≥1 mg SC; enrollment not confirmed in available abstracts; Phase I/II proofs of concept not powered for registration; both 2004 papers paywalled; intranasal route subsequently abandoned due to PK variability and CV risk. `rct` tier 2–3 [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]]

Counter-evidence (load-bearing):
- [Safarinejad & Hosseini 2008 — EoC flagged] — n=342 men with sildenafil-unresponsive ED; reported 33.5% vs 8.5% placebo positive response (p=.03); the Journal of Urology issued a formal **Expression of Concern January 10, 2023** (PMID 36626345) for data-integrity concerns; companion paper in women (J Sex Med 2008) was **fully retracted** for the same concerns. This paper should not be treated as reliable male efficacy evidence pending investigation resolution. `rct` tier 3 (integrity-flagged) [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]]
- [Simon et al. 2022 — subgroup] — prespecified RECONNECT subgroup analyses; no new data; same Palatin/AMAG-sponsored dataset; same author team with same COIs; confirms no independent Phase 3 corroboration exists. `rct` [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]]

## Prescribing-Practice Layer
See [[library/peptides/pt-141/research-report]] §4–7 for full detail. Bremelanotide is an FDA-approved pharmaceutical (Vyleesi); the prescribing-practice layer draws primarily from the approved label and the RECONNECT program.

- **FDA-approved label dose [regulatory]:** 1.75 mg SC via single-use autoinjector, self-administered to the abdomen or thigh at least 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity; no more than once in any 24-hour period; maximum 8 doses per month. This is the only fully evidence-grounded dosing regimen. Discontinue after 8 weeks if no symptom improvement.
- **Compounding pharmacy data sheets [compounding_data_sheet]:** 503A pharmacies have offered compounded bremelanotide (nasal spray, different concentrations) leveraging the "significant clinical difference" carveout for individualized prescriptions; estimated compounded cost ~$50–$150/month vs ~$800/4-pack list for Vyleesi. The FDA issued a 2020 warning letter to Tailor Made Compounding LLC citing bremelanotide among substances compounded without meeting 503A conditions. FDA's "essentially a copy" doctrine limits 503A compounding when the autoinjector formulation already exists commercially.
- **Practitioner reference texts [practitioner_protocol]:** No dedicated A4M/IFM bremelanotide monograph independently located. Grey-market and longevity-peptide community protocols vary widely in dose (0.5–2 mg SC or intranasal) and cycle frequency — none are evidence-grounded beyond the RECONNECT approved regimen.
- **Originator-group recommended dose:** The Palatin-developed, FDA-filed dose is 1.75 mg SC on-demand; earlier development-era intranasal doses (7–10 mg) were abandoned due to PK variability and cardiovascular risk.
- **Consensus practitioner dose:** Converges on the approved label regimen for SC use; compounded nasal preparations at lower mass doses (~1–2 mg) are used off-label without dose-finding evidence.
- **Gap between literature and practice:** The approved evidence base covers only premenopausal women with physician-confirmed HSDD. The grey-market "PT-141" economy uses substantially higher cumulative doses, more frequent dosing, and a broader (male + female + healthy) population for which no registration-quality evidence exists. The daily-dosing hyperpigmentation risk (38% vs 1% at label dose) makes grey-market "loading" protocols carry materially elevated MC1R-driven AE risk.

## Protocol
- dose: 1.75 mg SC on-demand (FDA-approved Vyleesi regimen, premenopausal women with HSDD); no validated dose for men, postmenopausal women, or desire-enhancement in non-clinical populations
- route: subcutaneous injection (abdomen or thigh) via single-use autoinjector; oral bioavailability negligible (peptide, GI proteolysis); intranasal route studied in Phase I/II but abandoned due to PK variability and cardiovascular risk
- timing: at least 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity (on-demand, not daily); T_max ~1 h
- cycle: not more than once in any 24-hour period; ≤8 doses per month per label; no approved chronic/daily regimen; 8-week efficacy assessment window (discontinue if no response)
- stack: n/a (library entry); note: PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil) have been combined with bremelanotide in Phase I/II male trials with additive erectile signal — no Phase 3 characterization of the combination; combination BP data lacking; use with caution
- source: Vyleesi (Cosette Pharmaceuticals, FDA-approved autoinjector); compounded 503A preparations (prescriber-documented significant clinical difference required; 503B outsourcing facilities: verify current FDA guidance); grey-market peptide vendors (purity and identity unverifiable; MT-II/PT-141 conflation is a documented risk; no cGMP oversight)
- regulated status: FDA-approved Rx pharmaceutical (Vyleesi; NDA 210557; premenopausal HSDD indication only); 503A compounding restricted per "essentially a copy" doctrine; WADA: NOT confirmed explicitly named on 2026 Prohibited List from primary source — S2 catch-all language likely applies but unverified; athletes must confirm with GlobalDRO.com or NADO before use
- cost: ~$800 list price / 4-pack autoinjector (Vyleesi brand); copay assistance available under prior Palatin program (verify current Cosette program); 503A compounded ~$50–$150/month (estimated); grey-market research-chemical pricing not assessed

## Reconstitution (peptides only)
- supplied as: Vyleesi — 1.75 mg/0.3 mL sterile solution in single-dose autoinjector (pharmaceutical-grade, sterility-validated, room temperature or refrigerated per label); grey-market PT-141 — typically lyophilized powder in unlabeled or vendor-labeled vials; purity and identity unverifiable; vendor COAs not subject to FDA cGMP oversight; MT-II/PT-141 compound-identity confusion is a documented grey-market risk
- diluent: Vyleesi autoinjector is ready-to-use; grey-market lyophilized reconstitution: bacteriostatic water (volume per vendor instruction — no standard); no pharmacopoeial reconstitution standard exists for grey-market product
- final concentration: Vyleesi 1.75 mg/0.3 mL (5.83 mg/mL); grey-market variable
- dose volume: Vyleesi single autoinjector dose = 0.3 mL; grey-market variable
- storage: Vyleesi — store at or below 77°F (25°C); brief excursions to 59–86°F permitted; do not freeze; keep in original carton; use immediately after removing from carton; single-use, discard after use

## Risk Profile
- adverse effects (literature):
  - **Nausea ~40%** (vs 1.3% placebo; RECONNECT pooled n=627 bremelanotide) — dominant AE and primary discontinuation driver; onset median ~1 h post-dose; partial attenuation with repeat dosing but 40.4% incidence persisted through 52-week extension; antiemetic pretreatment required in ~13%; severe in a subset; not a first-dose-only phenomenon
  - **Flushing 20.3%** (vs 0.3% placebo)
  - **Injection site reactions 13.2%** (vs 8.4% placebo)
  - **Headache 11.3%** (vs 1.9% placebo)
  - **Vomiting 4.8%**, fatigue 3.2%, hot flush 2.7%, paresthesia 2.6%, dizziness 2.2%
  - **Focal hyperpigmentation** — face, gingiva, breasts; 1% at label regimen (≤8 doses/month); rises to **38%** with daily dosing over 8 consecutive days; mechanism is MC1R agonism of melanocytes independent of UV exposure; **not confirmed reversible after discontinuation in all patients** (FDA label explicit)
  - **Transient BP elevation** — mean +6 mmHg systolic / +3 mmHg diastolic; peak 2–4 hours post-dose; resolves within 8–12 hours; compensatory HR decrease ~5 bpm; net myocardial workload not meaningfully elevated in screened trial population
  - **Rare hepatotoxicity** — one confirmed acute hepatitis case (ALT >1,000 U/L, resolved on discontinuation); LiverTox likelihood D (possible rare cause); no acute liver failure documented
- adverse effects (anecdotal): injection-site reactions consistent with literature; nausea consistent with literature; no novel grey-market-specific signal characterized with pharmacopoeial-grade certainty
- contraindications:
  - **Absolute:** uncontrolled hypertension; known cardiovascular disease (the transient pressor effect + absence of CV monitoring in grey-market use is the primary harm vector for off-label use)
  - **Relative:** postmenopausal women (not approved; evidence base is premenopausal only); men (not approved; no Phase 3 data; the only large male RCT is EoC-flagged); darker baseline skin pigmentation (elevated hyperpigmentation risk — label explicit); daily or high-frequency dosing patterns (hyperpigmentation risk escalates dramatically); pregnancy/nursing (no adequate human data; avoid); concurrent use of other melanocortin peptides (additive AE risk uncharacterized)
- monitoring: see Risk/Monitoring section below
- known interactions: no established PK drug-drug interactions (no CYP450 involvement); additive central arousal signal with PDE5 inhibitors in Phase I/II male trials — combination CV safety not characterized at Phase 3 level; do not mix with other drugs in same syringe; no established interaction with alcohol (contrast with flibanserin's boxed alcohol-interaction warning)

## Risk/Monitoring

**Monitoring rationale.** The two clinically significant ongoing risk concerns are (1) the transient cardiovascular pressor effect (requiring pre-use BP assessment and post-dose monitoring within the 2–4 hour peak window) and (2) focal hyperpigmentation (an MC1R-driven effect that may not reverse on discontinuation and accumulates with cumulative dose). A third concern — nausea — is monitored by patient report and managed with antiemetics rather than biomarker surveillance. Monitoring is drawn from the FDA label warnings and the clinical trial safety program.

| Parameter | Rationale | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| [[biomarkers/resting-heart-rate]] | The label documents a compensatory HR decrease up to ~5 bpm accompanying the pressor response; a falling HR at 1–2 hours post-dose is an expected pharmacodynamic signal; unexpectedly large HR decreases or bradycardia warrant clinical evaluation | Monitor within 1–4 hours of each dose, especially early in use |
| Blood pressure (clinical or ambulatory) | Primary CV monitoring objective: transient +6/3 mmHg systolic/diastolic peak at 2–4 hours post-dose; contraindicated in uncontrolled HTN and known CVD; BP should be characterized at baseline and confirmed controlled before initiating; ambulatory post-dose monitoring warranted in any patient with borderline BP or CV risk factors (White et al. 2017 substudy design) | Baseline (confirm BP controlled); consider ambulatory monitoring 2–4 hours post-first dose if CV risk factors present; each 8-week follow-up |
| Dermatologic exam (face, gingiva, breasts) | Focal hyperpigmentation is MC1R-mediated, dose-frequency-dependent (1% at ≤8 doses/month; 38% with daily dosing), and not confirmed reversible in all patients; baseline documentation of any pre-existing pigmented lesions establishes the comparator; patients with darker baseline skin should be counseled about elevated risk before first dose | Baseline photography/exam; every follow-up visit on-cycle; at discontinuation |
| Liver function (ALT/AST) | One confirmed case of acute hepatitis (LiverTox likelihood D); label does not require routine LFT monitoring; relevant if symptomatic (jaundice, right upper quadrant pain, unexplained fatigue) | As clinically indicated; baseline if prolonged use planned |
| Nausea/antiemetic pretreatment | Nausea ~40%; antiemetic pretreatment appropriate in nausea-prone patients; assess at follow-up visits | Patient-reported; each dose; follow-up at 4 and 8 weeks |

**Stopping criteria:** Discontinue on: uncontrolled or symptomatic BP elevation post-dose; symptomatic CV events (chest pain, syncope, dyspnea); new or progressive focal hyperpigmentation (no dose-reduction strategy established; decision to continue vs stop requires physician discussion); persistent severe nausea preventing intended use; allergic/hypersensitivity reaction; no symptom improvement after 8 weeks (per label guidance).

## Decision Notes
Per [[library/methodology/evidence-tiers]], PT-141 at evidence_tier=B / risk_tier=medium falls in the doctor-required cell for any clinical use. The evidence_tier=B reflects the genuine FDA approval and two replicated Phase 3 RCTs — the strongest evidence base in the melanocortin sexual class — while acknowledging the single-sponsor concentration, the modest absolute effect (d~0.27–0.39), and the absence of independent Phase 3 corroboration. For the approved indication (acquired generalized HSDD, premenopausal women, physician-confirmed, causing marked distress): evidence is genuine, regulatory approval is real, and the safety profile in the screened population is characterized. The drug is a reasonable clinical option for a specific, well-defined patient population when first-line relationship counseling and medication-review causes of HSDD have been addressed. **For off-label male use or desire-enhancement in non-clinical populations: there is no Phase 3 evidence base; the only large male RCT is EoC-flagged; the approved label explicitly excludes these populations; and the CV contraindication applies without the pre-screening that characterizes the trial population.** The grey-market "PT-141-for-everyone" positioning is not grounded in the evidence, and the compound's commercial arc (approved 2019; commercially failed by 2020; sold twice by 2023) is an independent empirical signal about the gap between projected and realized benefit. **Critical compound disambiguations (these are NOT the same drug):**
- **MT-II (Melanotan-II)** — unapproved parent compound, C-terminal amide (–NH₂), broader/stronger receptor engagement, more pronounced nausea/yawning/spontaneous erections in men, no regulatory approval anywhere; MT-II evidence does NOT generalize to PT-141.
- **PT-141 / bremelanotide / Vyleesi** — the approved compound described in this entry; C-terminal free acid (–OH); FDA-approved June 2019 for premenopausal HSDD in women.
- **Afamelanotide (Melanotan-I; Scenesse)** — structurally unrelated LINEAR 13-residue α-MSH analog; MC1R preferential; FDA/EMA-approved for erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP); does NOT carry the MC4R sexual pharmacology of PT-141; afamelanotide evidence is NOT relevant to bremelanotide.
- **Flibanserin (Addyi)** — the OTHER FDA-approved premenopausal-HSDD drug (approved August 2015); entirely different mechanism (5-HT1A agonist/5-HT2A antagonist; daily oral bedtime dosing; boxed alcohol-interaction warning); occupies the same regulatory indication niche as Vyleesi; both show similarly modest absolute effect sizes on FSFI-D-class endpoints.
- **Kisspeptin-10** — acts upstream via HPG axis / GnRH signaling; completely different receptor family, mechanism, and time course; evidence from kisspeptin does NOT generalize to PT-141.
