## Section C — Pineal/melatonin, other claimed effects & independent-replication assessment

### C.1 Pineal peptide bioregulator framing

Epitalon was synthesized as the putative "active tetrapeptide" of epithalamin, a crude bovine pineal extract that Khavinson's group developed at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology beginning in the 1970s. The theoretical frame is a "peptide theory of ageing": aging reflects declining synthesis of organ-specific peptide regulators; delivering the deficit peptide restores the organ's gene-expression set-point [1, mechanism_review]. For the pineal gland specifically, the proposed mechanism is that the Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG) sequence acts on epiphysis-specific transcriptional elements, restoring melatonin biosynthesis that the aging pineal gland progressively loses.

### C.2 Melatonin and circadian restoration claims

The principal source for the melatonin-restoration claim is a 2007 study by Korkushko et al. (n not specified in the abstract; species: rhesus macaques [Macaca mulatta] and elderly humans described in the abstract as having "pineal gland functional insufficiency") published in *Advances in Gerontology* [2, open_label]. That paper reported that peptide preparations — both epithalamin and epitalon — "recover night release of endogenous melatonin and lead to the normalization of the hormone circadian rhythm in the blood plasma." Basal glucose and insulin decreased in treated old monkeys alongside the melatonin rise. The journal (*Advances in Gerontology*) is a Russian-language venue edited within the bioregulator school (tier 3); no English-language, independently conducted randomized trial has replicated circadian melatonin normalization in either primates or humans.

An earlier framework review by Khavinson (2002, *Neuro Endocrinol Lett*, PMID 12374906) described the theoretical basis and summarized rodent, primate, and human observations for both epithalamin and epitalon across anti-aging endpoints including lifespan extension in SHR rats and Drosophila, restored circadian rhythmicity, and reduced tumor incidence [1, mechanism_review]. All primary data cited in that review originate from the same St. Petersburg institute.

### C.3 Other claimed effects

**Retinitis pigmentosa / retinoprotection.** The most prominent non-longevity clinical claim is Khavinson et al. (2002, *Neuro Endocrinol Lett*, PMID 12195242) [3, open_label]. The study used two models: (a) Campbell rats with hereditary retinal degeneration — epitalon preserved retinal morphological architecture and extended functional retinal activity; (b) human patients with "degenerative retinal lesions" receiving parabulbar epitalon injections daily for 10 days. The published abstract states "a positive clinical effect in 90% of the cases." No sample size is reported in the abstract, no control arm is described, randomization is not mentioned, and the journal (*Neuro Endocrinol Lett*) carried Khavinson as a frequent editorial contributor at this period (tier 3). A 2025 in vitro follow-up in an Italian–Russian collaboration (Gatta, Dovizio et al., University of Chieti-Pescara + St. Petersburg Institute, *Stem Cell Reviews and Reports*, PMID 40493162, PMC12356729) demonstrated that epitalon at 40–60 ng/mL reduced ROS generation, suppressed EMT-related gene downregulation, and restored wound-healing in high-glucose-injured human retinal pigment epithelium (ARPE-19 cells) [4, in_vitro]. This is a partial-independence study: Italian researchers led the cell work; Khavinson and Trofimova are listed co-authors, providing the compound and likely the intellectual frame.

**Antioxidant / metabolic.** In rodent models, epitalon has been reported to increase superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity by ~19% and total antioxidant capacity by ~36%, reduce lipid peroxidation products, and restore estrus cycle regularity in female rats (all Khavinson-school reports; tier 3 venues) [1, mechanism_review]. The 2025 Polish review (Araj, Brzezik, Mądra-Gackowska, Szeleszczuk; *Int J Mol Sci*, PMID 40141333) synthesizes 25 years of epitalon literature and endorses antioxidant, neuroprotective, and antimutagenic claims while acknowledging that "the quantity of physico-chemical and structural investigations of this peptide remains quite limited" and that uncertainty surrounds whether known mechanisms are the sole pathways [5, mechanism_review]. That review is by independent Polish researchers (no Khavinson co-authorship) and was published in a Tier 2 venue; it constitutes a secondary synthesis rather than original experimental replication.

**Immune / thymic.** Claims include increased thymocyte proliferation, elevated interferon-gamma from T-cells, and reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines in aged animals. Evidence is uniformly from Khavinson-school publications in Russian and tier-3 venues; no independent immune characterization has been published in indexed literature.

**Oocyte protection.** An independent replication in a biologically adjacent area: Yue et al. (2022, *Aging*, PMID 35413689; authors from Shanxi Medical University, Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of Zoology, University of Missouri — no Khavinson co-authorship) demonstrated that 0.1 mM epitalon added to in vitro culture medium significantly reduced ROS, improved spindle integrity, increased mitochondrial membrane potential and mtDNA copy number, and decreased early apoptosis in post-ovulatory aging mouse oocytes [6, in_vitro]. This is an unambiguously independent study. Mechanistic framing (antioxidant + mitochondrial protection) aligns with Khavinson-school proposals, but the experimental team and institution are fully independent.

**Anti-tumor (HER-2/neu model).** Anisimov et al. (2002, *Int J Cancer*, PMID 12209581) studied epitalon in FVB/N HER-2/neu transgenic female mice from age 2 months, finding reduced cumulative tumor number and a 3.7-fold reduction in HER-2/neu mRNA expression in mammary tumors [7, animal]. Anisimov is at the N.N. Petrov Research Institute of Oncology, St. Petersburg — a distinct institution from Khavinson's institute, and he held separate academic standing as President of the Gerontological Society of the Russian Academy of Sciences. However, Khavinson is a co-author on this paper, and Anisimov has co-authored extensively with Khavinson across this period; this study is best classified as Russian bioregulator school collaboration rather than independent external replication.

### C.4 Independent-replication assessment (CRITICAL)

**The central epistemic fact about epitalon is that the vast majority of its evidence base — encompassing human longevity data, melatonin restoration claims, retinitis pigmentosa outcomes, and most anti-aging mechanism work — originates from a single tightly connected research ecosystem: Vladimir Khavinson and colleagues at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology.** The large majority of indexed English-language epitalon references trace directly to Khavinson as author or co-author; a roughly equivalent volume of Russian-language literature (not accessible for international audit) exists on top of that. The human cohort mortality data (Khavinson & Morozov, 2003, *Neuro Endocrinol Lett*, PMID 14523363) claiming 4.1-fold mortality reduction over 6–8 years in 266 elderly patients treated with Thymalin + Epithalamin is the most striking claim in the literature [8, open_label] — but it is an unblinded Khavinson-school report in a tier-3 venue with no independent replication across four decades.

Two genuinely independent confirmations of limited scope have now appeared:

1. **Al-Dulaimi, Thomas, Matta, Roberts** (Brunel University London + Royal Brompton Hospital, *Biogerontology*, 2025, PMID 40908429, PMC12411320) [9, in_vitro]: Confirmed dose-dependent telomere length extension in normal human fibroblasts (IBR.3) and epithelial cells (HMEC) via hTERT/telomerase upregulation; cancer lines (21NT, BT474) showed ALT-pathway elongation instead. This is the first non-Russian, non-commercial laboratory replication of the telomerase mechanism — in cell lines only, with no longevity or organismal outcome.

2. **Yue et al.** (Shanxi Medical University / Chinese Academy of Sciences / University of Missouri, *Aging*, 2022, PMID 35413689) [6, in_vitro]: Independent confirmation that epitalon reduces oxidative stress and apoptosis in aging mouse oocytes. Mechanistically coherent with Khavinson claims; no human translational data.

The Italian collaboration (Gatta et al. 2025) [4, in_vitro] is semi-independent: Italian bench team, Khavinson co-authorship, Khavinson-supplied compound context.

No independent laboratory has replicated: the human melatonin normalization data, the retinitis pigmentosa clinical claims, the human longevity / mortality-reduction cohort, or any immune / thymic outcome. The HER-2/neu tumor work by Anisimov et al., while at a different St. Petersburg institution, was co-authored by Khavinson and belongs to the same Russian bioregulator ecosystem.

### C.5 Longevity-paradigm context and mainstream geroscience reception

Epitalon's theoretical frame — that telomerase reactivation via an exogenous peptide can extend somatic cell replicative lifespan and translate to organismal longevity — sits at the speculative frontier of geroscience rather than its mainstream. Mainstream geroscience (Hallmarks of Aging framework, mTOR/rapamycin, senolytics, caloric restriction mimetics) does not treat exogenous telomerase activation as a safe longevity strategy: the biology of telomere elongation and oncogenesis are mechanistically adjacent, and no telomerase-activation intervention has cleared regulatory scrutiny for healthy aging in any jurisdiction. The 2025 Biogerontology roadmap review (Yu et al.) notes that "no longevity intervention has yet been proven effective or ready for widespread clinical adoption" and specifically flags the absence of rigorous long-term human trials across the peptide bioregulator class [10, mechanism_review].

Khavinson's group has registered ~196 patents on bioregulator peptides and has commercial entities marketing these compounds, creating a potential conflict of interest that compounds the single-group replication problem. The Alzheimer's Drug Discovery Foundation has publicly noted the pattern of extraordinary claims from a single group over four decades without independent confirmation. No epitalon trials appear on ClinicalTrials.gov; no regulatory agency has reviewed the human longevity data. The 2025 Brunel study (Al-Dulaimi et al.) is a step toward independent mechanism verification — but mechanistic plausibility in cell lines is not clinical proof, and the organismal longevity claims remain entirely unverified outside the Khavinson ecosystem.

**2-sentence summary:**
The pineal/melatonin restoration claim and the retinitis pigmentosa clinical outcome (90% positive response rate in unspecified n) rest entirely on Khavinson-authored, unblinded, uncontrolled reports in tier-3 venues with no independent replication across two decades. The independent-replication verdict is unambiguous: the overwhelming majority of epitalon evidence — across all claimed endpoints including human longevity, melatonin normalization, and retinal protection — originates from a single tightly connected research group at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology, with only two genuinely external confirmations published to date (Yue et al. 2022, mouse oocytes in vitro; Al-Dulaimi et al. 2025, human cell line telomerase), neither of which addresses the organismal or human clinical claims.
