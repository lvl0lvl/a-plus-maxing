## Section A — Identity, origin & molecular mechanism

> **Evidence-tier ceiling — read before the mechanism detail below:** The entire evidentiary base for Epitalon's mechanism and efficacy is in vitro or open-label. Of the 8 citations in this section, all are either cell-culture experiments or uncontrolled observational studies; 6 of 8 are Khavinson-authored or co-authored (the sole independent mechanistic replication, Brunel 2025 [3], is also in vitro and diverges from the original mechanism on a key point). No controlled in-vivo animal study with independent authorship and no blinded human mechanistic trial exists in the published literature as of the date of this review. Consequently, the maximum defensible evidence tier for any claim in this section is low (D-equivalent): plausible in cell models, unconfirmed in vivo, unconfirmed in humans. A web search conducted for independent in-vivo or animal mechanistic studies found none meeting those criteria; the null finding is disclosed here rather than left implicit.

### A.1 Identity and nomenclature

Epitalon (also spelled Epithalon; CAS 307297-39-8 for the free peptide, 307297-40-1 for the acetate salt) is a synthetic tetrapeptide with the amino acid sequence **Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG)**, molecular weight ~390.35 Da. It is not a natural compound: it is a synthetic short peptide derived from the sequence analysis of **Epithalamin** (Epithalamine), a low-molecular-weight polypeptide fraction extracted from bovine pineal gland tissue. The two names are frequently conflated in the secondary literature but describe distinct substances: Epithalamin is the complex bovine pineal extract used in earlier Russian clinical work; Epitalon is the defined synthetic tetrapeptide that was developed to reproduce Epithalamin's reported biological activity in a chemically tractable, reproducible form [1, mechanism_review].

The compound belongs to the "peptide bioregulator" (cytomedine) class conceptualized by Vladimir Khavinson and colleagues at the **St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology** (North-Western Division of the Russian Academy of Medical Sciences), where the research programme was initiated in the 1970s–1980s. The core hypothesis of the programme is that short organ-specific peptides (2–4 amino acids) extracted from tissue function as gene-expression modulators in an age-dependent, tissue-specific manner, and that synthetic analogs can recapitulate these effects [1, mechanism_review]. Essentially all foundational identity and mechanistic literature on Epitalon originates from the Khavinson group or closely affiliated Russian institutions — a concentration-of-authorship signal that readers should weigh when assessing evidence tier.

### A.2 Claimed mechanisms

#### A.2.1 Telomerase activation and telomere elongation (headline mechanism)

The most widely cited mechanistic claim for Epitalon is induction of telomerase activity and consequent telomere elongation in somatic cells that normally lack telomerase expression.

**Foundational study (Khavinson group, in vitro):** Khavinson, Bondarev, and Butyugov (2003) reported that addition of Epithalon peptide to telomerase-negative human fetal fibroblast cultures induced expression of the catalytic subunit (hTERT), enzymatic telomerase activity, and measurable telomere elongation, suggesting reactivation of the telomerase gene in somatic cells [2, in_vitro]. The model was human fetal fibroblasts; the study is a single short report (3 pages) published in the Russian-translated Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine. All three authors are from the St. Petersburg Institute. No independent replication was available at the time of publication. Evidence tier: 3 (lower-tier Russian-translation journal, Khavinson-authored, no independent corroboration at time of publication).

**Independent partial replication (2025):** Al-Dulaimi et al. (2025) at Brunel University London published an in vitro study in *Biogerontology* that provides the first substantive independent test of the telomere-elongation claim [3, in_vitro]. Six human cell lines were used — cancer lines 21NT and BT474, normal fibroblast IBR.3, normal mammary epithelial HMEC, ALT-positive U2OS, and telomerase-expressing PC3-hTERT. At 0.5–1 μg/ml for four days, cancer cells showed dose-dependent telomere elongation (2.4–4 kb in 21NT, up to 8 kb in BT474), with hTERT expression upregulated 12-fold in 21NT and 5-fold in BT474. Unexpectedly, the cancer cell telomere elongation appeared to proceed via Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres (ALT) rather than canonical telomerase activation (10-fold increase in ALT activity in 21NT). Normal cells required 3-week exposure at 1 μg/ml for significant extension and showed conventional telomerase-dependent elongation. None of the Brunel authors are affiliated with the Khavinson group. Evidence tier: 2 (solid specialty journal, independent). Key caveats: all findings are in_vitro; concentrations have not been validated against physiologically achievable levels in vivo; the ALT finding in cancer cell lines introduces a mechanistic complexity absent from earlier Khavinson claims; no human or animal telomere-length data generated in this study.

**Summary:** The telomere-elongation claim has in vitro support from both the original Khavinson group and one independent 2025 replication. Mechanism (TERT induction vs. ALT) differs between cell types. No randomized or controlled human evidence for telomere length change exists. The claim as commonly stated — that Epitalon "activates telomerase in somatic cells, extending telomeres and lifespan" — is currently supported only at the in vitro level, with the mechanistic picture more complex than the simple TERT-reactivation framing.

#### A.2.2 Pineal / melatonin regulation

Epitalon's pineal origin motivates the claim that it restores melatonin secretion in aged individuals. Korkushko et al. (2007) administered pineal peptide preparations (Epithalamin and/or Epitalon) to aged rhesus monkeys and elderly humans, reporting recovery of the nocturnal melatonin peak and normalization of the circadian melatonin rhythm in plasma [4, open_label]. Published in the Russian journal *Advances in Gerontology*, with Khavinson as co-author (fourth of eight), the study lacked a placebo control arm and blinding details are not reported in the abstract; the human subject data are particularly underspecified. Khavinson's 2002 review in *Neuro Endocrinol Lett* similarly reports that in old rhesus monkeys (20–26 years) Epitalon administration restored the evening melatonin peak toward levels seen in young animals [1, mechanism_review]. Evidence tier: 3 for the 2007 Advances in Gerontology source (Russian journal, Khavinson co-authored, open-label); the melatonin claim is biologically plausible given the pineal derivation but rests on weak, non-blinded study designs. No RCT or double-blinded human evidence exists for melatonin restoration.

#### A.2.3 Gene expression / transcriptional / epigenetic regulation

Two distinct but related mechanistic proposals exist:

**Chromatin activation in aged cells (in vitro, human lymphocytes):** Khavinson et al. (2003) examined cultured lymphocytes from persons aged 76–80 years and reported that Epitalon induced activation of ribosomal genes, decondensation of pericentromeric structural heterochromatin, and release of genes repressed by age-related euchromatin condensation — collectively described as "chromatin activation at old age" [5, in_vitro]. All authors are Khavinson-group or affiliated. Evidence tier: 3 (Neuro Endocrinol Lett, Khavinson-led, single study in aged lymphocytes, no independent replication).

**Histone binding / epigenetic mechanism (in vitro, stem cells):** Khavinson et al. (2020) used human gingival mesenchymal stem cells (hGMSCs) and computational modeling to propose that AEDG peptide preferentially binds histone variants H1/3 and H1/6 at DNA-interacting sites, and that this interaction facilitates transcription of neuronal differentiation genes (Nestin, GAP43, β-Tubulin III, Doublecortin), with mRNA expression increased 1.6–1.8 fold [6, in_vitro]. This was published in *Molecules* (MDPI) and includes one non-Khavinson co-author group (University "G. d'Annunzio" Chieti-Pescara, Italy). Evidence tier: 2–3 (MDPI open-access, Khavinson-led with partial international authorship; in vitro only; the histone-binding mechanism is computationally inferred, not crystallographically established).

**Honest framing:** Both proposals rest on cell-model data from Khavinson-affiliated sources. The histone-binding hypothesis — that a 4-amino-acid peptide can selectively bind specific histone variants to derepress gene loci — is mechanistically asserted from in silico and cell data with no independent structural confirmation. It is not established as a human mechanism.

#### A.2.4 Antioxidant activity

Yue et al. (2022), a group at Shanxi Medical University / Chinese Academy of Sciences with no Khavinson affiliation, examined Epitalon's effects on post-ovulatory aging of mouse oocytes in vitro (ICR mice, 8 weeks old) [7, in_vitro]. At 0.1 mM, Epitalon significantly reduced intracellular ROS versus aged controls (4.4 ± 1.0 vs. 32.8 ± 5.8 relative fluorescence, P < 0.001), maintained mitochondrial membrane potential and mitochondrial DNA copy number, and reduced early apoptosis. The authors describe Epitalon as "a potent antioxidant, comparable to melatonin." Published in *Aging (Albany NY)*. Evidence tier: 2 (independent group, solid specialty journal, in vitro mouse oocyte model). Caveat: antioxidant ROS measurements in a single cell model do not establish systemic antioxidant activity in vivo or in humans.

### A.3 Broader context: Epithalamin in human longevity research

Prior to the development of Epitalon, the Khavinson group conducted longer-term work with the bovine pineal extract Epithalamin. A 6–8 year open-label clinical trial in 266 elderly participants (reported 2003) found that annual administration of Thymalin (thymus) and Epithalamin (pineal) peptide bioregulators reduced mortality 2.0–2.1 fold (Thymalin alone), 1.6–1.8 fold (Epithalamin alone), and 4.1 fold (combined) versus controls [8, open_label]. Published in *Neuro Endocrinol Lett* by Khavinson and Morozov. Evidence tier: 2–3 (Neuro Endocrinol Lett, Khavinson-authored, open-label design without placebo randomization, self-reported mortality data). This study provides context for Epitalon's precursor but does not constitute controlled evidence for the synthetic tetrapeptide's effects.
