## Section D — Safety, dosing, route, PK, regulatory & monitoring

### D.1 Dosing: study-derived vs. grey-market

**Two distinct preparations must be kept separate throughout this section.** Epithalamin is a crude bovine pineal polypeptide extract used in Soviet-era and Russian clinical programmes; Epitalon is the synthetic AEDG tetrapeptide derived from it. The human study record overwhelmingly used Epithalamin, not synthetic Epitalon [1, open_label][2, open_label][3, rct].

**Study-derived doses (Epithalamin, IM).**
Published Khavinson-group human trials employed epithalamin at approximately 10 mg intramuscularly per day, in courses of 10–20 consecutive days, administered one to three times per year in elderly cohorts (ages 60–85) [1, open_label][2, open_label]. One landmark 15-year cardiovascular study used six treatment courses over three years; individual course dosing details are incompletely reported in English-language abstracts [3, rct]. A circadian-rhythm study employed 0.5 mg/day sublingually for 20 days — the only published sublingual human dose on record for an epitalon-class compound [5, mechanism_review]. An ophthalmic trial in retinitis pigmentosa used 5.0 µg/eye via parabulbar injection for 10 days [5, mechanism_review].

**Important disambiguation.** The Khavinson-group studies that reported geroprotective outcomes used the polypeptide extract (Epithalamin), not the synthetic AEDG tetrapeptide. Direct extrapolation of those clinical findings to Epitalon is not validated.

**Grey-market / community convention (Epitalon, SC).** The current longevity-medicine grey market circulates protocols of 5–10 mg/day Epitalon subcutaneously for 10–20 days, run two to three times per year [6, anecdote_aggregate][7, anecdote_aggregate]. These figures have no regulatory or independent trial grounding. Per sourcing rules: grey-market figures are tagged `anecdote_aggregate` or `vendor_label` and may NOT ground any numerical safety claim, dose-response assertion, or efficacy statement in this wiki entry.

**Critical scale discrepancy.** Community SC protocols represent doses approximately 10–20 times higher than the doses documented in published human research [6, anecdote_aggregate]. No dose-escalation or maximum-tolerated-dose study exists.

### D.2 Route of administration

**Intramuscular (IM) — epithalamin, study-validated.** The route used in substantive Khavinson clinical trials [1, open_label][2, open_label][3, rct].

**Subcutaneous (SC) — epitalon, grey-market convention.** SC is the dominant route in current community use of synthetic Epitalon, supported only by community anecdote and vendor protocol sheets — not by controlled human trials [6, anecdote_aggregate][7, anecdote_aggregate].

**Sublingual.** Used in one published circadian study (0.5 mg/day × 20 days) [5, mechanism_review]. No replications.

**Intranasal.** Claimed in rodent and some veterinary-model work on CNS access via the olfactory route. No published human PK data support intranasal bioavailability in humans [5, mechanism_review].

**Parabulbar (ocular).** Used in ophthalmology trials; not relevant to systemic longevity dosing.

**Oral.** The peptide is predicted to be rapidly degraded by gastrointestinal proteases; oral use is not supported by any human absorption data and is not cited in any Khavinson human study [5, mechanism_review].

### D.3 Pharmacokinetics — near-total absence of human data

Modern human PK data for Epitalon is essentially absent. The following is what can be stated with confidence:

- **Molecular structure.** Epitalon is a tetrapeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly, MW ~390 Da). Short peptides of this class are generally susceptible to rapid protease degradation in plasma, with half-lives typically measured in minutes to low hours [5, mechanism_review].
- **Epithalamin half-life.** One secondary source cites an approximate half-life of ~3 hours for the crude epithalamin extract in Russian clinical use [9, practitioner_protocol]. This figure is not independently verified in a modern published PK study and applies to the extract, not the synthetic tetrapeptide.
- **Tissue distribution.** A rabbit study demonstrated rapid transfer of Epitalon across maternal-fetal tissues after administration, but systematic organ-level human distribution data do not exist [5, mechanism_review].
- **No ADME studies.** No formal absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion studies for synthetic Epitalon in humans have been published in peer-reviewed literature as of mid-2026 [5, mechanism_review].
- **Stereoisomer note.** Epitalon exists in eight stereoisomeric forms; only the all-L configuration (natural) has been studied. The PK and safety profiles of other stereoisomers are entirely uncharacterized [5, mechanism_review].

### D.4 Safety

#### D.4a What is known

**Short-term tolerability, Khavinson-group reports.** The Khavinson group's published human work describes epithalamin as well-tolerated at the studied doses in elderly populations [1, open_label][2, open_label][3, rct]. A retinitis pigmentosa clinical trial in 162 patients (Epitalon parabulbar) explicitly reported zero adverse events in the treatment group [5, mechanism_review]. However, the following structural limitations constrain what these data establish:

1. **Single-group provenance.** Virtually all published human safety data originates from the Khavinson institute in St. Petersburg. No independent replication in Western academic centres has been published [4, mechanism_review][5, mechanism_review].
2. **Study tier.** The clinical studies are primarily open-label observational cohorts or small prospective studies; none meet the design standards required by FDA, EMA, or ICH for formal safety characterisation (Tier 3 by this wiki's tier schema).
3. **Absent formal toxicology.** No regulatory-grade toxicology package exists: no repeat-dose toxicity, no reproductive/developmental toxicity, no carcinogenicity study, no formal dose-escalation or maximum tolerated dose (MTD) study has been published for Epitalon [5, mechanism_review].
4. **Community adverse event reports.** Anecdotal community self-reports describe mild injection-site reactions (redness, bruising, transient swelling) and brief fatigue in the first one to three doses [6, anecdote_aggregate]. These are unvalidated, self-reported, and not dose-characterised. They are noted for completeness only and may not ground any frequency claim.

#### D.4b The telomerase/oncogenic tension (load-bearing for risk-floor gate)

This is the principal unresolved theoretical safety concern and must be disclosed explicitly.

**Mechanism.** Epitalon induces expression of hTERT (the catalytic subunit of telomerase) and measurable telomerase activity in human somatic cells in vitro [10, in_vitro]. Telomere elongation has been demonstrated in fetal fibroblast culture [10, in_vitro] and in more recent cell-line work examining telomerase upregulation and alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT) [8, in_vitro].

**The theoretical oncogenic concern.** Telomerase reactivation is a near-universal hallmark of malignant transformation. Cancer cells depend on telomerase (or ALT) to escape replicative senescence and achieve immortality. A compound that activates telomerase in somatic cells therefore carries the theoretical risk of (a) promoting malignant transformation in cells that have acquired oncogenic mutations but retain functional checkpoints, and (b) supporting growth of occult pre-existing malignancy. This concern is biologically well-grounded and applies regardless of how the compound was classified originally [5, mechanism_review][8, in_vitro].

**The empirical counter-claim (Khavinson).** The Khavinson group's animal work has reported anti-tumor rather than pro-tumor effects: reduced spontaneous tumor incidence in ageing mice, 3.7-fold lower HER-2/neu mRNA expression in a transgenic mammary adenocarcinoma model, and delayed tumor onset in chemically induced colon cancer models [5, mechanism_review][4, mechanism_review]. Proponents interpret these findings as evidence that Epitalon's telomerase activation in normal somatic cells is oncostatic rather than oncogenic.

**Why the tension is not resolved.** The Khavinson anti-tumor data are:
- Produced by the same single group with the same tier limitations described in §D.4a.
- Based on animal models that may not predict human carcinogenicity.
- Short- to medium-term (months to a few years); chronic long-term carcinogenicity studies do not exist.
- Counteracted by the 2025 cell-line finding that Epitalon also activated the ALT pathway in cancer cell lines, an activity associated with aggressive tumor phenotypes [8, in_vitro].

No independent long-term carcinogenicity data exist for Epitalon in any species.

**Practitioner framing.** The telomerase oncogenic concern is a theoretical but biologically plausible risk. It cannot be dismissed on the basis of Khavinson animal work alone, and it cannot be confirmed on the basis of that same literature. The honest clinical position is: **the long-term oncogenic safety of chronic Epitalon use in humans is uncharacterised in independent studies.**

### D.5 Regulatory status

#### D.5a Russia

Epithalamin (the crude polypeptide extract, not synthetic Epitalon) has Russian Ministry of Health registration. Stated approved indications include menopause-related symptoms, anovulatory infertility, and hormone-dependent tumors [9, practitioner_protocol]. Epithalamin belongs to the cohort of six Khavinson-institute peptide bioregulators under serial production in Russia, alongside Thymalin, Thymogen, Prostatilen, Cortexin, and Retinalamin [4, mechanism_review].

**Synthetic Epitalon (AEDG tetrapeptide)** does not appear to hold a separate Russian drug registration distinct from epithalamin; published sources treat it as a research compound even in Russia. Practitioners should verify current Rosminzdrav GRLS registry status directly for the most current position.

#### D.5b United States (FDA)

Epitalon is not FDA-approved for any indication [5, mechanism_review][11, regulatory].

**503A compounding history:**
- Prior to September 2023: Epitalon was available through 503A compounding pharmacies as a nominated bulk drug substance under evaluation.
- September 2023 – April 2026: FDA placed Epitalon in Category 2 of the 503A bulk drug substances list, which effectively prohibited compounding pharmacies from using it in patient-specific preparations.
- April 15, 2026: FDA removed Epitalon (along with 11 other substances) from Category 2. This removal lifted the formal Category 2 prohibition but does NOT constitute placement on the 503A Bulks List and does NOT authorise compounding [11, regulatory].
- July 24, 2026 (scheduled): Epitalon is on the agenda of the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) for evaluation. A favourable PCAC recommendation would be a prerequisite for eventual 503A Bulks List inclusion, followed by formal FDA rulemaking — a process typically spanning additional months [11, regulatory].

**As of this writing (June 2026):** Epitalon is NOT lawful for 503A compounding. The July 2026 PCAC meeting has not yet occurred; its outcome is unknown at time of writing.

**503A regulatory status.** Epitalon is not an FDA-approved drug and is not currently eligible for 503A compounding. In the FDA's April 2026 Federal Register notice, epitalon (free base and acetate) was removed from the 503A Category 2 list, with a Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) hearing scheduled for July 23–24, 2026; this removal does NOT constitute authorization to compound [12, regulatory]. (A 2020 FDA warning letter to Tailor Made Compounding LLC, Case #594743, cited the firm for compounding non-503A-eligible bulk substances, but whether epitalon specifically was among the named substances could not be primary-verified at research time and is not relied upon here.)

#### D.5c WADA (World Anti-Doping Agency)

Epitalon is not explicitly named on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List [13, regulatory]. However, it is captured by Section S0 (Non-Approved Substances), which prohibits any pharmacological substance that does not hold regulatory authorisation for human therapeutic use in any jurisdiction and is not currently under active clinical investigation with regulatory oversight. Because Epitalon holds no such approval, S0 applies to it as a matter of WADA mechanism — it need not appear individually on the list to be prohibited in sanctioned sport [13, regulatory].

Athletes subject to WADA-compliant anti-doping programmes should treat Epitalon as prohibited in competition (and out of competition, if WADA extends S0 to OOC — verify current list text).

#### D.5d Other jurisdictions

Not approved by the EMA (European Medicines Agency) or any other major Western regulatory body. Status in other jurisdictions was not independently verified in this research pass.

### D.6 Contraindications, monitoring, and stopping criteria

The following reflects the theoretical risk profile and the absence of formal safety data. It is not a clinical guideline.

**Contraindications (consensus across clinical commentary):**
- Active malignancy (any type) — direct conflict with unresolved telomerase/oncogenic concern
- Prior malignancy — same concern; the risk that a compound activating telomerase could support occult recurrence is not dismissible without long-term safety data
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding — no reproductive or developmental toxicity data exist
- Autoimmune conditions — immune modulation reported in Khavinson animal work; human safety data absent

**Monitoring considerations (derived from theoretical risk profile):**
- Baseline cancer screening before any use: PSA, CEA where clinically indicated, dermatologic review, and age-appropriate malignancy screening (mammography, colonoscopy, etc.) — driven by the telomerase oncogenic concern
- Periodic re-screening at each proposed cycle, given the complete absence of long-term carcinogenicity data
- No validated biomarkers of Epitalon efficacy (telomere length assays are not standardised for clinical monitoring) [8, in_vitro]
- No established pharmacodynamic endpoint for dose adjustment

**Stopping criteria:**
- Any new malignancy diagnosis
- Any unexplained cell-proliferative signal on surveillance testing
- Pregnancy
- Onset of autoimmune flare

**The risk-floor statement (required by gate):** Given the near-total absence of independent human safety data, the uncharacterised long-term oncogenic risk of chronic telomerase activation, and the lack of formal dose-toxicity characterisation, the risk floor for Epitalon in humans is currently indeterminate. It cannot be asserted that any particular dose is safe for long-term use.
