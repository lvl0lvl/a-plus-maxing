# Section B: Reference Ranges, Units & Diagnostic Thresholds

## Overview

HbA1c is reported on two internationally recognized scales — NGSP percent (%) and IFCC mmol/mol — linked by a single master equation. Diagnostic cut points are issued by the ADA and WHO; treatment targets are individualized across a gradient from <6.5% to <8.0%.

---

## 1. Units and Standardization

The NGSP (National Glycohemoglobin Standardization Program) reports HbA1c as a percentage of total hemoglobin, aligned to the DCCT and UKPDS — the scale used in all ADA guidelines. The IFCC reference system [3, mechanism_review] expresses the same quantity as mmol HbA1c/mol Hb (mmol/mol); the IFCC formally adopted these units in 2007 to eliminate numerical confusion with NGSP values [4, regulatory].

### Master Conversion Equation

Established via formal network comparison across the US (NGSP), Japan, and Sweden [3, mechanism_review]:

> **NGSP (%) = [0.09148 × IFCC (mmol/mol)] + 2.152**

Rearranged: **IFCC (mmol/mol) = (NGSP % − 2.152) ÷ 0.09148**, equivalently (10.93 × NGSP %) − 23.50. This links IFCC values to DCCT/UKPDS clinical evidence [4, regulatory].

### Key Conversion Table (NGSP ↔ IFCC)

| NGSP (%) | IFCC (mmol/mol) |
|----------|-----------------|
| 5.0      | 31              |
| 5.7      | 39              |
| 6.0      | 42              |
| 6.5      | 48              |
| 7.0      | 53              |
| 7.5      | 58              |
| 8.0      | 64              |
| 9.0      | 75              |
| 10.0     | 86              |

*Source: NGSP Table 1 [4, regulatory]; 5.7% computed from master equation.*

## 2. Diagnostic Thresholds

### ADA Criteria (2025) [1, regulatory]

Three categories apply when HbA1c is measured on an NGSP-certified assay:

| Category | HbA1c (NGSP %) | HbA1c (IFCC mmol/mol) |
|----------|----------------|------------------------|
| Normal (low risk) | < 5.7% | < 39 mmol/mol |
| Prediabetes | 5.7–6.4% | 39–47 mmol/mol |
| Diabetes | ≥ 6.5% | ≥ 48 mmol/mol |

Diagnosis of diabetes requires confirmation on a repeat test on a separate day (absent unequivocal hyperglycemic symptoms). Point-of-care instruments not NGSP-certified for diagnostic use are excluded [1, regulatory].

### WHO Criteria (2011)

The World Health Organization's 2011 consultation report [2, regulatory] adopted ≥ 6.5% (≥ 48 mmol/mol) as the cut point for diagnosing diabetes, consistent with the ADA threshold. The WHO recommendation states:

> "HbA1c can be used as a diagnostic test for diabetes providing that stringent quality assurance tests are in place and assays are standardised to criteria aligned to the international reference values, and there are no conditions present which preclude its accurate measurement. An HbA1c of 6.5% is recommended as the cut point for diagnosing diabetes. A value of less than 6.5% does not exclude diabetes diagnosed using glucose tests."

Key WHO caveats [2, regulatory]: (1) a result < 6.5% does not exclude diabetes by glucose tests; (2) the 6.5% threshold rests on moderate-quality GRADE evidence linking it to microvascular risk; (3) assays must be calibrated against IFCC standards; (4) the WHO does **not** endorse a specific lower threshold for prediabetes — that 5.7–6.4% band is an ADA-specific construct.

## 3. Treatment Targets

### ADA 2025–2026 Glycemic Goals [1, 5, regulatory]

Treatment targets are individualized. The ADA framework defines goals across a spectrum:

| Goal Tier | HbA1c Target | IFCC Equivalent | When Appropriate |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| More stringent | < 6.5% | < 48 mmol/mol | Good health and function, low hypoglycemia risk, low treatment burden; short diabetes duration |
| General (most adults) | < 7.0% | < 53 mmol/mol | Most non-pregnant adults without significant hypoglycemia risk |
| Healthy older adults | < 7.0–7.5% | < 53–58 mmol/mol | Older adults with few coexisting conditions, intact cognitive/functional status |
| Intermediate older adults | < 8.0% | < 64 mmol/mol | Complex/intermediate health: multiple comorbidities, mild cognitive impairment, ≥2 IADL impairments |
| Less stringent / no A1C goal | < 8.0–8.5% or not set | < 64–69 mmol/mol | Very complex/poor health: long-term care, end-stage chronic illness, moderate-severe cognitive impairment, limited life expectancy |

ADA 2026 Recommendation 6.3a: "An A1C goal of <7% (<53 mmol/mol) is appropriate for many nonpregnant adults without severe hypoglycemia." Recommendation 6.4: "Lower A1C goals (e.g., <6.5% [<48 mmol/mol]) may be appropriate for individuals with good health and function and low treatment risks" [5, regulatory].

Factors favoring **more stringent** goals: short diabetes duration, low hypoglycemia risk, no cardiovascular complications. Factors favoring **less stringent**: long duration, high hypoglycemia risk, limited life expectancy, high treatment burden.

## 4. Estimated Average Glucose (eAG)

### ADAG Study Derivation

Nathan et al. (2008) [6, cohort] enrolled 507 participants (T1D, T2D, non-diabetic) across 10 international centers, capturing a median 52 days of continuous + 7-point capillary glucose monitoring per participant. Linear regression produced:

> **eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A1C (%) − 46.7** (R² = 0.84)
>
> **eAG (mmol/L) = 1.5944 × A1C (%) − 2.594**

The ADA's official eAG calculator [7, regulatory] publishes the following reference table:

### eAG Conversion Table (ADAG)

| HbA1c (%) | IFCC (mmol/mol) | eAG (mg/dL) | eAG (mmol/L) |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|--------------|
| 5.0       | 31              | 97          | 5.4          |
| 5.7       | 39              | 117         | 6.5          |
| 6.0       | 42              | 126         | 7.0          |
| 6.5       | 48              | 140         | 7.8          |
| 7.0       | 53              | 154         | 8.6          |
| 7.5       | 58              | 169         | 9.4          |
| 8.0       | 64              | 183         | 10.1         |
| 9.0       | 75              | 212         | 11.8         |
| 10.0      | 86              | 240         | 13.4         |

*eAG values from ADA eAG calculator [7, regulatory] and NGSP Table 1 [4, regulatory]; 5.7% eAG computed from ADAG equation. IFCC values from master equation [3, 4, regulatory].*

An HbA1c of 7.0% (the general treatment target) corresponds to an eAG of 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L) — substantially above the normal fasting glucose range of 70–99 mg/dL, reflecting the integration of fasting and postprandial glucose over the ~3-month erythrocyte lifespan.

## 5. Population Distribution and the "Normal" vs. "Optimal" Framing

### Observed Population Distribution

NHANES data (1999–2010, adults ≥18 years) [8, cohort] yield the following age-adjusted HbA1c distribution across the entire US adult population:

| Percentile | HbA1c (%) |
|------------|-----------|
| 5th        | 4.6–4.8   |
| 25th       | 5.0–5.2   |
| 50th (median) | 5.2–5.5 |
| 75th       | 5.5–5.8   |
| 95th       | 6.6–6.9   |

Among non-diabetic US adults specifically, the mean is ~5.3% (34 mmol/mol) and the mode is ~5.3–5.4% (34–36 mmol/mol), with a right-skew from undiagnosed prediabetes [9, cohort].

### "Normal" vs. "Optimal" Framing

The ADA's < 5.7% "normal" category is a **regulatory risk-stratification boundary**, not a biological optimum. The population median in non-diabetic US adults (~5.3–5.4%) falls well below it; many clinicians interpret the 5.0–5.4% range as physiologically typical. No ADA-designated "optimal" sub-range exists below 5.7%. Very low HbA1c (< 5.0%) appears in some population analyses to be associated with increased all-cause mortality, likely confounded by hemolytic conditions, chronic illness, or malnutrition causing falsely low values — this is not a basis for ADA guidance and requires clinical context.
