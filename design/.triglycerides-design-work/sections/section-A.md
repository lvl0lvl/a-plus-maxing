# Section A — Identity, Physiology & Causal Significance (Triglycerides)

## What triglycerides are, and where they travel

Triglycerides (TG) are the principal dietary and stored fat, transported in plasma not as free molecules but packaged inside **triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (TRLs)**: chylomicrons (intestinally derived, carrying dietary fat after a meal) and very-low-density lipoproteins (VLDL, hepatically derived). Lipoprotein lipase (LpL) at the capillary endothelium hydrolyzes the TG core, progressively shrinking these particles into smaller, cholesterol-enriched **remnants** [1, mechanism_review]. As lipolysis proceeds, the remnant becomes proportionally depleted of TG and enriched in cholesterol — VLDL remnants in particular are "smaller and more cholesterol-rich" after lipolysis, with prolonged circulation and enhanced arterial-wall penetration [1, mechanism_review].

## The central distinction: remnant cholesterol mediates, TG marks

The pivotal physiological point for interpreting a TG value is that **the TG molecule itself is not directly atherogenic** — it is hydrolyzed and used for energy, whereas it is the **cholesterol carried within TRL remnants that drives atherogenesis** through cholesterol deposition in the arterial wall [1, mechanism_review]. Remnant particles are small enough to enter and be retained in the intima, and per particle they can deliver "up to 4 times more cholesterol to the arterial wall than LDL particles" [1, mechanism_review]. A plasma TG measurement is therefore best read as a **marker of the concentration of atherogenic remnant cholesterol**, not as a directly causal toxin. This framing — remnant cholesterol as mediator, TG as the readily measured surrogate — is the honest description of the causal chain and governs everything downstream in this reference.

## Observational association

Across 29 Western prospective studies (10,158 incident coronary heart disease [CHD] cases among 262,525 participants), individuals in the top third of usual log-triglycerides had an adjusted odds ratio for CHD of **1.72 (95% CI 1.56–1.90)** versus the bottom third; the effect was "substantially attenuated" after adjustment for established risk factors (e.g., OR 1.76 [95% CI 1.39–2.21] in Reykjavik, 1.57 [95% CI 1.10–2.24] in EPIC-Norfolk) [8, meta_analysis]. The attenuation under adjustment is exactly why observational data alone cannot settle causality — TG travels with HDL-C, LDL-C, adiposity, and insulin resistance — and is the motivation for the genetic evidence below.

## Causal evidence: Mendelian randomization

Genetic instruments break the confounding by exploiting variants that randomly perturb lifelong TRL/remnant levels.

- **APOA5 −1131T>C (rs662799)** raises TG by 16.0% (95% CI 12.9–18.7), or 0.25 mmol/L (0.20–0.29), per C allele (p=4.4×10⁻²⁴), while being unassociated with LDL-C or most non-lipid risk factors. The per-allele CHD odds ratio was **1.18 (95% CI 1.11–1.26; p=2.6×10⁻⁷)**, concordant with the observational hazard ratio of **1.10 (95% CI 1.08–1.12) per 16% higher TG** — the genetic and observational estimates agreeing, the signature of causality. The variant also raised VLDL particle concentration (12.2 nmol/L, 95% CI 7.7–16.7), a plausible mediator [7, meta_analysis].
- **Do et al. (185 lipid variants)**: in a model accounting for each variant's effect on LDL-C and/or HDL-C, the strength of a polymorphism's effect on TG correlated with the magnitude of its effect on CHD risk, supporting that **TRLs causally influence CHD** independently of LDL-C/HDL-C [6, cohort].
- **Remnant cholesterol, genetically elevated** (Copenhagen cohorts, 60,608 individuals, 10,668 IHD cases): a causal risk ratio for ischemic heart disease of **3.3 (95% CI 2.1–5.2) per 1 mmol/L** of remnant cholesterol, alongside a causal **28% higher CRP (95% CI 10–48)** — i.e., remnant cholesterol causes both low-grade inflammation and IHD, whereas elevated LDL-C causes IHD **without** inflammation (no causal CRP association) [2, cohort]. This dual mechanism (lipid loading **plus** inflammation) is specific to the remnant pathway.
- **APOC3 loss-of-function** carriers (498 carriers, 110,472 noncarriers) had **39% lower TG** and a **40% lower CHD risk (OR 0.60, 95% CI 0.47–0.75)** — the mirror-image experiment: genetically lowering TRLs lowers disease [9, meta_analysis].
- **TRL cholesterol content directly**: in the Women's Health Study (480 cases), top-quartile TRL-cholesterol carried HR **3.05 (95% CI 1.46–6.39)** for myocardial infarction, independent of LDL-C and hsCRP — confirming the cholesterol *in* the remnant, not the TG, is the atherogenic quantity [5, cohort].

Taken together, raising TRLs (APOA5, remnant-C alleles) raises risk and lowering them (APOC3 LoF) lowers risk, with the cholesterol cargo as the consistent mediator.

## The residual-risk concept

Even with LDL-C well controlled on statins, "significant residual risk persists," and remnant cholesterol is a leading contributor to this **residual cardiovascular risk** independent of LDL-C management — the rationale for measuring and addressing TRLs after LDL-C targets are met [1, mechanism_review].

## A distinct, non-atherogenic danger: very-high-TG pancreatitis

The very-high-TG → acute pancreatitis hazard is mechanistically separate from atherosclerosis. The Endocrine Society strata are: normal <150 mg/dL (<1.7 mmol/L); mild 150–199 (1.7–2.3); moderate 200–999 (2.3–11.2); severe 1000–1999 (11.2–22.4); very severe ≥2000 mg/dL (≥22.4 mmol/L) [4, mechanism_review]. At these very high levels the LpL clearance system is **saturated** and **chylomicrons accumulate** (chylomicronemia) [4, mechanism_review]. "The risk of pancreatitis markedly increases with very severe triglyceride levels above 2000 mg/dl, but it can be prevented by maintaining triglyceride levels below 1000 mg/dl" [4, mechanism_review]. Thus the two domains diverge: "mild or moderate hypertriglyceridemia may be a risk factor for cardiovascular disease," whereas "severe and very severe hypertriglyceridemia increase the risk for pancreatitis" — the former a remnant/cholesterol problem, the latter a chylomicron-mass problem [4, mechanism_review].

## Fasting vs non-fasting biology (postprandial lipemia)

After a habitual meal, TRLs rise as dietary chylomicrons enter — but the maximal mean change in TG at 1–6 h was judged clinically insignificant at only **+0.3 mmol/L (26 mg/dL)** [3, mechanism_review]. Consequently, the European Atherosclerosis Society / EFLM consensus recommends **non-fasting lipid profiles for most patients**, flags abnormal non-fasting TG at **≥2 mmol/L (175 mg/dL)**, and reserves a repeat fasting sample for non-fasting TG **>5 mmol/L (440 mg/dL)** [3, mechanism_review]. Non-fasting measures predict cardiovascular risk at least as well as fasting [3, mechanism_review] — biologically sensible, since postprandial remnants are precisely the atherogenic species the test aims to capture.
