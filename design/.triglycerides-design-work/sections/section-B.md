# Section B — Reference Ranges, Measurement & Determinants (Triglycerides)

*Goal-agnostic canonical reference. All numeric claims carry inline `[ref, tag]`. Numbers are sourced only from Tier 1 primary literature, Tier 2 regulators, or society guidelines/consensus statements (cited as `mechanism_review`). No personalization.*

## 1. Reference ranges (fasting)

The reference classification of fasting serum/plasma triglycerides (TG) derives from the U.S. National Cholesterol Education Program (NCEP) Adult Treatment Panel III (ATP III) and is echoed across society guidelines. The four fasting bands are: **normal <150 mg/dL; borderline-high 150–199 mg/dL; high 200–499 mg/dL; very high ≥500 mg/dL** [1, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review][10, mechanism_review]. "Desirable" fasting TG is <150 mg/dL [3, mechanism_review], and the 2011 AHA scientific statement additionally designates an **optimal** fasting band of <100 mg/dL [10, mechanism_review].

The Endocrine Society (2012) uses a finer scheme: mild 150–199, moderate 200–999, severe 1,000–1,999, and very severe ≥2,000 mg/dL [1, mechanism_review].

**Pancreatitis thresholds.** Acute-pancreatitis risk rises with TG and is documented as increased at ≥500 mg/dL, becoming clinically significant at ≥880 mg/dL (~10 mmol/L) and dramatically elevated above 1,500–2,000 mg/dL [1, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review]; between 12% and 38% of acute-pancreatitis patients present with TG ≥1,000 mg/dL [1, mechanism_review]. Pancreatitis can usually be prevented by keeping TG below 250–500 mg/dL [3, mechanism_review].

## 2. Reference ranges (non-fasting) and unit conversion

Non-fasting sampling is increasingly the default. The AHA designates a non-fasting TG of **≥200 mg/dL** as the trigger for a confirmatory fasting panel within ~2–4 weeks [10, mechanism_review], while the European Atherosclerosis Society (EAS) / EFLM 2016 joint consensus flags non-fasting TG as **abnormal at ≥2 mmol/L (175 mg/dL)** and fasting TG as abnormal at ≥1.7 mmol/L (150 mg/dL) [2, mechanism_review]. A Women's Health Study analysis derived an optimal non-fasting cutpoint of **175 mg/dL (1.98 mmol/L)**, statistically superior (c-statistic 0.656) to the AHA 200 mg/dL value (0.628) [11, cohort].

**Unit conversion:** divide mg/dL by **88.5** to obtain mmol/L; i.e., a 1 mmol/L change in TG ≈ 88.5 mg/dL [1, mechanism_review]. Thus 150 mg/dL ≈ 1.7 mmol/L, 175 ≈ 2.0, 500 ≈ 5.6, and 880 ≈ 10 mmol/L [1, mechanism_review][2, mechanism_review].

## 3. Measurement

TG are quantified by an automated **enzymatic (GPO-PAP) assay**: lipase hydrolyzes TG to glycerol + free fatty acids; glycerol kinase phosphorylates glycerol to glycerol-3-phosphate; glycerophosphate oxidase (GPO) oxidizes it to hydrogen peroxide; and peroxidase couples the H₂O₂ with 4-aminoantipyrine and a phenol to form a red Trinder chromogen whose absorbance is proportional to TG [8, mechanism_review]. The assay measures the glycerol backbone, so endogenous **free glycerol** overestimates TG unless removed by a blanking pre-incubation with GPO + peroxidase [8, mechanism_review].

**Biological variability is high.** TG show the largest within-individual variation of the standard lipid panel — within-individual CV ≈ 25% (between-individual CV ≈ 35.9%) in healthy adults [5, cohort], with reported biological-fluctuation CV ranging ~5–25% and total intra-individual variability ~24% [5, cohort]; TG variability is at least double that of total-, HDL- and LDL-cholesterol [5, cohort]. This drives the post-meal change: TG rise a maximal mean of only +0.3 mmol/L (26 mg/dL) at 1–6 h post-meal, which underpinned the 2016 EAS/EFLM recommendation to use non-fasting profiles routinely, reserving a fasting draw for non-fasting TG >5 mmol/L (440 mg/dL) [2, mechanism_review].

## 4. Determinants

**Diet (major, modifiable).** Fructose (from sucrose and high-fructose corn syrup) and refined carbohydrate raise hepatic de-novo lipogenesis via SREBP1c/ChREBP, increase TG-rich VLDL secretion, and upregulate APOC3/apoC-III (an LPL inhibitor) — yielding hypertriglyceridemia and atherogenic remnants [9, mechanism_review]. Alcohol increases hepatic large-VLDL synthesis and acutely inhibits lipoprotein lipase, adding to the post-prandial TG peak [9, mechanism_review].

**Adiposity, insulin resistance, T2D.** Obesity, insulin resistance and uncontrolled type 2 diabetes are major secondary drivers (increased VLDL production with impaired LPL-mediated clearance); hypothyroidism also raises TG [1, mechanism_review][9, mechanism_review].

**Genetics.** Familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) is a rare autosomal-recessive monogenic disorder caused by biallelic variants in **LPL, APOC2, APOA5, GPIHBP1, or LMF1**; 50–90% of FCS cases are biallelic LPL, the remaining 10–50% in the other four genes [6, mechanism_review]. apoC-II (APOC2) and apoA-V (APOA5) are LPL cofactors, and GPIHBP1 transports/anchors LPL at the capillary endothelium [6, mechanism_review]. Far more common is **multifactorial (polygenic) chylomicronemia** — a cluster of minor variants exacerbated by secondary factors — affecting ~1 in 600 of the population [6, mechanism_review].

**Drugs that raise TG.** Oral estrogens, corticosteroids, retinoids (isotretinoin), beta-blockers, thiazides, tamoxifen/raloxifene, oral contraceptives, protease inhibitors, atypical antipsychotics, bile-acid sequestrants and immunosuppressants [1, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review].

## 5. TG-lowering therapies (reference magnitudes)

- **Fibrates:** ~30–50% TG reduction [3, mechanism_review]; in CV-outcome meta-analysis, 10% RR reduction for major CV events (95% CI 0–18) and 13% for coronary events (95% CI 7–19) [1, mechanism_review].
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** ~20–50% TG reduction [3, mechanism_review]; prescription omega-3-acid ethyl esters (Lovaza) are FDA-approved for very-high TG ≥500 mg/dL at 4 g/day, lowering TG ~39–45% in pivotal trials [7, regulatory].
- **Icosapent ethyl (high-purity EPA):** in REDUCE-IT (n=8,179; fasting TG 135–499 mg/dL on statin), 4 g/day reduced the primary composite CV endpoint from 22.0% to 17.2%, hazard ratio 0.75 (95% CI 0.68–0.83; ~25% relative risk reduction) [4, rct].
- **Statins:** modest, ~10–30% TG reduction (greater at higher baseline TG) [1, mechanism_review][3, mechanism_review].
- **Niacin** ~10–30% and **ezetimibe** ~5–10% for context [3, mechanism_review].
