## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — What Respiratory Rate Is & What the Device Measures:** **Respiratory rate (RR)** = the number of breaths per minute (**breaths/min, brpm**), controlled by the **brainstem respiratory centers (pre-Bötzinger complex / medulla + pons)**, modulated by central + peripheral chemoreceptors (CO2/pH the dominant drive, O2 secondary) and by metabolic demand, emotion, and voluntary override. It is one of the **four classic vital signs** (with HR, BP, temperature). **What WEARABLES measure:** consumer devices (Oura, Apple Watch, Fitbit, WHOOP, Garmin) DERIVE RR from the **PPG signal during sleep** via **respiratory modulation of the cardiac waveform — three mechanisms: respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA — frequency modulation of HR by breathing), baseline/amplitude modulation, and pulse-amplitude variation**; some also use accelerometry (chest/wrist micro-movement) or a dedicated respiration sensor. Devices typically report a **single nocturnal average RR** (the most stable, motion-free window). Note RR derived from PPG is an ESTIMATE of the true ventilatory rate; it is measured at rest/sleep, not during activity. Distinguish RR from tidal volume / minute ventilation (RR is rate only).
- **B — Interpretation, Norms & The Personal-Baseline:** **POPULATION NORMS:** the conventional normal adult resting RR is **~12-20 breaths/min** (commonly cited; some sources 12-18); **>20-24 = tachypnea**, **<12 = bradypnea** (context-dependent). RR is **higher in children** (age-dependent — give the pediatric context briefly) and rises modestly with some conditions. **THE LOAD-BEARING WEARABLE POINT: nocturnal RR is REMARKABLY STABLE within an individual night-to-night (a tight personal baseline, often varying <1 brpm) → small DEVIATIONS from one's own baseline are meaningful**; this is WHY wearable RR is a sensitive personal-trend signal (more so than the wide population range suggests). The personal-baseline-trend framing: an elevated nocturnal RR vs YOUR baseline is an early flag for illness/fever/stress/alcohol/altitude. Note RR is LESS inter-individually variable at rest than HRV but the within-person stability is the key asset. The measurement-consistency requirement (nocturnal, same device).
- **C — Measurement & Device Validity:** **Reference standards:** clinical RR is counted by **manual observation (counting chest rises over 30-60 s — the bedside standard, but error-prone + often inaccurately charted)**, **capnography (end-tidal CO2 — the gold standard for true breath detection)**, **respiratory inductance plethysmography (RIP belts)**, or **PSG (thermistor/nasal-pressure/RIP)** in sleep studies. **PPG-derived RR (the wearable method):** extracts RR from the respiratory-induced modulations of the PPG waveform (RSA, amplitude, baseline); **accuracy is HIGH at rest/during sleep — typically within ±1 breath/min (MAE often <1 brpm) vs capnography/PSG reference** — because nocturnal breathing is regular + motion-free; accuracy DEGRADES with motion, irregular breathing, low signal quality, and during apneas. Cite NAMED independent validation studies + a systematic review/meta (algorithm-comparison + Bland-Altman bias). Note the **consistency-of-conditions + within-device-trend** message; proprietary algorithms (not interchangeable across devices); most consumer RR is a **WELLNESS metric, NOT a diagnostic measurement** (does not diagnose apnea/respiratory failure, though some devices flag breathing-disturbance/SpO2 events). Disclose any vendor-funded-validation COI.
- **D — Determinants & Significance:** **What RAISES RR:** **FEVER / ILLNESS / INFECTION (a key early signal — RR rises with fever + respiratory/systemic infection; nocturnal RR elevation often PRECEDES other symptoms — demonstrated in wearable COVID-detection studies)**; **exertion / metabolic demand**; **anxiety / stress / panic (hyperventilation)**; **pain**; **metabolic acidosis (compensatory — e.g. DKA/Kussmaul breathing)**; **hypoxia / altitude / lung disease (asthma, COPD, pneumonia, PE, heart failure)**; **alcohol (acute changes — variable)**; **pregnancy** (mild rise); **stimulants**. **What LOWERS RR:** **opioids / sedatives / anesthesia (respiratory depression — a key safety signal)**; CNS depression; hypothyroidism; sleep (modest physiological reduction); trained calm/slow-breathing. **SIGNIFICANCE (load-bearing):** (1) **RR is a powerful but UNDERUSED clinical VITAL SIGN — an abnormal/rising RR is one of the EARLIEST + STRONGEST predictors of clinical DETERIORATION** (it is heavily weighted in **Early Warning Scores — NEWS2/MEWS**; cite the vital-sign/deterioration evidence + a guideline); RR is frequently the first vital to change before cardiac arrest/ICU transfer, yet is the most poorly measured/charted in practice. (2) **The wearable EARLY-ILLNESS-DETECTION use (the marquee consumer application): an elevated nocturnal RR vs personal baseline is among the most reliable wearable signals of incipient infection** (cite the wearable illness/COVID-detection cohorts — honest about sensitivity/specificity + false positives). (3) sleep-disordered-breathing screening (some devices). **LIMITATIONS (load-bearing):** consumer RR is a single nocturnal estimate (not continuous true ventilation); it does NOT capture tidal volume / minute ventilation / breathing quality; PPG-RR degrades with motion + in arrhythmia; it is a WELLNESS/trend metric NOT a diagnostic test (an elevated reading warrants context, not alarm); the illness-detection signal has false positives (alcohol, late meals, room heat, hard training all raise nocturnal RR); interpret the TREND vs personal baseline alongside other signals.
