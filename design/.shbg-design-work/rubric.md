# Rubric — shbg (biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin) = a **hepatically-synthesized glycoprotein** that binds sex steroids in plasma with high affinity — **DHT > testosterone > estradiol** (binds androgens > estrogens); homodimer, one steroid-binding site per dimer; the **regulator of sex-steroid bioavailability** (SHBG-bound hormone is NOT bioavailable); hepatic SHBG production is suppressed by **insulin** (and by androgens, and raised by **estrogen + thyroid hormone**) — the mechanistic basis for its metabolic associations; a small membrane/receptor-mediated signaling role (RSHBG) noted; what serum SHBG indexes (binding capacity governing free/bioavailable T + E2).
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Thresholds:** adult reference intervals (men ~**~18–54 nmol/L**; women ~**~40–120 nmol/L**, both assay/age-dependent — women higher); units nmol/L (mg/L rarely); the strong AGE + SEX dependence (rises with age in men; higher in women; falls with obesity); its primary clinical use — **interpreting total testosterone** (low SHBG → total T underestimates bioavailable androgen; high SHBG → total T overestimates) + computing **calculated free T** and the **free androgen index (FAI = total T/SHBG ×100)**; no single universal cutpoint; the lack of full assay harmonization.
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** measured by **immunoassay** (immunometric/sandwich, chemiluminescent) — note the lack of a reference-method standardization program comparable to testosterone's CDC HoSt (SHBG assay variation propagates into calculated free T); interferences (biotin, heterophile antibodies); biological variability; the SHBG input is a key error source in cFT (cross-ref free-T); sample stability; why method matters when SHBG feeds cFT/FAI.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **what LOWERS SHBG** — insulin resistance / hyperinsulinemia (central mechanism), obesity / central adiposity, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, NAFLD/MASLD, androgens / anabolic steroids, glucocorticoids, hypothyroidism, growth hormone / acromegaly, progestins, nephrotic syndrome; **what RAISES SHBG** — aging, estrogen / oral contraceptives / pregnancy, hyperthyroidism, hepatic cirrhosis, anorexia / caloric restriction, anticonvulsants, HIV; **SHBG as a biomarker in its own right** — LOW SHBG independently predicts incident **type 2 diabetes** and metabolic syndrome (cite named cohorts/meta-analyses + the Mendelian-randomization evidence suggesting SHBG may be causal, not just a marker, in T2D); associations with CVD, fatty liver; the testosterone-interpretation role (the dominant clinical use); limitations — not a standalone diagnostic, assay variation, must be paired with total T for androgen assessment.
