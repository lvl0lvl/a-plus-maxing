## Executive Summary

A genetics & pharmacogenomics specialist agent operates over a domain where the central, recurring fact is that **a sequence variant is not a diagnosis**. Variant meaning is a probabilistic judgment built from structured evidence (ACMG/AMP 2015 five-tier classification, reformulated as a Bayesian point system), modulated by penetrance and expressivity, anchored to ancestry-matched population frequency (gnomAD), and — for consumer data — only as trustworthy as the assay that produced the call. Direct-to-consumer (DTC) raw genotype files are SNP-array research artifacts with a documented ~40% false-positive rate on clinical confirmation and a positive predictive value as low as 4.2% for pathogenic BRCA-region calls; they are hypotheses, never diagnoses, and any actionable call requires clinical-grade confirmation. Pharmacogenomics is genuinely actionable for a defined set of high-evidence drug-gene pairs (DPYD/fluoropyrimidines, TPMT+NUDT15/thiopurines, CYP2C9+VKORC1/warfarin, SLCO1B1/simvastatin, HLA-B*57:01/abacavir, HLA-B*15:02 & B*58:01/carbamazepine & allopurinol), governed by CPIC, PharmGKB, and the FDA biomarker tables — but PGx **informs prescribing; it never authorizes it**, both because the genotype is one input among many and because phenoconversion (drug- and inflammation-induced) routinely uncouples genotype from real-time phenotype. Nutrigenomic "wellness" variants are real but small (FTO explains <1% of BMI variance) and dominated by gene×environment interaction; MTHFR testing is explicitly disrecommended by ACMG for its most common indications. Disease-risk variants (BRCA1/2, Lynch/MMR, familial hypercholesterolemia, hereditary thrombophilia, APOE-ε4) are not self-managed: they route to a certified genetic counselor + physician, with EMERGENCY-class findings (arrhythmia/cardiomyopathy genes) escalated. Genetic data is uniquely sensitive (predictive, familial, immutable, identifying), GINA's protections stop at health insurance and employment (life/disability/LTC insurance are exposed), and the agent's correct posture across all of it is a **literacy-and-routing layer over a clinician-governed domain**.

## Introduction

## Main Analysis — Findings

Each finding states the load-bearing claim, the evidence, the AGENT_TEMPLATE.md section(s) it informs, and the cross-section pattern it participates in.

### Finding 1 — Variant classification is a probabilistic five-tier judgment, not a binary call

The reference standard for germline sequence-variant classification in Mendelian-disease genes is the ACMG/AMP 2015 joint consensus (Richards et al. 2015) [mechanism_review], which defines five terms — Pathogenic, Likely Pathogenic, Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS), Likely Benign, Benign — and a framework of coded evidence criteria along two axes (direction: pathogenic vs benign; strength: very strong / strong / moderate / supporting). "Likely" denotes ~>90% certainty; the VUS bin absorbs the unresolved. The qualitative combining rules were shown by Tavtigian et al. (2018) [mechanism_review] to be closely approximated by a naive Bayesian classifier in which each strength level corresponds to an exponentially scaling odds-of-pathogenicity weight — giving the framework a quantitative backbone and underpinning later point-based scoring. ClinGen's Sequence Variant Interpretation (SVI) Working Group issues gene- and criterion-specific refinements so the framework is applied consistently. **Implication:** the agent must communicate classification as a tier with an associated confidence, never as a yes/no, and must surface VUS as genuinely uncertain rather than implying pathogenicity. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Communication.* *Pattern: "probabilistic, not binary" (shared with F2, F3).*

### Finding 2 — Penetrance and expressivity separate "carries a variant" from "has the disease"

The foundational error to guard against is equating a pathogenic genotype with the phenotype. **Penetrance** is the proportion of carriers who manifest the phenotype (reduced/incomplete when not all do); **expressivity** is the severity among those affected (variable when it differs). Cooper et al. (2013) [mechanism_review] catalog the molecular basis of reduced penetrance — age- and sex-dependent expression, trans-acting modifier genes, cis variation, epigenetic state, copy-number context, allelic-expression imbalance, and environment — and note that ostensibly healthy individuals carry many predicted-deleterious variants without disease. Penetrance estimates derived from ascertained, affected families systematically overstate risk for an individual found to carry the same variant incidentally. **Implication:** every positive finding is framed as a probability conditioned on incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity, never a determined outcome — this is the evidentiary basis of the "a risk variant is not a disease" safety floor. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Anti-Patterns.* *Pattern: risk-variant ≠ disease (shared with F12).*

### Finding 3 — Polygenic risk scores are real but have poor cross-ancestry portability

Disease architecture spans monogenic (high-effect single-gene, ACMG-interpreted) to polygenic (thousands of small-effect common variants summed into a polygenic risk score, PRS). Two clinical-utility limits are load-bearing: a PRS estimates relative position in a risk distribution (not a diagnosis) and typically explains modest variance; and PRS portability across genetic ancestries is poor — because most GWAS discovery samples are European-ancestry, prediction accuracy decays with ancestral distance. Martin et al. (2019) [mechanism_review] found prediction accuracy "several times" higher in European-ancestry individuals; their within-paper analysis put the average decrement at roughly **4.9-fold lower in individuals of African ancestry** relative to European (a figures/results-level number — the abstract states only "several times"), with intermediate reductions in East/South Asian groups, warning that clinical PRS deployment could exacerbate health disparities. Multi-ancestry GWAS and portability-correction methods are an active, partly-successful mitigation front (the field has moved since 2019), so the operative rule is calibration-by-evidence, not blanket discounting. **Implication:** any PRS-derived statement must be qualified by the ancestry composition of the underlying GWAS and treated as less reliable for non-European individuals unless a validated multi-ancestry score is used. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Ask vs Proceed, Anti-Patterns.* *Pattern: ancestry-dependence (shared with F4, F10, F11).*

### Finding 4 — Allele frequency (gnomAD), ancestry-matched, anchors benign criteria

Population allele frequency is among the most powerful single pieces of variant evidence. The Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD) flagship release (Karczewski et al. 2020) [cohort] aggregated 125,748 exomes and 15,708 genomes (141,456 individuals), identifying 443,769 high-confidence predicted loss-of-function variants. gnomAD's ancestry-stratified frequencies are the denominator for the ACMG benign criteria: stand-alone BA1 (allele frequency >5% in a reference population) and strong BS1 (frequency greater than expected for the disorder). ClinGen refined these thresholds gene-by-gene rather than a universal 5% cutoff. **Implication:** the agent must use ancestry-matched frequencies; an aggregate-population denominator misclassifies variants that are common in one subpopulation. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Context Loading.* *Pattern: ancestry-dependence.*

### Finding 5 — ClinVar/ClinGen require review-status discipline; a bare label is not ground truth

ClinVar (Landrum et al. 2018) [mechanism_review] is the NIH public archive of variant interpretations submitted by labs, research groups, and expert panels; it aggregates by variant–condition pair and flags "conflicting interpretations of pathogenicity" when submitters disagree within the five-tier scale. Conflicts are common and consequential — the same variant may be Pathogenic to one lab and VUS/Likely Benign to another. ClinGen's Variant Curation Expert Panels and the star-based review-status system exist precisely so downstream users weight expert-panel/practice-guideline assertions above single unverified submissions. **Implication:** the agent must inspect review status, submitter count/identity, and conflict flags — never treat a bare "Pathogenic" label as truth, and distinguish an outdated single submission from current expert consensus. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Context Loading.* *Pattern: provenance-discipline (shared with F6).*

### Finding 6 — DTC raw genotype data is not diagnostic (highest safety weight)

This is the most safety-critical subtopic. Consumer products (23andMe, AncestryDNA) use **SNP genotyping arrays** that interrogate several hundred thousand pre-selected positions via fluorescence-clustering across a batch — fundamentally different from clinical-grade sequencing. Array accuracy collapses for rare variants because clustering needs enough carriers to define a reliable cluster, and rare pathogenic variants by definition have almost none. The documented consequences: a clinical-laboratory case series of 49 confirmation samples (Tandy-Connor et al. 2018) [open_label] found **~40% of variants were false positives**, many in clinically significant cancer-risk genes; a population-based evaluation of 49,908 UK Biobank participants with both chip and sequencing data (Weedon et al. 2021) [cohort] found, for very rare variants (frequency <~0.001%), a chip positive predictive value of only **~16%**, and for pathogenic BRCA-region variants a PPV of just **4.2%** (of 889 chip-detected pathogenic BRCA calls, only 37 were true positives) — concluding that a positive chip result for a very rare pathogenic variant is more likely wrong than right and must be validated by a standard diagnostic test before any clinical action. The FDA's March 2018 authorization of the 23andMe BRCA1/BRCA2 (Selected Variants) report (FDA 2018) [regulatory] covered only three Ashkenazi-founder variants and stated the test "only detects three out of more than 1,000 known BRCA mutations," that "a negative result does not rule out" other mutations, and that it "should not be used as a substitute for seeing your doctor"; a peer-reviewed analysis (Kilbride et al. 2019) [regulatory] emphasized that even the authorized health report requires results be "confirmed in a clinical setting before taking any medical action." The unvalidated **raw** genotype file a consumer can export carries none of those controls. **Implication — the DTC safety floor:** a clinically actionable call read from DTC raw data is a hypothesis, not a result, and MUST be confirmed by clinical-grade testing in an accredited laboratory before any action or real-risk communication. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Anti-Patterns, Negative Examples.* *Pattern: provenance-discipline; DTC-raw≠diagnostic floor.*

### Finding 7 — Pharmacogenes follow a star-allele → activity-score → phenotype framework with CNV complexity

CYP variation is catalogued by PharmVar (Gaedigk et al. 2018) [mechanism_review] using the star (*) system (*1 = reference). A diplotype's per-allele function statuses/activity values sum to an **activity score** mapped to a metabolizer phenotype: Poor (PM), Intermediate (IM), Normal (NM), Rapid/Ultrarapid (RM/UM). For CYP2D6 the CPIC-led consensus (Caudle et al. 2020) [mechanism_review] standardized the mapping — activity score 0 → PM; 0.25–1.0 → IM; **1.25–2.25 → NM** (an activity score of exactly 1.25 is a Normal Metabolizer); >2.25 → UM — and downgraded *10 from 0.5 to 0.25. CYP2D6 is uniquely complex (adjacent pseudogenes, deletions [*5], duplications/multiplications, hybrid alleles); gene multiplication of a functional allele is the principal ultrarapid mechanism, which is why CYP2D6 needs copy-number-aware assays beyond SNP panels. The other principal pharmacogenes — CYP2C19 (*2/*3 no-function, *17 gain), CYP2C9 (*2/*3 reduced), CYP3A5 (*3 abolishes expression; most Europeans are non-expressers) — follow the same logic. **Implication:** the agent must reason in phenotype terms, flag that CYP2D6 calls from SNP-only panels may miss CNV, and never treat a metabolizer label as assay-independent. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Tools.* *Pattern: assay-literacy (shared with F6).*

### Finding 8 — CPIC/PharmGKB/FDA govern a defined set of high-evidence, life-relevant drug-gene pairs

CPIC (Relling et al. 2020) [mechanism_review] translates an already-available genotype into a prescribing recommendation (Level A "should change prescribing," Level B "could," C/D none); PharmGKB (Whirl-Carrillo et al. 2021) [mechanism_review] grades the underlying evidence (1A high → 4 negative); the FDA maintains a Table of Pharmacogenomic Biomarkers in Drug Labeling and a Table of Pharmacogenetic Associations (FDA, regulatory), which overlap but diverge from CPIC in scope (Pritchard et al. 2022) [mechanism_review]. The load-bearing pairs: **DPYD/fluoropyrimidines** (Amstutz et al. 2018) [mechanism_review] — DPD-deficient patients risk fatal toxicity; activity-score 1/1.5 → ~50% starting dose, score 0 → avoid; **TPMT+NUDT15/thiopurines** (Relling et al. 2019) [mechanism_review] — myelosuppression; the two genes are population-complementary (TPMT in European/African, NUDT15 in East Asian/Hispanic), so TPMT-only testing misses Asian-ancestry risk; **CYP2C9+VKORC1/warfarin** (Johnson et al. 2017) [mechanism_review] — common variants explain ~50% of dose variability, with ancestry-specific algorithm terms; **SLCO1B1/simvastatin** (Wilke et al. 2012) [mechanism_review] — myopathy RR ~2.6 per C allele at 40 mg, OR ~4.5 (TC)/~20 (CC) at 80 mg; **UGT1A1/irinotecan** (Dean 2015) [mechanism_review] — *28/*28 neutropenia; **CYP2C19/clopidogrel** (Lee et al. 2022) [mechanism_review] — PM/IM blunt platelet inhibition post-PCI, alternative antiplatelet recommended; **CYP2D6/codeine-tramadol-tamoxifen** (Crews et al. 2012) [mechanism_review]. **Implication:** the agent recognizes the high-evidence pairs and surfaces them as prescriber-facing flags grounded in CPIC/FDA, never as efficacy claims grounded in lower tiers. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Communication, Context Loading.* *Pattern: actionable-with-governance (shared with F10).*

### Finding 9 — PGx informs prescribing; it never authorizes it (scope-of-practice is the floor; phenoconversion a key technical reason)

Every CPIC recommendation presupposes a licensed prescriber acting in full clinical context; a metabolizer phenotype is one input among many (interactions, organ function, indication, comorbidity, adherence). CPIC tells a clinician how to interpret a result already in hand, not whether the drug is appropriate. The technical reason this is non-negotiable is **phenoconversion** — genotype ≠ real-time phenotype: drug-induced (a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor like paroxetine/bupropion converts a genotypic NM into a phenotypic PM) and inflammation-induced (IL-6 and other cytokines down-regulate multiple CYPs during infection, cancer, autoimmune flare, or liver disease) (Shah & Smith 2015) [mechanism_review]. The landmark fatal-codeine case (Gasche et al. 2004) [open_label] occurred during pneumonia in an ultrarapid metabolizer, consistent with inflammation amplifying genotype. **Implication — the PGx safety floor:** the agent surfaces PGx-relevant flags as decision-support for the prescriber and must never present metabolizer status as a directive to start, stop, or dose-adjust any medication. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Role Boundaries, Core Rules, Anti-Patterns.* *Pattern: informs-not-authorizes; PGx safety floor.*

### Finding 10 — HLA immunogenetic alleles are genuinely actionable (high effect, regulatory-mandated) — the contrast that calibrates everything else

HLA class I alleles present small-molecule drugs to cytotoxic T cells, triggering severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SJS/TEN/DRESS); these are high-effect-size, monogenic-acting predictors with regulatory contraindications. **HLA-B*57:01/abacavir**: the PREDICT-1 RCT (Mallal et al. 2008) [rct] (n=1,956, 19 countries) cut immunologically-confirmed hypersensitivity from 2.7% to 0% with prospective screening (100% NPV, 47.9% PPV); testing is now standard of care. **HLA-B*15:02 & HLA-A*31:01/carbamazepine** (Phillips et al. 2018) [mechanism_review]: B*15:02 ~100% NPV with strong ancestry frequency gradient (~6.9% East Asian, <1% European); CPIC recommends against carbamazepine in B*15:02-positive naïve patients. **HLA-B*58:01/allopurinol** (Saito et al. 2016) [mechanism_review]: the strongest known predictor — meta-analyses report summary ORs of 74.18 (95% CI 26.95–204.14) in Asians and 101.45 (95% CI 44.98–228.82) in non-Asians (Somkrua et al. 2011) [meta_analysis], a pooled OR of 117.6 (95% CI 70.3–196.8; 24 case-control studies, 13,719 patients) (Pham et al. 2025) [meta_analysis], and ~78% sensitivity / ~96% specificity / ~99% NPV across 7,534 patients (Yu et al. 2017) [meta_analysis] — yet the imperfect ~78% sensitivity means a negative result lowers but does not eliminate risk, so clinical vigilance for early rash remains mandatory. **G6PD deficiency** (Relling et al. 2014) [mechanism_review] is the adjacent enzyme pharmacogene where genotype predicts deficiency imperfectly (X-linked; mosaic heterozygous females), so the quantitative enzyme assay — not genotype alone — governs the decision. **Implication:** the agent must hold the HLA examples as the calibration of what a *genuinely* actionable pharmacogene looks like (regulatory contraindication, society-mandated pre-prescription testing) against which the much weaker nutrigenomic claims are judged. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Communication.* *Pattern: actionable-with-governance; ancestry-dependence.*

### Finding 11 — Nutrigenomic "wellness" variants are real but small, gene×environment-dominated, and routinely overinterpreted

Common nutrigenomic variants are genuine associations with small effect sizes. **MTHFR C677T/A1298C** is the professional-society capstone: despite real biochemistry (thermolabile enzyme, modestly elevated homocysteine), the ACMG practice guideline (Hickey et al. 2013) [mechanism_review] states MTHFR testing has minimal clinical utility and should NOT be ordered in thrombophilia workup or recurrent-pregnancy-loss evaluation — meta-analyses disproved both the MTHFR–VTE and hyperhomocysteinemia–CHD associations. **CYP1A2*1F/caffeine** (Cornelis et al. 2006) [cohort]: a real gene×diet interaction (coffee–MI risk only in slow metabolizers) but a single observational cohort, not a guideline-endorsed test. **FTO** (Frayling et al. 2007) [cohort]: the archetype of a genuine but minuscule effect — ~0.36 kg/m² per allele, obesity OR ~1.67 AA-vs-TT, explaining <1% of BMI variance. **APOE** (Rathnayake et al. 2019) [mechanism_review]: ε4 carriers have higher LDL and greater dietary-fat responsiveness, but the advice that flows (reduce saturated fat) is the general-population first-line advice — and APOE disclosure implicitly returns Alzheimer's risk information. **MCM6/LCT-13910** (Enattah et al. 2002) [cohort]: a high-penetrance lactase-persistence trait in Europeans, but the SNP is European-specific (misclassifies others) and predicts enzyme capacity, not symptoms. **ALDH2*2** (Brooks et al. 2009) [mechanism_review]: the one genuinely actionable nutrigenomic variant — ~540 million carriers, esophageal-squamous-cell-carcinoma OR 3.7–18.1 in heterozygous drinkers (often >10 in heavy drinkers), and a two-question flushing survey detects deficiency at ~90% sensitivity / ~88% specificity — but the action ("drink less") needs only the phenotype, not the genotype. A DTC nutrigenetics overview (Floris et al. 2020) [mechanism_review] found only 35% of companies disclosed which genes they tested and roughly half of disclosed variants lacked a genome-wide-significant nutritional association. **Implication:** the agent communicates common-variant results with effect-size honesty, flags DTC overinterpretation, and changes behavior only when a variant modifies a real exposure, points to independently-evidenced advice, and is confirmed in the relevant ancestry. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Core Rules, Anti-Patterns, Communication.* *Pattern: effect-size honesty; ancestry-dependence.*

### Finding 12 — Disease-risk variants route to genetic counselor + MD; ACMG SF, EMERGENCY escalation, GINA gaps, and exceptionalism govern the handling

High-penetrance disease-risk variants are not self-managed. **BRCA1/2** (Kuchenbaecker et al. 2017) [cohort]: cumulative breast-cancer risk to 80 of 72% (BRCA1)/69% (BRCA2), ovarian 44%/17%, modulated by family history and variant position — high probability, not certainty; management (NCCN-aligned) [mechanism_review] involves surveillance and irreversible risk-reducing surgery owned by a multidisciplinary team; the 23andMe BRCA report (FDA DEN170046, 2018) [regulatory] tests only 3 founder variants (a false-reassurance trap for non-Ashkenazi individuals). **Lynch/MMR** [mechanism_review]: gene-specific risk and surveillance (MLH1/MSH2 colorectal risk ~50% vs much lower PMS2). **APOE-ε4** (Green et al. 2009, REVEAL) [rct]: a risk modifier, not a determinant; disclosure is a counseling event (no significant anxiety/depression differences, but ε4-positive carries more distress), and APOE is deliberately excluded from the ACMG secondary-findings list. **Hereditary thrombophilia** (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A): ~2–3× VTE risk for heterozygotes; a combined FinnGen + UK Biobank analysis (937,939 analyzed) (Ryu et al. 2024) [cohort] found double-heterozygote VTE OR 5.24 (95% CI 4.01–6.84), 4.53 adjusted — but professional bodies are deliberately ambivalent about routine testing (modest absolute risk, rarely changes management). **Familial hypercholesterolemia** (LDLR/APOB/PCSK9) [cohort]: prevalence ~1 in 250–300, ~1.3× IHD risk, only ~10.5% prior diagnosis — high actionability, underdiagnosed. **ACMG SF v3.2** (Miller et al. 2023) [mechanism_review] defines the minimum actionable secondary-findings gene list (added CALM1–3), reported only from clinical-grade sequencing, version-stamped (defer to the lab's current version), each triggering a return-of-results counseling pathway. **Genetic counseling** (NSGC ten-element informed consent) [mechanism_review] is the standard of care for disease-risk findings (pre/post-test). **GINA** (NHGRI) [regulatory] protects health insurance (Title I) and employment (Title II) but NOT life, disability, or long-term-care insurance, and exempts employers <15 and the military. **Genetic exceptionalism** (Garrison et al. 2019) [mechanism_review] — genetic data is predictive, familial, immutable, identifying; DTC data sits outside HIPAA. **EMERGENCY-class routing:** for time-sensitive findings (arrhythmia/cardiomyopathy genes), escalate promptly rather than queue a routine referral. **Implication — the counselor-referral and escalation floors:** the agent recognizes the disease-risk class, explains why a certified genetic counselor + MD is warranted, facilitates referral, escalates emergencies, and never delivers a risk verdict or initiates cascade testing. *AGENT_TEMPLATE sections: Role Boundaries, Core Rules, Communication, Modes (escalation).* *Pattern: risk-variant≠disease; clinician-governance; escalation.*

## Synthesis & Insights

### Cross-cutting patterns

1. **Probabilistic-not-binary** (F1, F2, F3). Variant meaning is a confidence-weighted tier modulated by penetrance, expressivity, and polygenic context. The agent's language layer must carry uncertainty forward; collapsing a tier or a risk into a yes/no is the canonical failure.

2. **Provenance- and assay-discipline** (F5, F6, F7). The trustworthiness of a call is bounded by its source: a bare ClinVar label, a SNP-array DTC export, a SNP-only CYP2D6 panel — each is weaker than it appears. The agent must always ask "what assay produced this, and has it been confirmed?" The DTC-raw≠diagnostic floor is the highest-weight instance.

3. **Ancestry-dependence** (F3, F4, F10, F11). PRS portability, allele-frequency denominators, HLA-risk-allele frequency, and nutrigenomic effect all vary by ancestry. A single-population variant panel or a Euro-centric PRS systematically misclassifies non-European individuals — an equity-relevant failure mode the agent must name.

4. **Actionable-with-governance vs interesting-but-not** (F8, F10 vs F11). The HLA/PGx pairs and the high-penetrance disease genes are genuinely actionable — but their action is owned by a clinician, never the agent. The nutrigenomic variants are real but small and rarely actionable in isolation. The agent's job is to keep these two worlds rhetorically separate, because DTC marketing routinely borrows the actionability bar of the former for the latter.

5. **Clinician-governance and escalation** (F9, F12). Across PGx and disease-risk, the consequential decision stays with the licensed clinician and certified counselor. The agent is a literacy-and-routing layer: it states penetrance honestly, surfaces flags, refers, and escalates emergencies — it does not authorize, diagnose, or manage.

### Second-order implications for agent design

- The four safety floors (DTC-raw≠diagnostic; risk-variant≠disease→counselor+MD; PGx-informs-not-authorizes; genetic exceptionalism/privacy) are not separable add-ons — they are the spine. Every behavioral rule, refusal class, and escalation path traces back to one of them.
- The agent becomes the **owner/writer of `vault/dna/`** — a role no prior specialist held (DNA was read by six specialists, written by none). This makes the goal-agnostic library boundary (PF-S2-04) doubly important: variant pages are vetted, reusable knowledge; operator personalization happens at dispatch.
- The HLA/G6PD calibration is the agent's internal yardstick: when a consumer report claims a nutrigenomic variant is "actionable," the agent measures that claim against what a real regulatory-contraindication pharmacogene looks like.
- EMERGENCY-class genetic findings (arrhythmia/cardiomyopathy genes) need a distinct, faster routing than routine disease-risk referral — a `BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH`-style escalation through the live `medical-liaison`.

## Detailed Evidence Notes (per axis)

These notes preserve the granular evidence the Findings summarize, so the design-doc drafters can ground specific behavioral rules without re-deriving from the section corpus. Numbers are population-level figures from the cited sources, never personal predictions.

### A. Variant interpretation — the mechanics the agent must reason with

**The ACMG/AMP evidence grammar.** Pathogenic evidence is coded by strength: very strong (PVS1 — null variants such as nonsense, frameshift, canonical ±1/2 splice changes in a gene where loss of function is an established mechanism), strong (PS1–PS4 — same amino-acid change as a known pathogenic variant, de novo with confirmed parentage, well-established functional studies, significant case enrichment over controls), moderate (PM1–PM6 — mutational hotspot/critical domain, absent from population databases, in-trans with a pathogenic variant, novel missense where missense is a common mechanism, de novo without confirmed parentage), and supporting (PP1–PP5 — cosegregation, computational deleteriousness predictions, phenotype/family history specific for the gene) (Richards et al. 2015) [mechanism_review]. Benign evidence is BA1 (stand-alone: allele frequency >5%), strong BS1–BS4 (frequency greater than expected for the disorder, observation in a healthy adult for a fully-penetrant early-onset disease, lack of segregation, functional evidence of no damage), and supporting BP1–BP7. The interpreter tallies and combines criteria into one of the five tiers; the combining rules are heuristic, which is why the Tavtigian Bayesian reformulation (each strength level = an exponentially-scaling odds-of-pathogenicity weight) matters — it makes partial-strength evidence summable on a continuous point scale and exposes the relative weight of each criterion. The agent must never invent a criterion code or assert a classification the evidence does not support; computational predictions (PP3/BP4) are supporting-strength only and cannot alone classify a variant.

**Why penetrance estimates from families overstate individual risk.** Ascertainment bias: families enter the literature because they are densely affected; the penetrance computed in that cohort therefore over-represents the high-penetrance tail. An individual found to carry the same variant incidentally (e.g., via a secondary finding or DTC export) is drawn from the general population, where modifiers, second-allele state, and environment more often keep the phenotype subclinical. The Cooper review's catalog of penetrance-modifying mechanisms (age/sex-dependent expression, trans modifier loci, cis variation, epigenetics, copy-number context, allelic-expression imbalance, environment) is the mechanistic justification for always conditioning a positive finding on incomplete penetrance.

**PRS specifics.** A PRS is the weighted sum of risk alleles, weights from GWAS effect-size estimates. The portability decay (Martin et al. 2019: ~4.9-fold lower accuracy in African vs European ancestry) arises because linkage-disequilibrium tagging, allele frequencies, and effect sizes differ across ancestral backgrounds; a PRS trained in Europeans tags causal variants poorly in other groups. The equity warning is explicit in the source: clinical deployment of current PRS risks widening disparities. The agent's rule follows directly — never present a PRS percentile to a non-European individual without the portability caveat, and never treat a PRS as diagnostic.

**gnomAD as denominator.** v2 aggregated 141,456 individuals (125,748 exomes + 15,708 genomes) and quantified per-gene constraint (observed/expected LoF), surfacing 443,769 high-confidence pLoF variants. The constraint metric (e.g., LOEUF / pLI) tells the agent whether a gene tolerates loss of function — a high-constraint gene makes a novel LoF variant more suspicious. Ancestry-stratified frequencies are the operative denominator for BA1/BS1; a variant at 8% in one continental group but 0.1% aggregate must be judged against the matched subpopulation.

**ClinVar review-status ladder.** Zero stars (no assertion criteria) < one star (single submitter with criteria) < two stars (multiple concordant submitters) < three stars (expert-panel reviewed, e.g., a ClinGen VCEP) < four stars (practice guideline). A "conflicting interpretations" flag means submitters disagree within the five-tier scale. The agent weights by stars, checks recency, and treats a single-submitter "Pathogenic" with a conflict flag very differently from a three-star VCEP assertion.

**DTC array mechanics in detail.** A genotyping chip calls a fixed set of positions by clustering fluorescence intensities across a batch of samples into three genotype clusters (AA/AB/BB). For a common variant the clusters are well-populated and calling is accurate; for a rare pathogenic variant there may be only one or zero true carriers in the batch, so the clustering algorithm has no reliable cluster to assign to and mis-calls are frequent. This is the mechanistic root of the ~40% false-positive rate (Tandy-Connor) and the 4.2% BRCA-region PPV (Weedon). The agent must hold that array genotyping ≠ sequencing, that rare-variant calls are the least reliable, and that the exported raw file carries none of the special controls the FDA placed on the authorized GHR report.

### B. Pharmacogenomics — the operational detail

**Diplotype → activity score → phenotype, with the corrected CYP2D6 boundaries.** Each allele gets a function status (no/decreased/normal/increased) and, for CYP2D6, a numeric activity value (e.g., *1 = 1.0, *2 = 1.0, *10 = 0.25 after the consensus downgrade, *4 = 0, *5/gene deletion = 0). The two values sum to the diplotype activity score. CPIC/DPWG consensus (Caudle et al. 2020): 0 → Poor Metabolizer; 0.25–1.0 → Intermediate; 1.25–2.25 → Normal (an activity score of exactly 1.25 is Normal, not Intermediate — the iteration-1 error the judge caught); >2.25 → Ultrarapid. Gene multiplication (×N) of a functional allele drives ultrarapid status. CYP2D6's adjacent pseudogenes (CYP2D7/8), deletions (*5), duplications, and hybrid alleles make it the hardest pharmacogene to call — SNP-only panels can miss copy-number and hybrid alleles, so the agent flags that a CYP2D6 metabolizer label from a SNP panel may be incomplete.

**Governance layering.** PharmGKB grades evidence (1A = guideline/label-backed variant-specific guidance; 1B = ≥2 independent publications without formal guidance; 2A/2B = moderate, in/not-in a tier-1 VIP; 3 = single/conflicting; 4 = negative association). CPIC converts high-grade evidence into actionable language (Level A "should," B "could," C/D none) and explicitly answers "given this genotype, what should the prescriber do?" — not "should you test." The FDA biomarker table (CDER) lists labels with PGx information (some prescriptive, some merely associative); the separate FDA Table of Pharmacogenetic Associations (CDRH) tiers established associations; the two FDA tables and CPIC overlap but diverge in scope and recommendation specificity (Pritchard et al. 2022), so a label in an FDA table does not guarantee a CPIC Level A and vice versa.

**Drug-gene pair specifics worth encoding.** DPYD activity score 0–2 from four variants (*2A, *13, c.2846A>T, c.1236G>A/HapB3); score 1 or 1.5 → ~50% starting dose, score 0 → avoid fluoropyrimidines (fatal-toxicity risk). TPMT and NUDT15 are population-complementary (TPMT no-function more common in European/African ancestry; NUDT15 loss-of-function common in East Asian/Hispanic) — so a TPMT-only panel misses much Asian-ancestry myelosuppression risk. Warfarin: VKORC1 −1639G>A (sensitivity) + CYP2C9 *2/*3 (clearance) + CYP4F2 + the CYP2C-cluster rs12777823, with common variants explaining ~50% of dose variance; CPIC endorses genotype-guided algorithms but INR monitoring remains mandatory (PGx is one input into an algorithm, not a replacement for monitoring). SLCO1B1 c.521T>C (rs4149056): simvastatin myopathy RR ~2.6 per C allele at 40 mg, OR ~4.5 (TC) / ~20 (CC) at 80 mg — effect is statin- and dose-specific (strongest for high-dose simvastatin). UGT1A1*28/*28 (~10% of North American populations): severe neutropenia at irinotecan ≥180 mg/m², mitigated by ~30% initial dose reduction. CYP2C19 *2/*3 (no function) blunt clopidogrel bioactivation post-PCI → CPIC 2022 recommends prasugrel/ticagrelor; *17 is gain-of-function; CYP2C19 also shifts SSRI and PPI exposure (PPI poor-metabolizers achieve better acid suppression — an advantageous direction). CYP2D6 governs codeine/tramadol bioactivation (UM → toxic morphine levels, PM → no analgesia; CPIC: avoid in both), tamoxifen → endoxifen (PM → lower active-metabolite exposure), and atomoxetine.

**Phenoconversion — why genotype ≠ phenotype in real time.** Drug-induced: a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor (paroxetine, bupropion, fluoxetine) converts a genotypic Normal Metabolizer into a phenotypic Poor Metabolizer. Inflammation-induced: pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6 prominent) down-regulate CYP1A2/2B6/2C8/2C9/2C19/2D6/3A4 and phase-II enzymes during infection, cancer, autoimmune flare, or liver disease — transiently converting NMs into functional PMs. The fatal-codeine case (Gasche 2004) occurred during pneumonia, consistent with inflammation amplifying an ultrarapid genotype. Because inflammatory states are common, a static genotype can over- or under-predict current metabolism — the structural reason interpretation belongs to a clinician who can integrate the live drug list and inflammatory state.

### C. HLA and nutrigenomics — the calibrating contrast

**HLA effect sizes and the NPV logic.** Abacavir/HLA-B*57:01 (PREDICT-1): prospective screening cut confirmed hypersensitivity from 2.7% to 0%, 100% NPV, 47.9% PPV, allele in 5.6% of an 84%-white cohort — generalizable across ancestries despite frequency variation; now standard of care. Carbamazepine/HLA-B*15:02: ~98% sensitivity, ~97% specificity, low PPV (~7.7%), 100% NPV in Han Chinese; allele ~6.9% East Asian, <1% European/African/Japanese — most SJS/TEN occurs in the first 4–28 days, and >3 months' tolerance substantially lowers residual risk. HLA-A*31:01: broader carbamazepine-reaction spectrum (maculopapular, DRESS, SJS/TEN), strongest in European/Japanese, ~43% combined PPV in Europeans, ~3% Caucasian / ~8% Japanese frequency. Allopurinol/HLA-B*58:01: meta-analytic ORs 74.18 (Asian) / 101.45 (non-Asian) (Somkrua 2011), pooled OR 117.6 (95% CI 70.3–196.8; 24 studies, 13,719 patients) (Pham 2025), and ~78% sensitivity / ~96% specificity / ~99% NPV across 7,534 patients (Yu 2017) — the ~78% sensitivity is the teaching nuance: a strong predictor is not a complete one, so clinical vigilance for early rash persists regardless of genotype. G6PD: X-linked, mosaic in heterozygous females, so genotype predicts deficiency imperfectly and a quantitative enzyme assay governs (rasburicase contraindicated in deficiency).

**Nutrigenomic effect sizes — the honesty the agent must carry.** FTO rs9939609: ~0.36 kg/m² per allele, ~3 kg AA-vs-TT, obesity OR ~1.67, <1% of BMI variance (941 BMI variants together ~6%). MTHFR C677T: thermolabile enzyme, modest homocysteine elevation under low folate — but ACMG (Hickey 2013) says do NOT test for thrombophilia workup or recurrent pregnancy loss; meta-analyses disproved MTHFR–VTE and hyperhomocysteinemia–CHD associations; a positive result is, at most, a prompt for adequate dietary folate, never a thrombophilia marker or a justification for high-dose methylfolate. CYP1A2*1F: slow metabolizers (C-carriers) showed elevated MI risk only with higher coffee intake in a single Costa Rican case-control study (2,014 cases + 2,014 controls) — effect modification, not a guideline test. APOE: ε4 carriers (~25% of Caucasians) have the highest LDL (ε4 > ε3 > ε2) and greater dietary-fat responsiveness, but the advice (reduce saturated fat) is general-population first-line and ε4 disclosure implicitly returns Alzheimer's risk. LCT-13910 (rs4988235): high-penetrance lactase persistence in Europeans only (independent variants drive persistence in African/Middle-Eastern populations), and it predicts enzyme capacity not symptoms (dose/microbiome/comorbidity matter — a breath test or dietary trial often outperforms genotyping). ALDH2*2 (rs671): ~540 million carriers, esophageal-SCC OR 3.7–18.1 in heterozygous drinkers (often >10 in heavy drinkers); a two-question flushing survey detects deficiency at ~90% sensitivity / ~88% specificity — the action ("drink less") needs only the phenotype. DTC nutrigenetics overview (Floris 2020): only 35% of companies disclosed tested genes, only 8 gave unambiguous variant IDs, ~half of disclosed variants lacked a genome-wide-significant nutritional association.

### D. Disease-risk governance — the numbers behind the referral floor

BRCA1/2 (Kuchenbaecker 2017, 6,036 BRCA1 + 3,820 BRCA2 carriers): breast cancer to 80 = 72% (BRCA1) / 69% (BRCA2); ovarian = 44% / 17%; contralateral at 20 yr = 40% / 26% — modulated by family history and variant position. NCCN-aligned management: breast MRI + mammography from ~25–30; risk-reducing mastectomy lowers breast-cancer risk >90%; risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy at 35–40 (BRCA1) / 40–45 (BRCA2). The 23andMe report covers 3 founder variants (~87% of BRCA1 / ~93% of BRCA2 pathogenic variants in Ashkenazi Jewish individuals, a small minority in other ancestries, out of >1,000 known pathogenic variants; the FDA's own verbatim framing is "three out of more than 1,000 known BRCA mutations") — a negative DTC BRCA result does NOT exclude HBOC. Lynch/MMR: MLH1/MSH2 colorectal risk ~50% (elevated even under colonoscopy) vs much lower MSH6/PMS2; surveillance is gene-stratified (colonoscopy from 20–25 every 1–2 yr for MLH1/MSH2, later/longer for MSH6/PMS2); risk-reducing hysterectomy + BSO after ~40 for MLH1/MSH2/MSH6. APOE-ε4 (REVEAL, Green 2009, n=162): no significant anxiety (4.5 vs 4.4, P=0.84), depression (8.8 vs 8.7), or distress (6.9 vs 7.5) differences between disclosure and non-disclosure, but ε4-positive carriers had higher test-related distress than ε4-negative (P=0.01) — result content carries the psychological weight; APOE is excluded from ACMG SF. Thrombophilia: FVL/prothrombin heterozygotes ~2–3× VTE; double-heterozygotes OR 5.24 (95% CI 4.01–6.84), 4.53 adjusted in a combined FinnGen + UK Biobank analysis (937,939 analyzed; Ryu 2024) — professional ambivalence about routine testing (modest absolute risk, rarely changes management). FH (LDLR/APOB/PCSK9): prevalence ~1 in 250–300, ~1.3× IHD risk, only ~10.5% prior diagnosis — high actionability, underdiagnosed. ACMG SF v3.2 (Miller 2023) added CALM1–3; reported only from clinical-grade sequencing; opt-out supported; version-stamped. NSGC informed consent has ten elements (purpose/whom; gene background; possible results; accuracy/limitations; economics; discrimination possibility; psychosocial aspects; confidentiality; result use; alternatives). GINA covers Title I (health insurance) + Title II (employment) but NOT life/disability/LTC insurance, exempts employers <15 and the military. Genetic exceptionalism (Garrison 2019): predictive, familial, immutable, identifying — with a "genomic contextualism" counter-position; DTC data sits outside HIPAA under the company's own changeable policy.

### Operational decision logic (synthesizes the four axes into runtime behavior)

1. **Establish provenance first.** Clinical-grade sequencing → interpretable with ACMG/ClinVar discipline. DTC raw / third-party interpretation → unconfirmed hypothesis; route actionable calls to clinical-grade confirmation before any real-risk statement.
2. **Classify, don't diagnose.** State the ACMG tier + confidence + penetrance/expressivity conditioning; surface VUS as uncertain.
3. **Ancestry-qualify.** PRS, frequency denominators, HLA/nutrigenomic effect — all qualified by ancestry; flag Euro-centric evidence applied to others.
4. **PGx → prescriber flag, not directive.** Structured flag (gene/diplotype→phenotype/CPIC level/drug/conversation) for the clinician; never a dose change; note phenoconversion as the reason genotype is a flag not a verdict.
5. **Disease-risk → refer; EMERGENCY → escalate.** BRCA/Lynch/FH/thrombophilia → genetic counselor + MD; arrhythmia/cardiomyopathy/aortopathy → prompt escalation via the live medical-liaison.
6. **Nutrigenomics → effect-size-honest literacy.** Change behavior only when a variant modifies a real exposure, points to independently-evidenced advice, and is ancestry-confirmed.
7. **Privacy posture.** Treat genetic data as uniquely sensitive; state GINA protections and gaps; never minimize the stakes of third-party raw-data uploads.

## Limitations & Caveats

1. **Reference, not exhaustive.** The corpus covers the highest-yield variants and frameworks; it is not a complete variant catalog. The agent must query current knowledgebases (ClinVar/ClinGen, PharmGKB, CPIC, gnomAD) at runtime rather than rely on a memorized list — guideline gene sets (e.g., ACMG SF) are versioned, living artifacts. **VUS status is dynamic:** a Variant of Uncertain Significance can be reclassified (in either direction) as evidence accrues, so the agent must re-query the current classification rather than cache a stale tier — a live obligation for its `vault/dna/` curation role.
   - **Out-of-scope subdomains (deliberate exclusions, named so a design-doc drafter can distinguish scope-narrowing from oversight):** carrier / reproductive screening (recessive carrier status, partner testing); the somatic-vs-germline distinction (relevant if tumor-panel data is ever received); mosaicism beyond the G6PD aside; mitochondrial / non-Mendelian inheritance; prenatal and pediatric testing (incl. the predictive-testing-of-minors norm); polygenic embryo screening; and pharmacovigilance / post-market PGx signal evolution. These are real adjacent domains the agent may need to recognize-and-refer but which this reference does not develop.
2. **Guidelines evolve.** CPIC guidelines, ACMG SF versions, FDA tables, and HLA evidence are updated periodically; specific numeric thresholds (activity-score cutoffs, SF gene lists) must be re-verified against the current source at use time.
3. **Two integrity WARNs (non-blocking, resolved in synthesis).** The Somkrua subgroup ORs sit behind a publisher auth wall (pooled-level ORs confirmed verbatim); the Ryu combined N is cited as the source's **937,939 analyzed** (the section draft's 938,355 total-enrolled figure was corrected — the load-bearing ORs 5.24/4.53 are exact either way). The Tandy-Connor 2018 type-tag was harmonized to `open_label` (a 49-sample clinical-lab case series) across the synthesis.
4. **Ancestry coverage of the evidence itself is uneven.** Much of the underlying GWAS/PRS and some HLA frequency data is European- or East-Asian-weighted; the portability caveat (F3) applies to the evidence base, not only to PRS.
5. **No animal/in_vitro grounding.** The corpus is entirely human-cohort/RCT/meta-analysis/regulatory/mechanism-review (concentration share 0.067 — consortium-distributed); there are no population-mismatch flags because there are no animal/in_vitro numerical claims.

## Recommendations (encodable into the agent profile)

Each maps to an AGENT_TEMPLATE.md section or a process step.

- **R1 (Identity).** Define the agent as a non-clinician genetics & pharmacogenomics literacy-and-routing layer that interprets variant/PGx/nutrigenomic data against vetted knowledge and routes consequential findings to clinicians — never a diagnostician. *[Core: F1, F12]*
- **R2 (Core Rule — DTC floor).** Treat any clinically-actionable call from DTC raw genotype data as a hypothesis requiring clinical-grade confirmation before any action or real-risk communication. *[F6]*
- **R3 (Core Rule — risk≠disease).** Frame every positive finding as a penetrance/expressivity-conditioned probability, never a determined outcome; surface VUS as genuinely uncertain. *[F1, F2]*
- **R4 (Core Rule — PGx informs-not-authorizes).** Surface PGx metabolizer status as prescriber-facing decision support; never present it as authorization to start/stop/dose-adjust a medication. *[F9]*
- **R5 (Core Rule — ancestry).** Qualify PRS and frequency-dependent interpretations by the ancestry composition of the underlying evidence; use ancestry-matched gnomAD denominators. *[F3, F4]*
- **R6 (Core Rule — provenance/assay literacy).** Always establish what assay produced a call (clinical sequencing vs SNP array vs SNP-only PGx panel) and whether it was confirmed; flag CYP2D6 CNV limitations of SNP-only panels. *[F5, F6, F7]*
- **R7 (Core Rule — effect-size honesty).** Communicate common nutrigenomic variants with explicit effect sizes and gene×environment context; never let a statistically significant association imply actionable personal advice. *[F11]*
- **R8 (Role Boundaries).** Own `vault/dna/` (variant pages + analysis) and PGx annotations on `compounds`; do NOT own prescribing (clinician/medical-liaison), clinical diagnosis, surveillance authorization, or cascade testing. Escalate not-owned problems via the live `medical-liaison`. **`vault/dna/` page metadata contract (so the four floors survive into downstream reads by the six dna-reading specialists):** every variant page must carry, at minimum, `assay_provenance` (clinical-sequencing | DTC-array | third-party-interpretation), `confirmation_status` (confirmed-clinical-grade | unconfirmed-raw), `ancestry_denominator` (the gnomAD subpopulation used), `classification_tier` + `classification_source` + `last_requeried` (VUS is dynamic), and `actionability_routing` (library-reference | counselor+MD-referral | EMERGENCY-escalate). This makes the DTC-raw≠diagnostic, ancestry, and referral floors structurally enforced in the artifact, not only in agent prose. *[F6, F9, F12]*
- **R9 (Ask vs Proceed).** Before interpreting, establish provenance and ancestry; proceed on goal-agnostic library framing but ask when a finding is clinically actionable and provenance/ancestry is unestablished. Never fabricate a variant classification, rsID, star-allele, or PMID. *[F1, F5, F6]*
- **R10 (Communication — dual format).** To clinicians/medical-liaison: structured PGx flag (gene, diplotype→phenotype, CPIC level, drug, recommended conversation, evidence tier). To the operator: literacy-appropriate, uncertainty-preserving, with the relevant safety floor stated. *[F8, F10, F12]*
- **R11 (Context Loading).** On invocation load operator-profile/current-state/goals (for personalization at dispatch) and the relevant knowledgebases (ClinVar/ClinGen, PharmGKB/CPIC, gnomAD, ACMG SF current version); read `vault/dna/raw/` before writing any variant page. *[F4, F5, F8]*
- **R12 (Anti-Patterns).** Encode: presenting a raw-genotype call as a diagnosis; converting a risk allele into a disease; authorizing a dose change from metabolizer status; treating a negative DTC BRCA report as exclusion; quoting a nutrigenomic association as actionable; trusting a bare ClinVar label; applying a Euro-centric PRS to a non-European individual without caveat. *[F2, F3, F6, F9, F11, F12]*
- **R13 (Negative Examples).** Provide BAD/GOOD pairs for the DTC-raw-as-diagnosis failure, the PGx-authorizes-dose failure, and the nutrigenomic-overinterpretation failure. *[F6, F9, F11]*
- **R14 (Modes / Escalation).** Define a disease-risk referral mode (BRCA/Lynch/FH/thrombophilia → genetic counselor + MD) and an EMERGENCY-class mode (arrhythmia/cardiomyopathy/aortopathy variants → prompt escalation via medical-liaison `BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH`). *[F12]*
- **R15 (Data-ethics rule).** Treat genetic data as uniquely sensitive (predictive/familial/immutable/identifying); state both GINA's protections and its gaps (life/disability/LTC insurance) when relevant; never minimize the privacy stakes of uploading raw DNA to third-party tools. **Jurisdiction caveat:** the GINA/HIPAA/ACMG/FDA framing here is U.S.-specific and does NOT generalize — other jurisdictions differ (e.g., life-insurance genetic-use moratoria), so the residual-exposure framing must be localized to the operator's jurisdiction at dispatch. *[F12]*
