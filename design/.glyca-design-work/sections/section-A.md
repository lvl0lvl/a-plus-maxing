# Section A — Identity, Physiology & Significance (GlycA)

## Identity and analytical origin

GlycA (glycoprotein acetylation) is not a single molecule but a composite signal derived from
proton nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy of serum or plasma. The signal originates
from the mobile N-acetyl methyl protons of glycan side chains — specifically a subset of
N-acetylglucosamine residues — on enzymatically glycosylated acute-phase proteins [1, cohort].
The dominant contributors to the signal are five abundant acute-phase glycoproteins:
α1-acid glycoprotein, haptoglobin, α1-antitrypsin, α1-antichymotrypsin, and transferrin
[1, cohort]. Because the measured signal integrates BOTH the circulating protein levels
AND the glycosylation states of these proteins, GlycA functions as an aggregate readout of the
systemic acute-phase response rather than a measure of any one analyte [8, mechanism_review].

The contributing proteins are bona fide positive acute-phase reactants: α1-acid glycoprotein,
haptoglobin, α1-antitrypsin, and fibrinogen-family glycoproteins are all liver-derived proteins
whose synthesis is induced by interleukin-6 during inflammation [9, regulatory]. As a reference
point for the magnitude of the acute-phase response, C-reactive protein (CRP) can rise 100- to
1000-fold during acute inflammation, whereas fibrinogen rises later and only ~2-fold
[9, regulatory] — illustrating that a composite signal smooths over the very different kinetics of
its individual components.

## A more integrated, more stable inflammation marker

GlycA correlates moderately with conventional inflammation markers but is not redundant with them.
In the analytical validation cohort, GlycA correlated with high-sensitivity CRP (hsCRP) at
r = 0.56, with fibrinogen at r = 0.46, and with interleukin-6 at r = 0.35 (all P < 0.0001)
[1, cohort]. In the Women's Health Study the GlycA–hsCRP Spearman correlation was
r = 0.61 (P < 0.0001) [2, cohort], and in the PREVEND cohort the univariate GlycA–hsCRP
correlation was 0.67 [5, cohort]. These moderate (not high) correlations indicate GlycA and hsCRP
capture overlapping but partly distinct facets of inflammation [8, mechanism_review].

A defining property of GlycA is its stability. The NMR assay shows an intra-assay CV of 1.9% and
inter-assay CV of 2.6%, and intra-individual (biological) variability of only 4.3% over a 5-week
reassessment window [1, cohort]. Because the composite averages over multiple proteins,
GlycA exhibits lower intra-individual variability than hsCRP, which is known for high
within-person fluctuation [8, mechanism_review]. This makes GlycA a more cumulative, time-stable
index of low-grade systemic inflammation than any single acute-phase protein.

## Prospective associations with disease

**Incident cardiovascular disease.** In 27,491 initially healthy women in the Women's Health Study
(median follow-up 17.2 years, 1,648 incident CVD events), hazard ratios for CVD across GlycA
quartiles 1→4 were 1.00, 1.10 (95% CI 0.92–1.30), 1.34 (95% CI 1.13–1.58), and 1.64
(95% CI 1.39–1.93), Ptrend < 0.0001 — comparable to hsCRP [2, cohort]. After lipid adjustment the
per-1-SD HR was 1.15 (95% CI 1.08–1.21; P < 0.0001), and remained 1.08 (95% CI 1.01–1.15; P = 0.02)
even after mutual adjustment for hsCRP [2, cohort]. The association replicates in independent
cohorts: in PREVEND (n = 4,759; 298 events over 8.5 years), the fully adjusted per-1-SD HR for
incident CVD was 1.16 (95% CI 1.01–1.33; P = 0.04), and risk was highest when both GlycA and hsCRP
were elevated (HR 1.79; 95% CI 1.31–2.46; P < 0.001) [5, cohort].

**Incident type 2 diabetes.** In 26,508 diabetes-free women (Women's Health Study, 17.2-year median
follow-up, 2,087 incident cases), the unadjusted GlycA quartile-4-versus-1 HR for incident type 2
diabetes was 2.67 (95% CI 2.26–3.14), attenuating to 1.65 (95% CI 1.39–1.95) after adjustment for
conventional risk factors and to 1.11 (95% CI 0.93–1.33; Ptrend = 0.10) after mutual adjustment for
hsCRP [3, cohort].

**All-cause and cardiovascular mortality.** In the Women's Health Study extended to 27,524 women
(median follow-up 20.5 years, 3,523 deaths), the risk-factor-adjusted per-1-SD HR for all-cause
mortality was 1.21 (95% CI 1.06–1.40) at 5 years and 1.14 (95% CI 1.09–1.16) at maximal follow-up;
the JUPITER trial replication cohort (n = 12,527) gave a per-1-SD HR of 1.33 (95% CI 1.21–1.45)
[7, cohort]. In MESA (n = 6,523; median 12.1-year follow-up), the per-1-SD GlycA relative risk
ranged 1.05–1.20 for total death (n = 915), total CVD events (n = 922), and chronic
inflammatory-related disease (n = 1,324), persisting after adjustment for hsCRP, IL-6, and D-dimer
[4, cohort].

**Severe infection and hospitalization.** In the population-based FINRISK 1997 cohort (n = 7,599;
13.8-year follow-up), baseline GlycA predicted long-term infection risk: per-1-SD hazard ratios were
1.40 for hospitalization from non-localized infection (P = 2×10⁻⁹, 585 cases) and 1.48 for
respiratory-infection hospitalization (P = 3×10⁻¹², 571 cases); for fatal non-localized infection the
HR was 2.36 (P = 8×10⁻⁵, 29 cases), driven largely by septicemia (HR 2.25, P = 4×10⁻³, 18 cases)
[6, cohort].

## Significance and evidence-grade honesty

Across these prospective cohorts a consistent picture emerges: GlycA is a cumulative, NMR-derived
index of systemic low-grade inflammation that independently associates with incident CVD, incident
type 2 diabetes, all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, and severe infection — frequently retaining
significance after adjustment for hsCRP and other inflammatory markers, indicating it carries
non-redundant inflammatory-burden information [4, cohort][7, cohort][8, mechanism_review]. It is
also elevated with disease severity in rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and
psoriasis [8, mechanism_review].

Honest grading: GlycA reflects inflammation BURDEN rather than a specific disease mechanism, and it
remains a research/emerging-grade biomarker. It is offered by some laboratories but is NOT a
routine, guideline-endorsed clinical test, and society guidelines do not yet recommend it for
risk stratification [8, mechanism_review]. Claims that a single GlycA draw can replace serial hsCRP
measurements originate from assay-maker framing and are reported here only as context, never as an
efficacy basis [8, mechanism_review].
