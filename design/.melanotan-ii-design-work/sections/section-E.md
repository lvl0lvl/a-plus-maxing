## Section E — Concentration / origin audit, commercialization & grey-market realism

### E.1 Origin and Research-Group Concentration Audit

Melanotan-II (MT-II) was synthesized in the late 1980s at the **University of Arizona (UA)** by Mac E. Hadley (Department of Cellular Biology and Anatomy) and Victor J. Hruby (Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry), as part of a systematic program to design superpotent, enzymatically resistant α-MSH analogues [1, mechanism_review]. The goal was a pharmacologic tool for studying melanocortin receptor function and, potentially, reducing skin cancer incidence by inducing photoprotective tanning without UV exposure.

**Concentration finding — foundational pharmacology: approximately 90% single-origin (Arizona).**

Every primary human study of MT-II during its formal clinical investigation phase was conducted by the Arizona group or directly under its authors:

- The first-in-human pilot Phase I tanning study (1996) was authored by Dorr, Lines, Levine, Brooks, Xiang, Hruby, and Hadley — all University of Arizona, Cancer Center Division [2, open_label].
- The first double-blind, placebo-controlled human erectile dysfunction crossover study (1998) was authored by Wessells, Fuciarelli, Hansen, Hadley, Hruby, Dorr, and Levine — all Department of Pharmacology, University of Arizona College of Medicine [3, rct].
- The extended human sexual motivation study (2000) was authored by Wessells, Levine, Hadley, Dorr, and Hruby — all Section of Urology, University of Arizona College of Medicine [4, rct].
- A 2006 historical milestones and commercialization review was co-authored by Mac E. Hadley and Robert T. Dorr, both Department of Medicine, University of Arizona [1, mechanism_review].

One non-Arizona group contributed mechanistic (animal) data to the erection literature: the Giuliano group (France) conducted spinally transected rat studies demonstrating MT-II's facilitatory modulation of sympathetic efferents [5, mechanism_review]. Vemulapalli et al. conducted isolated rabbit corpus cavernosum experiments. Neither group ran human MT-II trials [5, mechanism_review].

The **clinical/adverse-event case literature** is diffuse and multi-origin: the Habbema et al. 2017 risk review (Leiden, Netherlands) aggregated four melanoma case reports from heterogeneous European clinical settings [6, mechanism_review]. Individual case reports originate from dermatology departments across Europe, Australia, and North America (e.g., Hjuler et al., Denmark 2014; Bonchev, Bulgaria 2026 [7, open_label]). These are surveillance/case literature, not primary mechanistic or efficacy research.

**Concentration verdict:** The core melanocortin pharmacology, receptor characterization, and all formal human MT-II trials are effectively single-origin — the University of Arizona program accounts for ~90% of primary experimental human data. The AE/case-report surveillance literature is genuinely diffuse (~10–15 distinct authorship groups across multiple countries), but none of those groups ran efficacy trials. The 70% single-origin concentration flag applies to the primary pharmacology layer; the clinical-surveillance layer is multi-group and does not concentrate it below the threshold.

### E.2 Commercialization History and COI

The University of Arizona's technology-transfer partner, **Competitive Technologies**, held the licensing rights to the melanocortin peptide portfolio and executed two key transactions [1, mechanism_review]:

1. **Melanotan I (afamelanotide) → Clinuvel Pharmaceuticals:** Licensed to the Australian startup Epitan (later renamed Clinuvel in 2006). Afamelanotide (Scenesse, a selective MC1R agonist) received EC marketing authorisation on 22 December 2014 (following a CHMP positive opinion on 23 October 2014) and FDA approval in October 2019 for erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP). Clinuvel is currently conducting a pivotal Phase III trial (CUV105) for vitiligo [8, regulatory]. This is the only commercially approved melanocortin tanning-related compound.

2. **Melanotan II → Palatin Technologies:** Licensed for the erectile dysfunction indication. Palatin discontinued MT-II development circa 2000 and pivoted to **bremelanotide (PT-141, Vyleesi)**, a likely metabolite of MT-II optimized for sexual dysfunction with reduced pigmentation side effects. Bremelanotide received FDA approval on June 21, 2019 for acquired generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women [1, mechanism_review; 8, regulatory]. Palatin then licensed commercial rights to AMAG Pharmaceuticals.

**MT-II itself was never approved as a drug anywhere in the world.** Its formal clinical development was a stepping stone: the Arizona group's human trials established proof of concept for MC4R-mediated erection and tanning but the compound was abandoned in favour of more selective successors (afamelanotide for skin, bremelanotide for sexual function). No pharmaceutical company currently holds an IND or marketing authorization for MT-II. The approved commercial descendants carry institutional COI toward the melanocortin platform broadly, but not toward MT-II specifically — both Clinuvel and Palatin have commercial incentives to distinguish their approved products from the grey-market compound.

### E.3 Grey-Market Realism

MT-II's orphan status — pharmacologically characterized but never commercialized — created the conditions for an unregulated injectable-tanning market that has operated since the late 1990s and expanded substantially via social media in the 2020s.

**Product forms and distribution:** Grey-market MT-II circulates as lyophilized powder in vials for reconstitution (self-injection, subcutaneous), nasal sprays (marketed as a needle-free alternative; popularized on TikTok as the "Barbie drug"), tablets, and creams [9, anecdote_aggregate]. Vendors typically sell as "research chemical — not for human use" to circumvent legal restrictions. The TGA (Australia) has taken active enforcement action against unlawful advertising of MT-II and related tanning products on digital platforms [9, anecdote_aggregate].

**Regulatory status across jurisdictions:**
- **Australia (TGA):** Not registered. Illegal to advertise or supply without prescription. TGA has explicitly warned that "its development as a potential medicine was halted some years ago due to safety reasons" [9, anecdote_aggregate].
- **United Kingdom (MHRA):** Illegal to sell and supply. The MHRA has issued public warnings and taken enforcement action against websites offering MT-II to UK customers [10, regulatory]. Classified as an unlicensed medicine.
- **United States (FDA):** Not approved. Not on any compounding allowlist. The FDA has issued enforcement actions against vendors.
- **European Union (EMA):** Not approved.

**Purity, identity, and sterility:** Products sourced from unregulated vendors carry unverifiable identity (MT-I vs. MT-II vs. PT-141 consumer confusion is documented), dose variability, undeclared excipients, and risk of microbial contamination — none of the manufacturing hygiene requirements that govern licensed injectables apply [9, anecdote_aggregate; 10, regulatory]. Cairns (The Conversation, 2025) notes "variable dosing, undeclared ingredients and potential microbial contamination" as the primary quality concerns [9, anecdote_aggregate].

**MT-I / MT-II / PT-141 consumer confusion:** The three compounds are frequently conflated by consumers. Melanotan-I (afamelanotide) is selective for MC1R (pigmentation, minimal CNS effects); MT-II is non-selective (MC1/3/4/5R, strong CNS — nausea, priapism, appetite suppression); PT-141 (bremelanotide) is derived from MT-II but marketed for sexual function. The grey-market "Melanotan" and "MT2" labels frequently do not clearly distinguish these, and nasal spray products may contain any of the three, or none.

**Dermatology case-report literature (AE surveillance context):** Habbema et al. 2017 (Leiden, Netherlands) reviewed case reports linking grey-market MT-II use to melanocytic changes in existing nevi, eruptive dysplastic nevi, and four melanoma cases emerging during or shortly after use [6, mechanism_review]. This is a case-report base — causality is not established — but the biological mechanism (MC1R-driven melanocyte proliferation) provides plausible mechanistic grounding. The grey-market context means dose, purity, and duration are unverifiable in every reported case.

**2-sentence summary for concentration-audit gate:**

The foundational pharmacology and all formal human trials of Melanotan-II originate from a single research group — the University of Arizona (Hadley, Hruby, Dorr, Wessells et al.), accounting for approximately 90% of primary experimental human data; the clinical surveillance/AE case-report literature is diffuse and multi-origin but represents secondary observation, not independent efficacy research.
