## Section B — Tanning / melanogenesis evidence & dermatologic-safety signal

### B.1 Mechanism: MC1R → eumelanin pathway

Melanotan-II (MT-II; [Nle⁴, D-Phe⁷]-α-MSH cyclic heptapeptide) binds melanocortin receptors with high potency and low receptor selectivity, hitting MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R. At MC1R on epidermal melanocytes, binding activates adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA → MITF upregulation → transcriptional induction of tyrosinase, TRP-1, and TRP-2. The net result is increased synthesis of eumelanin (brown-black, UV-absorbing) over phaeomelanin (red-yellow, photosensitizing) [1, open_label]. This cAMP cascade is the same pathway activated by natural α-MSH in response to UV-induced paracrine signaling from keratinocytes, but MT-II drives it pharmacologically and systemically, without UV gating.

The theoretical photoprotective rationale of the original University of Arizona program rested on this biology: pre-loading eumelanin in individuals with low baseline pigmentation (Fitzpatrick I–II) might reduce UV-induced DNA damage. This rationale remains mechanistically plausible but was never established in powered clinical trials for MT-II specifically [1, open_label].

### B.2 Human tanning evidence: the MT-II clinical data

**Dorr et al., 1996 (University of Arizona) — the pivotal early trial [1, open_label].**
Design: single-blind, alternating-day (saline or MT-II), dose-escalation trial. Population: n=3 healthy adult males. MT-II administered SC Monday–Friday ×2 weeks, starting at 0.01 mg/kg with increments to 0.025–0.03 mg/kg. Result: increased pigmentation evident in face, upper body, and buttocks in 2/3 subjects after only 5 low-dose injections; the authors concluded "MT-II has tanning activity in humans given only 5 low doses every other day by subcutaneous injection." Adverse effects were prominent: nausea at most dose levels, somnolence, fatigue, and spontaneous penile erections lasting 1–5 h (MC4R-mediated). This n=3 open-label dose-finding pilot is the founding human tanning evidence for MT-II — low tier by modern standards, but the mechanistic signal was reproducible.

**Important negative space:** No adequately powered, blinded, placebo-controlled RCT of MT-II for tanning in humans has been published. The drug exited formal development partly because MC4R-driven adverse effects (erections, nausea) are dose-limiting at doses required for robust tanning. The grey-market injectable-tanning market subsequently absorbed the compound without regulatory oversight.

**Contrast — Melanotan I (afamelanotide) vs. MT-II:** Afamelanotide (branded Scenesse) is a structurally distinct, MC1R-selective linear 13-amino-acid analog. It received EC marketing authorisation on 22 December 2014 (following a CHMP positive opinion on 23 October 2014) and FDA approval in October 2019 for erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP) — an ultra-rare photosensitivity disorder — at 16 mg SC implant q2 months [2, regulatory]. Afamelanotide is NOT a tanning drug approval; it is a disease treatment for EPP. Its MC1R selectivity avoids the MC4R-driven sexual and neurologic side effects that limited MT-II. Fitzgerald et al. (2006, Peptides, PMID 16293341, n=77 RCT) showed afamelanotide increased melanin density in Caucasians carrying MC1R variant alleles [3, rct] — but this is Melanotan I data, and must not be read as MT-II evidence.

### B.3 Photoprotection claims

The founding hypothesis (Hadley, Hruby, Arizona group) was that pre-loading eumelanin through MC1R activation would reduce UV-induced DNA double-strand breaks and pyrimidine dimer formation. This remains mechanistically coherent — eumelanin absorbs UV and quenches reactive oxygen species — but no controlled human photoprotection trial of MT-II (as distinct from afamelanotide) was completed and published to tier-1 standards. The claim that MT-II provides meaningful photoprotection in human use is extrapolated from mechanism, not established in powered clinical studies [1, open_label]. Regulatory agencies have never endorsed MT-II for photoprotection.

### B.4 The dermatologic safety signal — melanocytic nevi

This is the load-bearing safety concern. Multiple independent case reports from UK, European, US, and Australian dermatology services document a consistent pattern: MT-II use is temporally associated with (a) eruptive new melanocytic nevi, (b) rapid darkening and morphologic change in pre-existing nevi, and (c) histologically confirmed dysplasia. The signal is real, multi-sourced, and biologically coherent — MT-II is a non-selective melanogenesis driver that activates melanocytes without the homeostatic UV-gating or keratinocyte paracrine feedback that normally limits melanocyte proliferation.

**Case series evidence (tier 3 — case report / open_label, but convergent across independent sites):**

**Cousen, Colver, Helbling 2009 (British Journal of Dermatology) [4, open_label].**
Correspondence reporting cases of rapidly changing moles in patients self-injecting unlicensed melanotan I/II from internet sources. Histological findings ranged from "benign to severely dysplastic naevi." One case documented a rapidly developing melanoma in a patient who had recently completed a course of melanotan injections. Published in a tier-1 dermatology journal; provided the first systematic documentation of the nevi-change signal in the UK literature.

**Langan et al., "Change in moles linked to use of unlicensed 'sun tan jab'" BMJ 2009, PMID 19174439 [5, open_label].**
Two patients attending a UK dermatology clinic with rapidly changing moles and intense tan despite fair skin (Fitzpatrick I–II). Both were sunbed users and both had self-injected melanotan (I and II, purchased online) shortly before moles changed. The temporal association and concurrent UV exposure from sunbeds compound the melanocyte stimulatory burden. Published BMJ tier-1.

**Hueso-Gabriel et al. 2012 (Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas) [6, open_label].**
A 25-year-old male construction worker, Fitzpatrick II, completed a 4-week course of SC MT-II with concurrent sunbed use. Presentation: >100 melanocytic nevi on his back, most clinically and dermatoscopically atypical. Ten lesions excised; histopathology: severe dysplasia in 3 specimens, plus one pigmented basal cell carcinoma on the right shoulder. Authors conclude phototype I–II individuals with multiple nevi, family history of melanoma, and sunbed use are at highest risk, and that dermatologists should suspect melanotan use when faced with eruptive or transforming nevi.

**Schulze & Erdmann 2014 (European Journal of Dermatology, PMID 24334249) [7, open_label].**
A 24-year-old male bodybuilder reported eruption of multiple new nevi and darkening of pre-existing nevi within 24 h of a single subcutaneous injection of grey-market MT-II. The 24-hour time course from a single dose is the most striking demonstration that MT-II's melanocyte activation is pharmacodynamically rapid and does not require repeated dosing or UV exposure to trigger clinically observable nevi change.

**Sivyer 2012 (Dermatology Practical & Conceptual, PMID 23785612) [8, open_label].**
Particularly concerning case: a 16-year-old female with familial atypical multiple mole and melanoma (FAMMM) syndrome (>50 nevi, maternal history of melanoma) self-injected 0.5 mg MT-II daily for two months while attending UV tanning beds 2–3×/week. Within three weeks: generalized tanning, darkening of multiple nevi, one groin nevus enlarged with suspicious dermatoscopic features confirmed histologically as dysplastic compound nevus. At three months post-cessation: skin color and nevi lightened. This case illustrates the compounded risk in genetically predisposed individuals and raises the question of whether dermatoscopic nevi surveillance was obstructed by the diffuse melanin-darkening effect.

### B.5 Temporal association with melanoma — the oncologic signal

Beyond nevi dysplasia, case reports document melanoma diagnoses in temporal proximity to MT-II use. These are individual reports, not epidemiologically powered cohort studies; causality cannot be established from case reports alone. However, the biological plausibility is high: sustained non-selective MC1R/MC3R/MC4R agonism without UV gating activates melanocyte proliferation outside normal homeostatic controls, potentially accelerating pre-malignant evolution in already dysplastic clones.

**Paurobally, Jason, Dezfoulian, Nikkels 2011 (British Journal of Dermatology, 164(6):1403–5) [9, open_label].**
A 42-year-old white woman, Fitzpatrick III, injected MT-II (100 mg×2 days then 50 mg×5 days). Three months post-injection: a suspicious pigmented lesion on her abdomen showed enlargement and color change. Multiple pre-existing nevi also enlarged and darkened. Histology: melanoma, Breslow 0.30 mm, Clark level II; IHC positive for S100a, HMB45, Melan-A, NKIC3, tyrosinase; Ki-67 10%. Authors establish a temporal relationship, noting that systemic melanocyte stimulation may have promoted a pre-existing melanocytic lesion toward malignancy.

**Ong & Bowling 2012 (Australasian Journal of Dermatology, 53(4):301-2, PMID 22724573) [10, open_label].**
Case report of melanoma in situ in a young adult female following melanotan use. Authors note that injectable melanotropic peptides are available via internet despite being unlicensed with an unproven safety record; the case adds to the multi-continental documentation of the signal.

**Hjuler & Lorentzen 2014 (Dermatology, 228:34–6, PMID 24355990) [11, open_label].**
Case series documenting melanoma developing in MT-II users in Denmark. A patient conducted 3–4 weeks of self-injections with MT-II alongside sunbed use; melanoma was excised approximately 3 months later from a changing existing mole. Multiple case notes in this publication reinforce the pattern: MT-II use + concurrent UV exposure + changing pre-existing lesion → melanoma at excision. The authors call for dermatological monitoring in any patient using melanocortin peptides.

**Bonchev 2026 (Life (Basel), PMID 41752902) [12, open_label].**
A 42-year-old male self-administered 400 µg MT-II SC every other day for 64 days (32 injections; 12.8 mg cumulative; grey-market research peptide retailer) with concurrent sunbed use. Notable finding: symmetrical brown gingival and buccal mucosal pigmentation developed during treatment, regressing slowly over 3 months post-cessation. This recent case documents that MT-II's melanogenesis activates mucosal melanocytes, extending the surveillance obligation to oral mucosa — an area difficult to monitor in grey-market users.

### B.6 The monitoring problem: why grey-market use is specifically dangerous

The dermatologic-safety signal is compounded by the structural features of grey-market MT-II use:

1. **No baseline dermatoscopic mapping.** Approved drugs that stimulate melanocytes (e.g., afamelanotide for EPP) are administered in monitored clinical settings where baseline nevi mapping can be performed and changes tracked. Grey-market MT-II users receive no such baseline.

2. **Diffuse melanin darkening obscures surveillance.** The same mechanism that darkens the skin also darkens all nevi simultaneously, making it harder to detect a single nevus undergoing pathological change against a background of pharmacologically darkened skin and nevi.

3. **Concurrent UV exposure.** Grey-market users frequently combine MT-II with sunbed use to "amplify" tanning, adding direct mutagenic UV burden to chemically stimulated melanocytes — the worst-case combination documented in Hueso-Gabriel 2012, Langan 2009, and Hjuler 2014.

4. **Unregulated product quality.** Grey-market MT-II is sold as a lyophilized research peptide without pharmaceutical-grade purity guarantees, sterility testing, or accurate dosing. The FDA issued a warning letter to Melanocorp (c. 2007) regarding unlicensed marketing of injectable tanning products; MHRA, TGA, HPRA, and EMA have all issued public warnings.

5. **Not an approved tanning agent.** MT-II was never submitted for FDA or EMA approval as a tanning agent. Afamelanotide (Scenesse) — a structurally different, MC1R-selective drug — is approved only for EPP, a rare photosensitivity disease [2, regulatory]. The comparison between these two drugs is frequently misused in grey-market marketing to imply MT-II has a regulatory-approval analogue.
