## Section A — Identity & molecular mechanism

### A.1 Chemical identity and structural context

Melanotan-II (MT-II; also written Melanotan 2 or MT-2) is a synthetic, cyclic lactam heptapeptide analog of endogenous α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH). Its sequence is **Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-NH₂**, where the ring is formed by a lactam bridge between the Asp side-chain carboxyl and the Lys side-chain amine, and the peptide terminates in a C-terminal primary amide (NH₂) [1, rct]. Molecular formula: C₅₀H₆₉N₁₅O₉; molecular weight: **1024.2 Da** [2, regulatory]. The compound was designed and synthesized at the University of Arizona by Mac Hadley, Victor Hruby, and colleagues as part of a program to develop superpotent, enzymatically resistant melanocortin analogs; the cyclization was the key engineering choice — it constrains the bioactive β-turn conformation that is only transiently adopted by the linear endogenous peptide, yielding substantially greater potency and proteolytic stability relative to α-MSH [3, open_label].

**Three compounds are routinely conflated and must be distinguished:**

| Compound | Structure | MW | Terminus | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Melanotan-I (MT-I; afamelanotide; Scenesse®)** | Linear 13-residue α-MSH analog ([Nle⁴,D-Phe⁷]-α-MSH) | 1646.8 Da | Free acid (carboxyl) | EU/US approved for **erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP)** — the only approved melanocortin-based tanning agent |
| **Melanotan-II (MT-II)** | Cyclic lactam **heptapeptide** | 1024.2 Da | C-terminal **amide** (NH₂) | **Not approved** anywhere; unapproved research compound |
| **PT-141 / bremelanotide (Vyleesi®)** | Cyclic heptapeptide, same backbone as MT-II | 1025.2 Da | C-terminal **free acid** (OH) | FDA-approved 2019 for **hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)** in premenopausal women |

Afamelanotide (MT-I) is a **linear** 13-amino-acid analog — the full α-MSH sequence with two substitutions — and targets **MC1R** preferentially, which is why it is useful for increasing photoprotective eumelanin in EPP without the pronounced CNS sexual/erectogenic activity of MT-II [4, mechanism_review]. It shares no structural overlap with the 7-residue cyclic MT-II scaffold.

Bremelanotide (PT-141/Vyleesi) is a **cyclic heptapeptide with the same ring sequence as MT-II** but differs at the C-terminus: MT-II carries a primary amide (–NH₂), while bremelanotide carries a free carboxylic acid (–OH). The FDA-approved Vyleesi label specifies the structure as `Ac-Nle-cyclo-(Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys-OH)` [5, regulatory]. The des-amide relationship is structural: bremelanotide is the **C-terminal free-acid (des-amide) structural analog of MT-II**, developed directly by Palatin Technologies from the MT-II cyclic scaffold as an independent drug candidate. Whether MT-II undergoes in-vivo deamidation to yield bremelanotide as a metabolite has not been established in any cited pharmacokinetic study; no in-vivo metabolic-conversion pathway from MT-II to bremelanotide is asserted here. Bremelanotide has an FDA-documented terminal half-life of approximately **2.7 hours** (range 1.9–4.0 h) after SC injection, and its receptor potency order is MC1R > MC4R > MC3R > MC5R > MC2R [5, regulatory].

### A.2 The melanocortin system: endogenous biology

The melanocortins (α-MSH, β-MSH, γ-MSH, and ACTH) are cleaved from pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) and act at five G-protein-coupled receptor subtypes, MCR1–MCR5. Each subtype has a distinct expression pattern and physiological role [6, mechanism_review]:

- **MC1R**: Expressed primarily on cutaneous melanocytes and in skin. Binding of α-MSH or ACTH activates adenylate cyclase (cAMP/PKA pathway), driving transcription of melanogenic enzymes (tyrosinase, TYRP1, DCT) and the production of **photoprotective eumelanin** (brown-black pigment). MC1R is also a major melanoma susceptibility locus; loss-of-function variants impair eumelanin synthesis and increase UV-induced oxidative damage [7, mechanism_review].

- **MC3R**: Expressed in the hypothalamus and gut; participates in energy homeostasis and feeding regulation, though its specific contributions are less well delineated than MC4R.

- **MC4R**: Expressed in hypothalamic and brainstem nuclei (especially the paraventricular nucleus). POMC-derived α-MSH signaling through MC4R constitutively suppresses food intake and energy storage; loss-of-function MC4R mutations are the most common cause of monogenic human obesity. MC4R also mediates centrally driven erectile function and sexual motivation — the pharmacological basis of MT-II's and bremelanotide's pro-erectile and pro-libido effects [8, mechanism_review]. The side-effects of nausea, yawning, and stretching observed with MT-II in clinical trials [3, open_label] are likewise mediated through CNS melanocortin receptors.

- **MC5R**: Expressed in exocrine glands and peripheral tissues; function in humans is not fully characterized.

- **MC2R**: Exclusively activated by ACTH (not MSH peptides); mediates adrenocortical steroidogenesis.

### A.3 MT-II mechanism: non-selective melanocortin receptor agonism

MT-II is a **non-selective melanocortin receptor agonist**, activating MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R with high potency across all four subtypes. This is the single most pharmacologically important characterization of MT-II, and it is what distinguishes it from the two approved compounds:

- **Afamelanotide** is developed as an **MC1R-focused** agonist (the EPP indication requires eumelanin upregulation in skin without pronounced CNS MC4R-mediated sexual effects).
- **Bremelanotide** is developed primarily for **MC4R-mediated** central sexual arousal, though it also retains potent MC1R activity (the FDA label documents MC1R as its highest-potency target by binding affinity, with MC4R second).

MT-II's non-selectivity means that a single subcutaneous injection simultaneously drives:
1. **Cutaneous MC1R activation** → cAMP/PKA cascade in melanocytes → eumelanin synthesis → delayed-onset UV-independent tanning (the original development rationale as a "sunless tanning agent" and potential skin cancer chemopreventive) [3, open_label].
2. **Hypothalamic MC4R activation** → centrally mediated spontaneous penile erection and augmented sexual desire in men, observed within 1–5 hours of injection — the effect was documented in both a psychogenic ED cohort (8/10 men developed erections; PMID 9679884) and an organic ED cohort (12/19 injections produced erections vs. 1/21 placebo; PMID 11018622) [9, rct; 10, rct].
3. **Appetite suppression** (MC3R/MC4R): decreased appetite was noted as an adverse effect in clinical trials [9, rct].

The Wessells 2000 study (PMID 11035391) in the International Journal of Impotence Research characterized MT-II explicitly as "a non-selective melanocortin receptor agonist" and documented it initiating erections in 17/20 men with erectile dysfunction without sexual stimulation, with increased sexual desire following 68% of MT-II doses vs. 19% with placebo [1, rct].

### A.4 Pharmacology summary

**Route:** Subcutaneous injection is the established clinical and research route; the Phase I studies used SC dosing at 0.01–0.03 mg/kg [3, open_label]. Oral bioavailability in rats is low (~4.6%) due to peptide degradation [11, in_vitro].

**Half-life:** Pharmacokinetic data for MT-II specifically in humans are sparse in the peer-reviewed literature. The closely related bremelanotide (des-amide MT-II) has a documented human terminal t½ of ~2.7 h after SC injection [5, regulatory]. The lactam cyclization confers resistance to proteolysis relative to linear α-MSH, supporting a duration of effect extending 1–5 hours after SC injection as observed in clinical trials [3, open_label].

**Receptor profile contrast:**

| Compound | MC1R | MC3R | MC4R | MC5R | Primary clinical effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| α-MSH (endogenous) | +++ | ++ | ++ | + | Physiological pigmentation / appetite |
| MT-I (afamelanotide) | +++ (primary) | + | +/– | + | Eumelanin ↑ (approved EPP) |
| **MT-II** | +++ | ++ | +++ | ++ | Tanning + erection + appetite suppression (unapproved) |
| Bremelanotide (PT-141) | ++++ (highest) | ++ | +++ | ++ | HSDD (approved) |
