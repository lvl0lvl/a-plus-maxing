## Section C — Sexual/erectile effects & the PT-141 relationship

### C1. How the erectile field started: spontaneous erection as an incidental finding

The sexual pharmacology of MT-II was discovered accidentally. Researchers at the University of Arizona were studying MT-II as a subcutaneous tanning agent in the early 1990s when a male investigator who self-administered the peptide reported an unexpected, prolonged spontaneous erection — without sexual stimulation or intent. That observation redirected the program and launched the entire melanocortin-erectile research axis [1, mechanism_review].

The key receptor behind this effect is **MC4R**, expressed centrally in hypothalamic and limbic circuits. MT-II activates MC4R (as well as MC1R, MC3R, and MC5R; it is non-selective), producing centrally initiated tumescence independent of nitric-oxide-mediated peripheral vascular mechanisms. The review by King, Mayorov, Balse-Srinivasan, Hruby, Vanderah, and Wessells (2007) established MC4R as "the principal effector of melanocortin-induced erection," though MC3R involvement could not be excluded [2, mechanism_review].

### C2. Proof-of-concept trials in men — Wessells, University of Arizona

**Wessells et al. 1998 (J Urol) — psychogenic ED, double-blind RCT, n=10**

The first controlled human trial enrolled 10 men with psychogenic erectile dysfunction in a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design [3, rct]. Each participant received MT-II at 0.025 mg/kg subcutaneous (SC) and placebo on separate occasions, with penile rigidity monitored in real time via RigiScan over a 6-hour observation window. Eight of 10 men (80%) achieved clinically apparent erections following MT-II. Mean tip rigidity >80% lasted 38.0 minutes with MT-II versus 3.0 minutes with placebo (p = 0.0045). Side effects — nausea, yawning, stretching, and transient decreased appetite — occurred more often with active drug and resolved without intervention. The authors concluded MT-II had "potent erectogenic activity" at this dose [3, rct].

**Wessells et al. 2000 (Int J Impot Res) — mixed ED etiology, double-blind RCT, n=20**

A follow-on crossover trial expanded the population to 20 men with erectile dysfunction of both psychogenic and organic causes [4, rct]. MT-II again at 0.025 mg/kg SC. Erections (>80% rigidity) were induced in 17 of 20 participants without sexual stimulation. Mean rigidity >80% lasted 41 minutes with MT-II. Enhanced sexual desire was reported following 68% of MT-II doses versus 19% of placebo doses (P<0.01). Severe nausea occurred in approximately 12.9% of active-dose administrations — the principal tolerability signal. The investigators explicitly flagged the need for downstream compounds with improved selectivity to reduce the nausea/tanning burden while preserving the erectogenic effect [4, rct].

**Wessells et al. 2000 (Urology) — organic ED, double-blind RCT, n=10**

A parallel trial enrolled 10 men with organic (vasculogenic/neurogenic) erectile dysfunction [5, rct]. Subjective erections were reported after 12 of 19 MT-II doses versus 1 of 21 placebo doses. Mean tip rigidity >80% lasted 45.3 minutes with MT-II versus 1.9 minutes for placebo. Sexual desire also increased significantly versus placebo. Severe nausea occurred with 4 of 19 MT-II injections. This trial is notable for demonstrating central melanocortin-driven erection even in men with underlying organic pathology, pointing toward a mechanism distinct from PDE5 inhibition [5, rct].

**Evidence ceiling — single PI team, single site, no independent replication:** All three MT-II erectile RCTs above (PMID 9679884, 11035391, 11018622) originate from the same principal investigator (Wessells et al.) at a single institution (University of Arizona) and are small proof-of-concept crossover designs (n=10, n=20, n=10). None has been independently replicated at any other center. The `rct` design tag is accurate — these are properly controlled crossover studies — but the erectile-efficacy evidence base for MT-II constitutes a single-group proof-of-concept body, not a multi-center or independently replicated literature. Downstream conclusions about erectogenic efficacy should be read against this ceiling.

### C3. The PT-141/bremelanotide relationship (load-bearing)

PT-141 (bremelanotide, brand name Vyleesi) is the **direct descendant of MT-II**, developed specifically to produce a clinical candidate from the erectogenic/pro-sexual signal while reducing the non-target effects. Structurally, bremelanotide is a cyclic heptapeptide lactam with the sequence Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-OH. It is correctly described as a **metabolite of MT-II that lacks the C-terminal amide function** (i.e., the carboxylate derivative of MT-II), detected in vivo as MT-II undergoes hydrolysis [2, mechanism_review]. The term "des-glycinamide metabolite" is sometimes used in lay literature; the peer-reviewed characterization is "deaminated derivative and likely metabolite" [2].

Critically, **bremelanotide does not cleanly separate MC4R from MC1R at the receptor-binding level**: it retains strong affinity for MC1R, MC3R, and MC4R. The separation from MT-II's tanning effect is primarily pharmacokinetic and route-of-administration-based (SC injection at lower effective doses, route changed from intranasal in early development), not a receptor-selectivity redesign [2]. The development rationale was therefore pragmatic: PT-141 was the metabolite that was already forming in vivo, was stable enough to study as a standalone entity, and showed pro-sexual activity in early trials.

**Attribution boundary (mandatory):** The PT-141/bremelanotide clinical program produced its own evidence base and that evidence belongs to the PT-141 entry, not MT-II. What is attributable to MT-II: the 1998–2000 Wessells trials above. What is attributable to PT-141 (reference only): Phase 1/2 trials in men with ED (Diamond et al. 2004, Int J Impot Res [6, rct]; Rosen et al. 2004, Int J Impot Res [7, rct]) and the RECONNECT Phase 3 program in women (Kingsberg et al. 2019 [8, rct]; Clayton et al. 2016 dose-finding [9, rct]).

**FDA approval status:** Bremelanotide (Vyleesi) was approved by the US FDA on **21 June 2019** for the treatment of acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women — making it the **first FDA-approved as-needed pharmacological treatment for HSDD**. Approval was based on the two identically designed RECONNECT trials (combined efficacy population n=1,202 premenopausal women; 24-week treatment; 1.75 mg SC as needed): both trials met co-primary endpoints of statistically significant improvement in sexual desire (FSFI-desire domain) and reduction in distress (FSDS-DAO item 13) versus placebo [8, rct]. **MT-II has no approved therapeutic indication anywhere.** The approval history runs MT-II (University of Arizona animal/human research tool, 1990s–2000s) → PT-141 (Palatin Technologies investigational compound) → bremelanotide/Vyleesi (FDA-approved, 2019).

The Hadley and Dorr 2006 review in *Peptides* provides the historical arc from MT-I and MT-II through to PT-141's entry into Phase 3, documenting the commercialization trajectory and citing MT-II's role as the "discovery compound" that established the sexual-melanocortin signal [1, mechanism_review].

### C4. Appetite suppression and other MC4R-mediated effects

MC4R in the hypothalamic paraventricular nucleus and arcuate nucleus is a well-established regulator of energy balance. Activation suppresses food intake and increases energy expenditure; loss-of-function MC4R mutations cause severe hereditary obesity in humans (this mechanism is the basis for setmelanotide's approved indication in MC4R-pathway obesity). MT-II, as a non-selective MC4R agonist, predictably activates this axis.

**Important population qualifier:** the appetite-suppression evidence for MT-II specifically is **animal data**. The Wessells human trials noted transient decreased appetite as an adverse effect at the 0.025 mg/kg SC dose [3, 4, 5, rct], but they were not designed or powered to measure food intake or body weight, and no dedicated controlled human trial of MT-II for weight management has been conducted. The hindbrain MT-II food-intake study by De Jonghe et al. (2012, Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab) used intracerebroventricular injection in POMC-knockout mice — this is rodent work and does not translate directly to SC human dosing [10, animal]. The appetite effect in humans at research-used SC doses appears real but clinically unremarkable and secondary to nausea.

The nausea signal itself is mechanistically linked: the area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius both express MC4R. Nausea, yawning, stretching, and decreased appetite are therefore a pharmacodynamic cluster from the same receptor activation driving the pro-erectile effect — not separable at the MT-II receptor-binding level.
