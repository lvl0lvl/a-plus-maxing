# Section A — Identity / Composition / Manufacturing / Mechanism
## Cerebrolysin (FPF-1070) | Ever Neuro Pharma GmbH

---

## A.1 Identity and Regulatory Status

Cerebrolysin (trade name; former designation FPF-1070) is a parenterally administered, porcine-brain-derived peptide preparation manufactured by **Ever Neuro Pharma GmbH** (formerly Ever Pharma), Unterach, Austria. It is classified as a **peptidergic drug** — a complex, multi-component mixture resulting from controlled enzymatic proteolysis of porcine cortical protein, not a single defined molecular entity. It is registered and marketed under national authorizations in multiple countries (Austria, Germany, Russia, China, several Asian and Eastern European markets) and is under active clinical investigation in stroke and dementia indications globally; it does not hold EMA-wide centralized marketing authorization or US FDA approval as of 2026.

The preparation is supplied as a clear, amber, sterile injectable solution for **intravenous (IV) or intramuscular (IM) administration only**. It is not orally bioavailable — the peptide components are degraded in the GI tract and their intact CNS delivery requires parenteral administration [1, mechanism_review].

## A.2 Composition

### A.2.1 Gross Composition

Cerebrolysin is a heterogeneous mixture whose reported gross composition is approximately **85% free amino acids and 15% biologically active low-molecular-weight peptides** [13, in_vitro; 14, in_vitro]. All peptide components fall below **10 kDa molecular weight**, with most characterized fragments in the 1–5 kDa range [13, in_vitro]. Note: an earlier pharmacological characterization (Hartbauer et al. 2001) reported a 75%/25% split, suggesting the cited ratio may reflect batch or assay variation; the 85%/15% figure is the most widely cited in the primary literature. The preparation is formulated at approximately 215 mg/mL total protein/peptide material in aqueous solution, with pH adjusted for parenteral compatibility.

### A.2.2 Peptide Identity — Proteomic/Peptidomic Findings

Multiple mass-spectrometric analyses have now partially characterized the peptidome of Cerebrolysin. The most comprehensive published profiling study (Gevaert et al., 2015) identified **638 unique peptides** from commercially obtained samples using HPLC-ion trap and UHPLC-Q-IM-TOF mass spectrometry combined with UniProt pig-protein database searching and de novo sequencing [4, in_vitro]. The dominant protein sources of these peptides were **tubulin alpha- and beta-chain, actin, and myelin basic protein** — structural proteins abundant in neural tissue — rather than canonical neurotrophic factor proteins.

A critically important negative finding from Gevaert et al.: **no peptide fragments corresponding to GDNF, NGF, BDNF, or CNTF were detected** in the preparation [4, in_vitro]. This directly contradicts marketing language that describes the product as a "mixture of neurotrophic factors." The authors concluded that "the activities reported in the literature are likely the result of new, hitherto unknown cryptic peptides with nootropic properties" [4, in_vitro] — meaning the mechanistically active components remain incompletely characterized.

A subsequent study by Yang et al. (2023) using nanoLC-MS with PEAKS, pNovo, and novor software identified three candidate bioactive peptides in Cerebrolysin: two anti-inflammatory peptides (sequences **LLNLQPPPR** and **LSPSLRLP**, both from myelin basic protein) and one antioxidant peptide (**WPFPR**, from tubulin beta chain) [5, in_vitro]. These are proposed as contributors to the preparation's preclinical effects, but their causal roles have not been confirmed in controlled bioassay.

An earlier Russian peptidomic study (Gromova et al., 2019) identified 14,635 peptides from 1,643 porcine neuronal proteins in the "light" fraction, including 405 fragments corresponding to known bioactive peptides such as enkephalins and neuropeptide analogs, and found computational evidence that these could modulate 275 human signaling proteins and inhibit pro-apoptotic caspases and kinases [6, mechanism_review]. This study was conducted by investigators with declared institutional affiliations overlapping manufacturer interests, and the computational inference of bioactivity should be treated as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

A 2024 comparative analysis (Seidl & Aigner) found that peptide preparations marketed as compositionally similar to Cerebrolysin "lack relevant biological activity," highlighting that even modest compositional differences produce functionally distinct products and that Cerebrolysin's biological fingerprint is not easily replicated — consistent with the uncharacterized-active-component critique [7, in_vitro].

### A.2.3 The Undefined-Active-Component Critique

The fundamental characterization limitation is this: **the identity of the components responsible for Cerebrolysin's observed biological effects has not been established**. The three candidate cryptic peptides (Yang et al.) are consistent with a multi-target mechanism but have not been tested in isolation at physiologically relevant concentrations. The preparation cannot currently be reduced to a defined pharmacophore. This is a material scientific limitation that the manufacturer's own regulatory submissions acknowledge by categorizing Cerebrolysin as a "well-established use" biological rather than a defined new chemical entity.

## A.3 Manufacturing and Source

### A.3.1 Raw Material and Proteolysis

Cerebrolysin is manufactured from **porcine brain cortex** obtained from slaughterhouse-grade pigs. The protein fraction is subjected to a **standardized controlled enzymatic proteolysis** (specific proteases not publicly disclosed in detail) followed by ultrafiltration to remove high-molecular-weight fragments (>10 kDa cutoff), purification, sterile filtration, and filling into ampoules or vials [13, in_vitro; 14, in_vitro]. The manufacturing steps are described in the primary pharmacological literature as "a standardised enzymatic breakdown of lipid-free porcine brain proteins" [13, in_vitro]; the specific protease identities and precise ultrafiltration parameters are not publicly documented in peer-reviewed sources and are disclosed only in Ever Neuro Pharma's proprietary regulatory dossier (manufacturer product literature; not publicly available as of 2026) [15, vendor_label]. The manufacturer describes the process as producing a consistent, batch-standardized preparation, with quality specified in terms of total peptide content, free amino acid content, and biological activity markers using functional cell-based assays.

### A.3.2 Batch Standardization — Claim and Critique

Ever Neuro Pharma maintains that production batches are characterized for functional neurotrophic activity (e.g., neuronal survival assays) and that this serves as the batch-release criterion rather than full peptide sequence characterization. The Seidl & Aigner (2024) comparison data support the view that the original manufacturing process produces a distinct functional profile, but **no peer-reviewed primary literature documents the specific batch-to-batch peptide variability** of authentic Ever Neuro Pharma production lots. The 638-peptide profiling by Gevaert et al. was performed on Internet-purchased Cerebrolysin, not manufacturer-certified reference lots, introducing an additional confound. Batch consistency at the sequence level remains an open scientific question.

### A.3.3 Prion / TSE Risk — Theoretical Regulatory Consideration

Cerebrolysin is derived from **porcine central nervous system tissue** — a CNS-origin biologic. The European regulatory framework for biologics of animal origin (Note for Guidance EMEA/410/01) requires TSE (transmissible spongiform encephalopathy) risk assessment for any CNS-derived material. Pigs are not considered a recognized reservoir for classical scrapie or BSE, and no case of porcine TSE has been documented. However, the theoretical residual risk from a CNS-source biologic is non-zero by regulatory convention, and Ever Neuro Pharma is required to maintain geographically controlled sourcing of pigs from countries with negligible BSE risk and to validate the manufacturing process for prion-reduction capability. This is a **theoretical and regulatory consideration, not a documented case series risk** — but it is a material difference from small-molecule drugs and is relevant to long-term pharmacovigilance. No cases of TSE transmission from Cerebrolysin use have been reported in the published literature as of the time of this writing.

## A.4 Proposed Mechanisms of Action

Cerebrolysin's proposed mechanisms are **preclinically established and plausible, but not proven at the level of a well-characterized pharmacophore**. The principal hypotheses are:

### A.4.1 Neurotrophic Factor Mimicry

The dominant proposed mechanism is that Cerebrolysin peptides act as partial functional mimetics of endogenous neurotrophic factors — particularly BDNF, GDNF, NGF, and CNTF — activating their downstream intracellular signaling cascades without sharing their primary structures [1, mechanism_review; 8, mechanism_review]. Specifically, PI3K/Akt/GSK-3β signaling has been identified as a relevant downstream pathway: in APP transgenic Alzheimer's models, Cerebrolysin attenuated GSK-3β and CDK-5 activity, reducing tau phosphorylation and increasing synaptic density [8, mechanism_review]. *Note: [8] is authored by Eliezer Masliah, subject of the September 2024 NIH ORI misconduct finding (132 papers, 8 Cerebrolysin-specific); the GSK-3β/tau-reduction and synaptic density claims grounded primarily in [8] are provisional — the underlying preclinical papers this review synthesizes include retracted Rockenstein et al. studies.* In a cholinergic-deficit transgenic model, it modulated the pro-NGF/mature NGF ratio, increasing mature NGF immunoreactivity in the hippocampus and preserving nucleus basalis cholinergic neurons [9, animal].

**Critical framing:** The absence of detectable BDNF/GDNF/NGF/CNTF peptide fragments in the preparation (Gevaert et al.) [4, in_vitro] means the phrase "neurotrophic mimicry" describes the *functional consequence* of yet-unidentified cryptic peptides — not direct delivery of neurotrophic factor-derived sequences. The mechanism is functional analogy, not structural identity.

### A.4.2 Neuroprotection — Anti-Excitotoxic, Anti-Apoptotic, Anti-Calpain

Multiple preclinical studies support neuroprotective activity through at least two demonstrated pathways:

**Calpain inhibition:** Wronski et al. (2000) demonstrated that the brain-derived peptide preparation inhibits both mu-calpain and m-calpain through a non-competitive or tight-binding competitive mechanism, with dialysis experiments confirming reversibility. The inhibitory activity was attributed to calpastatin-like fragments within the preparation [10, in_vitro]. Calpain is a calcium-activated protease central to excitotoxic neuronal death; its inhibition by Cerebrolysin components in vitro provides a mechanistically grounded neuroprotection pathway.

**Cytoskeletal protein stabilization:** A companion study (Wronski et al., 2000) showed that Cerebrolysin-treated primary neurons retained significantly more MAP-2 (microtubule-associated protein 2) than controls, an effect shown to be independent of de novo protein synthesis — indicating degradation prevention rather than upregulation [11, in_vitro]. In the context of excitotoxicity and hypoxia, MAP-2 loss is a marker of irreversible neuronal injury.

**Anti-apoptotic effects:** In APP transgenic mice, Cerebrolysin significantly increased BrdU+ cells and DCX+ neuroblasts while reducing TUNEL+ and activated caspase-3 positive cells, suggesting preservation of neural progenitor viability through apoptosis suppression [12, animal]. The preparation "stabilizes the structural integrity of cells by inhibition of calpain and reduces the number of apoptotic cells" in ischemic models, while also decreasing infarct volume and edema [8, mechanism_review].

### A.4.3 Neurogenesis and Neuroplasticity

Masliah & Díez-Tejedor (2012) summarize preclinical evidence that Cerebrolysin enhances neurogenesis in the dentate gyrus in dementia models and promotes stroke-induced neurogenesis in the subventricular zone [8, mechanism_review]. *[8] is Masliah-authored (NIH ORI misconduct finding, Sept 2024); neurogenesis claims sourced primarily from [8] are provisional — treat as requiring corroboration from non-Masliah sources.* Rockenstein et al. (2007) demonstrated in APP/PS1 transgenic mice that Cerebrolysin-treated animals had significantly more BrdU+ neural progenitor cells and DCX+ neuroblasts than vehicle controls — an effect attributed to protection of progenitor survival rather than increased mitotic rate [12, animal]. Increased synaptic density and restored neuronal cytoarchitecture were also documented in these models.

### A.4.4 Amyloid and Tau Pathology Reduction

In Alzheimer's disease models, Cerebrolysin decreased β-amyloid deposition and reduced tau phosphorylation through modulation of GSK-3β and CDK-5 [8, mechanism_review]. *These claims are doubly provisional: [8] is Masliah-authored (NIH ORI misconduct finding, Sept 2024), and the primary transgenic-mouse papers this review synthesizes include retracted Rockenstein et al. studies (PMIDs 25047000, 26611895). Treat as hypothesis-generating until corroborated by non-Masliah, non-retracted primary sources.* Whether equivalent magnitudes occur in human Alzheimer's disease remains unresolved.
