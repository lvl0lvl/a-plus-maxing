# Section C — Mechanism Depth, Counter-Evidence, and Independent vs. Sponsor Replication

## C.1 Mechanistic Depth: The Neurotrophic-Mimicry Hypothesis

### Composition and the "Active Component" Problem

Cerebrolysin is a porcine-brain hydrolysate consisting of approximately **85% free L-amino acids and 15% short peptides below 10,000 Da** (Hutter-Paier et al. 1996; Sugita et al. 1993 — the most widely cited primary sources). An earlier characterization (Hartbauer et al. 2001) reported a 75%/25% split, reflecting possible batch or assay variation; the 85%/15% figure is used consistently across Sections A and D and in the primary analytical literature. No peptide constituent has been identified as the single active molecule. Critically, targeted mass-spectrometry has confirmed that **no fragments of known neurotrophic proteins** — BDNF, GDNF, NGF, or CNTF — are detectable in the preparation [1, mechanism_review]. The neurotrophic-mimicry claim therefore holds that uncharacterized cryptic peptides activate the same downstream signaling as these growth factors, not that the preparation contains them. This distinction is not pedantic: the mechanism is plausible but molecularly undefined, which makes dose-response characterization and batch-to-batch reproducibility inherently uncertain. No other manufacturer can replicate the preparation; there is no single CAS identifier.

### BDNF/GDNF/NGF-Like Signaling — What Preclinical Evidence Actually Shows

Preclinical work demonstrates that Cerebrolysin upregulates endogenous BDNF, VEGF, and IGF-1 in rodent brain tissue and suppresses TNF-α [2, mechanism_review]. The mechanistic interpretation is that the preparation triggers Trk-receptor–mediated signaling indirectly, through unidentified peptide ligands, rather than through direct neurotrophic-factor supplementation. **Note on source integrity:** [2] is authored by Eliezer Masliah, who is the subject of the September 2024 NIH ORI research-misconduct finding (figure fabrication/falsification across 132 papers, 8 Cerebrolysin-specific; see [4]). This review is not itself retracted as of mid-2026, but its claims should be weighed accordingly; where [2] is the sole or primary support for a mechanistic claim, that claim is treated as provisional pending corroboration from non-Masliah sources. The functional endpoints shown in animal and cell models include:

- **Synaptogenesis and dendritic arborization:** Increased synaptic density in hippocampal tissue from rodents after ischemic insult [3, animal; rat, embolic MCA occlusion model].
- **Anti-apoptotic/anti-calpain:** Inhibition of calpain (cytoskeletal protease) and caspase-mediated apoptosis in rat ischemia models, reducing infarct volume and edema [2, mechanism_review]. *This claim rests primarily on [2] (Masliah-authored; integrity caveat above); no independent non-Masliah primary source for these specific endpoints in Cerebrolysin ischemia models was identified in this section's source set — treat as provisional.*
- **Neurogenesis (Sonic hedgehog pathway):** In rats with embolic stroke, Cerebrolysin increased neural progenitor cell proliferation in the subventricular zone and upregulated Shh, its receptors Patched and Smoothened, and downstream mediators. Pharmacological Shh blockade with cyclopamine abolished these neurogenesis and oligodendrogenesis effects in vitro and in vivo. Behavioral improvement (weeks 3–5 post-stroke) was reversed by cyclopamine [3, animal; rat, embolic stroke]. A published erratum was issued for this paper in February 2025 (PMID: 39869713); the scope of the correction is unspecified, and the core findings have not been formally retracted.

### Amyloid/Tau in Alzheimer's Models — A Literature Under Integrity Review

Multiple preclinical publications reported that Cerebrolysin reduces amyloid-β deposition and tau hyperphosphorylation in APP and Tau transgenic mouse models, with proposed mechanisms involving glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK-3β) regulation [2, mechanism_review]. *The GSK-3β mechanism claim here draws on [2] (Masliah-authored; see author-integrity caveat in C.1 above); it is also embedded in the very literature cluster now under formal misconduct review, so the mechanistic claim is doubly provisional — the primary evidence base (Rockenstein transgenic mouse papers) is substantially retracted, and the narrative review summarizing that evidence is Masliah-authored.* **The integrity of this body of literature is now seriously compromised:**

NIH concluded a formal research misconduct investigation on 15 September 2024, finding that Eliezer Masliah (former NIA Neuroscience Division director) committed "falsification and/or fabrication involving re-use and relabel of figure panels" across a dossier of 132 papers [4, regulatory]. Eight of those papers concern Cerebrolysin specifically. A contemporaneous *Science* investigative report (Piller, published online 26 September 2024) detailed the dossier and its implications for the Alzheimer's and Parkinson's research fields [5, anecdote_aggregate]. Herbert Moessler, EVER Pharma's former general manager, co-authored 57 Cerebrolysin papers and co-founded Neuropore Therapies with Masliah in 2008, creating a direct commercial–scientific overlap.

Additionally, the Sharma cluster of Cerebrolysin preclinical publications faces severe integrity issues. Multiple Sharma papers on Cerebrolysin have been flagged on PubPeer and several have been retracted; an entire Springer book edited by Hari and Aruna Sharma (containing three Cerebrolysin chapters) was also retracted [5, anecdote_aggregate].

Directly retracted Cerebrolysin preclinical papers include:
- **Rockenstein et al. 2014** (tauopathy/mitochondria model, double-transgenic Tau×GSK-3β mice): Retracted BMC Neuroscience December 2025 for duplicated/manipulated figure panels [6, animal; mouse, double-transgenic Tau×GSK-3β]
- **Rockenstein et al. 2015** (Pick's disease triple-repeat Tau model): Retracted BMC Neuroscience March 2025 (retraction PMID: 40065222) [7, animal; mouse, triple-repeat Tau transgenic]

**What this means for the preclinical record:** The anti-amyloid/anti-tau claims in Alzheimer's rodent models rest substantially on a literature cluster now under active integrity review or formally retracted. Non-integrity-flagged animal work (e.g., neurogenesis in ischemia models) remains in the literature but reflects a narrow methodological base (predominantly rat, embolic or MCA-occlusion stroke). Extrapolation from rodent ischemia to human neurodegenerative disease remains unvalidated.

## C.2 The Cochrane Verdicts — Independent Synthesis

### Acute Ischemic Stroke (Ziganshina et al. 2023)

The most recent Cochrane update (pub7) included **7 RCTs with 1,773 participants** [8, meta_analysis; tier 1]. Key findings:

- **All-cause death:** RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.65–1.41) — moderate-certainty evidence of "little to no difference." 53 deaths in 922 treated vs 47 in 767 placebo.
- **Death or dependency (poor functional outcome):** NOT REPORTED in any of the 7 included trials. Death or dependency is the prespecified primary endpoint in virtually every contemporary phase-III stroke RCT — its universal absence across a sponsor-concentrated literature raises a **selective-outcome-reporting** concern beyond a simple evidentiary gap. When the most clinically meaningful composite endpoint disappears uniformly from a trial set with heavy funder concentration, that pattern is consistent with selective non-publication or non-prespecification of unfavorable outcomes.
- **Non-fatal serious adverse events:** RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23) — a potential increase with Cerebrolysin (moderate certainty).
- **Quality of life, time to work restoration:** Not reported in any trial.

Risk-of-bias findings: allocation concealment unclear in 6/7 studies; high attrition bias in 4 studies (16–29% loss to follow-up); two studies used last-observation-carried-forward imputation.

**Authors' conclusion:** Cerebrolysin "probably has no beneficial effect on preventing all-cause death" and shows "a potential increase in non-fatal serious adverse events." Moderate-certainty evidence. No recommendation for use.

### Vascular Dementia (Cui et al. 2019)

Six RCTs, 597 participants [9, meta_analysis; tier 1]. Findings:

- **Cognitive function (ADAS-cog):** SMD 0.36 (95% CI 0.13–0.58) — statistically significant but **very low-quality evidence** due to serious risk of bias, indirectness, and imprecision. I² for ADAS-cog+ analyses = 86% (extreme heterogeneity).
- **Global function:** RR 2.69 (95% CI 1.82–3.98), two trials, 379 participants — again very low-quality evidence.
- Only one of six trials received a low-risk-of-bias rating. Four trials received pharmaceutical industry support from EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH.

**Authors' conclusion:** Results "not definitive"; studies "had several issues that may have led to misleading results." Not recommended for routine use.

## C.3 Sponsor Concentration and the Independent-vs-Affiliated Evidence Gap

### The Structural Problem

The Cerebrolysin clinical trial literature has a documented funder-concentration problem. EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH (manufacturer) has supported trials through direct grants, provision of study drug and placebo, randomization codes, and statisticians [8, meta_analysis]. The Cochrane acute-stroke review notes this directly as a source of potential bias. The meta-analysis by Bornstein et al. (EVER Pharma-involved) pooling 9 RCTs (N=1,879) found significant functional benefit at day 30 (NNT 7.7) [10, meta_analysis; tier 2] — a conclusion strikingly at odds with the Cochrane null finding from the overlapping trial set.

Key structural observations on the sponsor-affiliated literature:
1. **Positive meta-analyses** (Bornstein 2018 [10]; CARS pool [11, rct]) include trials where EVER Pharma provided drug, placebo, randomization codes, or statisticians — all non-independent inputs.
2. **Geographic concentration:** Trials are predominantly conducted in Russia, Eastern Europe, China, and other post-Soviet/Asian countries where the drug already has regulatory approval and physician familiarity, introducing selection effects. Western academic neurology has not conducted independent efficacy trials.
3. **Funnel plot interpretation is contested:** The Bornstein meta-analysis claims no publication bias tendency, yet identifies the CASTA trial (the largest trial with the least-documented manufacturer involvement, N=1,070; see below) as a negative outlier in the funnel plot — the pattern consistent with small-study/positive-study inflation.

### The CASTA Trial — The Largest Trial, Lowest-Disclosed Sponsor Role

The **Cerebrolysin Acute Stroke Treatment in Asia (CASTA)** trial is the largest randomized controlled trial of Cerebrolysin to date [12, rct; tier 2]. Key facts:

- N=1,070 (529 Cerebrolysin, 541 placebo), multicenter, double-blind
- Randomized within 12 hours of stroke onset; 30 mL Cerebrolysin IV daily × 10 days
- **Primary endpoint (combined global directional test of mRS, Barthel Index, NIHSS):** NO SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCE between groups in the overall population
- Published: *Stroke* 2012;43(3):630–636 (Heiss WD et al.; PMID: 22282884)
- Sponsor: Study design and funding details not prominently disclosed in the abstract; classified as "Non-US Gov't" support. The CASTA full text is not open-access; the abstract carries no funding statement. Critically, Norbert Bornstein — a co-investigator on CASTA and lead author of the EVER Pharma–involved Bornstein 2018 meta-analysis — is a documented EVER collaborator. Wolf-Dieter Heiss appears as co-author on both CASTA and the sponsor-adjacent Bornstein 2018 pooling exercise. EVER's exact role in CASTA (drug supply, placebo, randomization codes, statistics) cannot be confirmed or excluded from publicly available sources.
- Post-hoc subgroup (NIHSS >12): Favorable trend for Cerebrolysin (20.2% vs 10.5% cumulative 90-day mortality in placebo vs treated), but confidence intervals crossed unity; post-hoc, hypothesis-generating only

**The CASTA finding represents the lowest-disclosed sponsor involvement in the Cerebrolysin stroke literature:** a well-powered, multi-center, geographically diverse trial found no statistically significant primary endpoint benefit. It cannot be characterized as cleanly "independent" given the overlap between its co-investigators and documented EVER Pharma collaborators; it is better understood as the largest trial with the least-documented manufacturer involvement, and its null primary result stands as the most robust single-trial finding in the literature.

## C.4 The Undefined-Mechanism Critique

Cerebrolysin cannot be replicated by any other manufacturer, has no single molecular identifier, and its active peptide constituents — though partially characterized by NanoLC-MS mass spectrometry — have not been matched to known receptor targets. This means:
- Mechanism of action is inferred from downstream functional effects in animal models, not from receptor-binding studies of identified active peptides
- Batch-to-batch variability in the peptide profile cannot be excluded
- The persistent discordance between the short plasma half-life of individual peptide components and the extended biological effects observed is unexplained (active metabolites? Tissue sequestration? Downstream signaling cascade persistence?)

This is not inherently disqualifying — many approved drugs have partial mechanism understanding — but it substantially limits mechanistic interpretation of clinical results and makes any future structure-activity refinement difficult [1, mechanism_review].

## C.5 The Honest Verdict

Cerebrolysin presents a genuinely unusual evidentiary profile. The **preclinical mechanistic rationale** — that a low-molecular-weight peptide mixture could activate neurotrophic signaling pathways (Trk receptors, Shh, anti-apoptotic cascades) without containing intact neurotrophic proteins — is biologically plausible and supported by functional data in rodent ischemia models. But the preclinical literature supporting the most specific claims (amyloid/tau reduction in Alzheimer's models) is now substantially compromised by documented research misconduct and mass retractions.

The **clinical evidence** shows a persistent sponsor-affiliated positive signal in small-to-medium trials and meta-analyses of those trials, set against a null result from the largest trial with the least-documented manufacturer involvement (CASTA) and null-to-cautious conclusions from two independent Cochrane syntheses covering acute stroke and vascular dementia. The geographic concentration of positive trials (Russia, Eastern Europe, China) and the EVER Pharma funder-concentration problem make it difficult to separate real effect from reporting and selection biases. The universal absence of the death-or-dependency endpoint across all 7 Cochrane stroke trials — a prespecified primary in modern stroke RCTs — is a selective-outcome-reporting concern, not merely a gap.

The **realistic appraisal**: plausible neurotrophic-mimicry mechanism, preclinical evidence in ischemia models (noting integrity questions in the AD/tau literature), and a large but sponsor-concentrated, geographically clustered clinical literature whose Cochrane syntheses find no mortality benefit in stroke and only very-low-quality cognitive signals in vascular dementia. No adequately powered, fully independently funded Western trial exists. Routine clinical use is not supported by the current evidence base.
