# Section D — Safety / Regulatory / Monitoring

**Compound:** Cerebrolysin (porcine-brain peptide hydrolysate; EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH, Austria)
**Mode:** Deep | Rigor: High

---

## D.1 Regulatory State

### D.1.1 Approvals Outside the United States

Cerebrolysin holds national marketing authorizations in approximately 50 countries — including Austria (its country of origin and manufacture), Russia, China, South Korea, and Germany — for indications spanning acute ischemic stroke, traumatic brain injury, vascular dementia, and Alzheimer's-type dementia [1, regulatory]. In Russia and several post-Soviet states it is classified as standard-of-care for neurological conditions. Germany's national neurorehabilitation guidelines have included Cerebrolysin, and it is incorporated into TBI guidelines in certain Eastern European jurisdictions [1, regulatory].

These approvals were granted largely through national procedures — not through the EMA's centralized procedure. The EMA's **centralized marketing authorization** procedure (which yields a single EU-wide approval, mandatory for many novel biologics and new active substances) was not used for Cerebrolysin. In Austria and some other EU member states, authorization was obtained through **national** (or, where applicable, mutual recognition/decentralized) procedures, reflecting long clinical experience rather than a modern centralized dossier [1, regulatory; 2, regulatory]. The distinction matters: a national authorization in Austria or Germany is a legitimate, lawfully granted regulatory approval within EU pharmaceutical law, but it is not an EMA-central authorization and does not automatically apply across all EU member states.

**The practical picture:** Cerebrolysin is a lawfully authorized prescription medicine in a substantial majority of prescribing jurisdictions worldwide, but it has never undergone the regulatory review processes of the U.S. FDA or EMA's centralized procedure. International approval cannot be conflated with FDA or EMA-central approval. Regulatory decisions in Russia, China, and some Eastern European markets were made at points in time and under evidentiary standards that differ from contemporary FDA or EMA requirements [3, meta_analysis].

### D.1.2 United States and Non-Approving Countries

The FDA has not approved Cerebrolysin for any indication. It is not legally marketed or sold for human use in the United States, the United Kingdom, or Australia [1, regulatory]. In the US, import for personal human use risks seizure by FDA enforcement, and it has no IND (Investigational New Drug) designation clearing a clinical-development pathway in the US market. It is sold through some online vendors (including Russia-based resellers such as Rupharma) for research use, and a substantial nootropic/biohacker community self-administers it via grey-market injectables — an unregulated, clinician-unsupervised use pattern carrying contamination and dosing risks beyond those in pharmaceutical-grade controlled-trial settings [4, anecdote_aggregate].

### D.1.3 Manufacturer

EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH, Unterach am Attersee, Austria. Formerly marketed as Ebwe Pharma. Manufactures the pharmaceutical-grade preparation under Austrian/EU GMP standards; the proprietary enzymatic hydrolysis process and exact peptide fractionation are trade secrets. Composition analytics reveal that even products sold as "cerebroprotein hydrolysate" by other manufacturers are compositionally non-comparable to EVER's Cerebrolysin — only EVER's preparation has demonstrated neuronal differentiation bioactivity in independent laboratory comparisons [5, in_vitro].

## D.2 Safety Profile

### D.2.1 General Tolerability

> **Population mismatch — healthy adults:** All safety and tolerability data in this section derive from RCTs in **elderly disease populations** (acute ischemic stroke patients aged ~60–75 with high vascular comorbidity; dementia patients), administered Cerebrolysin IV in acute clinical settings under physician supervision. These populations differ fundamentally from the healthy-adult nootropic/grey-market users who constitute the wiki's primary audience. The extrapolation gap is unquantified: no controlled safety data exist in healthy adults. The tolerability characterization below applies only to the studied elderly disease populations and should not be assumed to transfer to healthy adults.

Across its clinical trial history — predominantly in stroke, TBI, and dementia populations — Cerebrolysin has been reported as well-tolerated **in these elderly disease populations**, with aggregate adverse event rates comparable to placebo in most analyses. A 2021 meta-analysis (Strilciuc et al.; n = 2,202; 12 RCTs, acute ischemic stroke) found overall AE rates of 43.8% (Cerebrolysin) vs. 43.6% (placebo; RR = 0.98), with non-fatal SAE rates of 3.8% vs. 3.0% — a non-statistically significant difference [6, meta_analysis]. Common reported non-serious adverse events include **agitation, insomnia, dizziness, sensation of heat/warmth, nausea, headache, tremor, and injection-site pain/swelling** [7, meta_analysis].

**Critical caveat:** The safety data base is dominated by sponsor-associated or sponsor-supported trials. The 2023 Cochrane review identified that three of seven included studies received manufacturer support from EVER Neuro Pharma — either totally, or through provision of Cerebrolysin, placebo, randomization codes, research grants, or statisticians — and rated two studies at high risk of bias and five at unclear risk on this basis [8, meta_analysis]. Independent, non-manufacturer-associated long-term safety data are sparse.

### D.2.2 The Non-Fatal Serious Adverse Event Signal (Cochrane 2023)

The most important safety signal in the evidence base comes from the 2023 Cochrane systematic review (Ziganshina et al., 7th update; 7 RCTs, n = 1,773, acute ischemic stroke). Despite finding no significant difference in total SAEs or fatal SAEs, the review found a **statistically significant increase in non-fatal serious adverse events** with Cerebrolysin:

- **Non-fatal SAE: RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23)** — 3 trials, 1,335 participants; moderate-certainty evidence [8, meta_analysis]
- **Dose-response signal:** At the 30 mL × 10-day regimen (cumulative 300 mL), the non-fatal SAE excess was more prominent: **RR 2.87 (95% CI 1.24–6.69)** — 2 trials, 1,189 participants [8, meta_analysis]

The Cochrane reviewers characterize this as moderate-certainty evidence (meaning the true effect may differ from the point estimate but the signal should not be dismissed). The nature of the non-fatal SAEs is not individually enumerated in the Cochrane summary beyond the aggregate count. Fatal SAE rates showed no difference (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.59–1.38), and all-cause mortality was similarly null (RR 0.96, 95% CI 0.65–1.41) [8, meta_analysis].

A separate manufacturer-independent post-hoc analysis of the CEREHETIS trial (Kalinin & Khasanova; stroke patients, 30 mL regimen, no manufacturer funding) found heterogeneous treatment effects with no excess serious adverse events in that specific dataset [9, cohort]. The mechanistic safety picture is therefore not fully settled; the Cochrane non-fatal SAE signal remains the most concerning aggregate finding to date.

An earlier safety-focused review (Thome J & Doppler E, 2012, PMID 22514795) examining dementia and stroke trial data reported adverse reactions as "generally mild and transient" **in those elderly disease populations**, with the most commonly named events being vertigo, agitation, and feeling hot, and with no major changes in vital signs or laboratory parameters [7, meta_analysis]. No safety data from healthy-adult populations were included.

### D.2.3 Hypersensitivity / Anaphylactoid Reactions

In very rare cases, hypersensitivity or allergic reactions have been observed, including: **skin reactions, local inflammatory reactions, headache, neck pain, limb pain, fever, low back pain, dyspnea, chills, and shock-like state** [11, regulatory]. These represent the most clinically serious acute risks of administration. Stopping the infusion immediately and managing anaphylaxis per standard protocol are the implied clinical responses; no specific antagonist exists.

### D.2.4 The Prion / TSE Theoretical Concern

Cerebrolysin is derived from porcine (pig) brain tissue via enzymatic hydrolysis. Brain tissue is the highest-risk anatomical compartment for prion contamination in any species. Two considerations frame the risk:

**Biological argument for concern:** Pigs are not naturally known to develop spontaneous transmissible spongiform encephalopathy (TSE), and no naturally occurring porcine spongiform encephalopathy is described. However, experimental parenteral inoculation of BSE prions has produced disease in pigs, with incubation periods of 69–150 weeks [10, animal], demonstrating that pigs are not absolutely refractory to prion infection. A brain-derived injectable product from any mammalian species therefore carries a theoretical TSE transmission risk that cannot be categorically dismissed.

**Manufacturer mitigation:** EVER Neuro Pharma manufactures under EU GMP standards. Pharmaceutical-grade Cerebrolysin is stored in NaOH-buffered solution, which is a recognized partial decontamination measure for suspected prion-containing material. Austrian and EU regulations governing products of animal origin require sourcing documentation and health screening. However, EVER Neuro Pharma's specific TSE screening protocols (herd-of-origin certification, ante-mortem/post-mortem surveillance, specific elimination steps) are not publicly detailed in peer-reviewed literature [10, animal; 1, regulatory].

**Honest framing:** The prion risk is theoretical and regulatory in character — no human case of prion disease attributable to Cerebrolysin has been documented. It is nonetheless a legitimate regulatory consideration (particularly in countries like the US and UK that apply precautionary policies to bovine- and porcine-CNS-derived biologics). Grey-market or counterfeit Cerebrolysin from non-GMP sources carries a substantially higher contamination risk than pharmaceutical-grade product [5, in_vitro].

## D.3 Contraindications, Monitoring, and Stopping Criteria

### D.3.1 Labeled Contraindications

Based on available prescribing information (Austrian SmPC/product information; regional equivalents) [11, regulatory]:

1. **Known hypersensitivity** to any component of Cerebrolysin (including porcine-derived products)
2. **Epilepsy / status epilepticus / grand mal convulsions** — an increase in seizure frequency has been observed; use is contraindicated in active seizure disorders and especially status epilepticus
3. **Severe renal impairment** — low-molecular-weight peptide constituents may accumulate; this population has been excluded from clinical trials
4. **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — use is cautioned or contraindicated due to the absence of reproductive safety data; animal studies showed no teratogenicity but clinical data in humans are absent

### D.3.2 Clinician-Administered Drug: Monitoring Context

> **Population mismatch — healthy adults:** The contraindications, monitoring parameters, and stopping criteria in D.3 are derived from labeling and RCT data in stroke/dementia populations (see D.2.1 callout). They represent the minimum known risk surface, not a complete safety picture for healthy-adult off-label use, for which no controlled data exist.

Cerebrolysin is a prescription, clinician-administered IV (intravenous) or IM (intramuscular) drug in all approving jurisdictions. It is not an over-the-counter product. Standard monitoring considerations include:

- **During infusion:** Clinical observation for hypersensitivity reactions (skin changes, dyspnea, chills, hemodynamic instability) — particularly in the first 20–60 minutes of each infusion cycle. Infusion rate should be controlled (minimum 20–60 minutes for IV administration) to reduce the warmth/heat sensation signal [11, regulatory]
- **Pre-treatment baseline:** Renal function assessment (to exclude severe impairment); seizure history review (to exclude epilepsy contraindication); allergy history (especially porcine-product hypersensitivity)
- **Efficacy monitoring:** There is no validated laboratory biomarker for Cerebrolysin's neurotrophic effect. Outcome monitoring is functional-scale based (e.g., NIHSS, mRS, Barthel Index in stroke; ADAS-Cog in dementia). No surrogate biochemical endpoint guides dosing or continuation decisions [3, meta_analysis]
- **Drug interactions:** Avoid concurrent use with MAO inhibitors. Caution with antidepressants [11, regulatory]

### D.3.3 Stopping Criteria

- **Immediate cessation:** Any sign of hypersensitivity/anaphylactoid reaction during infusion (skin reactions, dyspnea, chills, hemodynamic instability)
- **Reassess:** New-onset seizures or increased seizure frequency during a treatment course
- **Escalating non-fatal SAEs:** Given the Cochrane dose-response signal at higher cumulative doses, any unexpected serious non-fatal events during a course warrant reassessment of benefit-risk

## D.4 WADA Status

**Cerebrolysin is not explicitly named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List.**

The relevant analytical framework:

**S0 (Non-Approved Substances):** WADA defines S0 as "Any pharmacological substance which is not addressed by any of the subsequent sections of the List and with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use" [2, regulatory]. Critically, because Cerebrolysin holds national marketing authorizations in Russia, Austria, China, South Korea, and approximately 45 other countries, it does have "current approval by governmental regulatory health authorities" — and therefore the S0 catch-all, as currently written, does not straightforwardly apply to it. This distinguishes Cerebrolysin from substances like BPC-157 (no national approval anywhere), which fall cleanly into S0 [2, regulatory].

**S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics):** WADA's S2 category prohibits specific growth factors and their mimetics — primarily those affecting muscle, tendon, and vascularization (e.g., IGF-1, FGFs, HGF, PDGF, VEGF). Neurotrophic growth factors (BDNF, NGF) are not explicitly listed in S2's enumerated substances in available search of the 2025/2026 list language. Cerebrolysin's claimed mechanism involves upregulation of endogenous BDNF, NGF, and other neurotrophins, but it is a heterogeneous porcine-brain peptide hydrolysate, not an exogenous recombinant growth factor — a categorical distinction from the proteins S2 contemplates [2, regulatory].

**Honest bottom line:** No authoritative WADA determination or GlobalDRO classification of Cerebrolysin as prohibited has been identified in the course of this research. Current evidence supports the assessment that Cerebrolysin is **likely not prohibited** under the 2026 WADA Prohibited List as written — it is approved in multiple jurisdictions (excluding it from S0 as written), and it is not a named S2 growth factor or mimetic. However, this is a reasoned inference from WADA's published list language; no explicit WADA ruling or official GlobalDRO check result for Cerebrolysin in a US, UK, or Canadian context has been confirmed. Athletes requiring a definitive ruling must check GlobalDRO.com for their sport and jurisdiction and/or submit a Therapeutic Use Exemption inquiry [2, regulatory]. This conclusion should NOT be read as a clearance — it is an honest statement of what the public list language shows.
