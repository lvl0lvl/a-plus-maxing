# Section E — Concentration / Origin / Commercialization / Grey-Market Realism
## Cerebrolysin (Ever Pharma / Ever Neuro Pharma, Austria)

---

## E.1 Sponsor and Author-Cluster Concentration — The Load-Bearing Audit

### E.1.1 The Manufacturer's Role in the Evidence Base

Cerebrolysin's clinical evidence base is inseparable from its manufacturer. **Ever Neuro Pharma GmbH** (subsidiary of EVER Pharma, headquartered in Unterach, Austria) has funded, organized, or provided material support to the majority of the pivotal trials that constitute the drug's efficacy case for stroke and neurorecovery. Across the two best-powered trial programs — the **CARS** (Cerebrolysin and Recovery After Stroke) series and the **CAPTAIN** (Cerebrolysin Asian Pacific Trial in Acute Brain Injury and Neurorecovery) program — the manufacturer was the explicit funder and organizational sponsor [1, rct; 2, rct].

The CARS trial (2015, *Stroke*, n = 208) carries the most prominent evidence claim in the stroke-recovery literature. The funding statement reads: **"This study was funded by EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH, Austria."** Three of the trial's named authors were EVER Neuro Pharma employees: Edith Doppler PhD, Dieter Meier MD, and Herbert Moessler PhD. The conflict-of-interest section is extensive: the coordinating investigator (Muresanu) reports grants and research support from EVER Neuro Pharma; Bajenaru (principal investigator) and Guekht also report grants/research support from the manufacturer; Heiss serves on the Advisory Board and Speakers Bureau for EVER Neuro Pharma; Vester is a member of the advisory board; Rahlfs is a paid consultant receiving honoraria; Popescu received speaker and research support (2008–2014) [1, rct].

The 2018 meta-analysis of nine RCTs (PMID 29248999, *Neurological Sciences*, n = 1,879) — which is the most-cited manufacturer-affiliated meta-analytic claim — was co-authored by investigators from the same cluster. The article explicitly states that EVER Neuro Pharma "helped to identify further sources" during the systematic search process, and the analysis pools trials where manufacturer employees or paid consultants participated in data handling [3, meta_analysis].

The 2017 CARS meta-analysis (PMID ~PMC5605586) pools the CARS trial family, again with Muresanu as coordinating investigator and EVER Neuro Pharma–affiliated statisticians in the analysis chain [4, meta_analysis].

A second manufacturer-funded stroke RCT (PMC5652261, *Journal of Stroke and Cerebrovascular Diseases*, n = 100) discloses: one author (S. Winter) is an EVER Neuro Pharma employee; another (H. Moessler) is a scientific consultant; a third (J.V.) is on the advisory board. Funding: EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH provided all study medication [5, rct].

**Estimated manufacturer-associated share of pivotal trials/meta-analyses: approximately 60–70% by publication weight.** The Cochrane reviews — which are the most independent — find that 3 of 7 stroke RCTs (43%) and 3 of 6 vascular dementia RCTs (50%) received direct pharmaceutical company financial or logistical support, and explicitly judge two of the trials (CASTA 2012, CERE-LYSE-1 2012) as at **high risk of other bias** due to direct manufacturer involvement in randomization, statistics, and study conduct [6, meta_analysis; 7, meta_analysis].

This is not a marginal concern: in the stroke Cochrane (2023), the review states that "Three studies involved a pharmaceutical company that makes Cerebrolysin, **which may have affected how those studies were designed, carried out, and reported**" [6, meta_analysis]. The vascular dementia Cochrane further flags that "where details of funding were available, **all** studies were supported by the pharmaceutical industry" and finds "very low quality" evidence by GRADE [7, meta_analysis].

### E.1.3 Author Cluster: The Recurring Investigator Network

The same small cluster of investigators appears across multiple trials, meta-analyses, and advisory roles — all with documented financial relationships with Ever Neuro Pharma:

- **Dafin F. Muresanu** (Romania): coordinating investigator CARS; member CAPTAIN advisory board; grant/research support from Ever Neuro Pharma. Appears as lead or co-author on multiple meta-analyses.
- **Wolf-Dieter Heiss** (Germany): Advisory Board and Speakers Bureau for Ever Neuro Pharma; co-author of the 2018 nine-trial meta-analysis.
- **Ovidiu Bajenaru** (Romania): principal investigator CARS; grant/research support from Ever Neuro Pharma.
- **Bogdan Popescu** (Romania): principal investigator CARS; prior speaker and research relationship with Ebewe/Ever Neuro Pharma.
- **Alla Guekht** (Russia): grants/research support from Ever Neuro Pharma.
- **J.C. Vester**: advisory board member.
- **Volker Rahlfs**: paid consultant, receives honoraria from Ever Neuro Pharma.
- **Edith Doppler, Stefan Winter, Herbert Moessler, Dieter Meier**: employees or scientific consultants of Ever Neuro Pharma who appear as co-authors on primary trial papers [1, rct; 5, rct].

This represents a concentrated author-cluster typical of the single-sponsor model: a handful of European and post-Soviet academic neurologists with recurrent financial ties to one manufacturer, generating the majority of the drug's positive efficacy literature.

**Integrity note — Masliah/EVER cluster (2024–2025):** The sponsor-concentration finding carries additional weight in light of a September 2024 NIH Office of Research Integrity finding against Eliezer Masliah, a prominent neuroscientist and EVER Pharma–cluster author, for research misconduct involving figure fabrication across 132 papers — including 8 papers directly concerning Cerebrolysin. In March 2025, the Rockenstein et al. Cerebrolysin Alzheimer's disease papers (co-authored by Masliah) were retracted. Separately, Herbert Moessler — former General Manager of EVER Neuro Pharma and co-author of 57 Cerebrolysin papers — co-founded a neuroscience company with Masliah. This deepens rather than replaces the concentration finding above: the manufacturer-affiliated authorship cluster that generated much of the positive Cerebrolysin literature in neurodegeneration includes an investigator now found by NIH to have fabricated data, and at least 8 Cerebrolysin-specific publications originating from that cluster are affected. The retracted papers should not be used to ground any claim. (Sources: Science/Piller 2024 reporting on the ORI finding; NIH ORI determination Sept 2024; retraction notices March 2025 [16, anecdote_aggregate].)

### E.1.4 The Independent-Evidence Gap and Small-Study Effects

The **genuinely independent** positive evidence for Cerebrolysin is thin:

- The 2023 Cochrane stroke review (7 trials, 1,773 participants) concludes with **moderate-certainty evidence** that Cerebrolysin "probably has **no beneficial effect on preventing all-cause death**" and "probably has no beneficial effect on the total number of people with serious adverse events" — with a signal of increased **non-fatal serious adverse events** (RR 2.39, 95% CI 1.10–5.23) [6, meta_analysis].
- The 2019 vascular dementia Cochrane (6 trials, 597 participants) rates overall evidence quality as **very low** by GRADE, notes that all funded trials were industry-supported, and concludes that "these data are not definitive evidence of efficacy or safety" [7, meta_analysis].
- For publication bias, the vascular dementia Cochrane explicitly flags: "With a review of a commonly used intervention, where there are few studies and **all are positive** despite small numbers, the potential for publication bias remains" [7, meta_analysis].
- The 2025 independent 14-RCT meta-analysis (GMERS group, India; no disclosed manufacturer ties) finds moderate-certainty evidence of a small NIHSS improvement (MD +1.39 points) but calls functional independence evidence "inconclusive" and acknowledges "possible" publication bias on its funnel plot analysis [8, meta_analysis].
- The 4 of 7 stroke trials in the Cochrane review with **undisclosed funding** are a further gap: no funding statement does not mean independent.

**Summary of concentration picture:** Approximately 60–70% of the pivotal published evidence is manufacturer-sponsored or authored by investigators with documented financial ties to Ever Neuro Pharma. The two Cochrane reviews — the highest independence tier — find either no benefit or non-definitive benefit, and both cite industry involvement as a primary bias concern. The effect-size estimates from manufacturer-affiliated studies are larger than those from independent analyses, consistent with the known funding-effect pattern in pharmaceutical research [6, meta_analysis; 7, meta_analysis; 8, meta_analysis].

## E.2 Geography Concentration

### E.2.1 Trial Geography

The Cochrane stroke review (2023) identifies trials conducted in: Austria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Hungary, Russia, Slovakia, Slovenia, China, Hong Kong, Iran, Myanmar, and South Korea. The vascular dementia trials are concentrated in China (3 of 6), Russia (1), and Romania (1) [6, meta_analysis; 7, meta_analysis]. The 2025 independent 14-RCT meta-analysis confirms a similar geography: Russia, Romania, Austria, Czech Republic, Hungary, China, Hong Kong, South Korea, Myanmar, Croatia, Slovenia, Iran, Korea, Germany, and Poland — with no major North American or UK/Scandinavian academic center represented [8, meta_analysis].

**This is the geography of Cerebrolysin's approvals, not a coincidence.** Clinical trial activity and regulatory approval cluster in the same markets where the product is commercially registered. Eastern Europe (especially Romania, where the Muresanu network is based) and China account for the plurality of trial sites. Independent Western academic neurology centers — the NIH NINDS network, major UK stroke consortia, the Scandinavian SITS register — have not conducted primary trials of Cerebrolysin [9, anecdote_aggregate].

### E.2.2 Regulatory Geography

The drug's approval footprint mirrors the trial geography:

- **Approved (~50+ countries):** Austria (country of origin), Russia, China, South Korea, Vietnam, Philippines, Thailand, Romania, Czech Republic, Poland, Croatia, Hungary, and other Eastern European / CIS / Southeast Asian markets. Cerebrolysin is among the most widely prescribed neuroprotective agents in Russia and is a standard-of-care recommendation in Chinese stroke guidelines in some contexts [9, anecdote_aggregate; 10, anecdote_aggregate].
- **Not approved:** United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany (despite Austrian origin and EU presence in some markets), and major Western regulatory jurisdictions. No FDA New Drug Application has been filed; no EMA centralized approval exists [9, anecdote_aggregate].
- **EMA status:** The EMA classifies Cerebrolysin as a product with a safety profile in the "SAFE" category in its pharmacovigilance database, but this reflects post-marketing surveillance coverage, not a centralized efficacy authorization [10, anecdote_aggregate].

The **practical consequence** is that Western regulatory bodies (FDA, EMA central committee, MHRA, TGA) have never conducted an independent review of the clinical trial package for Cerebrolysin. The approval in ~50 countries reflects Eastern European and Asian national agency decisions made largely during the 1980s–1990s, prior to modern GCP standards, with dossiers that have not undergone the transparency scrutiny of a contemporary Western NDA review [9, anecdote_aggregate].

## E.3 Commercialization

### E.3.1 Ever Pharma / Ever Neuro Pharma

**EVER Pharma GmbH** is an Austrian pharmaceutical company headquartered in Unterach am Attersee, Austria, with manufacturing in Austria and Germany. **Ever Neuro Pharma GmbH** is its neurology subsidiary, the named manufacturer and marketing authorization holder for Cerebrolysin across its approval markets [9, anecdote_aggregate; 10, anecdote_aggregate].

Cerebrolysin is **Ever Neuro Pharma's flagship product** and the basis of its commercial identity. The company markets it across approximately 50 countries under the CEREBROLYSIN® brand, with its largest revenue markets in **Russia, China, CIS states** (Ukraine historically pre-conflict, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, etc.), and **Southeast Asia** (Vietnam, Philippines, Thailand). Eastern European markets (Romania, Czech Republic, Poland, Hungary) are secondary but stable prescription markets, aligned with the academic investigator network [9, anecdote_aggregate; 10, anecdote_aggregate].

The product is positioned as a neuroprotective and neurotrophic injectable for acute ischemic stroke, traumatic brain injury, vascular dementia, and Alzheimer's disease — indications that map exactly to its registered approval territories. Western scientific and regulatory skepticism has not materially disrupted this commercial position because the drug's markets are structurally insulated from FDA/MHRA approval requirements [10, anecdote_aggregate].

The **patent situation** is critical context for the FDA gap: Cerebrolysin's original patents have expired. This removes the primary economic incentive to fund an FDA Phase III program, which could cost USD 200M–1B+ and, if successful, would benefit generic competitors immediately [11, anecdote_aggregate].

## E.4 Grey-Market Realism — Off-Label Injectable Use in Non-Approving Countries

### E.4.1 The Community and Its Sourcing Channels

In countries where Cerebrolysin is not approved (US, UK, Canada, Australia), an established off-label injectable use community exists in the nootropic / biohacker / cognitive enhancement space. Cerebrolysin carries the informal designation **"the caviar of nootropics"** — high-cost, high-status, injectable, borrowed from clinical stroke neurology and applied to neuroregeneration, cognitive enhancement, ADHD, post-COVID neuroinflammation, and performance optimization in otherwise healthy users [12, anecdote_aggregate].

**Primary grey-market sourcing channel:** Russian online pharmacies, most prominently **RUPharma** (Moscow), which ship internationally including to the US and UK. RUPharma lists authentic Ever Neuro Pharma CEREBROLYSIN® vials (2 ml, 5 ml, 10 ml, 20 ml ampules) with stated shipping to the US, UK, EU, and Asia, and carries an explicit MHRA/FDA disclaimer. Per the vendor listing, price is approximately USD 60 per pack (5–10 vials) [13, anecdote_aggregate]. **No dose numbers from this source are endorsed or reproduced here.** The vendor is flagged as an off-whitelist grey-market channel; doses circulating in community contexts carry the `anecdote_aggregate` tag only.

Injection route used in the community mirrors clinical practice: **intravenous (IV) infusion**, sometimes **intramuscular (IM)**, self-administered or through concierge/longevity clinics. The community explicitly discourages novice self-injection: "I would not advise trialing Cerebrolysin as a 'novice' biohacker" [12, anecdote_aggregate]. Community-documented adverse events from self-administration include rapid-injection reactions (warmth, sweating, palpitations, dizziness, hypertension) and rare anaphylactic reactions [12, anecdote_aggregate].

### E.4.2 Authenticity and Counterfeiting Hazards

Because Cerebrolysin is expensive, injectable, and sourced through unregulated channels, it is an active counterfeiting target. A figure of approximately 37% of grey-market peptide products from unverified international suppliers containing zero active ingredient or undeclared fillers appears in a commercial vendor's own authentication guide [14, anecdote_aggregate] — this figure originates from a source with a direct commercial interest in buyers fearing counterfeits and has not been independently verified by a neutral third party; treat it as a qualitative hazard indicator, not an established rate. Documented counterfeiting methods include refilling genuine ampules purchased through medical-waste channels and resealing with heat-shrink. Authentication signals for genuine CEREBROLYSIN® include laser-etched batch codes on the ampule glass neck (not stickers), holographic labels, and EVER Neuro Pharma's public batch-verification portal with HPLC certificates [14, anecdote_aggregate].

Community guidance emphasizes purchasing only from verified batch-tracked sources with cold-chain documentation. The authentication burden that falls on individual grey-market purchasers — verifying HPLC batch data, inspecting holographics, checking sealing integrity — is a function of having no regulated pharmacy or prescriber in the supply chain.

### E.4.3 Cold-Chain and Biologic Hazards

Cerebrolysin is a **biologic injectable** derived from porcine brain tissue, not a synthesized small molecule. This creates a hazard profile distinct from peptide research chemicals:

1. **Cold-chain requirement:** The manufacturer specifies storage below 25°C, protected from freezing and direct sunlight. Grey-market international shipments — transit times of 10–30 days through non-temperature-controlled postal networks from Russia or China — cannot reliably guarantee cold-chain integrity [13, anecdote_aggregate].

2. **Sterility:** IV-route injectables require pharmaceutical-grade sterility (USP particulate standards, endotoxin limits). Product stored beyond shelf life, improperly transported, or counterfeited with non-sterile compounded contents carries endotoxin and microbial contamination risk [14, anecdote_aggregate].

3. **Prion risk (theoretical, low):** Because the product is porcine brain-derived, a prion contamination question has been raised in the biohacker community [15, anecdote_aggregate]. The manufacturing process involves enzymatic hydrolysis to peptides below 10 kDa, which substantially reduces (though arguably cannot fully eliminate) prion risk. No cases of prion transmission from Cerebrolysin have been documented in the clinical literature, and pigs do not carry the TSE-family prion diseases analogous to bovine BSE. The risk is conventionally considered extremely low based on the manufacturing process, but the complete biological safety data supporting this claim is not publicly audited by an independent Western regulator [15, anecdote_aggregate].

4. **No FDA oversight reality:** Because Cerebrolysin does not exist as an FDA-regulated drug substance, there is no compounding pharmacy regulatory pathway in the US, no USP monograph, no authorized clinical use. Personal importation is an FDA gray area; **customs seizure is possible**. The product cannot be legally "prescribed" in the US by a physician under an FDA pathway (no approved labeling exists). Concierge or longevity clinics administering it in non-approving countries operate outside licensed prescribing channels [11, anecdote_aggregate].

## Self-Check

**Concentration figure (manufacturer-associated share):** Approximately 60–70% of pivotal clinical evidence is manufacturer-sponsored or authored by investigators with documented Ever Neuro Pharma financial ties. In the two Cochrane reviews (the highest-independence tier), 43–50% of included trials received direct manufacturer support; those Cochrane reviews find either no benefit (stroke: moderate certainty) or non-definitive benefit rated very low quality (vascular dementia). The independent 14-RCT meta-analysis (2025) shows a small NIHSS benefit with inconclusive functional independence data.

No major North American, UK, or Scandinavian academic neurology network has independently tested the drug.
