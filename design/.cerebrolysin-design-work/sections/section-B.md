# Section B — Clinical Efficacy: Indications and Evidence Verdict

## Overview

Cerebrolysin (Ever Pharma / EVER Neuro Pharma, formerly EBEWE Pharmaceutical) has been studied across four primary indication clusters: acute ischemic stroke, Alzheimer's disease (AD), vascular dementia (VaD), and traumatic brain injury (TBI). A large body of positive RCT data exists — predominantly from sponsor-affiliated investigators and concentrated in Russia, China, and Eastern Europe — yet independent Cochrane synthesis has repeatedly returned null or very-low-certainty verdicts on the most clinically important endpoints. That tension is the load-bearing fact of this section.

---

## B.1 Acute Ischemic Stroke

### Evidence base

The stroke indication carries the largest RCT literature and the most rigorous independent scrutiny.

**CASTA (Cerebrolysin Acute Stroke Treatment in Asia)** is the pivotal industry trial [1, rct]. Heiss et al. (2012) enrolled 1,070 patients with acute hemispheric ischemic stroke at >50 Asian centers; participants were randomized within 12 hours to 30 mL Cerebrolysin IV daily × 10 days vs. placebo plus aspirin [1, rct]. The confirmatory primary endpoint — a global directional test combining mRS, Barthel Index, and NIHSS at 90 days — showed no significant difference between groups [1, rct]. A pre-specified post-hoc subgroup analysis in patients with severe stroke (NIHSS >12) reported a favorable trend toward Cerebrolysin (NIHSS OR 1.27; mRS OR 1.27; 90-day mortality 10.5% vs. 20.2%; HR ~1.97), which the authors acknowledged required confirmation [1, rct].

**CARS (Cerebrolysin and Recovery After Stroke)** is a smaller multicenter RCT by Mureșanu et al. (2016), enrolling early post-stroke rehabilitation patients 24–72 hours after onset [2, rct]. The mITT-LOCF analysis population at day 90 comprised 205 patients (Cerebrolysin n=104; placebo n=101); total enrolled is not reported in the published abstract. Participants received 30 mL Cerebrolysin IV daily × 21 days alongside standard rehabilitation. The primary outcome — Action Research Arm Test (ARAT) at day 90 — showed large, statistically significant superiority for Cerebrolysin (Mann-Whitney estimator 0.71; 95% CI 0.63–0.79; p < 0.0001) [2, rct]. A composite global outcome across 12 scales showed small-to-medium superiority (MW 0.62; 95% CI 0.58–0.65; p < 0.0001). The authors noted the trial was exploratory in size and required replication in a larger confirmatory study [2, rct].

### Cochrane verdict (PRIMARY FINDING — stated prominently)

The Cochrane review on Cerebrolysin for acute ischemic stroke has been updated serially since 2010, with the most recent version published in October 2023 by Ziganshina et al. [3, meta_analysis]. The 2023 update included 7 RCTs (6 Cerebrolysin + 1 Cortexin) totaling 1,773 participants [3, meta_analysis].

**The Cochrane conclusions are null and, on one key outcome, signal potential harm:**

- **All-cause death:** "Cerebrolysin or Cortexin probably result in little to no difference in all-cause death" — RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.65–1.41); **moderate-certainty evidence** [3, meta_analysis].
- **Total serious adverse events (SAEs):** "Cerebrolysin probably results in little to no difference in total SAEs" — RR 1.16 (95% CI 0.81–1.66); **moderate-certainty** [3, meta_analysis].
- **Non-fatal SAEs — the safety signal:** Cerebrolysin showed a **statistically significant increase in non-fatal serious adverse events** — RR 2.39 (95% CI 1.10–5.23); the higher 30 mL/10-day dose drove a stronger signal (RR 2.87; 95% CI 1.24–6.69) [3, meta_analysis].
- **Total adverse events:** RR 1.03 (95% CI not significant); **low-certainty** [3, meta_analysis].

Earlier Cochrane iterations reached similar null conclusions. The 2010 version (PMID 20393954) stated "there is not enough evidence to evaluate the effect" [4, meta_analysis]. The series as a whole consistently finds no demonstrated benefit on clinically meaningful outcomes (death or dependency) [3, 4, meta_analysis].

### Geography and sponsor concentration

The Cochrane reviewers noted that Cerebrolysin use and the trials supporting it are overwhelmingly concentrated in Russia, China, Eastern Europe, and post-Soviet and Asian countries [3, meta_analysis]. The CASTA trial was funded with manufacturer involvement; lead authors of the 2022 narrative review (Mureșanu, Brainin) disclosed direct financial relationships with EVER Neuro Pharma as speakers, consultants, and principal investigators in manufacturer-funded trials [5, mechanism_review]. The independent Cochrane synthesis, which applied a systematic search including Russian-language databases, returned the null verdict despite this positive-trial corpus — the classic pattern of sponsor-associated heterogeneity.

## B.2 Alzheimer's Disease

### RCT and meta-analytic signal

Cerebrolysin has been evaluated in mild-to-moderate AD in a series of RCTs, all using 30 mL/day IV for four-week courses. The manufacturer-associated meta-analysis by Gauthier et al. (2015) pooled six double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs using individual patient data [6, meta_analysis]. Key findings:

- **Cognitive function at 4 weeks:** statistically significant superiority over placebo (SMD −0.40; p = 0.0031), though the 6-month cognitive difference was not significant [6, meta_analysis].
- **Global clinical change:** significant at both 4 weeks (OR 3.32; p = 0.0212) and 6 months (OR 4.98; p = 0.0150) [6, meta_analysis].
- **Combined global benefit (Mann-Whitney):** significant at 4 weeks and 6 months (p = 0.0006 and p = 0.0010) [6, meta_analysis].

The earlier meta-analysis by Wei et al. (2007) — including six double-blind placebo-controlled trials — found a significant improvement in Clinical Global Impression (log OR 1.18; 95% CI 0.75–1.61; p < 0.05), but concluded that "more convincing evidence is needed for the efficacy of Cerebrolysin on cognitive performance" [7, meta_analysis]. The investigators communicated directly with EBEWE Pharmaceutical (the then-manufacturer) during the study [7, meta_analysis].

### Evidence quality caveats

Both meta-analyses have manufacturer involvement in design or data access, small component trial sizes, and short treatment/follow-up windows (4–6 weeks to 6 months). No independent Cochrane review specifically covering AD monotherapy with Cerebrolysin has reached a strong positive verdict. A 2025 umbrella systematic review on vascular cognitive impairment (Masserini et al.) found "small to moderate improvements in cognition" for Cerebrolysin alongside several established agents, consistent with a modest positive signal that does not reach the certainty level of approved AD therapies [8, meta_analysis].

## B.3 Vascular Dementia

The 2019 Cochrane review by Cui, Chen, Yang et al. (PMID 31710397) is the primary independent synthesis for VaD [9, meta_analysis]. It included 6 RCTs with 597 participants total.

**Findings:**
- **Cognitive outcomes:** Three trials (MMSE/ADAS-cog+) showed statistically significant benefit — SMD 0.36 (95% CI 0.13–0.58) — but graded **very low-quality evidence** [9, meta_analysis].
- **Global function/responder rates:** Two trials (n=379) showed improvement — RR 2.69 (95% CI 1.82–3.98) — but again rated **very low-quality evidence** [9, meta_analysis].
- **Adverse events:** Two trials (n=379) found no difference in adverse event rates — RR 0.91 (95% CI 0.29–2.85); **very low-quality evidence** [9, meta_analysis].

**Authors' conclusions:** While intravenous Cerebrolysin showed improvement in cognition and global function, the data are "not definitive" due to substantial heterogeneity and high risk of bias. The evidence base is described as "weak." Adequately powered, methodologically robust RCTs are needed [9, meta_analysis].

The **very low GRADE** ratings across all outcomes mean the true effect could be substantially different from — or opposite to — the estimated effect.

## B.4 Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)

### CAPTAIN trial program

The CAPTAIN (Cerebrolysin and Recovery After TBI) program comprises two phase IIIb/IV trials. Both were conducted in Asian-Pacific centers with manufacturer-associated authorship.

**CAPTAIN I** (Poon et al., 2020; PMID 31494820): n=46 (small); moderate-to-severe TBI [10, rct]. The primary multivariate analysis narrowly missed significance in the ITT population; the per-protocol analysis showed significant superiority across cognitive domains (Color Trails 1 and 2, Stroop). Safety comparable to placebo [10, rct].

**CAPTAIN II** (Muresanu et al., 2020; PMID 31897941): n=142 (139 analyzed); adults with moderate-severe TBI (GCS 7–12) [11, rct]. Dosing: 50 mL Cerebrolysin IV daily × 10 days, then two additional 10-day cycles at 10 mL/day. Primary outcome: multidimensional ensemble of 13 outcome scales at day 90. **Result: MWcombined = 0.59 (95% CI 0.52–0.66; p = 0.0119)** — a small-to-medium statistically significant effect [11, rct]. Safety and tolerability were comparable between groups [11, rct].

**CAPTAIN prospective meta-analysis** (Vester et al., 2021; PMID 33620612) pooled CAPTAIN I + II (n=185; mean admission GCS 10.3) [12, meta_analysis]. Results at both day 30 and day 90 reached significance with small-to-medium effect sizes (Day 90: MWcombined = 0.60; 95% CI 0.52–0.68; p = 0.0146; SMD = 0.34; OR = 1.77) [12, meta_analysis]. Authors described the findings as "confirming safety and efficacy" and opening "a new horizon for neurorecovery" [12, meta_analysis].

### TBI evidence quality

The CAPTAIN program is small (combined n=185 across both RCTs), without an independent replicated trial, and authored by investigators with manufacturer ties. No independent Cochrane review specific to TBI with Cerebrolysin currently exists. The effect sizes, while statistically significant in the meta-analysis, are small-to-medium and based on composite multidimensional scoring rather than a single pre-specified clinical outcome (e.g., GOS-E). The heterogeneous outcome ensemble, the small total sample, and the undisclosed CAPTAIN I ITT miss collectively limit confidence.

## B.5 Vascular Cognitive Impairment and Other Uses

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis on vascular cognitive impairment (Masserini et al.; PMID 41198594) found "small to moderate improvements in cognition" for Cerebrolysin alongside acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, memantine, and other agents [8, meta_analysis]. Individual early RCTs in hypertension-associated mild cognitive impairment (n=40; PMID 18379494) and a 2001 double-blind trial in arterial hypertension/atherosclerosis (PMID 11494441) showed favorable cognitive trends at 28-day courses, both underpowered [not separately cited — evidence quality too low for standalone bib entry].

**Pediatric / developmental uses:** No adequately powered or indexed RCTs were identified in pediatric populations (autism, cerebral palsy, developmental disability) in the English-language or major indexed literature. Claimed uses in these populations lack a credible evidence base.

## B.6 The Load-Bearing Tension

The Cerebrolysin evidence base presents a near-canonical case of **sponsor-associated positive-trial heterogeneity vs. independent null/cautious synthesis**:

| Feature | Manufacturer-associated trials | Independent Cochrane reviews |
|---|---|---|
| Stroke (death/dependency) | Positive subgroup trends (CASTA severe stroke; CARS motor) | NULL (GRADE moderate) — no benefit on death or dependency [3] |
| Stroke (SAE) | Generally "comparable to placebo" reported | Significant increase in non-fatal SAEs (RR 2.39) [3] |
| Vascular dementia | Positive cognitive/global signal | Signal present but ALL outcomes graded VERY LOW certainty [9] |
| TBI (CAPTAIN) | Small-to-medium significant effect (meta-analysis) | No independent Cochrane review exists |
| AD | Significant global impression; cognitive signal weaker | No independent Cochrane; manufacturer-meta dominate |

Three structural concerns apply across the entire evidence base:

1. **Sponsor concentration.** EVER Neuro Pharma (and predecessor EBEWE) is directly or indirectly associated with most positive RCTs and the meta-analyses showing benefit. Lead investigator disclosures in the 2022 stroke review (Mureșanu, Brainin) explicitly list speaking fees, consultancies, and trial PI roles for the manufacturer [5, mechanism_review].

2. **Geographic concentration.** Trials cluster heavily in Russia, Eastern Europe, and China — regions with regulatory environments and publication channels less subject to independent replication pressure. Cochrane searches explicitly included Russian-language databases and still returned null verdicts on primary outcomes [3, meta_analysis].

3. **Heterogeneity and small-study effects.** The positive meta-analytic signals (especially in VaD and AD) arise from few small trials with high heterogeneity, graded very low certainty by GRADE. Small-study effects in such an environment are a well-recognized source of overestimated efficacy.

The honest bottom line: Cerebrolysin does not have an independent, large-scale, replication-verified RCT demonstrating benefit on a clinically meaningful primary endpoint (death, disability, dependency) for any indication. The one large placebo-controlled stroke trial (CASTA, n=1,070) was null on its primary endpoint. Independent Cochrane synthesis across stroke and VaD finds no proven benefit and, for stroke, flags a potential non-fatal SAE signal at the standard 30 mL dose.
