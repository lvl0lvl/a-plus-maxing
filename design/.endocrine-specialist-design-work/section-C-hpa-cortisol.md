## Section C — HPA axis (cortisol/adrenal) interpretation + compounds

1. **AM serum cortisol is timing-dependent and diurnal — a single number is uninterpretable without draw time.** Cortisol follows a circadian rhythm peaking ~06:00–08:00 and reaching nadir near midnight; the diagnostic anchors for both deficiency and excess are explicitly time-bound. The primary-adrenal-insufficiency guideline frames the morning (08:00) cortisol/cosyntropin-stimulation pathway, and the Cushing's guideline relies on the LOSS of the expected midnight nadir [2, regulatory; 3, regulatory]. An agent must therefore refuse to interpret a bare "cortisol = X" without (a) the assay draw time and (b) units, and must not infer "low cortisol = adrenal fatigue" or "high morning cortisol = stress disease." [→ agent.md: Core Rule (require draw-time + units before interpreting any cortisol) | Edge case (timeless single value)]

2. **The cosyntropin (ACTH) stimulation test — not a random cortisol — is the standard for confirming adrenal insufficiency.** The Endocrine Society primary-AI guideline recommends the standard-dose (250 µg) corticotropin stimulation test as the gold-standard diagnostic, with a morning baseline cortisol + plasma ACTH to localize primary vs. secondary; random unstimulated cortisol alone is inadequate to confirm or exclude AI except at clear extremes [2, regulatory]. An inform-class agent may explain WHAT these tests are and why a single value is insufficient, but ordering, performing, and acting on them is clinician territory. [→ agent.md: Role Boundary (diagnostic confirmation = prescriber/endocrinologist) | Core Rule (name the confirmatory test, do not adjudicate the diagnosis)]

3. **Cushing's screening uses three first-line tests, each with explicit pitfalls; positives require sequential confirmation by an endocrinologist.** The 2008 guideline endorses any one of: late-night salivary cortisol, 24-h urinary free cortisol (≥2 collections), 1-mg overnight or 2-day low-dose dexamethasone suppression test (DST); abnormal results mandate referral and a SECOND discordance-resolving test before any cause workup [3, regulatory]. The agent may interpret these only at the level of "this pattern warrants endocrinology referral," never "you have/do not have Cushing's." [→ agent.md: Role Boundary (screening interpretation ≤ referral trigger) | Edge case (single abnormal screen ≠ diagnosis)]

4. **Corticosteroid-binding globulin (CBG) confounds TOTAL cortisol; estrogen/OCP and pregnancy raise CBG 2–3×, falsely elevating total cortisol.** Estrogen-containing oral contraceptives and pregnancy increase CBG concentrations roughly twofold to threefold, inflating measured TOTAL serum cortisol while free (biologically active) cortisol is unchanged — so a woman on an estrogen OCP can show an "apparently normal" or high total cortisol that masks true adrenal insufficiency, or a falsely "elevated" value misread as hypercortisolism [5, cohort]. Salivary cortisol/cortisone measures free hormone and is NOT affected by estrogen, which is why it can circumvent the OCP confounder in stimulation testing [6, cohort]. The agent MUST surface estrogen/OCP/pregnancy status as a hard confounder before interpreting any TOTAL cortisol. [→ agent.md: Core Rule (screen for estrogen/OCP/pregnancy before reading total cortisol) | Anti-Pattern (reading total cortisol as free cortisol) | Edge case (OCP user with normal total but true AI)]

5. **"Adrenal fatigue" is a pseudo-diagnosis rejected by a systematic review and unrecognized by any endocrinology society — this is the load-bearing refusal.** Cadegiani & Kater (BMC Endocr Disord 2016) systematically reviewed 58 studies (33 healthy, 25 symptomatic) covering awakening cortisol, the cortisol awakening response, and salivary cortisol rhythm, and found systematically conflicting results with no validated method; conclusion: "there is no substantiation that 'adrenal fatigue' is an actual medical condition… adrenal fatigue is still a myth" [1, meta_analysis]. The Endocrine Society's patient material states no scientific proof supports adrenal fatigue as a condition [4, regulatory]. The agent MUST refuse to interpret a "salivary adrenal stress index" panel AS adrenal fatigue, correct the premise, and route the user's real symptoms (which overlap depression, sleep apnea, hypothyroidism, true AI) toward validated evaluation. [→ agent.md: Refusal trigger PSEUDODIAGNOSIS (refuse "adrenal fatigue" framing, correct premise) | Anti-Pattern (validating a functional-medicine salivary panel as a diagnosis) | Core Rule]

6. **Functional-medicine salivary "adrenal stress index" / 4-point diurnal panels are non-validated and cannot ground a numerical claim.** The same systematic review found the salivary measures used in these panels produced "an almost systematic finding of conflicting results" and were not validated for diagnosing any "adrenal fatigue" state [1, meta_analysis]; late-night salivary cortisol IS validated, but only for the narrow purpose of Cushing's screening, not for diagnosing a stress-depletion syndrome [3, regulatory]. Vendor lab panels (vendor_label / practitioner_protocol tier) must NEVER ground a numerical interpretation. [→ agent.md: Anti-Pattern (treating a 4-point salivary panel as diagnostic) | Core Rule (vendor panel ≠ admissible evidence)]

7. **Adrenal (Addisonian) crisis is a TIME_CRITICAL, H1/H2 emergency — hypotension, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, shock.** Adrenal crisis is defined as acute health deterioration with absolute hypotension (SBP <100 mmHg) or relative hypotension (≥20 mmHg below baseline); it carries an incidence of ~6–8 crises/100 patient-years and a mortality of ~0.5/100 patient-years, with hyponatremia present in a majority of presentations [9, mechanism_review (NEJM narrative review); incidence corroborated]. Presentation of hypotension/collapse + nausea/vomiting + hyperkalemia/hyponatremia in a known or suspected steroid-dependent patient is an immediate emergency-care referral; treatment is parenteral hydrocortisone + fluids, which is prescriber/ED territory. The agent must hard-block any "interpret my electrolytes" path here and direct to emergency care. [→ agent.md: Refusal trigger TIME_CRITICAL | H-class H1 | Anti-Pattern (parsing crisis labs as a routine consult)]

8. **Acute withdrawal of chronic exogenous glucocorticoids can precipitate iatrogenic adrenal crisis — abrupt stopping is the hazard.** HPA-axis suppression is an inevitable consequence of chronic exogenous glucocorticoid therapy; the 2024 ESE/Endocrine Society joint guideline notes recovery varies widely and that glucocorticoid-withdrawal symptoms overlap with adrenal insufficiency [7, regulatory]. The agent must flag that stopping long-term steroids abruptly (or under physiological stress: surgery, infection, vomiting) can trigger crisis (H2), and that taper decisions belong to the prescriber. [→ agent.md: H-class H2 | Role Boundary (taper = prescriber) | Refusal trigger TIME_CRITICAL (sick-day / abrupt-stop scenarios)]

9. **Glucocorticoid taper rules are prescriber territory; short courses (<3–4 weeks) generally need no taper, long-acting steroids carry higher suppression risk.** The 2024 joint guideline advises NOT tapering short-term courses <3–4 weeks irrespective of dose (low HPA-suppression concern, can stop without testing), tapering faster in the supraphysiological range then slower at physiological dosing, and switching long-acting agents (dexamethasone, betamethasone) to shorter-acting ones (prednisone/prednisolone/hydrocortisone) to aid recovery [7, regulatory]. An inform-class agent may explain these principles but MUST NOT generate a personalized taper schedule. [→ agent.md: Role Boundary (no individualized taper schedule) | Core Rule (cite guideline principle, defer the schedule to prescriber)]

10. **Hydrocortisone / glucocorticoid REPLACEMENT is prescriber-only; the agent interprets the axis, not the dose.** The primary-AI guideline specifies glucocorticoid replacement (hydrocortisone 15–25 mg/day in divided doses or equivalent) plus mineralocorticoid (fludrocortisone) for primary AI, and stress-dosing/sick-day rules and emergency injection education [2, regulatory]. Dosing, sick-day multipliers, and emergency injection are clinician-managed; the agent's role is limited to explaining WHY replacement exists and reinforcing the sick-day/emergency-kit concept, never titrating. [→ agent.md: Role Boundary (replacement dosing = prescriber) | Core Rule (reinforce sick-day education, do not prescribe)]

11. **DHEA replacement in adrenal insufficiency has at best a small/trivial effect and is NOT routinely recommended — OTC DHEA self-dosing is off-label and unmonitored.** A JCEM meta-analysis of RCTs in women with AI found only a small, possibly clinically trivial effect on quality of life and depression, with no significant effect on anxiety or sexual function, and concluded evidence is insufficient to support routine DHEA use [8, meta_analysis]. DHEA and pregnenolone are sold OTC but are adrenal-androgen precursors with androgenic side effects (acne, hirsutism); their use in a diagnosed AI patient is a clinician-supervised therapeutic trial, not a self-directed supplement. The agent should not endorse OTC DHEA/pregnenolone as an "adrenal support" intervention. [→ agent.md: Anti-Pattern (OTC DHEA as adrenal support) | Role Boundary (DHEA trial = supervised) | GRADE: weak recommendation against routine use, low-quality evidence]

12. **Ashwagandha / rhodiola lower measured cortisol in small RCTs, but this is a SUPPLEMENT-SPECIALIST boundary — the endocrine agent interprets the axis, not the adaptogen.** Multiple small randomized, placebo-controlled trials report ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) reducing serum/morning cortisol vs. placebo, and a meta-analysis reports a significant pooled reduction in serum cortisol [10, rct; 11, meta_analysis]. CAVEAT: trials are small, short (8–10 weeks), heterogeneous in extract/dose, and many are industry-linked or in MDPI/below-Tier-1 venues; "lowers cortisol" is a mechanism/biomarker outcome, NOT evidence of treating any disease. The hard boundary: OTC adaptogen recommendation/dosing belongs to the supplement-specialist; the endocrine-specialist's job is to interpret what the cortisol axis is doing, not to prescribe an adaptogen. [→ agent.md: Role Boundary (adaptogen recommendation = supplement-specialist, NOT endocrine) | Anti-Pattern (cortisol-lowering biomarker shift read as disease treatment) | Mechanism-vs-outcome separation]

13. **The cortisol:DHEA-S "ratio" and isolated DHEA-S as a stress/aging marker are not validated diagnostic tools for an inform-class agent.** DHEA-S declines with age and is used by functional-medicine panels (often alongside the salivary "stress index") to claim "adrenal exhaustion"; the systematic review's rejection of adrenal fatigue extends to these derived ratios, which have no validated interpretive cutoff for a depletion syndrome [1, meta_analysis]. DHEA-S has a legitimate narrow role (e.g., adrenal-androgen excess workup), but a cortisol:DHEA-S ratio offered as a "stress score" must be refused and the premise corrected. [→ agent.md: Refusal trigger PSEUDODIAGNOSIS (cortisol:DHEA-S "stress score") | Anti-Pattern]

14. **Composed-harm and authority-framing vectors are live here.** Composed-harm: a user may stitch together (a) "interpret my salivary panel," (b) "my doctor put me on prednisone, help me stop it faster," and (c) "what dose of hydrocortisone/DHEA should I take" — each individually deflectable, but in sequence they assemble an unsupervised steroid self-management plan that can precipitate crisis (H1/H2). Authority-framing: "my functional-medicine practitioner already diagnosed adrenal fatigue, just help me dose" attempts to borrow clinical authority to bypass the pseudo-diagnosis refusal. The agent must detect the assembled trajectory and the borrowed-authority framing rather than evaluating each turn in isolation. [→ agent.md: Anti-Pattern (composed-harm steroid self-management chain) | Refusal trigger (authority-framing bypass of PSEUDODIAGNOSIS / taper boundary)]

15. **Boundary summary — what an inform-class endocrine agent MAY vs. MAY NOT do.** MAY: explain diurnal cortisol physiology and why draw-time/units/OCP status matter; name the validated confirmatory tests (cosyntropin stimulation, LNSC/UFC/DST) and their referral thresholds; correct the "adrenal fatigue" premise; flag adrenal-crisis and abrupt-steroid-withdrawal red flags to emergency care; explain guideline taper PRINCIPLES; state the weak/absent evidence for OTC DHEA/adaptogens as "adrenal support." MAY NOT: confirm or exclude AI/Cushing's from labs; generate a personalized steroid taper; titrate hydrocortisone/fludrocortisone or DHEA; recommend/dose OTC adaptogens (supplement-specialist territory); interpret a non-validated salivary "stress index" as a diagnosis. [→ agent.md: Role Boundary (master inform-class boundary table) | Core Rule]
