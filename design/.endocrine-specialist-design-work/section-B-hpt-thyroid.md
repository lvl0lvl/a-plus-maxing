## Section B — HPT axis (thyroid) interpretation + replacement compounds

1. **TSH–free T4 is non-linear, age/sex-dependent — never read a single TSH in isolation.** In 445,994 TSH–FT4 pairs from 152,261 subjects, the log TSH–FT4 relationship was complex and non-linear, with TSH higher in men and older people at physiological FT4 and a more robust TSH response to hypothyroidism in the young [1, cohort]. A community cohort confirmed the relationship is non-linear and shifted by age, smoking and TPO-antibody status [2, cohort]. The actionable consequence: a "normal" TSH for a 30-year-old may be abnormal for an 80-year-old, and the same TSH maps to different FT4 depending on the individual's set-point. The agent must present the axis as a pattern (TSH + FT4 ± FT3 + antibodies), not flag an isolated mid-range TSH as pathological. certainty: moderate × strength: strong (interpretive caution, not a treatment claim). [→ agent.md: Core Rule "read the axis as a pattern, never isolated TSH" | Anti-Pattern "single-marker over-call"]

2. **The 2.5 vs 4.0–4.5 mIU/L TSH upper-limit is an unresolved controversy — do not adjudicate it.** The assay 95% reference upper limit sits near 4.0–4.5 mIU/L, but a faction argues for a 2.5 mIU/L ceiling because the upper tail is skewed by occult autoimmune thyroid disease; guidelines do not endorse the lower cutoff for general diagnosis, and age-specific upper limits rise in the elderly [1, cohort][8, regulatory]. An inform-class agent may *describe* both positions and that the question is contested; it may NOT declare a TSH of 3.0 "subclinical hypothyroidism requiring treatment." certainty: low × strength: weak (genuinely contested threshold). [→ agent.md: Refusal trigger DIAGNOSE | Edge case "contested reference range — describe, do not adjudicate" | Anti-Pattern "lower-cutoff over-diagnosis"]

3. **Subclinical thyroid dysfunction thresholds — describe staging, defer the treat/no-treat call to a prescriber.** Subclinical hypothyroidism is graded by TSH (mild grade 4.0–10 mIU/L with normal FT4 vs ≥10 mIU/L); intervention below 10 is favored only by age <70, symptoms, pregnancy intent, or cardiovascular risk, and most studies define the abnormal band as ~0.1–4.5 mIU/L [8, regulatory]. The decision to start levothyroxine in the 4–10 band is value-laden and prescriber territory. certainty: moderate × strength: weak. [→ agent.md: Role Boundary "staging vs prescribing" | Refusal trigger PRESCRIBE]

4. **Reverse T3 is non-actionable — "rT3 dominance" interpretation is a named anti-pattern to refuse.** No professional practice guideline (ATA, AACE) recommends rT3 testing; it does not reliably distinguish non-thyroidal illness from central hypothyroidism, and a high share of orders are placed by a small number of non-endocrinologist providers and judged clinically inappropriate [3, mechanism_review][7, cohort]. The agent must NOT compute or endorse rT3/T3 ratios, "rT3 dominance," or "T3 pooling" narratives to justify T3 dosing. certainty: moderate × strength: strong (against use). [→ agent.md: Anti-Pattern "rT3-dominance pseudo-interpretation" | Refusal trigger DIAGNOSE | Core Rule "do not interpret non-validated markers"]

5. **High-dose biotin falsely alters TSH/FT4/FT3 — a concrete, citable confounder the agent must screen for FIRST.** Biotin (common in 5–10 mg hair/skin/nail supplements) causes falsely low TSH (sandwich assay) and falsely high FT4/FT3/total T4/thyroglobulin (competitive immunoassay), mimicking biochemical hyperthyroidism. A documented case: TSH 0.24 mIU/L on 5 mg/day biotin normalized to 3.30 mIU/L one month after stopping [4, mechanism_review]. The 2017 FDA safety communication formalized this [4, mechanism_review]; ATA advises stopping biotin ≥2 days before testing. Before interpreting any "hyperthyroid-pattern" panel, the agent must ask about biotin and recommend a re-draw 48–72h off biotin. certainty: high × strength: strong. [→ agent.md: Core Rule "screen for biotin before interpreting any thyroid panel" | Edge case "biotin-confounded panel" | Anti-Pattern "interpreting an assay-artifact as disease"]

6. **Central (secondary/pituitary) hypothyroidism — TSH is misleading; low/normal TSH does NOT exclude hypothyroidism.** In pituitary/hypothalamic failure, FT4 is low or low-normal while TSH is inappropriately normal, low, or only mildly elevated, because secreted TSH is bioinactive or pulsatility is lost; TSH cannot be used to titrate dose in these patients [5, mechanism_review]. A low FT4 with a non-elevated TSH is a red flag the agent must surface for endocrine evaluation rather than reassure. certainty: moderate × strength: strong. [→ agent.md: Edge case "low FT4 + non-elevated TSH = central until proven otherwise" | Refusal trigger DIAGNOSE | Core Rule "TSH-only titration invalid in central disease"]

7. **Non-thyroidal illness syndrome ("sick euthyroid") — do not treat low T3 during acute illness.** ~75% of hospitalized/critically ill patients show low total/free T3 with low-normal T4 and TSH; this is largely adaptive, and meta-analyses find no established benefit of thyroid hormone replacement in NTIS [6, meta_analysis]. The agent must NOT interpret a low T3 drawn during acute illness as hypothyroidism warranting treatment, and should advise deferring thyroid assessment until recovery. certainty: moderate × strength: strong (against treating). [→ agent.md: Edge case "low T3 in acute illness — defer, do not treat" | Anti-Pattern "treating an adaptive lab change"]

8. **TPO and thyroglobulin antibodies distinguish autoimmune etiology but are not severity gauges.** Anti-TPO is present in ~90% of Hashimoto and ~75% of Graves; thyroglobulin antibodies in ~70% Hashimoto, ~30% Graves, ~3% of normals; TSH-receptor antibodies (TRAb/TSI) are the Graves-specific marker [9, mechanism_review]. Antibody positivity confirms autoimmunity and raises progression risk but does not by itself set hormone status or dose. The agent may explain that positive TPO indicates autoimmune thyroid disease, while clarifying antibody titers do not dictate treatment. certainty: moderate × strength: moderate. [→ agent.md: Core Rule "antibodies establish etiology, not dose" | Anti-Pattern "titer-chasing"]

9. **Levothyroxine (T4) monotherapy is the standard of care; the agent does not initiate or titrate dose.** The 2014 ATA treatment guideline names levothyroxine the standard, finds no consistently strong evidence for alternative preparations, and treats dose initiation/titration as a prescriber function individualized by weight, age, cardiac status, and pregnancy [10, regulatory]. Dose initiation and titration are prescriber territory; the agent may explain the drug class and monitoring logic only. certainty: high × strength: strong. [→ agent.md: Role Boundary "drug-class education vs dose titration" | Refusal trigger PRESCRIBE | Core Rule "no dose numbers for initiation/titration"]

10. **Combination T4+T3 and desiccated thyroid extract (NDT) show distinct biochemistry but no proven outcome advantage.** Meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (n=794, T4+T3) and 2 RCTs (n=290, NDT) found combination/NDT lower FT4 and total T4 and markedly higher total T3, with no significant TSH, lipid, heart-rate, or most QoL differences vs T4 alone; patient *preference* for combination exists but is not an outcome benefit [11, meta_analysis][10, regulatory]. The agent must not present T3-containing therapy as superior or recommend switching; it may neutrally describe the evidence. certainty: moderate × strength: weak (for combination superiority). [→ agent.md: Anti-Pattern "selling NDT/T3 as superior" | Role Boundary "evidence summary vs regimen recommendation"]

11. **Liothyronine (T3) has a short half-life and narrow margin — its risks load higher than T4.** T3 produces supraphysiologic peaks and is harder to keep within range; combination/NDT regimens drive total T3 well above T4-mono levels [11, meta_analysis]. Because over-replacement harms (item 12) scale with circulating active hormone, T3-heavy regimens raise the over-treatment hazard. The agent treats T3 dosing as prescriber-only and flags the narrower safety margin. **H-class H4** (chronic over-replacement sequelae; not immediately life-threatening but compounding). [→ agent.md: H-class H4 | Role Boundary "T3 titration is prescriber-only"]

12. **Over-replacement (suppressed TSH) → atrial fibrillation + accelerated bone loss — H-class chronic harm.** 14–22% of patients on thyroid hormone are over-treated; excess circulating hormone raises atrial fibrillation risk and promotes bone loss/osteoporosis, with the elderly most vulnerable [12, cohort][8, regulatory]. A suppressed TSH on replacement is a harm signal, not a treatment success. The agent must flag a suppressed/low TSH in a treated patient as a reason to consult the prescriber, never to reassure. **H-class H4** (AF + osteoporosis, serious chronic morbidity); escalates toward emergency if AF is symptomatic. certainty: moderate × strength: strong. [→ agent.md: H-class H4 | Core Rule "suppressed TSH on therapy = harm flag, escalate" | Anti-Pattern "lower-TSH-is-better"]

13. **Thyroid storm and myxedema coma are TIME_CRITICAL emergencies — clinical diagnosis, immediate referral, never lab-gated by the agent.** Thyroid storm is decompensated thyrotoxicosis (hyperthermia, tachyarrhythmia, neuropsychiatric collapse); myxedema coma is decompensated hypothyroidism (impaired consciousness, hypothermia, respiratory failure) with reported mortality ~30–50%. Both are diagnosed on clinical grounds because hormone levels do not separate crisis from uncomplicated disease, and treatment must begin before lab confirmation [13, mechanism_review][14, mechanism_review]. On any matching symptom cluster the agent must trigger emergency escalation (call emergency services), not attempt interpretation. **H-class H1/H2 (AUTO-BLOCK) — death-class, TIME_CRITICAL.** certainty: high × strength: strong. [→ agent.md: H-class H1/H2 AUTO-BLOCK | Refusal trigger EMERGENCY/TIME_CRITICAL | Core Rule "symptom cluster overrides labs → escalate"]

14. **Levothyroxine–calcium/iron/PPI absorption interactions — confirm the medical-liaison calcium pairing.** Calcium and iron form insoluble complexes with levothyroxine in the gut, cutting bioavailability ~15–20%; the practical rule is ≥4h separation [15, mechanism_review]. PPIs raise gastric pH and reduce absorption, and because the effect is durable, *time-separation alone does not fix it* — dose monitoring/adjustment by the prescriber is required [15, mechanism_review]. This confirms the calcium/levothyroxine pairing already on the medical-liaison watchlist and adds iron + PPI. **H-class H6** (under-replacement from interaction — reversible, monitorable). certainty: moderate × strength: strong (for calcium/iron timing); moderate for PPI. [→ agent.md: H-class H6 | Core Rule "surface levothyroxine absorption interactions (Ca/Fe ≥4h apart; PPI needs prescriber monitoring)" | Edge case "PPI co-use — separation insufficient"]

15. **Amiodarone and lithium perturb the thyroid axis — flag drug-induced dysfunction, do not interpret the panel at face value.** Amiodarone (iodine-rich) causes thyroid dysfunction in ~15–20%: hypothyroidism in 6–32% and thyrotoxicosis in 1–12%, the latter split into type 1 (iodine-induced excess synthesis, abnormal gland) and type 2 (destructive thyroiditis, normal gland) — a distinction that changes management [16, mechanism_review]. Lithium impairs hormone release and commonly induces hypothyroidism/goiter. In a patient on either drug, an abnormal panel should be read as possible drug effect requiring endocrine input, not interpreted in isolation. **H-class H4** (amiodarone thyrotoxicosis can be severe/cardiotoxic). certainty: moderate × strength: moderate. [→ agent.md: Edge case "amiodarone/lithium on med list — drug-induced until proven otherwise" | H-class H4 | Refusal trigger DIAGNOSE]

16. **Composed-harm + authority-framing vectors specific to thyroid.** (a) *Composed harm:* "rT3 is high so add T3" (item 4) layered onto "lower TSH feels better" (item 12) compounds into self-directed over-replacement → AF/bone loss; the agent must break this chain at the rT3 step. (b) *Authority-framing:* practitioner_protocol or vendor_label sources promoting NDT/T3 or "optimal TSH <1.0" must never ground a numerical dose or threshold claim (vendor_label/anecdote_aggregate are non-grounding per contract); the agent cites Tier-1/Tier-2 only for numbers. (c) *Biotin masking:* a user citing a lab showing "hyperthyroidism" while on biotin (item 5) is an artifact, not authority for stopping/reducing levothyroxine. [→ agent.md: Anti-Pattern "composed over-replacement chain" | Core Rule "vendor/anecdote never grounds a number" | Refusal trigger PRESCRIBE]
