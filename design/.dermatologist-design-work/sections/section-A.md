# Section A — Photoaging + Skin-Barrier Topical Actives

Scope: vetted dermatology-literature knowledge on topical actives for photoaging and skin-barrier function. Goal-agnostic library research — no operator/patient personalization. Every numerical efficacy/AE claim is type-tagged `[N, tag]`; bottom-line per active carries two-axis GRADE (certainty + strength).

## A.1 Topical retinoids

### Mechanism (collagen / procollagen / receptors)
Tretinoin (all-trans retinoic acid) activates nuclear retinoic acid receptors RARα/β/γ, driving gene-expression changes that induce collagen synthesis and recycling, prevent collagen loss, and reduce matrix metalloproteinases MMP-1 and MMP-8 [12, mechanism_review]. The upstream photoaging mechanism: UVB induces matrix-degrading metalloproteinase mRNA, protein and activity in human skin in vivo within hours of exposure, at doses well below the skin-reddening threshold; all-trans retinoic acid applied *before* UVB substantially reduces AP-1 and MMP induction (transrepression of AP-1) [7, mechanism_review]. Note: the foundational Fisher 1996 finding combined in-vivo human-skin biopsy data with molecular pathway analysis; the MMP-induction time-course and AP-1 transrepression are mechanistic-pathway claims, not clinical-endpoint claims.

### Photoaging efficacy (RCT / meta-analysis)
- Foundational RCT: in a 16-week randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial (n=30 completers), all completing patients showed statistically significant photoaging improvement on tretinoin-treated forearms but not vehicle forearms; 14/15 facial-tretinoin patients improved vs 0 vehicle (statistically significant between-group difference), with corroborating histologic changes [1, rct].
- The 2005 Cochrane review of 30 trials concluded topical tretinoin ≥0.02% improves the appearance of mild-to-moderate photodamage on face and forearms in the short term, with a dose-response relationship for *both* effectiveness and irritation; tazarotene 0.01–0.1% and isotretinoin 0.1% benefit moderate photodamage with associated irritation/erythema; effectiveness of other interventions remained uncertain [2, meta_analysis].

### Acne efficacy (RCT / network meta-analysis)
- Adapalene gel 0.3% phase-III RCT (n=653, 2:2:1 randomization, 12 weeks): adapalene 0.3% was significantly superior to adapalene 0.1% and vehicle on IGA success rate (23.3% vs 16.9% vs 10.0%), total and inflammatory lesion counts, with a dose-dependent effect; AEs mostly mild-to-moderate and transient [4, rct].
- Network meta-analysis of 40 trials / 18,089 participants (mild-to-moderate acne): adapalene+benzoyl peroxide ranked most effective for patient-reported global improvement (OR 3.65 vs vehicle, moderate confidence); adapalene monotherapy and benzoyl peroxide each superior to vehicle; no convincing evidence that antibiotic-containing topicals outperform non-antibiotic options [5, meta_analysis].

### Adverse-effect profile
Most frequent topical-tretinoin AEs are itching, scaling, erythema and dryness ("retinoid dermatitis"), typically mild-to-moderate; percutaneous absorption is minimal (1–2%) even after repeated application [12, mechanism_review]. Photosensitivity / increased UV reactivity during retinoid use is the basis for the standard "apply at night + daytime photoprotection" practice (consistent with the Fisher mechanism that retinoids and UV act on the same MMP/AP-1 axis) [7, mechanism_review].

### Pregnancy handling — topical vs oral (categorical distinction)
- The precautionary labeling exists because the human teratogenic exposure threshold for topical retinoids is *unknown*, not because topical exposure is shown to be teratogenic. Meta-analysis of first-trimester topical-retinoid exposure (654 exposed vs 1,375 unexposed pregnancies) found NO significant increase in major congenital malformations: OR 1.22 (95% CI 0.65–2.29); authors state results are adequate to *reassure* inadvertently-exposed women but statistical power is insufficient to *justify* deliberate use in pregnancy [6, meta_analysis].
- CATEGORICAL DISTINCTION (flag): this topical evidence is NOT interchangeable with ORAL isotretinoin. Oral isotretinoin is a known potent human teratogen (the reason for the iPLEDGE risk-management program); topical retinoid systemic absorption is minimal (1–2%) [12, mechanism_review]. Conflating the two is a known reasoning error — the downstream agent must keep topical-vs-oral separate.

### OTC vs Rx status (FDA)
FDA approved topical tretinoin for acne vulgaris (1971) and for palliative photoaging treatment (1995); tretinoin, adapalene 0.3% (Rx-strength historically), tazarotene are prescription actives, while adapalene 0.1% became OTC in 2016 and OTC retinol/retinaldehyde are cosmetic (not FDA drug-approved for photoaging) [12, regulatory]. Retinaldehyde and retinol have comparison data vs tretinoin in split-face/whole-face studies, but tretinoin remains the most-established retinoid with approved wrinkle/photoaging indications; OTC retinol/retinaldehyde efficacy evidence is weaker and more formulation-dependent [12, mechanism_review].

**Bottom line — topical retinoids (photoaging):** certainty: high; strength: strong. (Cochrane + foundational RCT + 2025 meta-analysis converge.)
**Bottom line — topical retinoids (acne):** certainty: high; strength: strong.
**Bottom line — topical-retinoid pregnancy safety:** certainty: low-moderate (observational meta-analysis, underpowered for rare defects); strength: conditional — i.e., reassurance after inadvertent exposure is supported, but a *strong* "safe to use in pregnancy" claim on this certainty would be a HALT-pair and is NOT asserted.

## A.2 Photoprotection / sunscreen

### Concepts
SPF quantifies UVB-driven erythema protection; UVA (longer wavelength, dominant in photoaging/pigment) is addressed by "broad-spectrum" labeling and UVA filters (e.g., avobenzone, zinc oxide). SPF is a UVB-erythema ratio, not a UVA or a photoaging metric.

### Photoaging prevention (landmark RCT)
Nambour community RCT (n=903 adults <55), 4.5 years, daily vs discretionary SPF15+ sunscreen, blinded skin-microtopography grading: daily users showed 24% less skin aging, relative odds 0.76 (95% CI 0.59–0.98); β-carotene supplementation had no effect on skin aging [8, rct]. This is the strongest human evidence that sunscreen *prevents* photoaging (not merely treats appearance).

### Skin-cancer prevention (RCT)
Same Nambour cohort design: daily sunscreen over 4.5 years reduced cutaneous squamous-cell carcinoma but not basal-cell carcinoma; β-carotene had no effect on either [9, rct].

### FDA systemic-absorption studies
FDA maximal-use RCT (n=24; 23 completers): avobenzone, oxybenzone, octocrylene and ecamsule all exceeded the FDA 0.5 ng/mL plasma threshold on day 1 (geometric-mean peaks: oxybenzone 169.3–209.6 ng/mL, avobenzone 1.8–4.3, octocrylene 2.9–7.8, ecamsule 1.5) [10, rct]. CRITICAL framing: the authors explicitly state these results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen" — absorption above a toxicology-trigger threshold is a regulatory data-gap signal, NOT evidence of harm.

### Regulatory status of filters
FDA's September 2021 proposed order: zinc oxide and titanium dioxide proposed GRASE (generally recognized as safe and effective) up to 25%; PABA and trolamine salicylate proposed NOT GRASE on safety grounds; 12 organic filters (incl. oxybenzone, avobenzone, octocrylene, homosalate, octinoxate, octisalate) deferred pending additional data; the order remains non-final as of this writing [11, regulatory].

**Bottom line — sunscreen for photoaging prevention:** certainty: high; strength: strong.
**Bottom line — sunscreen for SCC prevention:** certainty: moderate-high; strength: strong (SCC); insufficient/weak for BCC.
**Bottom line — filter systemic-absorption safety:** certainty: low (data-gap, no clinical-harm endpoint); strength: conditional — regulators flagged need for further toxicology, not a withdrawal recommendation.

## A.3 Evidence-graded actives (niacinamide, vitamin C, AHA/BHA, barrier)

### Niacinamide
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled split-face RCT (n=50 women 40–60, 12 weeks, 5% niacinamide vs vehicle): significant improvement vs control in fine lines/wrinkles, hyperpigmentation spots, texture, red blotchiness and sallowness; well tolerated [11b, rct]. CONCENTRATION-AUDIT / DOMINANCE CAVEAT: this anchor RCT was sponsored by The Procter & Gamble Company (the niacinamide-cosmeceutical manufacturer), and much of the early topical-niacinamide photoaging evidence traces to the same group — single-sponsor dominance warrants a certainty downgrade until independent replication.
- **Bottom line — niacinamide (photoaging appearance):** certainty: low-moderate (small RCT, single-sponsor dominance); strength: conditional.

### Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid)
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (6 months, 5% ascorbic-acid cream vs excipient, photoaged female volunteers): significant improvement in global score and skin microrelief density with decreased deep furrows; cutaneous biopsy showed ultrastructural evidence of elastic-tissue repair [13, rct]. (Note: histologic finding here is *elastic-tissue* ultrastructure; broader "collagen-synthesis" claims for topical vitamin C rest substantially on in-vitro/mechanistic data on the collagen-biosynthesis cofactor role — those are `mechanism` not clinical-endpoint claims.)
- **Bottom line — topical vitamin C (photoaging):** certainty: low-moderate (small RCTs, formulation/stability dependent); strength: conditional.

### Alpha-hydroxy acids (glycolic, lactic)
Randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial (n=74 women 40–70, 22 weeks, 8% glycolic and 8% L-lactic acid creams vs vehicle): on face, ≥1 grade of improvement achieved by 76% glycolic / 71% lactic / 40% vehicle (p<0.05); on forearms, lactic acid improved overall severity, mottled hyperpigmentation, sallowness and roughness (p<0.05); authors called the agents "modestly useful" [14, rct]. A separate 5% glycolic-acid RCT (n=75, 3 months) found significant texture/discoloration improvement but only a non-significant trend for wrinkle reduction [14b, rct].
- **Bottom line — AHAs (photoaging):** certainty: moderate (consistent small RCTs); strength: conditional/weak — effect modest, strongest for texture/pigment/sallowness, weakest for wrinkles.

### Beta-hydroxy acid (salicylic acid)
Salicylic acid is a desmolytic/comedolytic BHA penetrating sebaceous follicles; in a 12-week double-blind RCT, a chloroxylenol 0.5% + salicylic acid 2% cream matched benzoyl peroxide 5% gel for inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesion reduction (62%/56% vs 60%/54%), with no statistically significant between-group difference [15, rct]. Its comedolytic/desmolytic action is concentration-dependent (mechanistic) [SA-mech, mechanism_review].
- **Bottom line — salicylic acid (acne):** certainty: moderate (RCT support, modest comparative trials); strength: conditional.

### Barrier / moisturization (ceramides, urea, glycerin, hyaluronic acid)
Randomized, investigator-blinded, intra-individual RCT (27 days, ceramide-precursor moisturizer vs untreated control leg, atopic-dermatitis patients): significantly greater reduction in transepidermal water loss and dryness scores, increased hydration (all p<0.01), and higher stratum-corneum ceramide by Raman spectroscopy (p<0.05) [16, rct]. Broader meta-analytic data support ceramide-containing moisturizers reducing TEWL and improving barrier in atopic dermatitis [16b, meta_analysis]. Urea, glycerin and hyaluronic acid are well-established humectants/occlusives with hydration/TEWL benefit; specific high-certainty photoaging-reversal claims for these are not supported — their role is barrier/moisturization, not photoaging treatment.
- **Bottom line — ceramide/barrier moisturizers (barrier function):** certainty: moderate (RCT + meta-analysis in AD; healthy-skin photoaging evidence weaker); strength: conditional.
