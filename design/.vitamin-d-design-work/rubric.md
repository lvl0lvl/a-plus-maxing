# Rubric — vitamin-d (serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D / 25-OH-D, biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** vitamin D = a **secosteroid prohormone**; two sources — **cutaneous synthesis** (UVB converts 7-dehydrocholesterol → previtamin D3 → **cholecalciferol (D3)**) + diet/supplements (D3 animal / **ergocalciferol D2** plant/fungal); two hydroxylations — hepatic **25-hydroxylase (CYP2R1) → 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D]** (the STORAGE form + the measured marker, half-life ~2-3 weeks), then renal **1α-hydroxylase (CYP27B1) → 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D [1,25(OH)2D / calcitriol]** (the ACTIVE hormone, tightly PTH/FGF23/calcium-regulated, short half-life). **THE LOAD-BEARING MEASUREMENT CONCEPT: serum 25(OH)D is measured (not 1,25) because it reflects total-body vitamin D STATUS/stores** — 1,25 is homeostatically regulated + can be normal/high even in deficiency (so 1,25 is NOT the status test; reserve it for specific disorders). Function: the VDR → intestinal calcium + phosphate absorption, bone mineralization (with PTH), + the broad non-classical/pleiotropic VDR roles (immune, muscle). Vitamin D binding protein (DBP) + albumin carry it (the free-hormone question). Note D2 vs D3 differences (assay + potency).
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & The Threshold Debate (load-bearing):** units **ng/mL (US) ↔ nmol/L (SI, ×2.496)**; **THE CENTRAL CONTROVERSY — the cutoffs DISAGREE between bodies:** the **Endocrine Society** (deficiency <20 ng/mL [<50 nmol/L], insufficiency 20-29, sufficiency ≥30) vs the **Institute of Medicine / NAM** (deficiency <12 ng/mL [<30 nmol/L], "at risk" 12-20, sufficient ≥20 ng/mL [50 nmol/L] for the population) — the IOM frames ≥20 as adequate for bone health in ~97.5% of the population; the Endocrine Society's ≥30 targets a clinical/at-risk population. Lay out BOTH + WHY they differ (population-bone-health vs individual-risk framing). **Toxicity** (>100 ng/mL / >150 with hypercalcemia). The newer **2024 Endocrine Society guideline** shift (de-emphasizing routine screening + routine 25(OH)D-guided supplementation in healthy adults — the empiric-supplementation recommendation in select groups). Note the assay/standardization caveat makes a single universal cutoff problematic (cross-ref measurement).
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** the assays — **immunoassays** (automated, common; can have **D2 cross-reactivity issues + DBP-interference + 3-epi-25(OH)D cross-reactivity**) vs **LC-MS/MS** (the reference-grade method; separates D2/D3 + the C3-epimer); the historically POOR inter-method/inter-lab agreement → the **Vitamin D Standardization Program (VDSP)** + the **NIST SRM 972a** + the CDC VDSCP traceability effort to harmonize results to the reference measurement procedure (this is WHY threshold debates are confounded — a "20 ng/mL" differs by assay); the **C3-epimer (3-epi-25(OH)D)** (significant in infants; an interference); free vs total 25(OH)D + DBP polymorphisms (the bioavailable-vitamin-D hypothesis — relevant across ancestries); D2 vs D3 measurement; pre-analytic stability (25(OH)D is stable).
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **what LOWERS 25(OH)D / deficiency:** low UVB exposure (latitude/season/sunscreen/indoor/clothing/skin pigmentation — darker skin needs more UVB), low dietary intake, malabsorption (celiac, IBD, bariatric surgery, cholestasis), obesity (volumetric sequestration in adipose → lower serum), CKD/liver disease (impaired hydroxylation), drugs (anticonvulsants, glucocorticoids, antiretrovirals accelerating catabolism), aging (less cutaneous synthesis), nephrotic syndrome (DBP loss). **Deficiency consequences (the SOLID evidence):** **rickets (children) / osteomalacia (adults)** — the unambiguous causal disease; secondary hyperparathyroidism + bone loss; proximal myopathy. **The OBSERVATIONAL associations vs the RCT reality (load-bearing honest framing):** low 25(OH)D is associated in OBSERVATIONAL cohorts with CVD, cancer, all-cause mortality, autoimmune disease, infection/respiratory — BUT the large **RCTs (VITAL [Manson NEJM 2019, N≈25,871], D-Health, ViDA, the calcium+D trials) largely FAILED to show that supplementation reduces CVD or total cancer in replete/general populations** (with nuances — VITAL cancer-MORTALITY + autoimmune-incidence [VITAL ancillary] signals; possible benefit in deficient subgroups) — so the associations are heavily **confounded (reverse causation: illness → less sun/lower D)**. State this honestly: 25(OH)D is a robust STATUS marker + the bone evidence is causal, but the pleiotropic-disease supplementation evidence is largely null in the replete. The **supplementation dosing** (the IOM RDA 600-800 IU; the upper level; the "treat deficiency, don't chase high-normal" framing). Limitations: assay dependence; total vs free; the threshold debate; a marker of status NOT proven a causal lever for most non-bone outcomes.
