# Rubric — recovery-strain (composite recovery/readiness & strain/load scores, WEARABLE biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).
**WEARABLE-ADAPTED SHAPE + THE CENTRAL HONESTY:** `category: wearable`, `source: wearable`, device-derived (unit: score/index — brand-specific scale). This is a **PROPRIETARY COMPOSITE SCORE, NOT a physiological measurement** — it aggregates (mostly-validated) inputs (HRV, RHR, sleep, respiratory rate, skin temperature, activity/load) into a (mostly-UNVALIDATED-as-a-composite, undisclosed-algorithm, brand-specific, NON-interchangeable) readiness/recovery or strain/load number. The load-bearing frame across ALL sections: **the INPUTS are validated; the COMPOSITE OUTPUT mostly is not** — it is a convenience trend nudge, not a measured quantity. Section C is **construct/criterion validity of the composite** (NOT assay standardization).

## TAG-ENUM PRE-EMPTION (critical — the recurring wearable lesson)
The ONLY admissible tags are the 12-enum: **rct, meta_analysis, cohort, open_label, animal, in_vitro, mechanism_review, regulatory, compounding_data_sheet, vendor_label, practitioner_protocol, anecdote_aggregate.** Map BEFORE writing AND match the tag to the ACTUAL STUDY TYPE: a **meta-analysis → `meta_analysis`**; a **narrative/scoping/systematic review without pooling → `mechanism_review`**; a device/score **validity / observational cohort → `cohort`**; a **single-arm / single-participant / case-series → `open_label`**; an **RCT (e.g. recovery-guided-training trial) → `rct`**; an **issuing-body consensus/standard → `regulatory`**. **A DEVICE-MANUFACTURER's own white-paper / self-validation = `vendor_label` and may NOT ground a validity/accuracy number — use independent peer-reviewed validation; DISCLOSE any vendor-funded-study COI** (the recovery-score literature is HEAVILY brand-authored — WHOOP/Oura/Garmin/Polar-funded — so this is central). NEVER write `[N, validation]`/`[N, observational]`/`[N, methods]`/`[N, systematic-review]`/`[N, systematic_review]`/`[N, guideline]` — inline OR compound. **The inline tag MUST equal the bib tag AND match the study type.**

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — What Recovery/Strain Scores Are & What the Device Computes:** define the two poles: **RECOVERY / READINESS** (a daily score — typically 0-100% or a 1-100 index — estimating how RECOVERED / ready-to-perform the body is, computed mainly from **nocturnal HRV + resting HR + sleep + respiratory rate + sometimes skin temperature**) and **STRAIN / LOAD** (a score estimating the cardiovascular + physical LOAD accumulated — e.g. WHOOP's 0-21 logarithmic Strain from time-in-HR-zones; training-load analogues like TRIMP / training impulse). Name the brand instances + their scales: **WHOOP Recovery (0-100%) + Strain (0-21)**; **Oura Readiness Score (0-100)**; **Garmin Body Battery (0-100) + Training Readiness / Training Load**; **Polar Nightly Recharge / Recovery Pro**; **Apple/Fitbit analogues**. **THE LOAD-BEARING FRAME (state up front): these are DERIVED, PROPRIETARY COMPOSITE scores — algorithmic aggregations of underlying signals, NOT a directly-measured physiological quantity; each brand uses an UNDISCLOSED algorithm + its OWN scale, so the scores are NOT interchangeable across devices.** The inputs (HRV/RHR/sleep/RR) are the validated parts (cross-ref the sibling entries); the composite is the brand's interpretation layer.
- **B — Interpretation, Scales & The Personal-Baseline:** there is **NO universal population norm** for a recovery/strain score (each is brand-specific + relative to the user's OWN rolling baseline — most devices explicitly normalize to personal baseline). Describe how each is meant to be read: **recovery/readiness — green/yellow/red or a % vs your baseline → a nudge to push or hold back training; strain/load — accumulated cardiovascular load vs your recovery capacity (the "strain-vs-recovery balance" framing).** The ONLY defensible use is the **personal trend + the relative day-to-day signal**, NOT the absolute number + NOT cross-brand comparison. Be explicit that a single score is nearly uninterpretable out of context, and that the headline number hides the inputs (a low recovery score is most useful when you look at WHICH input drove it — poor HRV? high RHR? short sleep?). The over-reliance / "orthosomnia-adjacent" caution (fixating on the score). The scales (WHOOP 0-21 strain is logarithmic; recovery %s are not linear physiological units).
- **C — Validity: What's Actually Validated (load-bearing honesty):** the CENTRAL section. **The INPUTS are independently validated** (HRV, RHR, sleep/wake, RR — cite the sibling-validation evidence) **but the COMPOSITE SCORE itself is largely NOT independently validated as a predictor of its claimed construct** (recovery/readiness/performance). Cover: (1) the **algorithms are PROPRIETARY + undisclosed** → not reproducible, not standardized, not auditable; (2) the **independent validation of the composite is sparse + mixed** — what little exists (e.g. studies testing whether WHOOP recovery / Oura readiness predict next-day performance, perceived recovery, or training outcomes) shows **weak-to-modest + inconsistent** relationships; cite NAMED independent studies + flag the **HEAVY vendor-funding / brand-authorship** of much of the literature (WHOOP/Oura/Garmin-funded validations = `vendor_label`, may not ground a validity claim); (3) the scores **change across firmware/algorithm updates** (a moving target — your "recovery" can shift without your physiology changing); (4) cross-device + cross-version NON-interchangeability. Honest bottom line: **the composite is a reasonable convenience summary of validated inputs, but the claim that the SCORE itself is a validated readiness/performance predictor is NOT well-supported** — treat it as a trend heuristic, not a measurement.
- **D — Determinants & Significance:** **What LOWERS recovery / RAISES strain-relative-to-recovery (i.e. drives the score toward "under-recovered"):** the SAME determinants as the inputs — **poor/short sleep, ALCOHOL (a major + reproducible recovery-score hit), illness/infection, high training load / overreaching, acute stress, late meals, heat, dehydration**; AGE; deconditioning. **What RAISES recovery:** good sleep, rest days, aerobic fitness/training adaptation, recovery practices, abstaining from alcohol. **SIGNIFICANCE / USE:** (1) **training-load management / recovery-guided training** — adjusting training based on the recovery/readiness score (the marquee use); **HRV/recovery-guided-training RCTs show comparable-or-modestly-better outcomes vs fixed plans** (cite verified RCTs/meta — but be HONEST: most evidence is for HRV-guided training specifically, NOT the proprietary composite scores; the evidence that acting on a BRANDED recovery score improves outcomes is THIN); (2) over-training / illness / alcohol monitoring (the score as a convenience aggregator of the early-warning signals); (3) behavior-change nudging (the score motivates sleep/recovery behaviors — itself a plausible benefit). **LIMITATIONS (load-bearing):** the composite is PROPRIETARY + largely UNVALIDATED-as-a-composite + a MOVING TARGET (firmware) + NON-interchangeable + brand-funded-evidence-heavy; it is a WELLNESS/trend nudge, NOT a measured physiological quantity or a diagnostic; over-reliance / score-anxiety risk; the actionable information is really in the INPUTS (HRV/RHR/sleep/RR) — the score is a convenience layer; interpret as a personal trend alongside how you actually feel + perform.
