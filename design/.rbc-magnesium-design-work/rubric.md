# Rubric — rbc-magnesium (red blood cell magnesium, biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** **magnesium (Mg²⁺)** = the 2nd most abundant INTRACELLULAR cation (after K⁺); a cofactor for **>300-600 enzymatic reactions** (all ATP is biologically active as **Mg-ATP**; DNA/RNA synthesis, the Na/K-ATPase, glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, PTH secretion/action). Body distribution (load-bearing): **~50-60% in BONE, ~25-40% in muscle/soft tissue (intracellular), and only ~0.3-1% in SERUM/extracellular** — so **serum magnesium reflects a tiny extracellular pool that is NOT a reliable index of total-body or intracellular Mg status** (the central concept motivating RBC-Mg). **RBC magnesium = the magnesium content of erythrocytes** — proposed as a better surrogate for INTRACELLULAR Mg status than serum (RBCs are accessible nucleate-free cells reflecting the intracellular compartment over the ~120-day RBC lifespan); still imperfect (RBC-Mg lags acute changes; the true gold standard is the **Mg-loading/retention test** or tissue [muscle] Mg). Homeostasis: **renal regulation** (the kidney is the main controller — TRPM6/TRPM7 channels in the distal tubule; ~95-99% filtered Mg reabsorbed, mostly in the thick ascending limb via paracellular claudin-16/19) + **intestinal absorption** (TRPM6 + paracellular) + bone as a reservoir. PTH, vitamin D, and the Na/K/Ca cross-talk.
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & The "Serum-Is-a-Poor-Marker" Problem (load-bearing):** **RBC-Mg** units typically **mmol/L (of packed cells) or mg/dL** — reference roughly **~4.0-6.4 mg/dL / ~1.65-2.65 mmol/L** (assay/lab-specific — wide method variability; state it's lab-dependent + NOT well-standardized). **SERUM Mg** reference **~1.7-2.4 mg/dL / 0.7-1.0 mmol/L** for contrast. **THE LOAD-BEARING PROBLEM: normomagnesemia (normal SERUM Mg) does NOT exclude magnesium deficiency** — a substantial fraction of Mg-deficient patients have normal serum Mg ("chronic latent magnesium deficiency" / "normomagnesemic magnesium deficiency"); serum Mg has poor sensitivity for total-body depletion. RBC-Mg is PROPOSED as more sensitive for the intracellular deficit, BUT the evidence that RBC-Mg reliably outperforms serum is MIXED/limited + RBC-Mg assays are poorly standardized → present this HONESTLY (RBC-Mg is a reasonable intracellular surrogate but not a validated gold standard; the Mg-tolerance/retention test remains the reference for diagnosing deficiency). The interpretation: low-normal serum + symptoms → consider RBC-Mg or an Mg-loading test. Note the ionized-Mg fraction.
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** **serum Mg** by colorimetric (xylidyl blue / calmagite / methylthymol blue) or atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS, the reference); **RBC-Mg measurement** — requires separating + washing erythrocytes, lysing, measuring Mg by AAS/ICP-MS or colorimetry, normalized per RBC count/hematocrit/Hb → multiple normalization conventions = a source of inter-lab variability (load-bearing: RBC-Mg is NOT well-standardized, results are not interchangeable across labs/methods, no universal reference interval — a key caveat). **Pre-analytic pitfalls (load-bearing): HEMOLYSIS falsely raises serum Mg** (RBCs are Mg-rich, like K⁺); EDTA/citrate/oxalate anticoagulants CHELATE Mg → falsely low; delayed serum separation. Ionized vs total Mg (ion-selective electrodes, not routine). The Mg-loading/retention test as the functional reference. Why RBC-Mg's analytical messiness compounds the interpretive uncertainty.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **DETERMINANTS of LOW magnesium (hypomagnesemia / depletion):** **GI loss** (diarrhea, malabsorption, IBD, bariatric, **chronic PPI use** [a recognized cause — FDA warning], steatorrhea), **RENAL loss** (loop + thiazide **diuretics**, alcohol, **uncontrolled diabetes/glycosuria**, hypercalcemia, **drugs — aminoglycosides, amphotericin, cisplatin, calcineurin inhibitors, cetuximab/EGFR-inhibitors**, tubulopathies [Gitelman/Bartter], post-obstructive diuresis), **inadequate intake** (poor diet, processed-food/refined diets, alcoholism — common), **redistribution** (refeeding, "hungry bone," insulin/catecholamines). **CONSEQUENCES (load-bearing):** **the electrolyte interlock — Mg deficiency causes REFRACTORY HYPOKALEMIA (renal K wasting via ROMK disinhibition — must replace Mg to correct K) + HYPOCALCEMIA (impaired PTH secretion/action)**; **cardiac — arrhythmias (incl. torsades de pointes; Mg is the treatment), + the association of low Mg with hypertension, atrial fibrillation**; **neuromuscular — tremor, tetany, seizures, weakness**; **metabolic — low Mg associated (observationally) with insulin resistance / type 2 diabetes / metabolic syndrome, and (in cohorts) with cardiovascular disease + mortality** (cite NAMED cohorts/meta — honest about confounding; some Mg-supplementation RCTs show BP/glycemic benefit in deficient/at-risk groups). **HIGH magnesium (hypermagnesemia):** uncommon — mainly **renal failure** (+ Mg-containing antacids/laxatives, or IV Mg in obstetrics) → hyporeflexia → respiratory/cardiac depression. **Limitations (load-bearing):** RBC-Mg is a SURROGATE with limited validation + poor standardization; neither serum nor RBC-Mg perfectly captures total-body/tissue Mg; the Mg-loading test is the functional reference; interpret with the clinical picture + K/Ca + the deficiency risk factors; a normal serum Mg does NOT exclude deficiency.
