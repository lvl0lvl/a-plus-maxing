# Rubric — free-t3 (biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** triiodothyronine (T3) as the ACTIVE thyroid hormone (binds nuclear thyroid receptors TRα/TRβ with ~10–15× the affinity of T4); ~80% of circulating T3 from peripheral T4→T3 outer-ring deiodination (D1/D2), ~20% direct thyroidal secretion; **free T3 (~0.2–0.4% unbound; T3 less protein-bound than T4)** is the active fraction; the deiodinase system (D1, D2 activating; D3 inactivating to rT3/T2); why fT3 reflects tissue-level thyroid hormone ACTION but is a less stable HPT-feedback signal than fT4/TSH; intracellular vs serum T3 (serum fT3 imperfectly reflects tissue T3 — local deiodinase control).
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Thresholds:** typical adult free-T3 reference interval (~**2.3–4.2 pg/mL** or **~3.5–6.5 pmol/L**, assay-dependent) — give the pg/mL↔pmol/L conversion (×1.536); method-dependence; diagnostic role — fT3 adds value mainly in HYPERthyroidism (T3-toxicosis: high fT3 with normal/borderline fT4) and is NOT recommended for routine hypothyroid diagnosis/monitoring (fT3 is preserved until late hypothyroidism); the low-T3 of non-thyroidal illness; age/sex/pregnancy variation; the TSH/fT4/fT3 grid where fT3 matters.
- **C — Measurement & Interferences:** the measurement challenge — fT3 is present at very LOW concentration (picomolar), making it harder to measure accurately than fT4; reference method (equilibrium dialysis/ultrafiltration + LC-MS/MS) vs routine analog immunoassays (estimates, binding-protein-sensitive); standardization status (less mature than T4); interferences — biotin, heterophile/anti-streptavidin antibodies, anti-T3 autoantibodies (can falsely raise or lower), abnormal binding proteins; **non-thyroidal illness / low-T3 syndrome** (the dominant confounder — D1 down, D3 up → low T3, high rT3); the in-vitro sample-handling effects; why total T3 is sometimes preferred.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** fT3's main clinical role — confirming/grading HYPERthyroidism (Graves', toxic nodular goiter, T3-predominant toxicosis), assessing severity; limited value in hypothyroidism (don't use fT3 to diagnose/monitor primary hypothyroidism — TSH + fT4 do that); the **non-thyroidal-illness / low-T3 syndrome** and its prognostic associations (low T3 predicts mortality in cardiac/critical illness — cite cohorts cautiously, note it's a marker of illness severity, not necessarily a treatment target); the T3/T4 ratio + the ongoing combination-therapy (LT3/LT4) debate (deiodinase polymorphism DIO2, persistent symptoms on LT4); raises (hyperthyroidism, exogenous T3/liothyronine, T3-toxicosis, high-iodine, recovery from NTI) / lowers (hypothyroidism late, NTI/low-T3 syndrome, caloric restriction/fasting, severe illness, beta-blockers + amiodarone + glucocorticoids + PTU inhibiting D1); limitations — low-concentration assay imprecision, NTI confounding, poor serum-to-tissue correlation, not for routine hypothyroid workup.
