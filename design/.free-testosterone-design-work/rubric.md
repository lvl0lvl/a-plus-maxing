# Rubric — free-testosterone (biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** free testosterone = the ~**2% of total T circulating UNBOUND** (not SHBG- or albumin-bound) — the immediately bioavailable/active fraction per the free-hormone hypothesis; the relationship free T < bioavailable T (free + albumin-bound ~50%) < total T; why free T matters when SHBG is abnormal (SHBG-bound T is not bioavailable, so total T misleads); the binding equilibrium (T ⇌ SHBG, T ⇌ albumin; affinity constants); DHT/estradiol also bound by SHBG; the conceptual basis for free vs bioavailable vs total.
- **B — How It's Determined: Measurement vs Calculation (load-bearing):** the THREE approaches — (1) **equilibrium dialysis / ultrafiltration + LC-MS/MS** = reference method (gold standard, not widely available); (2) **calculated free T** (cFT) from total T + SHBG + albumin via the **Vermeulen equation** (and Sodergard) — the practical clinical standard, validated against dialysis; (3) **direct analog/"free T" immunoassay** = UNRELIABLE/discouraged (Endocrine Society explicitly advises against it — does not accurately reflect free T). Give the typical adult male free-T reference (~**5–9 to ~30 ng/dL** total varies; commonly **~46–224 pg/mL** or **~150–700 pmol/L** by method — assay/method-dependent, state the wide method variation); units (pg/mL, ng/dL, pmol/L; ×3.467 pg/mL→pmol/L); % free (~1.5–2.5%).
- **C — Calculation Inputs, Standardization & Pitfalls:** the cFT depends on accurate TOTAL T (LC-MS/MS-preferred) + accurate SHBG (immunoassay variation) + an albumin value + the model's assumed binding constants (the constants debate — Vermeulen vs newer models like Zakharov/multistep, which give different values); discordance between methods (cFT vs dialysis vs analog can differ materially); the lack of harmonized free-T reference intervals; interferences propagate from total T + SHBG (biotin, heterophile, the SHBG assay); when each method is appropriate.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** when free T adds value over total T — **whenever SHBG is abnormal** (obesity/insulin resistance/T2D LOWER SHBG → total T underestimates androgen status but free T may be preserved; aging/hyperthyroidism/estrogen/hepatic disease RAISE SHBG → total T may look normal while free T is low); diagnosing hypogonadism in men with borderline total T + altered SHBG (Endocrine Society: measure free T by dialysis or calculate when total T is near the lower limit or SHBG abnormal); women/PCOS (free androgen index, hyperandrogenism); the same determinants as total T (raise: TRT/androgens, hCG; lower: aging, obesity, opioids, glucocorticoids, illness) but FILTERED through SHBG; limitations — method dependence (calculated vs measured vs analog disagree), no harmonized reference range, error propagation from SHBG + total-T assays, the analog-immunoassay unreliability.
