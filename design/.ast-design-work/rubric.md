# Rubric — ast (biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** AST (aspartate aminotransferase, formerly SGOT/GOT) = a pyridoxal-5'-phosphate-dependent enzyme catalyzing transamination of aspartate + α-ketoglutarate → oxaloacetate + glutamate (part of the malate-aspartate shuttle + amino-acid metabolism); exists in TWO isoenzymes — **cytosolic (cAST/GOT1) and mitochondrial (mAST/GOT2)**; tissue distribution is BROAD — high in **liver, cardiac + skeletal muscle, red blood cells, kidney, brain** — so AST is LESS liver-specific than ALT (a key contrast); released on cellular injury; the mitochondrial isoform release marks more severe/necrotic injury (alcoholic liver disease, the AST>ALT pattern); shorter half-life than ALT (~17 h vs ALT ~47 h); the AST/ALT (De Ritis) ratio introduced.
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Thresholds:** conventional adult reference (~**up to ~35–40 U/L**, sex-dependent — men higher); units U/L (IU/L); the lower-healthy-threshold question (similar to ALT but less studied); the De Ritis ratio interpretation as the load-bearing framing (AST:ALT >2 alcoholic, <1 MASLD/viral, >1 advancing fibrosis/cirrhosis); ×ULN bands; the importance of pairing with ALT + CK (to distinguish liver vs muscle vs cardiac source); age/sex dependence; macro-AST (a benign cause of persistent isolated AST elevation).
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** the IFCC reference measurement procedure (37°C, with/without P5P — the P5P effect, like ALT); the coupled enzymatic assay (AST → oxaloacetate, reduced by malate dehydrogenase with NADH→NAD+, 340 nm); **hemolysis is a MAJOR interference for AST** (RBCs contain ~10-40× plasma AST → even mild hemolysis falsely elevates AST, a key pre-analytic pitfall — contrast with ALT's resistance); macro-AST (immunoglobulin-complexed AST causing spurious persistent elevation, confirmed by PEG precipitation); the cytosolic vs mitochondrial isoenzyme measurement; standardization status; method-specific reference intervals.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **what RAISES AST** — hepatic (same differential as ALT but interpreted via the ratio: MASLD, viral, the AST>ALT alcoholic pattern from mitochondrial injury + B6 depletion, drug-induced, ischemic/toxin for marked elevation); **CARDIAC** (historically a cardiac-injury marker pre-troponin — AST rises in MI but now superseded by troponin; still relevant context); **SKELETAL MUSCLE** (rhabdomyolysis, strenuous exercise, myopathy, muscular dystrophy — AST often higher than ALT in muscle source; correlate with CK); **hemolysis/hemolytic anemia** (RBC source, in vivo); thyroid disease; **the De Ritis ratio** as a prognostic + diagnostic tool (>2 ALD; the ratio's association with fibrosis, cirrhosis, and with cardiovascular + all-cause mortality in cohorts — cite NAMED); **macro-AST** as a benign cause; **what LOWERS** — B6 deficiency (assay-dependent), uremia/dialysis; AST as a metabolic/prognostic marker (the AST:ALT ratio + cardiovascular/mortality associations); limitations — AST is NON-specific (liver, heart, muscle, RBC sources), must be interpreted WITH ALT + CK + the clinical picture + hemolysis status; a normal AST does not exclude liver disease.
