## Section A — Identity, the Selank parent & molecular mechanism

### A.1 The Parent: Selank

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide with the sequence **Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro** (single-letter: TKPRPGP), molecular formula C₃₃H₅₇N₁₁O₉, molecular weight **751.89 g/mol** (PubChem CID 11765600). It was developed by the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences, principally by the group of Nikolay F. Myasoedov, beginning in the 1980s–1990s. Selank is a stabilized synthetic analog of **Tuftsin** (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg), an endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide derived by two-step proteolytic cleavage of the Fc region of IgG. Tuftsin was first characterized by Najjar and colleagues; its structure–function pharmacology was systematically reviewed by Fridkin and Gottlieb [2, mechanism_review], establishing it as a phagocytosis-stimulating tetrapeptide acting on specific receptors on mononuclear phagocytes and polymorphonuclear granulocytes. Selank extends the native Tuftsin sequence with a C-terminal **Pro-Gly-Pro** tripeptide — a modification designed to resist rapid enzymatic degradation that otherwise destroys Tuftsin within minutes in plasma, and to prolong CNS bioavailability [3, in_vitro].

Selank has been registered in Russia as a pharmaceutical nasal spray (trade name Selanc) for generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) and neurasthenia, placing it in the small group of peptide anxiolytics that have cleared Russian regulatory review — a fact relevant to assessing the body of evidence, which is almost entirely Russian-origin.

### A.2 The Analog: N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate

**N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate** is a grey-market research compound consisting of the Selank heptapeptide with two terminal chemical modifications: **N-terminal acetylation** (an acetyl group — CH₃CO — added to the alpha-amine of the N-terminal Thr residue) and **C-terminal amidation** (replacement of the C-terminal carboxylate –COOH with –CONH₂ on the terminal Pro residue). These modifications are conventional peptide-stability strategies: N-terminal acetylation blocks aminopeptidase attack on the free N-terminus, and C-terminal amidation blocks carboxypeptidase hydrolysis and also removes a negative charge that may limit membrane partitioning. Combined, they are expected to extend plasma and mucosal half-life relative to the unmodified parent and may (per general peptide pharmacology) modestly improve mucosal and blood-brain barrier penetration — though the magnitude of these effects in CNS-active peptides is not uniformly established and depends on peptide size, sequence, and administration route [general peptide pharmacology context only; no N-acetyl-amidate-specific study]. **Pharmacological-uncertainty caveat for N-terminal acetylation:** the Tuftsin receptor-recognition literature [2, mechanism_review] implicates the free α-amine of the N-terminal Thr residue as a key element of the receptor-binding epitope in Tuftsin-family peptides. Acetylating that amine eliminates it. This means N-terminal acetylation could disrupt the very binding epitope that mediates Tuftsin-family receptor engagement — so the analog's retention of Selank's activity is NOT guaranteed by the stability rationale alone, and remains an open empirical question absent analog-specific binding data.

**CRITICAL HONESTY FINDING:** A systematic search of PubMed, PubChem, EuropePMC, and the broader indexed scientific literature identified **zero peer-reviewed primary studies specifically on N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate** as a distinct compound. Vendor and commercial-information sites (peptides.org, labofrad.com, biotechpeptides.com, purehealthpeptides.com, oroviabiolab.com) explicitly acknowledge this: "There is a lack of research regarding the potential effects of N-Acetyl Selank Amidate" and "Studies on N-acetyl Selank are lacking." All primary evidence cited for this analog is actually evidence from the Selank parent compound, extrapolated by assumption of pharmacological equivalence. That extrapolation may be directionally reasonable, but it is not validated. The mechanism, dosing, safety, and clinical relevance of N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate specifically are **unestablished in peer-reviewed science as of the search date**.

### A.3 Selank's Proposed Mechanisms (the evidentiary basis for the analog's claimed effects)

Because N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has no independent mechanistic literature, everything below describes Selank's mechanism in the Selank literature. The analog's mechanism is **assumed** to be equivalent; this assumption has not been tested.

**A.3.1 Enkephalin-degrading enzyme inhibition.** The earliest proposed mechanistic account — and the one most directly tied to human clinical biology — is inhibition of enkephalin-degrading enzymes (aminopeptidase N / APN, dipeptidyl peptidase IV / DPP-IV) in plasma and CNS. Zozulya et al. [3, in_vitro] demonstrated in a mixed human–biochemical study that patients with GAD show shortened enkephalin half-life in plasma; Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro) inhibited enkephalin catabolism in a dose-dependent manner in vitro (IC₅₀ ~15 µM), exceeding the inhibitory potency of reference peptidase inhibitors bacitracin and puromycin. A parallel clinical observation in 62 patients (GAD and neurasthenia, n=62; 30 Selank vs 32 medazepam) showed that Selank treatment increased enkephalin half-life parameters, correlating with symptom improvement [4, rct]. This model proposes that Selank prolongs the action of endogenous anxiolytic opioid peptides (leu-enkephalin, met-enkephalin) without directly binding opioid receptors.

**A.3.2 GABAergic modulation.** Selank modulates the expression of genes involved in GABAergic neurotransmission in rat frontal cortex. Volkova et al. [5, animal] (rat model, frontal cortex transcriptomics, n not specified in abstract) found that Selank administration at 1 h post-injection altered 45 GABA-related genes, with a positive correlation to effects seen with direct GABA application; at 3 h, 22 genes remained altered, including marked upregulation of *Gabre*, *Gabrq*, and *Hcrt* (individual fold-changes ranging from ~13-fold up to ~128-fold for the most-changed genes). The authors interpret this as allosteric modulation of the GABAergic system rather than direct receptor binding — consistent with Selank's lacking the amnestic/sedative side effects typical of direct GABA-A agonists. A follow-up in vitro study [6, in_vitro] (human neuroblastoma IMR-32 cell line) found Selank alone produced no significant changes in GABA-related gene expression, but markedly potentiated the gene-expression effects of co-administered olanzapine — a finding that suggests context-dependence and that Selank's GABAergic influence may be indirect or conditional on co-stimulation. Electrophysiological evidence from rat hippocampal CA1 pyramidal neurons (ex vivo slice preparation, 1–8 µM Selank) showed increased amplitude and discharge rate of spontaneous inhibitory postsynaptic currents (sIPSCs) [7, animal], consistent with net enhancement of inhibitory GABAergic tone at the synaptic level.

**A.3.3 Serotonergic and monoaminergic effects.** Semenova et al. [8, animal] (87 Wistar rats, serotonin depletion model via PCPA) showed that Selank (300 µg/kg) enhanced 5-HT metabolism in the brainstem at 30 min post-injection, with memory-consolidation enhancement (assessed at 24 h, 7 days, 30 days follow-up). Importantly, Tuftsin (the parent tetrapeptide) produced no such brainstem serotonin effect, suggesting the C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro extension is functionally important for this mechanism. A strain-comparative study in BALB/C and C57Bl/6 mice [9, animal] found Selank (0.3 mg/kg) produced strain-specific monoamine effects: increased norepinephrine in the hypothalamus of both strains; increased dopamine metabolites (DOPAC, HVA) in C57Bl/6 but decreased them in BALB/C; and decreased 5-HT and 5-HIAA in the hippocampus of BALB/C only. The strain-dependence is an important qualifier: it demonstrates that Selank's monoamine effects are not uniform and likely interact with baseline genetic neurotransmitter tone.

**A.3.4 BDNF upregulation.** Intranasal administration of Selank has been shown to regulate BDNF expression in the rat hippocampus in vivo [1, animal]. Inozemtseva et al. (2008) used reverse-transcriptase PCR and immunoenzyme assay to measure BDNF mRNA and protein levels in rat hippocampal tissue following intranasal Selank, providing primary molecular evidence for this neurotrophin effect. A subsequent study by Kolik et al. (2019) [11, animal] confirmed BDNF involvement in a hippocampus and prefrontal cortex model of ethanol-induced memory impairment, with Selank preventing ethanol-induced BDNF dysregulation — corroborating neurotrophin regulation as a genuine mechanism.

**A.3.5 Immunomodulation.** As a Tuftsin analog, Selank retains partial immunomodulatory activity. It has been reported to modulate IL-6 and the Th1/Th2 cytokine balance; a ScienceDirect-indexed study examined temporal dynamics of inflammation-related gene expression under Selank, but the full text was unavailable (HTTP 403) and is therefore not cited as primary evidence here [excluded per sourcing rules]. *Excluded-study traceability note: one immunomodulation study (ScienceDirect-indexed; inflammation-related gene expression under Selank) was identified during the search but returned HTTP 403 on access and could not be retrieved or verified; it is excluded from the evidence base per sourcing rules and noted here for traceability.*

### A.4 Human Clinical Evidence for Selank (the analog's only indirect evidence base)

Two Russian-language open-label comparative trials constitute the principal human evidence base for Selank:

- Zozulia et al. (2008) [4, rct]: 62 patients with GAD/neurasthenia; Selank vs. medazepam (a benzodiazepine); comparable anxiolytic efficacy on Hamilton and Zung scales; Selank showed additional antiasthenic and psychostimulant effects absent with medazepam; no sedation, tolerance, or dependence documented in the study period.
- Medvedev et al. (2014) [10, open_label]: 60 patients with phobic-anxiety and somatoform disorders (ICD-10 F40.2–F41.9, F45.0–1); Selank vs. phenazepam (a more potent Russian benzodiazepine); Selank showed pronounced anxiolytic and mild nootropic effects, with therapeutic effect persisting for approximately one week after the final dose.

**Evidence quality caveat:** Both studies were conducted at Russian institutions, published in Russian-language neurology/psychiatry journals (Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S S Korsakova), lack independent replication outside Russia, and would not meet current FDA or EMA RCT standards for primary anxiolytic registration (they lack placebo arms, blinding details are not specified in available abstracts, and n values are small). The evidence supports proof-of-concept but does not constitute the level of evidence required to establish efficacy in international regulatory contexts.
