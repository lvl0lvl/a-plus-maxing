## Section E — Concentration / origin audit, commercialization & grey-market realism

### E.1 Institutional lineage of the Selank evidence base

Selank (heptapeptide Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro) was synthesised at the **Institute of Molecular Genetics (IMG), Russian Academy of Sciences (RAS)**, under the direction of Nikolai F. Myasoedov and Lyudmila Andreeva [1, mechanism_review; 5, mechanism_review]. The entire foundational chain — from synthetic chemistry to preclinical pharmacology to Russian regulatory approval — flows through two Moscow institutions within the Russian Academy system: IMG RAS and the **V.V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology, Russian Academy of Medical Sciences (RAMS)** [5, mechanism_review; 6, rct].

**Concentration calculation:** Of the approximately 15 identifiable primary research groups/publications, 13–14 carry authorship from IMG RAS and/or the Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology (which co-developed Selank with IMG). The Medvedev/RUDN group (2 publications, both Russian-language clinical studies) represents the only clearly independent clinical lineage outside the developer institutions. This gives an **IMG RAS + Zakusov lineage share of approximately 87–93%** of the primary Selank evidence base. The concentration flag threshold (≥70% single institute/lineage) is exceeded with substantial margin. **Verdict: HIGH concentration. The Selank evidence base is not independent of its developer; it is almost entirely author-continuous with the inventing institution and its direct co-development partner.**

### E.2 N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate: analog-specific evidence absence

The N-acetyl, C-terminus-amidated analog of Selank — marketed as "N-Acetyl Selank Amidate," "NASA Selank," or "NA Selank Amidate" — has **zero analog-specific primary literature in PubMed or indexed scientific databases** [7, vendor_label; 8, anecdote_aggregate]. Multiple vendor-side sources explicitly acknowledge this: "Studies on N-acetyl Selank are lacking" and "no human studies have been performed on NA-Selank" [7, vendor_label; 8, anecdote_aggregate]. There are likewise no animal pharmacokinetic studies of the dual-modified analog published in indexed journals. The modifications (N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation) are claimed to improve enzymatic stability and blood-brain barrier penetration by structural analogy to other acetylated/amidated peptides — but this is a mechanistic hypothesis, not an empirically characterised pharmacokinetic profile for this specific molecule [7, vendor_label; 8, anecdote_aggregate].

**Concentration figure for the analog specifically: undefined / not applicable.** There is no analog-specific primary literature from which a concentration can even be computed. The "evidence base" for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate is 100% extrapolation from the parent Selank literature — which is itself highly concentrated in the developer institutional lineage.

### E.3 Russian registration and Western approval status

Selank (the unmodified parent) was registered by the Russian Federation Ministry of Health under the trade name Selanc (selank 0.15% nasal drops) for generalised anxiety disorder and neurasthenia, with registration finalised around 2009 following trials conducted entirely within RAMS-affiliated and government-linked Russian institutions [5, mechanism_review; 6, rct]. It is available in Russian and some CIS-country pharmacies as a prescription product.

**N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate holds no drug registration anywhere in the world.** The modified analog is not an approved pharmaceutical in Russia, the European Union, the United States, or any other jurisdiction. No pharmaceutical company — Western or Russian — has filed a development programme for this analog. There is no IND, NDA, EMA marketing authorisation, or equivalent for it [7, vendor_label; 8, anecdote_aggregate].

In the US regulatory context, Selank acetate (the parent, TP-7) was placed on the FDA's Category 2 interim 503A bulk substances list (significant safety risk designation, barring compounding-pharmacy use) and was removed from Category 2 in September 2024 after the nominator withdrew the nomination — it was not cleared; it moved to a pending-evaluation status awaiting PCAC review [9, vendor_label]. This timeline is drawn from a vendor-authored summary of public FDA regulatory actions (Newtropin, April 2026); the underlying FDA actions are publicly documented but are summarised here via a vendor secondary source, not directly from FDA.gov. N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate, as a chemically distinct entity, is not on any FDA compounding-review list and has no regulatory standing.

### E.4 Grey-market commercialisation and realism

N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate exists commercially as a **grey-market research-chemical product** sold by a proliferating set of US-based "research peptide" vendors (Umbrella Labs, Iron Mountain Labz, BC9.co, Limitless Life Nootropics, BioEdge Research Labs, Orovia BioLab, and others) [7, vendor_label; 8, anecdote_aggregate]. Products are offered as lyophilised powder vials and as pre-formulated nasal sprays, with volume and concentration varying by vendor. Vendors uniformly claim ≥99% HPLC purity and third-party Certificates of Analysis, though the independence and analytical rigour of those certificates is unverifiable from the outside — no centrally accessible, independently validated purity database exists for these products.

Key grey-market risk realities:

1. **Identity conflation.** Marketing materials routinely present N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate as equivalent to or superior to Selank by virtue of improved stability, transferring the parent's Russian clinical data to the analog without evidentiary basis. Buyers sourcing the product for anxiety or cognitive purposes may not distinguish the analog from the parent compound, and the vendor ecosystem does not consistently draw this distinction.

2. **Sterility and formulation.** Pre-formulated nasal sprays sold as research chemicals are not subject to compounding pharmacy sterility, stability, or endotoxin standards. The FDA's warning-letter enforcement against peptide vendors (including letters to US Chem Labs in February 2024 and Summit Research Peptides in December 2024) cited unsubstantiated drug claims and dosing guidance rendering these products unapproved new drugs and misbranded [9, vendor_label]. (The warning letters are publicly indexed on FDA.gov; [9] is a vendor-authored secondary summary of those public actions.)

3. **Language barrier in the evidence base.** The entire foundational Selank clinical literature is in Russian (published in *Zhurnal Nevrologii i Psikhiatrii im. S.S. Korsakova*) and was conducted inside the Russian regulatory system. Western buyers extrapolating to an unmodified analog are relying on a body of work they typically cannot read, produced by institutions they cannot independently audit, for a different molecule than the one actually studied [6, rct].

4. **No independent replication.** The Selank clinical programme (approximately 200–400 total patients across ~3 published human trials) has never been independently replicated by a non-Russian, non-developer group in a Western regulatory context. The analog adds a further layer of extrapolation on top of an already-unverified foundation.
