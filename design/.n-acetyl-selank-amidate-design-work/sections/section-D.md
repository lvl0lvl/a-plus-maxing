## Section D — Safety, dosing, route, PK, regulatory & monitoring

---

### D.1 Safety — What the Evidence Actually Covers

**The critical framing first:** There is NO direct safety data for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate. Every safety inference in this section is extrapolated from the parent compound Selank (heptapeptide TKPRPGP). The acetylation at the N-terminus and C-terminal amidation alter enzymatic recognition sites and are claimed to extend half-life relative to Selank; whether these modifications change the adverse-effect profile is unstudied. This extrapolation gap is not a minor caveat — it is the central safety unknown for this compound.

**Selank's clinical safety record (the extrapolation source):**

Three small Russian clinical trials form the entire published human safety record for Selank. In a 62-patient RCT comparing Selank to medazepam (benzodiazepine) in generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia, Selank demonstrated anxiolytic effects comparable to medazepam while showing additional antiasthenic and psychostimulant properties; the abstract does not report specific adverse events, suggesting none were notable enough to headline [1, rct]. In a 60-patient comparative trial against phenazepam (another benzodiazepine), Selank showed "pronounced anxiolytic and mild nootropic effects" with therapeutic benefit persisting approximately one week post-treatment; the study compared "tolerability" but the abstract does not specify adverse event rates [2, open_label]. A third trial combining Selank with phenazepam (70 patients: 30 monotherapy, 40 combination) found that adding Selank to phenazepam reduced the benzodiazepine's own adverse effects — including attention/memory impairment, asthenia, sedation, increased sleep duration, sexual disturbances, emotional indifference, and orthostatic effects — with quality-of-life improvement measured by SF-36 and cognitive assessment by Stroop/verbal fluency tests; tolerability was assessed with the UKU scale [3, rct].

The consistent pattern across these trials: Selank is reported as well-tolerated, non-sedating compared to benzodiazepines, and apparently non-addictive at studied doses. A 2026 review in *Int J Mol Sci* summarizes Selank as "non-sedative, non-addictive" with uncommon adverse effects described as mild daytime drowsiness or dry mouth, and notes it has been used "for weeks to months with no reported complications" [4, mechanism_review] (MDPI review, lower-trust).

**Fundamental limitations of this safety record:**
- All trials are Russian-conducted, small (n ≤ 70), and published in Russian-language journals not subject to Western regulatory-trial standards
- No long-term safety data (> weeks) exists in any published source
- No placebo-controlled safety analysis with pre-specified AE reporting has been published
- No Western regulatory safety data exists (FDA, EMA)
- Population studied: Russian clinical patients with anxiety/neurasthenia — not necessarily representative of broader populations

**Cardiovascular safety (animal data):** An acute study in anaesthetized cats using intravenous Selank at 300 μg/kg found a transient blood pressure decrease of approximately 32% in the first 1–3 minutes post-injection, normalizing thereafter; heart rate and respiratory rate were unaffected; cerebral blood flow increased ~24% transiently [5, animal]. This was an acute i.v. study in a terminal animal model and does not directly inform the clinical intranasal route, but provides the only formal cardiovascular safety signal data available.

### D.2 Pharmacokinetics — Selank and the Modification Claims

**Selank PK (verified):** Following intranasal administration in mice, Selank's primary metabolites are the pentapeptide TKPRP, tripeptide TKP, and dipeptides RP and GP — products of sequential enzymatic cleavage [6, animal]. Intranasal delivery was established as "optimal for the delivery of glyproline molecules in the CNS" across a four-route comparison (intranasal, intraperitoneal, intragastric, intravenous) in rats [7, animal]. Route-dependent pharmacological differences are documented: intranasal Selank affects NMDA receptor density (+23% binding sites in frontal cortex), while intraperitoneal administration preferentially affects GABAergic signaling (+38% GABA receptor binding in frontal cortex) — indicating distinct biotransformation pathways by route [8, animal]. The peptide reaches brain tissue via intranasal delivery and can be tracked by metabolite profiles in brain regions.

**Half-life context:** No published half-life value for Selank appears in peer-reviewed sources identified in this search. Selank is a heptapeptide subject to rapid enzymatic degradation by tissue peptidases; the rapid metabolite profile (pentapeptide, tripeptide, dipeptide) confirmed in brain tissue [6] implies a short effective half-life for the intact heptapeptide.

**N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate modification claim:** The N-terminal acetylation blocks aminopeptidase cleavage at the N-terminus; C-terminal amidation blocks carboxypeptidase cleavage, together protecting both termini from the primary enzymatic attack points. This is the structural rationale for claimed extended half-life compared to parent Selank. This rationale is biochemically plausible and consistent with known peptide modification pharmacology — but it is UNVERIFIED in any published PK study for this specific analog. No pharmacokinetic data (half-life, Cmax, Tmax, bioavailability) exists in the published literature for N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate.

### D.3 Dosing and Route — Studied vs. Grey-Market

**Selank (studied doses and route):**

The intranasal route is Selank's registered formulation and the route studied in human trials. Doses referenced in the animal studies include 0.3 mg/kg/day administered intraperitoneally in rats [9, animal; 8, animal]; human trial doses are not specified in available English-language abstracts — the full Russian-language text would be required for exact clinical doses. The registered Russian formulation is an intranasal drop solution. A single i.v. dose of 300 μg/kg was used in the cardiovascular safety cat study [5, animal]; this is an acute research dose, not a therapeutic one.

**N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate (grey-market conventions — NOT grounded in any study):**

Grey-market sources and practitioner community reports describe subcutaneous injection or nasal spray as the two common routes for this analog. Subcutaneous injection is favored in some practitioner protocols because the analog's modified structure is claimed to survive gastrointestinal enzymatic attack less poorly than unmodified Selank but better than oral biologics generally. The claimed longer half-life vs. Selank is the stated rationale for preferring this analog. **No dosing number from any vendor or community source can be cited as a grounded figure** — this section intentionally does not report a grey-market dose range because vendor_label and anecdote_aggregate sources cannot ground a number per this document's sourcing rules.

**Route-specific caution for the analog:** If subcutaneous injection is used, sterility is entirely dependent on the manufacturing quality of an unregulated research-chemical supplier. Unlike the registered Selank nasal drop, N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate has no pharmaceutical-grade manufacturing standard, no lot-testing requirement, and no regulatory body verifying identity, purity, or sterility. Injection of a grey-market peptide carries risk of contamination, incorrect identity, incorrect concentration, and immune-mediated injection-site reactions that are absent from the Selank clinical record.

### D.4 Regulatory Status

**Selank — Russia:** Selank is a registered medicine in the Russian Federation, approved for anxiety disorders and neurasthenia. The 2026 review in *Int J Mol Sci* confirms it is "approved in Russia for the treatment of anxiety" [4, mechanism_review] (MDPI review, lower-trust); a 2026 orthopedics review in *J Am Acad Orthop Surg Glob Res Rev* similarly describes Selank as "approved in Russia" [10, mechanism_review]. Direct registry verification via the Russian Federal State Registry of Medicines (GRLS, grls.rosminzdrav.ru) was attempted but the search interface returned only navigation structure without retrievable registration records in this session — the registration claim rests on the two peer-reviewed review articles cited.

**Selank — FDA/EMA:** Selank is NOT approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration or the European Medicines Agency. It has no Investigational New Drug (IND) application on record in the FDA's publicly searchable drug database. It cannot be legally compounded under 503A/503B in the United States because it is not an FDA-approved active pharmaceutical ingredient and is not on the FDA's bulk drug substances list for compounding.

**N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate:** NOT approved anywhere in the world. It is a research chemical / grey-market product with no registration in any national medicine authority, no pharmaceutical-grade specifications, and no marketing authorization. In the United States it occupies the "research chemical" grey zone — not a scheduled controlled substance (no DEA Schedule listing identified), but also not a legally marketable drug for human use. Purchasing, possessing, and self-administering it occurs outside any regulatory framework.

**WADA status — two independent layers of uncertainty:**

**Layer 1 — Source confirmation uncertainty:** A 2026 peer-reviewed review in *Int J Mol Sci* (PMC13164565) states that "WADA prohibits both [Semax and Selank], classifying them as peptides acting on the hormonal axis (category S2) or unapproved substances, for their potential neuroenhancement effect" [4, mechanism_review] (MDPI review, lower-trust). This claim cites reference 85 in that article; the full reference 85 was not accessible in the truncated reference list retrieved, and direct access to WADA's 2025 Prohibited List PDF returned empty content in this session. The prohibition claim must therefore be treated as: reported in a 2026 peer-reviewed source but NOT independently confirmed against the primary WADA document in this research run.

**Layer 2 — Category-assignment uncertainty (S2 vs. S0):** Even if Selank/this analog is prohibited, characterizing it as S2 ("Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics") is categorically non-obvious. WADA S2 was designed for compounds that modulate the hormonal axis — EPO, growth hormone, IGF-1, and their releasing factors. Selank is a GABAergic/anxiolytic heptapeptide derived from tuftsin with no documented hormonal axis activity; applying S2 to it requires a stretch of the category's stated rationale. The far more probable bucket for any non-approved research peptide with no regulatory standing is **WADA S0 ("Non-Approved Substances")**, which prohibits in-competition use of any pharmacological substance not approved by any governmental regulatory authority for human therapeutic use — a description that unambiguously covers N-Acetyl-Selank-Amidate. S0 applies regardless of whether S2 does or does not. The review's ambiguous "S2 or unapproved substances" framing should not be read as establishing S2 as the operative category; it may simply reflect that the review authors were uncertain themselves.

**Athlete guidance:** Any individual subject to anti-doping rules should assume prohibition is possible and verify directly with their National Anti-Doping Organization (NADO) or via GlobalDRO.com before use. Do not rely on this document or the MDPI review's S2 framing as a definitive categorization.

### D.5 Contraindications, Monitoring, and Stopping Criteria

**Absolute contraindications (extrapolated from Selank's mechanism and standard peptide drug principles):**
- Pregnancy and lactation: No safety data; contraindicated by default for any unregistered investigational compound
- Known hypersensitivity to Selank, tuftsin, or any component of grey-market formulations
- Concurrent use with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants without medical supervision: Selank potentiates GABAergic signaling; pharmacodynamic interaction possible even if additive sedation is reportedly mild in clinical studies [3, rct]
- Active psychiatric conditions requiring monitored pharmacotherapy (anxiety disorders, PTSD, depression): Self-administration of an unregistered research chemical in lieu of evidence-based care carries risks beyond the compound itself

**Monitoring — the honest reality:**

There is no validated efficacy or safety biomarker for Selank or this analog. No peer-reviewed protocol specifies laboratory monitoring for Selank use. The following are sensible minimum-safety suggestions based on general principles, not on a published monitoring protocol. The objective markers listed below (hs-CRP, cortisol, HRV) are safety-and-mechanism-adjacent surveillance adjuncts — none is a validated efficacy endpoint for anxiolytic or nootropic claims.

- *Symptom diary:* Track anxiety scores (e.g., GAD-7), cognition, sleep quality, and any adverse effects weekly; the one-week post-treatment persistence effect [2, open_label] means benefit and adverse effects may outlast dosing periods
- *Blood pressure:* Given the transient hypotensive signal in the animal cardiovascular study [5, animal], baseline and periodic BP checks are prudent, especially in users with baseline hypotension or concurrent antihypertensives
- *General safety labs at baseline:* CBC, CMP (renal and hepatic function); not because hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity has been identified but because no human safety data rules it out, and a baseline establishes reference values if symptoms emerge
- *Injection-site assessment (if subcutaneous route):* Check for redness, induration, or abscess at each injection site; any sign of infection is a stopping criterion given the sterility uncertainty of grey-market supplies
- *Immunogenicity surveillance (subcutaneous route):* Repeated subcutaneous injection of a modified peptide with no endogenous counterpart carries an immune-sensitization risk not present in intranasal Selank use and not addressed in any published safety data. The N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation create a molecular surface not recognized by normal self-tolerance. Progressive injection-site reactions (increasing induration, urticaria, or systemic symptoms with repeated dosing) should be treated as a possible immune response — not merely a sterility failure — and constitute an absolute stopping criterion.
- *Mental health monitoring:* The "psychostimulant" effect noted in one RCT [1, rct] could theoretically trigger or unmask anxiety or agitation in susceptible individuals; if anxiety worsens acutely, discontinue and reassess
- *Population-direction reversal risk (healthy users specifically):* The psychostimulant/activating effect documented in the RCT [1, rct] is THERAPEUTIC in anxiety patients — it counteracts benzodiazepine-induced sedation and asthenia. In healthy off-label users without baseline anxiety disorder, this same activation signal may instead manifest as unwanted anxiety, sleep disruption, or agitation. This is a population-direction-reversal: what is beneficial in the clinical population (reduced sedation) is an adverse effect in a healthy user seeking anxiolysis. Monitor explicitly for activation effects that were absent before use.
- *[[biomarkers/hs-crp]] (hs-CRP) — objective inflammatory/immune-sensitization surveillance:* The immunogenicity risk from repeated subcutaneous injection of a terminally modified non-endogenous peptide is the most specific safety signal for this route of administration. hs-CRP is the appropriate objective inflammatory marker to track this risk: a rising hs-CRP trend on the injection schedule — absent intercurrent infection or another identifiable cause — is the earliest laboratory signal of immune-sensitization or subclinical inflammatory response to the peptide. Baseline hs-CRP before first injection, rechecked at 4–6 weeks of repeated dosing, is the minimum surveillance window. This is an immune-safety adjunct for a risk the Selank clinical trials (intranasal route, no terminal modifications) never had occasion to study — not an efficacy marker.
- *[[biomarkers/cortisol]] — HPA/stress-axis mechanism-adjacent surveillance:* Selank's anxiolytic mechanism operates partly on the GABAergic and enkephalinergic axes, both of which interact with HPA-axis stress responses. Cortisol is not a validated efficacy biomarker for Selank; the Selank literature does not track it. As a mechanism-adjacent marker of the HPA/stress axis the anxiolytic claim implicates, morning serum or salivary cortisol provides an objective correlate of stress-axis state. Significant HPA disruption (suppression or elevation trending outside normal range) during the dosing period would be a clinically relevant observation, particularly given the healthy-user population-direction-reversal risk. Surveillance adjunct only, not an efficacy endpoint.
- *[[biomarkers/hrv]] (Heart Rate Variability) — autonomic/anxiety surveillance:* HRV is a non-invasive index of autonomic nervous system balance correlated with anxiety states, GABAergic tone, and parasympathetic activity — all relevant to Selank's proposed mechanism axis. Tracking HRV via consumer wearable (resting-state HRV at wake) provides an objective, time-series window on autonomic/anxiety state changes over the dosing period. Clinically meaningful HRV deterioration during use would be an early objective signal complementing the subjective GAD-7 diary. Autonomic surveillance adjunct, not a validated efficacy biomarker for Selank or any anxiolytic peptide.

**Stopping criteria (symptom-based, none biomarker-validated):**
- Worsening anxiety or emergence of panic symptoms
- Any injection-site infection signs (fever, redness, purulence)
- Systemic signs following injection (urticaria, flushing, dyspnea — anaphylaxis cannot be excluded for any peptide in a sensitized individual)
- Significant sedation or cognitive slowing inconsistent with reported Selank profile (raises concern about product identity/contamination)
- Any neurological symptoms not present at baseline
- If WADA prohibition is confirmed and the individual is subject to anti-doping rules: immediate cessation and NADO consultation
