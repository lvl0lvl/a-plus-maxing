# Rubric — hrv (heart rate variability, WEARABLE biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).
**WEARABLE-ADAPTED SHAPE:** this is a `category: wearable`, `source: wearable`, device-derived metric — NOT a serum lab analyte. Section C is **device validity/accuracy** (NOT assay standardization); Section B emphasizes **individual-baseline + trend** (NOT a population reference interval, which is far less useful for HRV). No serum units.

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — What HRV Is & What the Device Measures:** **Heart rate variability (HRV)** = the beat-to-beat variation in the time interval between successive heartbeats (the **R-R / inter-beat interval**), reflecting **autonomic nervous system** modulation of the sinoatrial node — predominantly **parasympathetic (vagal) tone**, with sympathetic + respiratory (respiratory sinus arrhythmia) influences. **The metrics (load-bearing):** **time-domain — rMSSD** (root mean square of successive differences; the most vagally-mediated + the most device-robust metric), **SDNN** (overall variability); **frequency-domain — HF** (high-frequency, ~parasympathetic), **LF** (low-frequency, mixed), and the contested **LF/HF ratio** (NOT a clean sympatho-vagal-balance index — note the caveat). Units **milliseconds (ms)** (rMSSD/SDNN) or normalized units / "HRV score" (proprietary). **What WEARABLES measure:** most consumer devices derive HRV from **photoplethysmography (PPG)** (optical, wrist/finger) or chest-strap **ECG/electrical**, typically during **sleep or a controlled morning reading**; they usually report **nocturnal rMSSD or a derived "recovery/readiness" score**. The physiology: HIGHER HRV generally indicates greater parasympathetic/recovery capacity + cardiovascular fitness, but it is highly context- + individual-dependent (a higher number is not universally "better"; interpret against one's own baseline).
- **B — Interpretation, Values & The Individual-Baseline Principle (load-bearing):** **THE LOAD-BEARING WEARABLE CONCEPT: HRV is enormously INTER-INDIVIDUAL + strongly age- and sex-dependent → a single population "normal range" is far LESS useful than each person's PERSONAL BASELINE and its TREND/deviation.** Give the rough population context (rMSSD spans ~tens of ms with a very wide spread; HRV DECLINES with age; sex differences) but emphasize that **the actionable signal is the individual's rolling baseline + day-to-day deviation (a coefficient-of-variation / how far below baseline)**, NOT crossing a fixed cutoff. The **readiness/recovery framing** (devices flag "low HRV vs your baseline" → reduced recovery/readiness). The morning/nocturnal measurement standard + the importance of CONSISTENT conditions (same time, posture, device). Why absolute cross-person comparisons mislead (different devices/algorithms/conditions). Trends over days-weeks matter more than any single reading.
- **C — Measurement & Device Validity (load-bearing — replaces "assay standardization"):** **PPG (optical) vs ECG (the gold standard):** ECG measures the true R-R from the electrical signal; PPG measures the **pulse-to-pulse interval (peripheral, pulse-rate variability)** — a good but imperfect proxy that **degrades with motion + during the day; rMSSD is the most PPG-robust HRV metric** (validation studies). The **validity literature:** consumer wearables (Apple Watch, Garmin, Oura, WHOOP, Polar chest straps, Fitbit) show **generally good agreement with ECG at rest / during sleep for rMSSD/mean R-R**, with accuracy DEGRADING during movement + at higher heart rates (cite named validation studies + meta/systematic reviews). **The dominant message: consistency of measurement conditions (same device, time, posture, state) matters MORE than the absolute device choice; cross-device + cross-day comparisons under different conditions are unreliable.** No universal standardization/reference-material exists (unlike a lab assay) — devices use proprietary algorithms + report proprietary scores; reproducibility/test-retest; the artifact-correction problem (ectopic beats). Note the metric is NOT a regulated clinical measurement on consumer devices (most are wellness, not diagnostic).
- **D — Determinants & Significance:** **What RAISES HRV:** **aerobic/endurance fitness + regular training (chronic adaptation — higher vagal tone)**, good sleep, younger age, recovery/parasympathetic states. **What LOWERS HRV (the actionable signals):** **acute physical/psychological stress; overtraining / under-recovery / high training load; ILLNESS / infection (an HRV drop often PRECEDES symptoms by a day — used as an early-illness flag); ALCOHOL (a large acute overnight HRV suppression — one of the most reproducible findings); poor/short sleep; dehydration; aging (HRV declines with age); some medications (beta-blockers raise, others vary)**. **SIGNIFICANCE / use:** (1) **the prognostic/epidemiologic evidence — LOW HRV is associated with increased cardiovascular morbidity + all-cause mortality** in population + clinical cohorts (cite NAMED cohorts/meta — but honest: mostly from clinical short-ECG HRV, not consumer wearables; associational); (2) **training-readiness / HRV-guided training** — using daily HRV vs baseline to modulate training load; **HRV-guided training RCTs show comparable or modestly better fitness outcomes vs fixed plans** (cite verified RCTs/meta — honest about effect size); (3) stress/recovery monitoring. **Limitations (load-bearing):** the individual-baseline requirement (a single value is nearly uninterpretable without context); heavy confounding (sleep, alcohol, position, hydration, measurement conditions); consumer-device HRV is a WELLNESS metric, NOT a diagnostic test; the population-prognostic data are largely from clinical ECG-HRV not wearables; LF/HF is not a clean autonomic-balance index; interpret as a TREND with the clinical/training picture.
