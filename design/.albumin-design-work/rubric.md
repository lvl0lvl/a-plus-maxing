# Rubric — albumin (serum albumin, biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** serum **albumin** = the most abundant plasma protein (~50-60% of total plasma protein, ~35-50 g/L), synthesized EXCLUSIVELY by the **liver** (hepatocytes, ~10-15 g/day); functions — the principal determinant of **plasma oncotic/colloid pressure** (~75-80% of it), a **transport carrier** (free fatty acids, bilirubin, calcium, hormones [thyroid/cortisol fraction], many drugs — affecting free-drug fractions), **antioxidant** + thiol/redox buffer, acid-base; long plasma **half-life ~3 weeks (~19-21 days)** — so it reflects synthesis over WEEKS, not acutely; distributed ~40% intravascular / ~60% extravascular (interstitial); a **NEGATIVE acute-phase reactant** (falls in inflammation — IL-6-driven hepatic reprioritization); the determinants of serum level = synthesis (liver, nutrition/amino-acid supply) − losses (renal/GI) − dilution (volume) − redistribution (capillary leak) − catabolism.
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Interpretation:** conventional adult reference **~3.5-5.0 g/dL (35-50 g/L)** (method + age dependent — lower in elderly, pregnancy [dilution], children); units **g/dL (US) ↔ g/L (×10)**; **hypoalbuminemia** thresholds (<3.5 g/dL mild; <2.5 g/dL marked → edema/ascites risk); the interpretation framework — a LOW albumin is rarely primary protein deficiency alone; it integrates liver synthetic function + inflammation + nutrition + protein loss + volume status; **hyperalbuminemia** is almost always **dehydration/hemoconcentration** (or prolonged tourniquet) — not a true excess; the role in composite scores (Child-Pugh for cirrhosis; the albumin-bilirubin "ALBI" grade; part of nutritional indices [GNRI, PNI]); corrected calcium (albumin-adjusted calcium); the anion-gap albumin correction.
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** the dye-binding assays — **bromocresol green (BCG)** vs **bromocresol purple (BCP)** — the LOAD-BEARING measurement issue: **BCG OVER-estimates albumin** (it binds other proteins, esp. at low albumin / in CKD), **BCP reads lower + can UNDER-read in renal failure**; the two methods are NOT interchangeable → method-specific reference intervals + a systematic BCG-BCP bias (quantify, ~2-5 g/L typical); immunoassay/immunonephelometry as the more specific reference; the IFCC/standardization status + the certified reference material; interferences (hemolysis, lipemia, icterus, certain drugs); **urine albumin (albuminuria/ACR)** as a DISTINCT measurement (immunoassay; the kidney-damage marker — cross-link to eGFR/CKD but note it is a different analyte from serum albumin); sample considerations (posture/tourniquet → hemoconcentration).
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **what LOWERS serum albumin (hypoalbuminemia — the common, clinically important direction):**
  - **Decreased synthesis:** chronic liver disease/cirrhosis (synthetic-function marker — Child-Pugh/ALBI), malnutrition/protein-energy undernutrition, malabsorption.
  - **INFLAMMATION (the dominant real-world driver):** albumin is a NEGATIVE acute-phase reactant — acute + chronic inflammation (infection, sepsis, trauma, surgery, cancer, chronic disease) lowers it via IL-6 hepatic reprioritization + capillary leak (transcapillary escape) — so a low albumin in an ill patient is more an inflammation/severity marker than a nutrition marker (correct a common misconception).
  - **Protein loss:** nephrotic syndrome (urinary), protein-losing enteropathy (GI), extensive burns/skin loss.
  - **Dilution/redistribution:** fluid overload, capillary leak (sepsis), pregnancy.
  - **what RAISES:** essentially only **dehydration/hemoconcentration** (relative).
  - **PROGNOSTIC SIGNIFICANCE (load-bearing):** serum albumin is one of the strongest routine predictors of **all-cause + cardiovascular mortality, surgical/hospital outcomes, and frailty** across populations — cite NAMED cohorts/meta-analyses with effect sizes (e.g. the classic Goldwasser & Feldman / Phillips albumin-mortality data, or a large modern cohort/meta — VERIFY). Note the association is heavily confounded by inflammation/illness severity (it is a marker, not necessarily causal).
  - **limitations:** the ~3-week half-life makes it a POOR marker of acute nutritional change (slow to move); it is non-specific (integrates liver/inflammation/loss/volume); the BCG/BCP method dependence; should be interpreted with CRP (inflammation), liver enzymes, and the clinical picture.
