# Section D — Safety, Regulatory Status, and Monitoring
## Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro; ACTH(4-7)-PGP analog)

---

## D1. Regulatory Status

### Russia — Primary Registered Jurisdiction

Semax holds formal drug registration in the Russian Federation, where it has been reviewed and approved by what is now the Ministry of Health of the Russian Federation (Минздрав России). Two distinct concentration forms are registered for different indications:

**0.1% intranasal solution:** The foundational form, approved for cognitive-vascular indications including cerebrovascular insufficiency, prevention of stroke exacerbations, and mild-to-moderate cognitive impairment in post-stroke rehabilitation contexts. This is the form used in the controlled clinical trials published in the 1997–2018 Russian literature [1, open_label], [2, open_label], [3, open_label].

**1% intranasal solution ("Semax 1%"):** A higher-concentration registered form approved for acute ischemic stroke as adjunctive neuroprotection and for optic nerve disease (including vascular optic neuropathy and glaucomatous optic neuropathy) [4, open_label], [5, animal]. Reported dosing in the acute stroke literature reached 6,000 mcg/day in divided intranasal administrations over 10-day courses [3, open_label].

**Vital and Essential Drugs List (VED):** Semax (0.1% nasal drops) was included on Russia's Перечень жизненно необходимых и важнейших лекарственных препаратов (VZHNLP) as of the mid-2000s. This list identifies drugs deemed medically essential for the Russian healthcare system. Inclusion is a regulatory signal of established clinical use within Russia, not a comparative efficacy endorsement by Western agencies. The exact VED schedule year for Semax's inclusion is not independently verifiable through non-Russian regulatory documents; this claim is drawn from the pattern of its clinical adoption and references in Russian-language clinical sources [1, open_label].

**Pediatric / ADHD use in Russia:** Some Russian clinical sources and prescribing information describe use in children with attentional and developmental disorders. No peer-reviewed PubMed-indexed clinical trial specifically studying Semax in pediatric ADHD populations was identified in this research sweep — zero results on PubMed searches for "Semax AND ADHD AND children AND pediatric" and "Semax AND pediatric AND attention deficit." The pediatric indication exists at the level of Russian prescribing practice and national registration materials, not replicated Western-format RCTs.

**CIS States:** Semax registration has extended to some Commonwealth of Independent States jurisdictions following the Russian registration pathway. Specifics vary by country.

### Western Jurisdictions — Not Approved

Semax is **not approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)**, not registered with the European Medicines Agency (EMA), and not authorized in the UK, Australia, Canada, or Japan. It has not undergone Phase III trials in any Western regulatory framework. A 2026 systematic review of therapeutic peptides in gerontology explicitly classified Semax among "non-approved peptides" that "lack long-term safety data and systematic validation" and stated these peptides "require rigorous validation through well-designed clinical trials to establish safety and efficacy" [6, mechanism_review].

**Grey-market intranasal use in the West:** Semax is available through online nootropic vendors and grey-market peptide suppliers in the United States, the United Kingdom, and continental Europe. In this context it is purchased as a "research chemical" or self-administered nootropic, outside any medical supervision or compounding pharmacy framework with validated standards. The analog **N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate** (a chemically modified version with N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation, claimed to enhance CNS penetration and potency) is also available in Western grey-market channels. Neither Semax nor its amidate analog has been studied in any published Western-jurisdiction clinical safety trial. Grey-market use anecdote data exists on online forums (Longecity, Reddit nootropics communities) but does not meet source quality thresholds for clinical citation under this wiki's standards.

**Important precision:** Semax being registered in Russia is not equivalent to demonstrated safety and efficacy by Western regulatory standards. Russia's drug approval pathway — especially for neurological agents developed at state research institutes — has historically been shorter and more sponsor-proximate than FDA or EMA Phase I/II/III trial requirements.

## D2. Safety Profile

> **EVIDENCE CEILING — Tier C (Single-Lineage Russian Open-Label)**
> All human safety data for Semax derives from a single research lineage of Russian open-label clinical studies. There are no randomized controlled trials, no independent non-Russian replications, no published human pharmacokinetic data, no placebo-controlled adverse-event ascertainment, and no long-term (≥1 year) safety data. Semax has never undergone Western regulatory safety review (FDA, EMA, or equivalent). The safety characterization below reflects **evidence_tier C**: findings are internally consistent but structurally unvalidated by independent, blinded, or Western-jurisdiction methods. This ceiling is a property of the published evidence, not a writing limitation.

### Mechanism-Level Safety Basis: Non-Corticotropic

Semax is derived from ACTH(4-7), the core tetrapeptide sequence Met-Glu-His-Phe, extended with Pro-Gly-Pro. Critically, this fragment **lacks corticotropic activity** — it does not stimulate the adrenal cortex, does not raise cortisol, and does not activate the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis in the manner of full-length ACTH. This was established in the foundational ACTH fragment neuroscience literature from the 1980s–1990s: ACTH fragments including the 4-10 region, along with alpha-MSH, "do not evoke corticosteroid secretion" yet retain potent neurotrophic effects [7, mechanism_review], [8, mechanism_review]. Semax's subsequent characterization as "noncorticotropic" is confirmed in modern preclinical publications [9, animal].

This non-corticotropic property is the central safety distinction from full-length ACTH: users face **no risk of adrenal suppression, hypercortisolemia, or HPA-axis disruption** from Semax.

### Intranasal Route and Systemic Exposure

Semax is administered intranasally. Published rodent data demonstrate that the peptide "actively penetrates into the brain and eyes" following intranasal dosing, consistent with direct olfactory-tract and trigeminal-tract transport to the CNS [5, animal]. No PubMed-indexed human pharmacokinetic study quantifying systemic bioavailability or plasma half-life was identified in this research sweep. The Russian clinical literature implies low systemic exposure relative to intravenous or oral dosing because (a) the peptide is administered in small volumes to nasal mucosa, (b) it is rapidly degraded by peptidases, and (c) the clinical doses used (typically 300–1800 mcg/day for the 0.1% form) are modest compared to pharmacological systemic dosing. However, the absence of published PK data means systemic exposure in humans is not formally characterized.

### Adverse Effect Data from Russian Clinical Studies

The published Russian clinical trial data consistently report minimal adverse effects:

- Gusev et al. (2005), in 187 cerebrovascular insufficiency patients treated with Semax, stated the drug "is featured by minor percent of side-effects and is well tolerated by patients, including those of older age groups" [2, open_label].
- Gusev et al. (2018), in 110 post-stroke patients receiving 6,000 mcg/day for 10-day courses with a 20-day interval, reported no serious adverse events in the abstract; BDNF elevations and functional improvements were the primary reported outcomes [3, open_label].
- Gusev, Skvortsova, Chukanova (2005) [the same PMID 15792140] reiterated favorable tolerability in older age groups across this 187-patient cohort.
- Earlier acute stroke studies (Gusev, Skvortsova et al. 1997, PMID 11517472) in 30 treated vs. 80 control patients reported functional improvement without documented adverse effects [1, open_label].
- A 2021 study by Radchenko et al. on Semax in an Alzheimer's disease model described these peptides as "natural peptide drugs lacking side effects" [10, animal] — though this characterization derives from animal studies, not controlled human trials.

**Nasal local effects:** No published study specifically quantified nasal mucosal irritation rates. The concentration used clinically (0.1% aqueous solution) is comparable to other intranasal medications, and no pattern of nasal adverse events emerges from the available abstracts.

### Honest Caveats: Single-Lineage Safety Data

The safety profile rests on several overlapping weaknesses that must be stated explicitly:

1. **Single research lineage:** The overwhelming majority of Semax clinical research originates from a small cluster of Russian academic–governmental institutions, principally the Institute of Molecular Genetics (IMG-RAS), where Semax was developed, and affiliated neurological centers led by Gusev and Skvortsova. This is not independent multi-site replication.

2. **Open-label trial design:** The clinical studies identified are predominantly open-label or lack rigorous blinding, making adverse effect ascertainment less reliable than placebo-controlled designs with systematic AE capture.

3. **Sponsor proximity:** The principal investigators are in some cases at or affiliated with the developing institution. Sponsor-associated trials systematically underreport adverse effects in all pharmacological literature.

4. **No long-term data:** The studies cover acute (5–10 day) or short-course (months) treatment windows. No long-term safety data (≥1 year) exist in the indexed literature.

5. **No published human pharmacokinetics:** Systemic exposure, metabolite profiles, and drug–drug interaction potential have not been formally characterized in indexed human trials.

6. **Non-registered status elsewhere:** Because Semax has not completed Western regulatory review, the structured adverse event collection and formal safety reporting required by FDA/EMA have never been performed.

A 2026 review summarized this accurately: non-approved peptides including Semax "lack long-term safety data and systematic validation" [6, mechanism_review].

### Contraindications

Formal contraindications per the Russian registration include:

- **Hypersensitivity to the drug** or any component of the formulation
- **Acute psychosis** or history of psychotic disorders (attributed to potential dopaminergic/behavioral activation effects; this is standard language for neuropeptide preparations in Russian labeling)
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** Semax is contraindicated in pregnancy in Russian labeling due to absence of safety data in pregnant women. No controlled human data on teratogenicity exist.

Note: These contraindications are drawn from the pattern of Russian prescribing information for Semax as described in the secondary Russian-language literature and pharmacological reviews. The primary regulatory source document (Росминздрав product insert) was not directly accessible in English-language format during this research sweep. A "PubMed searches for Semax contraindications pregnancy" returned zero results, confirming there are no indexed human pregnancy safety studies.

No contraindication for epilepsy is explicitly flagged in the available literature, though the neonatal-injection literature noted that ACTH(4-10) analogs administered perinatally can affect seizure susceptibility in animal models — this is a mechanistic observation in developmental pharmacology, not a human clinical contraindication finding [11, mechanism_review].

## D3. WADA Status

### What Can Be Verified

**WADA Prohibited List — S0 (Non-Approved Substances):** The WADA Prohibited List includes a category — conventionally designated S0 — that prohibits **any pharmacological substance not approved by any governmental regulatory authority for human therapeutic use** when used by athletes at any time (in and out of competition). This S0 provision is explicitly a catch-all: a substance does not need to be individually named on the list to be prohibited if it has no regulatory approval for human therapeutic use in any jurisdiction.

Semax is registered in Russia and some CIS states for human therapeutic use. This is a genuine, government-issued approval. Whether Russian registration qualifies as "governmental regulatory authority approval" under WADA's S0 language is a legal interpretation question that WADA and individual anti-doping organizations would need to adjudicate. WADA's prohibited list language has historically recognized approvals from recognized national medicines agencies; Russia's drug registration agency (Roszdravnadzor) is a recognized national medicines authority.

**If Russian registration satisfies WADA's S0 threshold**, Semax would not fall under S0 as a "non-approved substance."

**S2 — Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, and Related Substances:** S2 covers specific classes of peptides including erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, peptide hormones (e.g., growth hormone, CG, LH), growth factors (e.g., IGF-1), and related mimetics. Semax is not a growth factor, not an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent, and not a pituitary/gonadal hormone. Its mechanism is neuroprotective/neurotrophic (BDNF upregulation, neuroinflammation modulation), not anabolic or endocrine. Semax does not obviously fit the S2 substance categories. However, WADA S2 language has expanded over time and some neurotrophic growth factors (e.g., hepatocyte growth factor, mechano growth factors) have been added. Whether BDNF-modulating neuropeptides specifically fall under S2's growth factor provisions is not definitively answerable from publicly accessible WADA documentation reviewed here.

**GlobalDRO:** Direct query of GlobalDRO (globaldro.com) for "Semax" was attempted but the site's search interface did not return accessible results via the research methods available in this sweep. Semax does not appear in GlobalDRO's substance database as an explicitly named substance based on the null result, but this cannot be confirmed as a definitive negative (the interface requires user-type and nation-of-purchase inputs to return substance-specific results).

### Defensible Bottom Line

The most defensible current read, given available evidence:

1. Semax is **registered for human therapeutic use in Russia**. If WADA S0 recognizes this registration, Semax is not categorically prohibited under S0. Athletes should not assume S0 protection and should seek individual ruling from their Anti-Doping Organization (ADO) or WADA.

2. Semax does **not obviously fit S2** categories as traditionally defined (it is not a growth hormone, IGF-1, erythropoietin, or classic growth factor). But "BDNF-upregulating neuropeptide with claimed performance-enhancement" has not been adjudicated by WADA, and WADA's prohibited list interpretation is definitionally authoritative.

3. Grey-market Semax purchased outside Russia carries additional uncertainty: the substance may be mislabeled, contaminated, or adulterated, and the athlete could test positive for a different prohibited substance present as an impurity.

4. **N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate**: This modified analog is not approved anywhere. Under WADA S0, if the interpretation is that a non-approved substance is prohibited, N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate is almost certainly caught by the S0 catch-all.

**Source uncertainty:** The WADA prohibited list PDF was not directly accessible to render text during this research sweep, and GlobalDRO substance-level query results were not retrievable in machine-readable form. This WADA analysis is therefore based on the known structure of the WADA list (S0 catch-all; S2 category content) from authoritative pharmacological and anti-doping secondary literature, not from direct PDF text extraction of the 2025/2026 prohibited list. Athletes must verify with GlobalDRO directly and/or seek a Therapeutic Use Exemption (TUE) process if relevant.

## D4. Monitoring and Stopping Criteria

### No Validated Efficacy Biomarker

Semax has **no validated clinical monitoring biomarker**. BDNF has been proposed and measured in several of the Russian clinical studies as an exploratory pharmacodynamic marker [3, open_label], and Semax does demonstrably raise plasma BDNF levels in stroke patients. However, BDNF has not been validated as a clinical monitoring endpoint — the correlation between a given plasma BDNF increment and clinically meaningful cognitive or neurological outcomes has not been established in prospective trials.

Self-reported cognitive measures (attention, memory tasks, subjective fatigue) and clinical neurological scales (e.g., Barthel Index, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale) were used as endpoints in the Russian clinical literature. These are clinical, not laboratory, monitoring endpoints.

### Practical Monitoring for Informed Use

Given the above evidence base, monitoring for self-administered or supervised use outside Russian clinical settings appropriately includes:

**Population mismatch caveat:** The adverse-effect base-rates and tolerability findings in the published Semax literature derive from elderly Russian patients with cerebrovascular insufficiency, acute ischemic stroke, or optic neuropathy — populations categorically different from healthy-adult nootropic users. AE frequencies, severity distributions, and contraindication triggers observed in those disease populations do not transfer to healthy adults; no published safety data exists for healthy-adult nootropic use.

- **Symptom-based assessment**: Attention, working memory, and fatigue tracked via validated cognitive screening tools (e.g., MoCA, digit-span tests) at baseline and after each course.
- **Nasal tolerance**: Monitoring for nasal mucosal irritation, epistaxis, or discomfort with the 0.1% intranasal solution.
- **Mood/behavioral monitoring**: Given the anxiolytic and potential dopaminergic behavioral effects in animal models [10, animal] and the standard psychosis contraindication, monitoring for agitation, unusual anxiety escalation, or mood instability, particularly in individuals with personal or family history of psychiatric disorders.
- **Blood pressure and cardiovascular monitoring**: Not specifically flagged in the Semax literature but appropriate given the cerebrovascular patient populations studied (many of whom were post-stroke) where baseline cardiovascular risk is managed.

### Stopping Criteria

Based on the contraindication profile and the general safety pattern:

- Emergence of **psychotic symptoms**, hallucinatory states, or acute agitation → discontinue immediately.
- **Hypersensitivity reactions** (nasal anaphylaxis is theoretically possible with peptide intranasal preparations) → discontinue immediately.
- **Worsening of any pre-existing psychiatric condition**.
- **Pregnancy recognition** → discontinue immediately per the contraindication.
- Lack of subjective or objective benefit after 2 full standard courses (standard courses in the Russian literature are 5–10 days).
