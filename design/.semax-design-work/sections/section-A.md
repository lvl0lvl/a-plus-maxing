# Section A — Identity, Composition, and Mechanism of Action
## Semax (ACTH(4-7)PGP Heptapeptide)

---

### 1. Identity and Chemical Composition

Semax is a synthetic heptapeptide with the amino acid sequence **Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro** (MEHFPGP), molecular weight approximately 813.9 Da. It was developed in the 1980s at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG-RAS) under the direction of academicians **Ivan P. Ashmarin** and **Nikolai F. Myasoedov** [1, mechanism_review]. The compound is a registered pharmaceutical in Russia, used clinically for ischemic stroke and optic nerve pathology; it was added to Russia's List of Vital and Essential Drugs in 2011 [1, mechanism_review].

**Structural origin.** Semax is derived from the **ACTH(4-10)** fragment of adrenocorticotropic hormone (sequence Met-Glu-His-Phe-Arg-Trp-Gly). The biologically active core of this fragment is the **ACTH(4-7)** tetrapeptide Met-Glu-His-Phe, which retains melanocortin-receptor affinity and neurotropic activity. The ACTH(4-7) label in the formal name ACTH(4-7)PGP reflects this four-residue active core; "PGP" denotes the C-terminal **Pro-Gly-Pro** tripeptide extension [2, animal]. Note: some older literature — including several of the source papers cited here — labels Semax as "ACTH(4-10) analogue" because the truncated parent fragment they compared it to was ACTH(4-10); both usages refer to the same heptapeptide MEHFPGP.

**The Pro-Gly-Pro design rationale (key engineered feature).** The parent ACTH(4-10) heptapeptide is rapidly degraded by serum and tissue carboxypeptidases and endopeptidases, limiting its CNS residence time to minutes. The addition of the C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide was a deliberate design decision: the proline residues flank the glycine and resist carboxypeptidase cleavage, dramatically extending metabolic stability and allowing sufficient residence time for nose-to-brain transport, receptor engagement, and downstream gene-expression cascades [1, mechanism_review]. The PGP tripeptide is not merely a passive scaffold — preclinical data show it activates neurotrophin gene transcription in its own right after cerebral ischemia, contributing pharmacologically to Semax's profile [7, animal].

**Physical formulation.** Semax is administered intranasally as aqueous nasal drops, typically in 0.1% (1 mg/mL) and 1% (10 mg/mL) formulations. The intranasal route exploits the olfactory/trigeminal pathways for direct nose-to-brain delivery, bypassing systemic hepatic first-pass metabolism.

### 2. Distinction: Parent Semax vs. N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate

The grey-market nootropic community frequently sells **N-Acetyl-Semax-Amidate** (Ac-MEHFPGP-NH₂) as an enhanced variant. This is a *distinct chemical entity* from the pharmaceutical Semax (H-MEHFPGP-OH). The modifications — N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation — provide additional exopeptidase resistance at both termini, theoretically extending half-life further. However:

- The free N-terminus of parent Semax is structurally important: N-acetylation alters the copper(II) coordination chemistry (converting a CuN₄ to a distorted CuN₃O chromophore) and, critically, **abolishes the neuroprotective effect against copper-induced cytotoxicity** in vitro [8, in_vitro].
- Peer-reviewed clinical and preclinical data for the N-acetyl-amidate form are sparse; the regulatory-registered compound with documented human efficacy data is the **unmodified parent Semax** [1, mechanism_review].
- The two compounds should not be treated as interchangeable in evidence appraisal.

---

### 3. The Russian "Regulatory Peptide" Paradigm

Semax belongs to the Russian neuropeptide-drug tradition articulated principally by Ashmarin's laboratory. This framework — not mainstream in Western pharmacology — holds that short neuropeptides act as "regulatory peptides": broad modulators of neural gene expression cascades rather than classical single-receptor ligands. On this view, a heptapeptide need not occupy a specific receptor for extended periods; instead, its transient binding triggers amplified, multi-gene transcriptional programs (a "neuropeptide cascade") whose downstream effects outlast the peptide's own serum half-life [1, mechanism_review].

This paradigm explains the apparent mismatch between Semax's brief pharmacokinetic window (hours) and its prolonged functional effects. **Western mainstream pharmacology has not adopted or validated this framework as a mechanistic model**, and the mechanistic claims remain largely preclinical and sourced from Russian-language or Russia-affiliated research groups. The honest framing for a health wiki is: Semax has demonstrated preclinical efficacy in rodent models of ischemia and neurotrophin upregulation, has Russian regulatory approval based on clinical studies conducted within Russia, and operates through multiple proposed mechanisms (detailed below) none of which has been validated by a large, independent Western trial.

### 4. Proposed Mechanisms of Action

> **Evidence-tier ceiling for this entire section — read before the subsections below.**
> All mechanism claims in §4 are **evidence tier C (preclinical)** unless explicitly noted otherwise. The body of evidence is **predominantly rodent in vivo and in vitro**, sourced **predominantly from a single research lineage (IMG-RAS / Myasoedov laboratory and affiliated Russian groups)**; no independent Western replication of any mechanistic finding has been published. The one human signal in this section — plasma BDNF elevation in ischemic stroke patients (PMID 29798983, §4a) — is **open-label, single-center, unreplicated, and at best tier B**. There are **no independent RCTs and no blinded human trials of Semax for any cognitive or neuroprotective endpoint**. No mechanism claim below should be read as established at the standard required for clinical guidance.

Multiple mechanisms have been proposed and partially characterized, primarily in preclinical (rat) models and Russian clinical studies. No single mechanism is established by Western-standard RCT evidence.

#### 4a. Neurotrophin Upregulation — BDNF and NGF

The best-characterized preclinical mechanism is **upregulation of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and nerve growth factor (NGF)** gene expression and protein levels.

- Intranasal Semax (50 µg/kg) in Wistar rats produced rapid, gene- and region-specific increases in *Bdnf* mRNA in hippocampus, brainstem, and cerebellum; *Ngf* mRNA increased in hippocampus but decreased in frontal cortex — the response is not uniform [3, animal; rats, in vivo mRNA quantification].
- A complementary study tracking temporal dynamics of *Ngf* and *Bdnf* expression in hippocampus, frontal cortex, and retina found complex biphasic kinetics, with initial suppression followed by sustained upregulation at 90 minutes in certain regions, highlighting that simple "BDNF boost" framing omits the nuance [4, animal; Wistar rats].
- At the protein level, intranasal Semax (50 and 250 µg/kg) increased BDNF protein specifically in rat **basal forebrain** at 3 hours post-administration, with no comparable elevation in cerebellum, and showed saturable, reversible binding (KD 2.4 ± 1.0 nM, Bmax 33.5 ± 7.9 fmol/mg protein) in basal forebrain membranes [5, animal; rats, radioligand binding + ELISA]. This study (Dolotov 2006, J Neurochem) is the primary evidence that Semax **binds specifically** in rat brain, though the identity of the binding site was not definitively established.

  *(Replication-status note: citations [3]–[7] in this BDNF/NGF cluster are all Myasoedov-laboratory / IMG-RAS papers — there is no independent, non-IMG-RAS, non-Russian replication of these mRNA or protein findings; see §C for the full single-lineage concentration audit.)*
- Semax also upregulates the BDNF receptor **TrkB** (phosphorylated) and its mRNA in rat hippocampus, alongside BDNF protein, accompanying improved conditioned avoidance performance [6, animal; rats].
- After cerebral ischemia (rat permanent MCAO), both Semax and PGP alone activated transcription of *Bdnf*, *TrkC*, *TrkA*, *Nt-3*, and *Ngf* at 3–72 hours post-occlusion, with Semax producing more selective activation of neurotrophin genes versus the broader, less specific PGP response [7, animal; Wistar rats, permanent MCAO].

**Human BDNF data (limited).** A Russian open-label clinical study of 110 ischemic stroke patients given intranasal Semax (6,000 µg/day × 10 days × 2 courses) found that Semax, regardless of rehabilitation timing, **increased plasma BDNF levels** that remained elevated throughout the study period, with higher BDNF correlating with better motor recovery (Barthel index) [9, open_label]. This is the primary human BDNF-elevation signal; it is a single open-label study published in a Russian-language journal and has not been independently replicated in a blinded trial.

#### 4b. Melanocortin Receptor Interactions — Non-Corticotropic Activity

As an ACTH-derived peptide, Semax retains structural elements that interact with **melanocortin receptors** (principally MC3R and MC4R in the CNS). These receptors mediate attention, arousal, and cognitive flexibility through cAMP/PKA and MAPK/ERK signaling — pathways that converge on BDNF gene transcription.

Critically, **Semax is devoid of steroidogenic / corticotropic activity**: it does not stimulate cortisol or corticosterone production from the adrenal cortex. This was a primary design goal — retaining the neurotropic effects of ACTH-family fragments while eliminating the peripheral hormonal effects of full-length ACTH [1, mechanism_review]. This has been confirmed pharmacologically: noncorticotropic ACTH(4-10) analogs including Semax and Melanotan II reverse chronic stress-induced anhedonia and hippocampal BDNF suppression in rats without adrenal hypertrophy reversal at the BDNF level and without corticosterone stimulation [10, animal; Sprague-Dawley rats, chronic unpredictable stress].

The specific melanocortin receptor subtype(s) responsible for Semax's CNS effects have not been definitively identified. The non-selectivity of short ACTH fragments across MC3R/MC4R/MC5R means receptor attribution remains an open question.

#### 4c. Dopaminergic and Serotonergic Modulation

Semax modulates both monoaminergic systems in rodents. Intranasal administration raised striatal 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA) levels (+25% at 2 hours), indicating increased serotonin turnover, and enhanced dopamine release potentiation under D-amphetamine challenge [11, animal; rats, microdialysis + HPLC]. In a developmental serotonin-disruption model (neonatal fluvoxamine exposure), Semax normalized brain biogenic amine levels, reduced anxiety-like behavior, and improved maze learning [12, animal; Wistar rats]. These data are consistent with indirect monoaminergic modulation downstream of melanocortin receptor activation, but direct receptor pharmacology at 5-HT or DA receptors has not been established for Semax.

#### 4d. Anti-inflammatory and Neuroimmune Mechanisms

Transcriptome-level analyses after ischemia-reperfusion in rats show Semax suppresses inflammatory gene programs (IL-1β, IL-6, Ccl3, Socs3, Hspb1, c-Fos) and activates neurotransmission-related gene clusters [2, animal; Wistar rats, tMCAO]. Proteomic analysis confirms suppression of JNK kinase and MMP-9 and elevation of phospho-CREB in ischemic cortex, suggesting a coordinated anti-apoptotic, anti-inflammatory response [13, animal; Wistar rats, tMCAO]. The immune mechanism appears partly independent of the BDNF arm: Semax enhances antigen-presentation and interferon signaling pathways while Pro-Gly-Pro separately suppresses immune activation [14, animal; rats, genome-wide RNA-Seq after focal ischemia].

#### 4e. Summary — Demonstrated vs. Marketed

| Claim | Evidence level | Honest framing |
|---|---|---|
| BDNF/NGF mRNA upregulation (rodent) | Multiple consistent animal studies | Established preclinically (rat) |
| TrkB protein elevation (rodent) | Single protein-level study [5, 6] | Preclinical, specific to basal forebrain and hippocampus |
| Plasma BDNF elevation (human stroke) | One open-label Russian study [9] | Single-center, unblinded, not independently replicated |
| Non-corticotropic / no steroidogenic activity | Consistent across studies | Well established; by design |
| Melanocortin receptor binding | Specific binding shown in rat [5]; receptor subtype uncharacterized | Preclinical; receptor identity not confirmed |
| Anti-inflammatory gene regulation | Transcriptome studies in rat MCAO [2, 14] | Preclinical only |
| Dopamine/serotonin modulation | Rat microdialysis studies [11, 12] | Preclinical only |
| Human cognitive enhancement | No peer-reviewed RCT data identified | Not demonstrated in Western-standard trials |
