# Section E — Concentration / Origin / Commercialization / Grey-Market Realism

## E.1 Single-Lineage Concentration Audit

### The Inventor Cluster and Institutional Core

Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) was designed in the early 1980s at the **Institute of Molecular Genetics, Russian Academy of Sciences (IMG-RAS)**, Moscow, by a team led by academician **I.P. Ashmarin** (who conceived the project) and academician **N.F. Myasoedov** (who led the synthesis and pharmacological development programme) [1, mechanism_review]. The compound was engineered to solve a specific stability problem: the natural ACTH(4-10) fragment retained cognitive-enhancing properties but was rapidly degraded in vivo; appending a Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide to the C-terminus yielded a molecule with markedly improved proteolytic resistance while preserving neurotropic activity [1, mechanism_review].

The **inventor co-authorship cluster** that appears across the foundational publications comprises: Myasoedov NF, Grivennikov IA, Levitskaya NG, Kamensky AA, Dolotov OV, Andreeva LA, Alfeeva LYu, Karpenko EA, Inozemtseva LS, Seredenina TS, Filippenkov IB, Stavchansky VV, Dergunova LV, Limborska SA, and Dmitrieva VG. This cluster is remarkably stable across three decades of publication.

The second institutional pillar is the **V.V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology (RAMS)**, Moscow (now NRC "Kurchatov Institute"), where a parallel pharmacological characterization programme ran under Grivennikov and Myasoedov, producing neurochemical mechanistic work [4, animal].

### Concentration Estimate

Of the nine verifiable English-language peer-reviewed publications enumerated above [2, animal; 5, animal; 6, animal; 7, open_label; 9, open_label] (the remaining four cited elsewhere in this section):

- **7 of 9 (≈78%)** carry at least one author from IMG-RAS directly or carry Myasoedov as a named co-author regardless of institutional label. When Zakusov-affiliated work by Grivennikov/Myasoedov is included, the inventor-lineage share rises to **8 of 9 (≈89%)**.
- The single partial exception is Gusev et al. 2018 (PMID 29798983) — a clinical outcomes study from Pirogov Russian National Research Medical University that does not list inventor-cluster co-authors — but it is still wholly Russian, conducted within the Russian state healthcare system, and published in a Russian-language journal. **It is independent of the inventor cluster but not independent of the national monopoly on Semax clinical access.**
- **Zero** of the studies in the verifiable English-language set originate from a non-Russian institution working with independently sourced compound. There are no Western Phase II or Phase III trials, no European replication studies, and no US-based independent pharmacology laboratory publications on Semax in humans.

The parallel of the **Sikiric/BPC-157 single-lab concentration** is exact in structure, though the Semax lineage spans two institutions (IMG-RAS + Zakusov) rather than a single Zagreb group. The functional effect is identical: virtually all human-relevant evidence passes through a closed institutional network that both invented the compound and controls Russian-market access to it. This does not constitute fabrication or misconduct, but it means the evidence base has never been stress-tested by independent groups with the ability to obtain pharmaceutical-grade compound at known concentration through a supply chain they did not control [1, mechanism_review].

### Inventor Co-Authorship Pattern

Myasoedov NF appears as a named author on at least 5 of the 9 enumerated studies, spanning 2005–2025, and remains an active corresponding/senior author as of the 2025 Radchenko et al. Acta Naturae paper [8, animal] — a 40-year publication arc on a single compound. Grivennikov IA appears on at least 3 studies as a Zakusov-affiliated co-author. Levitskaya NG, Kamensky AA, Dolotov OV, Andreeva LA, Limborska SA, and Dergunova LV form the stable second ring of the cluster. The Jürgen Engele (Leipzig) co-authorship on Dolotov et al. 2006b [3, animal] is the sole identified non-Russian co-author in the core literature — a single German collaborator added to an IMG-RAS-led animal study, not an independent replication.

## E.2 Origin and Commercialization

### Development History

Semax was synthesized at IMG-RAS, Moscow, beginning in the early 1980s under the Soviet Academy of Sciences. Russian Ministry of Health registration occurred in the mid-1990s (registration cited across sources as approximately 1994–1996); the compound gained label indications for **acute ischemic stroke, transient ischemic attack, optic nerve atrophy, and cognitive impairment following craniocerebral trauma** [1, mechanism_review]. *Note: these regulatory-status facts (registration timeline, approved indications) are author-reported via the Deigin et al. 2022 narrative review [1]; they are not sourced from a primary Russian regulatory filing (GRLS registration record or Ministry of Health decree) — no machine-accessible primary filing was retrievable for independent verification.* Semax 0.1% and 1.0% nasal drops are the approved pharmaceutical forms. The 1.0% formulation is used in the acute stroke setting; the 0.1% formulation covers the outpatient cognitive/optic indications [1, mechanism_review].

### Peptogen and the IMG-RAS Spinout

**JSC "Innovative Research-and-Production Center 'Peptogen'"** (Peptogen, Russian Federation) was incorporated in 2005 as a joint-stock company with direct participation of the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences [1, mechanism_review]. *Note: the Peptogen incorporation details and IMG-RAS ownership structure are author-reported via the Deigin et al. 2022 narrative review [1], not sourced to a primary corporate registry or government filing.* Peptogen is the manufacturer of record for Semax nasal drops on the Russian market. This structure — a state-academy spinout owning the exclusive manufacturing rights to a compound that same academy's scientists invented and studied for 20 years — is the institutional form of single-lineage concentration. There is no separation between the science-producer and the commercial beneficiary.

### Russian-Market Positioning and CIS Distribution

Semax is listed on Russia's **List of Vital and Essential Drugs (ЖНВЛП)**, which designates drugs subject to state price regulation and ensures formulary inclusion in Russian public healthcare. This listing (approved by Russian government decree, most recently December 2011) confers significant market stability independent of export demand [1, mechanism_review]. *Note: the ЖНВЛП listing and decree date are author-reported via the Deigin et al. 2022 narrative review [1]; the specific government decree number was not machine-accessible from the Russian regulatory portal (GRLS/ЖНВЛП) during research for this section.* Semax is available via prescription pharmacies in Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, and other CIS states. It is not registered with the EMA, FDA, Health Canada, TGA, or any other Western regulatory authority. Russia is not a member of the ICH, and its clinical trial standards are not directly harmonized with ICH E6 GCP [1, mechanism_review].

## E.3 Grey-Market Realism

### Western Nootropic/Biohacker Market

Semax reached the Western nootropic community via two routes: (1) direct import of licensed Russian pharmaceutical product (Peptogen nasal drops, 0.1% or 1.0%), available through importers such as CosmicNootropic, which ships Russian-registered pharmaceutical product internationally; and (2) synthesis by US and international peptide research chemical vendors who produce unlicensed powder or pre-mixed nasal spray under the "research chemicals, not for human use" legal designation. Both routes operate without FDA oversight; neither constitutes a legal approved drug in the US, EU, or UK [anecdote_aggregate].

The compound circulates extensively in communities such as r/Nootropics, Longecity, and peptide-focused biohacker forums, with anecdotal reports focused on acute cognitive focus enhancement, BDNF-related mood effects, and stroke-recovery adjunct use. None of these reports constitute evidence; they are cited here as documentation of the grey-market use pattern, not as data [anecdote_aggregate].

### N-Acetyl Semax Amidate — The Modified Analog

**N-Acetyl Semax Amidate (NASA)** is a synthetic modification of Semax in which an acetyl group is added to the N-terminus and the C-terminus is amidated. Vendors and biohacker sources claim this modification: (a) improves resistance to aminopeptidase cleavage at the N-terminus, extending half-life; and (b) increases lipophilicity, potentially improving blood-brain barrier penetration. These claims are biologically plausible as a class effect of N-acetylation/C-amidation but are **not supported by any published peer-reviewed study of NASA specifically** [anecdote_aggregate]. There are no animal studies, no pharmacokinetic data, no safety studies, and no clinical data for NASA as a distinct compound. Its situation is structurally parallel to N-Acetyl Selank Amidate relative to Selank: an analog marketed as superior to its parent compound on the basis of chemical reasoning alone, with a near-zero independent evidence base of its own [anecdote_aggregate].

NASA is sold by multiple US vendors as intranasal spray (at concentrations such as 0.02% / 200 mcg per spray per some vendor labels) and is also available on Amazon under "Industrial & Scientific" classification [anecdote_aggregate]. It is not approved anywhere in the world, has no INN, no pharmacopoeial monograph, and no regulatory dossier. The "not for human use" label is a legal fiction that does not affect actual consumer use patterns [anecdote_aggregate].

**No dose for NASA should be cited as evidence-grounded.** Circulating dose conventions (e.g., vendor nasal spray concentrations, community "protocol" documents) are vendor- or anecdote-derived. They are documented here to characterize the grey-market landscape, not as clinical guidance.

### Grey-Market Hazards

The hazards specific to the Semax/NASA grey market include:

1. **Purity and identity.** Research chemical vendors synthesizing Semax or NASA outside pharmaceutical GMP controls cannot guarantee the absence of: residual coupling reagents from solid-phase peptide synthesis, incorrect sequence peptides (from depurination, incomplete deprotection, or sequence scrambling), aggregates, or microbial contamination. Intranasal delivery bypasses the hepatic first pass and places any contaminant in direct proximity to the olfactory epithelium and cribriform plate — a higher-risk exposure route than oral dosing for CNS-active contaminants [anecdote_aggregate].

2. **No pharmacovigilance.** The US, EU, and UK have no adverse event reporting system capturing Semax or NASA harms because neither compound is approved. Harms that occur remain invisible to regulators [anecdote_aggregate].

3. **Concentration uncertainty.** Vendor-stated concentrations (e.g., "99% purity," "30 mg/10 mL") are not independently audited by any regulatory authority. Even vendors offering third-party CoAs issue them against in-house or contracted HPLC methods with no reference standard traceability to a pharmacopoeial authority [anecdote_aggregate].

4. **Analog evidence extrapolation.** Community protocols frequently extrapolate Semax clinical data (itself already from a concentrated single-lineage evidence base, in Russian-language journals, under non-ICH-GCP standards) to NASA on the assumption that the modifications are inert from an efficacy/safety standpoint. This is undemonstrated [anecdote_aggregate].

5. **Legal status ambiguity.** In the US, Semax and NASA are not scheduled controlled substances, but the FDA has authority over unapproved drugs intended for human use; the "research chemical" classification is a commercial framing, not a safe harbor from enforcement. Import of Russian-manufactured Semax for personal use has not been systematically enforced but remains legally exposed [anecdote_aggregate].
