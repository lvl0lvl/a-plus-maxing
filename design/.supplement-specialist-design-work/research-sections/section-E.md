# Section E — Non-English literature coverage

Scope: non-English peer-reviewed literature for supplement / nootropic / herbal compounds, surveyed under Tier NE of `vault/library/_source-whitelist.md` (non-English peer-reviewed = admissible under the SAME tier rules as English). Goal-agnostic per PF-S2-04 — this section catalogs what the non-English corpus contains and where its structure (single-group / single-country / manufacturer-linked concentration) downgrades confidence; it does NOT pre-filter for any operator goal. Anti-hallucination: inline `[N, type_tag]` per claim; `[population-mismatch:<species>]` on animal numericals.

The recurring structural finding across all three corpora (Russian, Chinese, originator-country) is the same: a large literature volume that is NOT equivalent to a large *independent* evidence base. Originator-country / single-group / manufacturer-linked papers are a concentration-of-evidence hazard and carry a `[non-English-literature]` single-source-language caveat — they must be counted as ONE source-lineage for confidence purposes, not as N independent replications.

---

### Finding E1 — Russian nootropic peptides (Semax, Selank, Noopept, Mexidol, Cerebrolysin) have a deep but originator-country-concentrated evidence base; volume must not be mistaken for independent replication

**Claim:** Semax, Selank, Noopept, Mexidol and Cerebrolysin each carry a substantial Russian-language clinical + preclinical literature and Russian regulatory approval, but the evidence is dominated by originator-country / single-group / manufacturer-linked trials, so the corpus is a confidence-DOWNGRADING concentration signal, not corroborating replication.

Verified primaries:
- A Russian preclinical study compared selank (300 µg/kg/day), noopept (1 mg/kg/day) and semax (0.6 mg/kg/day) by elevated-plus-maze behavior in BALB/c vs C57BL/6 mice, finding anxiolytic efficiency higher after intraperitoneal and nootropic efficiency higher after intranasal route in BALB/c, with NO behavioral effect in C57BL/6 [1, animal] `[population-mismatch:mouse]` (dose figures are mouse mg/kg — not human-translatable). Full-text behind Springer paywall; effect direction confirmed via abstract + indexer. The strain-dependence is itself a caution: effects appeared in only one of two mouse strains.
- The **Mexidol EPICA trial** (Stakhovskaya LV et al., 2017, *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr Im S S Korsakova*) is a Russian multicenter (Moscow, St. Petersburg, Kazan, Samara, Novosibirsk) randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial, n=150 randomized, reporting lower mean modified Rankin Scale score vs placebo in hemispheric ischemic stroke (p=0.04) [2, rct]. English abstract; full text Russian-only — numerical mRS claim is abstract-stated so admissible at high level, but `[non-english-untranslated]` applies to any within-text secondary endpoint.
- The **Cochrane review of Cerebrolysin for acute ischaemic stroke** (Ziganshina LE et al., 2023, CD007026.pub7) is the load-bearing independent appraisal: it judged risk of bias HIGH wherever there was clear manufacturer sponsorship, manufacturer involvement in trial design / sequence generation / medication provision / statistics / blinding / reporting, or declared author relationships to the Cerebrolysin (or Cortexin) manufacturer [3, meta_analysis]. (Cochrane full-text was 403/CAPTCHA-gated to the fetcher; sponsorship-as-bias-driver finding confirmed via the Cochrane record summary returned in search.)

**What the non-English coverage adds:** confirms these compounds are real, regulated, dosed in humans in Russia, with decades of clinical use the English literature barely indexes; supplies route- and dose-convention substrate (`practitioner_protocol`-adjacent) and the only human RCT-level stroke data for Mexidol.

**What it fails to add / single-source-language concentration caveat:** independent, non-originator, non-manufacturer replication. `[non-English-literature]` — Semax/Selank trace to one institute (Institute of Molecular Genetics RAS); Cerebrolysin's positive trials are heavily manufacturer-linked (Cochrane's explicit bias finding); Mexidol's EPICA is single-country. Per the whitelist's own admissibility logic, these are ONE source-lineage each for confidence, NOT N independent studies. Any wiki entry must carry an explicit `evidence-concentration: originator-country/single-group` flag and downgrade confidence accordingly.

### Finding E2 — Chinese-language RCT corpus for botanicals (berberine, Rhodiola) is large and CNKI/Wanfang/VIP-indexed, but concentrated in China with uneven study quality and standardization heterogeneity

**Claim:** Major botanicals carry substantial Chinese-language RCT literature retrievable via CNKI / Wanfang / VIP, and pooled effects look favorable — but trials are quality-uneven, China-concentrated, and standardization-heterogeneous, which downgrades confidence below the raw trial count.

Verified primaries:
- **Berberine in type 2 diabetes**: Guo et al., 2021, *Oxidative Medicine and Cellular Longevity*, systematic review/meta-analysis of 46 RCTs / 4,158 participants, searching eight databases INCLUDING the three Chinese databases CNKI, Wanfang and VIP. Pooled: HbA1c MD −0.73, FPG MD −0.86, 2hPG MD −1.26, plus lipid and inflammatory improvements [4, meta_analysis]. The authors explicitly state "literature qualities were uneven" and "most of the trials were conducted among Chinese patients, which limited the widespread application of this data." Full-text (PMC) verified.
- **Rhodiola spp.**: a quality-evaluation systematic review (PMC8266448) graded 39 RCTs (R. rosea 23 [22 English, 1 Chinese]; R. crenulata 8 [6 Chinese]; R. wallichiana 8 [all Chinese]) against CONSORT 2010 + Cochrane RoB. NONE met all CONSORT criteria; mean CONSORT scores were low (R. rosea 0.33, R. crenulata 0.25, R. wallichiana 0.17); most studies rated unclear RoB; R. crenulata and R. wallichiana trials occurred exclusively in China [5, meta_analysis]. Full-text verified.

**What the non-English coverage adds:** materially larger trial count and species coverage than English-only retrieval (R. wallichiana and most R. crenulata data exist ONLY in the Chinese corpus); confirms berberine's metabolic effect signal is not a Western artifact.

**What it fails to add / single-source-language concentration caveat:** clean methodology and geographic diversity. `[non-English-literature]` + publication-bias concern: the berberine signal is China-concentrated by the authors' own admission, and the Rhodiola Chinese-only species are exactly the ones with the lowest CONSORT scores. Standardization is unsettled (Rhodiola doses 100–680 mg/day across heterogeneous extracts). Confidence in pooled effect sizes must be downgraded for both the China-concentration and the documented reporting-quality floor — the volume inflates apparent certainty.

### Finding E3 — Originator-country botanical literature (Ayurvedic for Withania, German Commission E for European herbals) supplies traditional-use and regulatory framing but is NOT primary efficacy evidence

**Claim:** Originator-country corpora — classical Ayurvedic texts for ashwagandha and the German Commission E monographs for European herbals (ginkgo, St. John's wort) — provide traditional-use indication, dose-convention and regulatory-status substrate, but neither grounds modern efficacy claims, which still require Tier 1/2 RCTs.

Verified sources:
- **Ashwagandha / Withania somnifera**: classical Ayurvedic texts (Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Vagbhata) document it as a rasayana; modern human-trial systematic reviews use root-extract standardized to withanolide content (e.g. trials standardized around <0.9% withanolides) across stress, anxiety, sleep, sexual-health endpoints [6, mechanism_review / review-of-trials]. The classical literature is `anecdote_aggregate`-equivalent for efficacy (traditional-use only, never numerical); the standardization figure and indication breadth are the usable substrate.
- **German Commission E monographs** (published 1984–1994): regulatory monographs approving ginkgo (EGb 761, flavone glycosides 22–27%, terpene lactones 2.8–3.4%, bilobalide 2.6–3.2%) for "cerebral organic performance deficits" and St. John's wort for mild-to-moderate depression [7, regulatory]. These are `regulatory` for approved-status / standardized-extract definition only; an independent assessment notes "insufficient evidence to substantiate the claim that G. biloba can improve memory in healthy subjects," and the EMA has since superseded the Commission's framework.

**What the originator-country coverage adds:** the standardized-extract definitions (EGb 761 phytochemical spec; withanolide-% standardization) that anchor what "the compound" even means in trials, plus regulated-status and traditional indication framing.

**What it fails to add / caveat:** efficacy grounding. Classical Ayurvedic text is traditional-use only (downgrade to qualitative); Commission E monographs are 1984–1994 regulatory determinations with an evidence basis weaker than a modern systematic review, and at least one major claim (ginkgo memory in healthy subjects) is contradicted by later independent assessment. No single-source-language *concentration* hazard here (these are regulatory/traditional layers, not a body of trials masquerading as replication) — but a TIER hazard: do not let monograph/classical status substitute for RCT efficacy evidence.
