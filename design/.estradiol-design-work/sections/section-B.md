# Section B: Reference Ranges, Units & Thresholds

## Overview

Serum estradiol (E2) reference ranges are among the most context-dependent in routine clinical chemistry. Sex, menstrual-cycle phase, reproductive stage, and the analytical method used all exert large effects on the reported interval. No single universal cutpoint defines "normal"; a result that is appropriate in one context may be frankly pathological in another. Clinicians ordering E2 should always document the sex, cycle day (for premenopausal women), and the assay platform, because method-to-method variation can exceed 30% [1, regulatory; 2, mechanism_review].

---

## Units and Conversion

E2 is reported in either picograms per milliliter (pg/mL) or picomoles per liter (pmol/L). The molecular weight of 17β-estradiol is 272 g/mol, giving a conversion factor of approximately **3.671–3.676**.

| pg/mL | pmol/L | Clinical context |
|-------|--------|-----------------|
| 10    | 37     | Low-normal male / postmenopausal floor |
| 20    | 73     | Mid-normal male range |
| 30    | 110    | Upper normal male / postmenopausal range |
| 40    | 147    | Upper male range |
| 50    | 184    | Lower HRT therapeutic target |
| 80    | 294    | Early follicular phase, cycling women |
| 100   | 367    | Mid follicular / HRT upper target |
| 150   | 551    | Mid-follicular rising phase, women |
| 200   | 734    | Luteal phase, women |
| 300   | 1,101  | Ovulatory/preovulatory range, women |
| 500   | 1,836  | Upper ovulatory peak, women |

> Use: pg/mL × 3.671 = pmol/L. A result reported as 88 pmol/L ≈ 24 pg/mL — well within the normal male range, not elevated [1, regulatory].

## Reference Ranges by Population

All ranges below represent 2.5th–97.5th percentile intervals unless otherwise noted. Ranges are assay- and laboratory-dependent; the issuing laboratory's printed reference interval takes precedence over generic tables [1, regulatory; 2, mechanism_review].

### Premenopausal Women (Menstrual-Cycle Phase)

E2 fluctuates roughly tenfold across a normal 28-day cycle. The pattern: low in early follicular, a sharp preovulatory surge, a secondary plateau in the luteal phase, then a decline that triggers menses.

| Phase | Days (approx.) | Range (pg/mL) | Range (pmol/L) | Source |
|-------|---------------|---------------|----------------|--------|
| Early follicular | 1–7 | 12–80 | 44–294 | [3, cohort] |
| Late follicular / preovulatory | 8–14 | ~85–500+ | ~312–1,836+ | [2, mechanism_review; 3, cohort] |
| Luteal | 15–28 | 40–260 | 147–955 | [2, mechanism_review; 3, cohort] |
| Premenopausal (cycle-spanning) | — | 15–350 | 55–1,285 | [4, regulatory] |

The preovulatory E2 surge drives the LH/FSH surge that triggers ovulation; levels typically peak 24–36 h before oocyte release. In the luteal phase, both the corpus luteum and the follicle that did not ovulate maintain a secondary rise; menses typically occur when E2 falls to approximately 50–100 pg/mL (184–367 pmol/L) [4, regulatory].

LC-MS/MS-based assays (the current reference standard) tend to yield somewhat different absolute concentrations than immunoassays at low ranges. In early follicular phase, GC/MS-based measurements have reported mean values around 55 pg/mL, while direct immunoassays on whole serum without extraction have historically read lower [2, mechanism_review]. The Verdonk et al. LC-MS/MS study in 30 healthy premenopausal women confirmed daily E2 variation across the menstrual cycle and found the follicular low-phase concentration was well below the LOQ of many conventional immunoassays [3, cohort].

### Postmenopausal Women

After menopause, ovarian follicular activity ceases. Circulating E2 falls dramatically and is derived primarily from peripheral aromatization of adrenal androgens in adipose tissue. Levels plateau at:

- **~<10–30 pg/mL (<37–110 pmol/L)** (no hormone therapy) [4, regulatory; 2, mechanism_review]
- By GC/MS, early postmenopausal women (<5 years from LMP): ~4.9 pg/mL mean; >5 years from LMP: ~1.3 pg/mL [2, mechanism_review]

Women and older men in the lowest quartile of E2 (typically <5 pg/mL / <18 pmol/L) are at increased risk of osteoporotic fractures [4, regulatory].

### Men (Adult)

In adult men, up to 50% of circulating E2 comes from direct testicular secretion; the remainder from peripheral aromatization of testosterone, primarily in adipose tissue, bone, and brain [5, mechanism_review].

- **Typical range: ~10–40 pg/mL (~37–147 pmol/L)** [1, regulatory; 4, regulatory]
- Frederiksen et al. 2020 (LC-MS/MS, n=1838): adult male E2 reference interval approximately **50–150 pmol/L (~14–41 pg/mL)**; no significant age-related decline observed across ages 30–60 [6, cohort]
- Direct immunoassays perform poorly at these concentrations; LC-MS/MS or extraction-based RIA is preferred for clinical decisions in men [2, mechanism_review]

### Children and Puberty

- **Prepubertal (<10 years):** <15 pg/mL in both sexes [4, regulatory]
- During mini-puberty (~3 months of age in girls): transient E2 elevation to <100 pmol/L (<27 pg/mL), then suppression until pubertal onset [6, cohort]
- Rising through Tanner stages; girls typically reach adult cycling levels by Tanner V (age ≤18) [4, regulatory]

### Pregnancy

E2 rises sharply and progressively through gestation. Early first-trimester concentrations: ~188–2,497 pg/mL; late third trimester can reach tens of thousands of pg/mL as the fetoplacental unit becomes the dominant source. Estriol (E3), not E2, is the primary clinically monitored estrogen in pregnancy.

## Method Dependence and Assay Caveats

The Endocrine Society's position statement [2, mechanism_review] identified E2 measurement as uniquely challenging:

1. **Direct immunoassays** (the most common clinical platform) have a limit of quantitation of 30–100 pg/mL — above most male, prepubertal, and postmenopausal concentrations. Results in these populations from direct immunoassays are unreliable.
2. **LC-MS/MS** is the current gold standard and is recommended by the Endocrine Society and other international bodies for all clinical decisions based on low E2 levels.
3. Reference intervals established by immunoassay cannot be directly compared to LC-MS/MS-derived intervals; labs must publish the method alongside the range.

## Diagnostic Uses of Estradiol Testing

### In Women
- **Menstrual and ovarian function:** Baseline early-follicular E2 (with FSH) evaluates ovarian reserve. An elevated day-3 E2 (>60–80 pg/mL) with elevated FSH suggests diminished reserve. Low E2 with high FSH/LH confirms primary ovarian insufficiency or menopause.
- **Menopause confirmation:** Postmenopausal E2 (<10–30 pg/mL) with FSH >40 IU/L confirms menopausal status.
- **IVF / ovulation induction monitoring:** Serial E2 during controlled ovarian stimulation tracks follicular growth and predicts ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) risk. An E2/mature follicle ratio of 200–300 pg/mL is associated with better oocyte quality and clinical pregnancy rates [7, cohort].
- **Precocious or delayed puberty:** E2 elevation in a prepubertal girl with breast development may indicate central or peripheral precocious puberty; persistently low E2 in an adolescent with absent menses suggests hypogonadism.
- **PCOS and estrogen-producing tumors:** Irregular or absent menses with normal or elevated E2 may indicate polycystic ovarian syndrome, androgen-producing tumors, or estrogen-producing tumors [4, regulatory].
- **HRT monitoring:** Target E2 during hormone replacement in reproductive-age women: within premenopausal reference range, with E2 > E1 [4, regulatory].

### In Men
- **Gynecomastia workup:** Male breast enlargement correlates with an increased E2:testosterone ratio rather than with absolute E2 alone. E2 levels above ~40 pg/mL with suppressed testosterone are a key diagnostic signal [5, mechanism_review].
- **Aromatase excess / obesity:** Increased adipose aromatase converts testosterone to E2, raising E2 and lowering the testosterone:E2 ratio. Testicular or adrenal tumors may secrete E2 directly.
- **Testosterone:estradiol balance:** In men receiving testosterone replacement or anabolic androgens, serial E2 monitoring detects over-aromatization (supraphysiologic E2 >40–50 pg/mL) that may suppress the HPG axis or produce gynecomastia.
- **Bone health:** E2 <10 pg/mL in older men predicts accelerated bone loss; a minimum of ~19 pg/mL (70 pmol/L) is associated with improved insulin sensitivity and adiposity prevention [5, mechanism_review].
- **Infertility / hypogonadism:** Elevated E2 may suppress gonadotropins; very low E2 is associated with low libido and insulin resistance [5, mechanism_review].

---

## Key Caveats

- No single numerical cutpoint defines pathological E2 without knowledge of sex, cycle day, reproductive stage, and assay method.
- Immunoassay results in men, children, and postmenopausal women are frequently unreliable; LC-MS/MS or extraction-based RIA should be requested when clinical decisions depend on low-range accuracy [2, mechanism_review].
- Laboratory-specific reference intervals (e.g., Mayo Clinic, LabCorp, Quest) reflect the assay platform used in that lab; the printed range on the result always takes precedence over generic population tables [4, regulatory].
