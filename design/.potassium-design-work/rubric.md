# Rubric — potassium (serum potassium, biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** **potassium (K⁺)** = the principal INTRACELLULAR cation (~98% of total body K⁺ is intracellular; intracellular ~140-150 mmol/L vs serum ~3.5-5.0 mmol/L — a steep ~30-40:1 gradient maintained by the **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase**); only ~2% is extracellular, so **serum K⁺ is a small, tightly-defended window that does NOT directly reflect total-body K⁺** (a key concept — large total-body deficits can exist with near-normal serum K, and serum K can shift acutely with no change in body stores). The gradient sets the **resting membrane potential** → critical for **cardiac + neuromuscular excitability** (the basis of the lethal arrhythmia risk). Regulation has TWO arms: **(1) acute — transcellular shifts** (insulin drives K⁺ INTO cells [Na/K-ATPase], β2-adrenergic catecholamines drive K⁺ in, **acid-base: acidosis shifts K⁺ OUT / alkalosis shifts K⁺ in**, plasma osmolality, cell turnover); **(2) chronic — renal excretion** (the kidney is the dominant route; **aldosterone → distal-nephron principal-cell K⁺ secretion** via ENaC-driven electrogenic gradient; flow/distal Na delivery); a smaller colonic component. The insulin + catecholamine shift biology underlies the acute hyperkalemia treatments.
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Interpretation:** reference **~3.5-5.0 mmol/L (= mEq/L)** (serum slightly higher than plasma by ~0.2-0.5 mmol/L because K⁺ is released from platelets during CLOTTING — note serum vs plasma); units mmol/L ↔ mEq/L (1:1); **hypokalemia <3.5** (mild 3.0-3.4, moderate 2.5-2.9, severe <2.5) + **hyperkalemia >5.0-5.5** (mild 5.5-5.9, moderate 6.0-6.4, severe ≥6.5); **the FIRST interpretive question is "is it REAL or spurious?"** — pseudohyperkalemia must be excluded (cross-ref measurement); then transcellular-shift vs total-body-change vs renal-handling. **The ECG correlation (load-bearing for danger):** hyperkalemia → **peaked T waves → PR prolongation → widened QRS → loss of P waves → sine wave → VF/asystole**; hypokalemia → **flattened T waves, U waves, ST depression, ↑ arrhythmia + digoxin toxicity risk**; note ECG changes correlate imperfectly with level + the RATE of change matters. The hypokalemia–hypomagnesemia link (Mg needed to correct refractory hypokalemia).
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** **ion-selective electrode (ISE)** is standard (direct + indirect, like sodium). **THE LOAD-BEARING ISSUE — PSEUDOHYPERKALEMIA (spurious high K⁺ from K⁺ released ex-vivo):** the causes — **(1) HEMOLYSIS (the #1 cause** — RBCs are K⁺-rich; even mild hemolysis falsely raises K⁺; the hemolysis index; quantify); **(2) fist-clenching / prolonged tourniquet during a fasting draw** (forearm-muscle K⁺ release — a classic outpatient artifact); **(3) thrombocytosis + extreme leukocytosis** (K⁺ released from platelets/WBCs during clotting → SERUM pseudohyperkalemia; use a PLASMA sample); **(4) delayed analysis / cold storage / pneumatic-tube transport** (Na/K-ATPase fails → K⁺ leaks out); **(5) hereditary/familial pseudohyperkalemia**. The corollary — **reverse pseudohyperkalemia** + how to confirm a true value (plasma vs serum, whole-blood/blood-gas direct ISE, hemolysis index, repeat). Also: the standardization/traceability; reference-interval method dependence; the serum-vs-plasma offset.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **HYPERKALEMIA (>5.0-5.5 — the acutely LETHAL one):**
  - **decreased renal excretion (the dominant cause):** acute + chronic kidney disease/renal failure; **RAAS inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, the MRAs spironolactone/eplerenone, direct renin inhibitors)** + potassium-sparing diuretics (amiloride/triamterene) + **NSAIDs** + calcineurin inhibitors + trimethoprim/heparin; **hypoaldosteronism / Addison's / type-4 RTA**.
  - **transcellular shift OUT of cells:** metabolic **acidosis**, insulin deficiency/hyperglycemia (diabetic), **tissue breakdown — rhabdomyolysis, tumor lysis syndrome, massive hemolysis, burns**, β-blockers, digoxin toxicity, succinylcholine.
  - **increased intake** (usually only with impaired excretion); **pseudohyperkalemia (exclude first — cross-ref measurement).**
  - **HYPOKALEMIA (<3.5):** **renal/GI loss** — **diuretics (thiazide + loop — a leading cause)**, vomiting/diarrhea/laxatives, **hyperaldosteronism (primary/secondary), Cushing's, Bartter/Gitelman**; **transcellular shift INTO cells** — insulin/glucose, **β2-agonists**, alkalosis, **refeeding syndrome**, thyrotoxic periodic paralysis; **hypomagnesemia (causes refractory hypokalemia — must replace Mg)**; poor intake (rare alone).
  - **CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE (load-bearing):** the **acute arrhythmia/cardiac-arrest danger** (both extremes — hyperkalemia → VF/asystole, hypokalemia → arrhythmia esp. with digoxin/QT drugs); the **U-shaped association of serum K⁺ with mortality** in CKD, heart failure, and general populations — cite NAMED cohorts/meta (VERIFY); the RAAS-inhibitor–hyperkalemia tradeoff (the drugs that most help the heart/kidney also raise K⁺ — the central management tension; K-binders).
  - **Limitations:** serum K⁺ is a CONCENTRATION + a SMALL extracellular window — does NOT reflect total-body K⁺; transcellular shifts move it without changing stores; the pseudohyperkalemia artifacts MUST be excluded; interpret WITH renal function (eGFR), acid-base, magnesium, medications, and the ECG.
