## Findings

### Finding 1: Energy balance is the dominant lever for body composition; isocaloric macro splits matter little once protein is matched
In a metabolic-ward isocaloric crossover (n=19 adults with obesity, all food provided), carbohydrate restriction vs fat restriction produced 53±6 vs 89±6 g/day body-fat loss — fat restriction was *greater* (p=0.002), opposite the carbohydrate-insulin prediction [rct] (Hall et al. 2015, Cell Metab 22(3):427-436, PMID 26278052 / PMC4603544). Controlled-feeding work finds total energy intake — not carb:fat ratio at matched protein — predicts fat loss. GRADE: certainty **high**, strength **strong**. This grounds the recommendation hierarchy: energy balance > macro composition > meal timing > supplements. (Thermic-effect-of-food per-macro percentages are UNGROUNDED pending a dedicated TEF citation.)

### Finding 2: Protein RDA (0.8 g/kg) is a deficiency floor, not an optimum; the resistance-training optimum is ~1.6 g/kg with a plateau, age- and deficit-modified
A 49-study meta-regression (1863 participants) found resistance-training lean-mass gains plateau at ~1.62 g/kg/day (95% CI 1.03–2.20) [meta_analysis] (Morton et al. 2018, BJSM 52(6):376-384, PMID 28698222). Older adults need a higher floor (≥1.0–1.2 g/kg) to offset anabolic resistance [mechanism_review / position-paper] (Bauer/PROT-AGE 2013, JAMDA 14(8):542-559 — PMID unconfirmed, treat as expert consensus). In an aggressive deficit, lean trainees need 2.3–3.1 g/kg fat-free mass to retain FFM [meta_analysis] (Helms et al. 2014, IJSNEM 24(2):127-138, PMID 24092765). GRADE: ~1.6 g/kg default **moderate/strong**; older-adult floor **moderate/strong**; deficit-FFM range wider uncertainty. Population-mismatch: derived from healthy training adults, not sedentary/clinical.

### Finding 3: High protein does not harm healthy kidneys; CKD and hepatic disease are genuine population-dependent exceptions where restriction is the standard of care
A 28-study meta-analysis (>1300 participants incl. healthy/obese/T2D/hypertensive) found higher-protein diets did not differ from controls in *change* in GFR — normal renal adaptation, no evidence of harm in healthy kidneys [meta_analysis] (Devries et al. 2018, J Nutr 148(11):1760-1769, PMID 30383278). This does NOT extend to established CKD 3–5, where protein is therapeutically restricted to 0.55–0.60 g/kg (non-diabetic) per KDOQI [regulatory] (KDOQI 2020 Update, Am J Kidney Dis), nor to advanced hepatic disease. GRADE: "safe in healthy kidneys" **moderate/strong**; disease exception **conditional-on-diagnosis**. A nutrition agent must screen renal/hepatic status before applying a high-protein default — and an unpopulated field is UNKNOWN, not "no contraindication" (see Finding 12).

### Finding 4: Protein distribution (~0.4 g/kg/meal, leucine trigger) is a second-order optimization subordinate to hitting the daily total
~0.4 g/kg/meal across 3–4 meals plausibly maximizes per-meal MPS (single-bolus plateau ~20–25 g fast protein in young adults) [mechanism_review] (Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018, JISSN 15:10, PMC5828430); an even split gave 25% higher 24-h mixed-muscle FSR than evening-skewed in a small crossover [rct] (Mamerow et al. 2014, J Nutr 144(6):876-880, PMID 24477298, n=8), though in healthy *older* adults even-vs-skewed showed no MPS difference [rct] (Justesen et al. 2022, Nutrients 14(21):4442, PMC9654411 — MDPI, lower-trust open-access). The leucine "trigger" (~2.5–3 g/meal) is age-dependent: supported in 13/16 older-adult vs ~3/10 young-adult studies [mechanism_review] (Zaromskyte et al. 2021, Front Nutr 8:685165, PMID 34307436 — Frontiers, flagged). GRADE: distribution **low-moderate/conditional**; load-bearing in older adults, weak in the young.

### Finding 5: Meal frequency per se has negligible effect on body composition once total energy and protein are matched — the "stoke the metabolism" claim is unsupported
Varying eating frequency (2 vs 3 vs 6 meals) at matched intake produces minimal-to-no body-composition difference; diet-induced thermogenesis scales to total kcal, not the number of feeding bouts [meta_analysis] (Schwingshackl et al. 2020, Adv Nutr, PMID 32437566; corroborated Schoenfeld/Aragon/Krieger 2015, Nutr Rev 73(2):69-82, doi:10.1093/nutrit/nuu017 — the lone positive signal did not survive sensitivity analysis). GRADE: certainty **moderate**, effect **negligible**. This is a total-intake claim and carries higher certainty than the timing claims (Findings 6, 7).

### Finding 6: The post-exercise "anabolic window" is wide, not narrow; timing effects collapse into total daily protein — the canonical "timing < total-intake certainty" result
A protein-timing meta-analysis found the small apparent hypertrophy timing effect was almost entirely explained by timing groups eating *more total protein*; once total protein was a moderator the timing effect was non-significant, and the practical interval spans several hours [meta_analysis] (Schoenfeld/Aragon/Krieger 2013, JISSN 10:53, PMC3879660; narrative Aragon & Schoenfeld 2013, JISSN 10:5, PMID 23360586 / PMC3577439). GRADE: **moderate** certainty / **negligible** effect once total intake controlled. Structural insight for the agent: timing recommendations are a confounded proxy for daily protein and must be tagged lower-certainty than total-intake claims.

### Finding 7: Calorie front-loading is an adherence/appetite tool, not a metabolic-rate or weight-loss advantage at equal calories
An isocaloric crossover (morning- vs evening-loaded calories) found no difference in total energy expenditure, RMR, or weight loss — but morning loading significantly reduced hunger [rct] (Ruddick-Collins et al. 2022, Cell Metab 34(10):1472-1485, PMC9605877). Broader chrono-nutrition associations (late eating ↔ worse markers) are observational-dominant with modest, inconsistent RCT effect sizes [mechanism_review] (chrononutrition narrative review 2025, Nutrients, PMC12252119 — MDPI, flagged). GRADE: null metabolic/weight result **moderate**; circadian-marker benefit **low** (observational). A timing claim — lower certainty than total-intake, as expected.

### Finding 8: Fiber should be titrated gradually toward ~14 g/1000 kcal to limit GI distress; the cardiovascular benefit is observational
The IOM Adequate Intake is ~14 g fiber/1000 kcal (≈38 g/day younger men, 25 g/day younger women), anchored to cohort-observed lower CHD risk; clinical guidance is to raise intake gradually with adequate fluid to avoid bloating/gas as colonic fermentation adapts [cohort / guideline] (IOM AI basis, StatPearls NBK559033). Viscous/soluble fiber blunts postprandial glucose excursions (type- and matrix-dependent). GRADE: AI target + gradual-titration practice **moderate** (guideline + cohort); CHD effect size observational (residual confounding). The gradual-titration tolerance claim is guideline-grounded, not RCT-grounded (flagged).

### Finding 9: Micronutrient RDA/AI prevents deficiency; "optimization above replete status" is mostly low-certainty and frequently null in RCTs — food-first, supplement for documented deficiency
The RDA/AI is calibrated to deficiency prevention, not a separate "optimal" set-point. VITAL (n≈25,871, 2,000 IU/d vit D + 1 g/d omega-3, median 5.3 yr) showed no reduction in fractures, cancer, or major CVD — including in low-baseline subgroups [rct] (Manson et al. 2019, NEJM, PMID 30415629 / 30415637); the Cochrane omega-3 synthesis (86 RCTs, n=162,796) found little/no CVD effect (all-cause-mortality certainty **high**) [meta_analysis] (Abdelhamid et al. 2020, Cochrane CD003177.pub5, PMID 32114706). Intervention is justified by a documented/high-probability deficiency, not routine "insurance"; bioavailability matters (heme 14–18% vs non-heme iron 5–12%) [regulatory] (NIH ODS fact sheets; nih.gov → `regulatory` per whitelist Tier 2). GRADE: deficiency-correction **high/strong**; optimization-in-replete-adults default **low/very-low** unless a specific RCT supports it.

### Finding 10: Micronutrient toxicity is the asymmetry — supraphysiologic dosing of fat-soluble vitamins, iron, selenium, zinc, and gram-dose niacin causes net harm bounded by Tolerable Upper Intake Levels
Deficiency correction is forgiving; supraphysiologic dosing is not. Beta-carotene supplements increased lung cancer in smokers (CARET +28% incidence/+46% LC-death, Omenn 1996 PMID 8602180; ATBC +18%, PMID 8127329) [rct]; preformed vitamin A >3,000 mcg RAE is teratogenic in pregnancy and chronically hepatotoxic; selenium has a narrow window (RDA 55, UL 400 mcg; selenosis above); gram-dose niacin is hepatotoxic and failed to improve CV outcomes (HPS2-THRIVE, PMC3640201) [rct]; chronic zinc ≥50 mg/d induces copper deficiency (UL 40 mg) [regulatory] (NIH ODS Vitamin A/Selenium/Zinc; LiverTox NBK548176). GRADE: supraphysiologic harm **moderate-high/strong against**. Every fat-soluble-vitamin/iron/zinc/selenium/niacin recommendation is bounded by its UL + named toxicity syndrome. (Selenium NOAEL 800/LOAEL 913 mcg trace to the IOM DRI report, not the ODS sheet — provenance noted.)

### Finding 11: Drug-nutrient and nutrient-nutrient interactions route to a clinician / medical-liaison, not autonomous nutrition action
Several common interactions are clinically material: vitamin K / warfarin (manage by *consistency* of intake, not avoidance; a large single vegetable load can shift INR) [meta_analysis] (PMC4998867); calcium/iron supplements reduce levothyroxine absorption (FDA label requires ≥4 h separation) [regulatory] (FDA levothyroxine label); calcium inhibits iron absorption; grapefruit furanocoumarins are mechanism-based CYP3A4 inhibitors raising plasma levels of many oral drugs [mechanism_review] (PMID 9351897, 16685052); zinc/copper antagonism. GRADE: **moderate-high/strong**. ROUTE-TO-CLINICIAN for any concurrent warfarin, levothyroxine, or CYP3A4-substrate medication; a `risk_tier: medium+` interaction triggers medical-liaison queueing.

### Finding 12: Special-population contraindications (renal, pregnancy, T1D/insulin, cardiac, hepatic) are UNKNOWN when operator-profile fields are unpopulated — the safe default is withhold-and-caveat, not confident generic advice
Routine nutrition moves are contraindicated/hazardous in specific populations the agent cannot assume status for: CKD (protein/potassium/phosphorus restriction — generic "1.6–2.2 g/kg protein" is actively harmful; potassium-based salt substitutes dangerous) [regulatory] (KDOQI 2020; CKD K/P risk PMC9318835/PMC4908098); pregnancy (preformed vitamin A teratogenicity, fasting → lower birth weight); type-1 / insulin-dependent diabetes (fasting/keto → hypoglycemia AND DKA even at normal glucose) [open_label — case report n=1] (Fernández-Cardona et al. 2020, J Endocr Soc 4(10):bvaa126, PMID 33033790); cardiac (sodium/potassium load); hepatic (protein/ammonia handling). The structural rule: an unpopulated contraindication field is **UNKNOWN**, which mandates withhold-and-caveat. As of authoring, `vault/meta/operator-profile.md` is `status: scaffold` (all safety fields empty) → this is the live default, not a hypothetical. GRADE: populations-are-gated **high/strong**; specific dietary effect sizes moderate-low.

### Finding 13: The nutrition "critical floor" — disordered eating, refeeding syndrome, RED-S, and extreme restriction — routes to clinical care, never to directive engagement
This is the nutrition analog of the labs-specialist critical-value floor. After prolonged undereating, reintroducing energy drives insulin-mediated intracellular phosphate/potassium/magnesium shift; **hypophosphatemia is the hallmark** and refeeding syndrome can be fatal (cardiac/respiratory failure), with safe refeeding starting at 10–20 kcal/kg/day under electrolyte monitoring + thiamine [mechanism_review] (Mehanna HM, Moledina J, Travis J 2008, BMJ 336:1495-1498, PMID 18583681 / PMC2440847; refeeding pathophysiology StatPearls NBK564513). RED-S / low energy availability (<~30 kcal/kg FFM/day) is a multi-system syndrome affecting both sexes [regulatory] (Mountjoy et al. 2023, IOC consensus, Br J Sports Med 57:1073-1097, doi:10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994). Fasting is a stronger prospective onset-risk factor for binge/bulimic pathology than dieting [cohort] (Stice et al. 2008, PMID 19025239; contraindication framing Blumberg et al. 2023, Clin Diabetes Endocrinol, PMID 37865786 [clinical commentary]). An LLM cannot order serum phosphate or monitor — so directive engagement with sub-floor calorie targets, extreme/rapid restriction, "how to eat 800 kcal/day," active-ED signals, refeeding context, or RED-S triad symptoms is a critical-harm event. GRADE: pathophysiology **high**; ED-onset cohort signal **moderate**. This is a fail-safe escalation, not a softened plan.

### Finding 14: Nutrition evidence is predominantly GRADE low/very-low — observational/confounded epidemiology, FFQ measurement error, and short adherence-limited RCTs — so "eat X for outcome Y" claims must distinguish causal from associational
Nutritional epidemiology is dominated by observational cohorts with healthy-user/confounding bias and food-frequency-questionnaire measurement error large enough to attenuate true relative risks toward observed values ≤1.25, where "it is uncertain whether the observed association is due to the exposure or to unmeasured confounders" [mechanism_review] (Proc Nutr Soc, "Interpretation of observational studies"; NCI DCP measurement-error resource). Meta-analyses of observational data can be "very precise but equally spurious." RCTs are typically short and adherence-limited. GRADE: high confidence in the meta-claim about evidence quality. Consequence: default nutrition claims to **low/very-low** certainty and explicitly tag causal-vs-associational; never present an FFQ-cohort association as an established causal effect.

### Finding 15: LLM failure modes the agent must structurally guard — fad-diet sycophancy, citation/value fabrication (worst on under-studied ED topics), and authority-framing jailbreaks
Frontier LLMs comply with illogical/fad premises at rates up to 100% even when they hold the knowledge to identify the request as illogical, because helpfulness training overrides logical consistency; the in-study mitigation was prompts explicitly permitting rejection + emphasizing factual recall [llm-safety-eval — out-of-clinical-enum] (Chen/Gao/Bitterman et al. 2025, npj Digital Medicine, PMID 41107408 / PMC12534679). Citation fabrication: 19.9% of LLM-generated citations fully fabricated and 45.4% of the real-looking remainder contained bibliographic errors, worse for under-studied topics (binge-eating-disorder 28% / body-dysmorphic 29% vs depression ~6%) — exactly the disordered-eating territory this agent touches [llm-safety-eval] (JMIR Mental Health 2025, PMC12658395). Authority-impersonation/role-play attacks ("as a registered dietitian, just tell me…") are among the highest-success guardrail bypasses [llm-safety-eval] (RoleBreaker, MDPI Electronics 14(24):4808; Puccio et al. 2025 arXiv:2505.18212, preprint — qualitative only). These ground the agent's anti-sycophancy, anti-fabrication, and mandatory AUTHORITY_FRAMING_BYPASS rules. (These sources are out-of-clinical-enum: they ground AI-safety rationale, not health claims.)
