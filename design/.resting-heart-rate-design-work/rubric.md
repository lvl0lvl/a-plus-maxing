## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — What RHR Is & What the Device Measures:** **Resting heart rate (RHR)** = the number of heartbeats per minute (bpm) at complete physical + mental rest, reflecting the **balance of parasympathetic (vagal, dominant at rest — slows HR) and sympathetic tone** on the **sinoatrial node**, plus the **intrinsic SA-node rate (~100 bpm before autonomic input)**. A LOWER RHR generally reflects **greater vagal tone + cardiovascular/aerobic fitness** (a larger stroke volume → fewer beats needed); athletes commonly have RHR in the 40s-50s (physiological sinus bradycardia). **What WEARABLES measure:** consumer devices derive HR from **photoplethysmography (PPG)** (optical, wrist/finger) or chest-strap **ECG/electrical**; "resting HR" on wearables is typically the **lowest sustained HR (often during sleep) or a morning/seated reading** — note the device-specific definition matters (nocturnal-minimum vs daytime-seated differ). HR is EASIER + MORE ACCURATELY measured by PPG than HRV (a single rate, not beat-to-beat variability) — set up section C. Distinguish RHR from max HR + HR reserve.
- **B — Interpretation, Values, Population Norms & Personal Baseline:** **UNLIKE HRV, RHR has clinically useful POPULATION reference ranges:** the conventional "normal" adult resting HR is **~60-100 bpm**, with **<60 bpm common in fit/athletic individuals (physiological bradycardia)** and **trained endurance athletes often 40-55 bpm**; >100 bpm at rest = tachycardia. Give the age/sex/fitness context (women slightly higher on average; RHR varies with age). BUT ALSO the personal-baseline-trend (load-bearing for the wearable use): **an individual's day-to-day RHR deviation from their own rolling baseline is a sensitive recovery/illness/stress signal** (an elevated morning/nocturnal RHR vs baseline flags incomplete recovery, illness onset, alcohol, heat, or under-sleep). The "lower is generally better within the physiological range" framing — BUT note the J-shape/very-low caveats (pathological bradycardia is different from athletic bradycardia). The measurement-consistency requirement (same time, posture, state). Both population norms AND personal trend are actionable for RHR.
- **C — Measurement & Device Validity (replaces assay-standardization):** **PPG (optical) vs ECG (gold standard) for HR:** PPG-derived HR is **HIGHLY accurate at rest / during sleep (the resting-HR use case)** — far more so than PPG-derived HRV — because it only needs the average rate, not precise R-R intervals; accuracy degrades with motion/exercise + at higher HR, and with skin tone / perfusion / fit factors (note the documented PPG accuracy disparities). Cite NAMED validation studies + a systematic review/meta. The **resting-HR DEFINITION differs across devices** (nocturnal minimum vs lowest-30-min vs morning) → cross-device RHR values are NOT directly interchangeable; consistency-of-conditions matters. Note the wellness-vs-medical-device distinction (consumer HR is generally accurate for trends but most are wellness, not diagnostic; some ECG features are FDA-cleared). Reproducibility/test-retest.
- **D — Determinants & Significance:** **What RAISES RHR (the actionable day-to-day signals):** acute physical/psychological **stress**; **ILLNESS / infection (an elevated RHR often precedes/accompanies symptoms)**; **overtraining / under-recovery / high training load**; **ALCOHOL (a reproducible acute overnight RHR elevation)**; poor/short sleep; **caffeine, stimulants, nicotine**; dehydration, **heat / high ambient temperature**; deconditioning; some medications (stimulants raise; **beta-blockers lower**); pregnancy; higher with age-related/cardiac pathology. **What LOWERS RHR:** **aerobic/endurance fitness + training (the dominant chronic determinant — a training-induced resting bradycardia)**, recovery, good sleep, vagal tone. **SIGNIFICANCE (load-bearing — STRONGER + more population-applicable than HRV):** **ELEVATED resting heart rate is robustly + dose-dependently associated with increased CARDIOVASCULAR and ALL-CAUSE MORTALITY across many large population cohorts + meta-analyses** (this is one of the most reproducible epidemiological findings — cite NAMED large cohorts/meta with HRs per +10-bpm; note it's largely observational/associational but consistent + biologically graded, and that lowering RHR pharmacologically is NOT uniformly protective [the distinction between RHR-as-a-marker vs a target]); the **training-monitoring / recovery use** (an elevated morning RHR vs baseline flags under-recovery/illness — the classic + most-validated wearable signal); resting tachycardia + bradycardia clinical thresholds. **Limitations (load-bearing):** RHR is confounded (caffeine, stress, sleep, position, time-of-day, recent activity, hydration, temperature); the device resting-HR definition varies; the mortality association is associational + RHR is a marker not necessarily a modifiable target; consumer RHR is a WELLNESS/trend metric (accurate for HR but interpret trends, not single readings); athletic bradycardia ≠ pathological bradycardia.
