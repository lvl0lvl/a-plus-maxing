## Section D — Safety, dosing, route, PK, regulatory & monitoring

### D.1 Human dosing — no established dose

There is **no established or approved human dose** for exogenous MOTS-c. MOTS-c has not completed a dedicated safety/dose-finding trial in humans as a standalone native peptide. Grey-market vendors circulate doses of 5–15 mg administered subcutaneously once daily, with purported cycling protocols (e.g., 5-on/2-off); these figures originate exclusively from vendor labeling and community aggregation [1, vendor_label][2, anecdote_aggregate] and are NOT grounded in human pharmacology data. They may not be used to support a numerical safety or efficacy claim in this wiki.

The only human clinical pharmacology data available is for **CB4211**, an engineered analog of MOTS-c developed by CohBar, Inc. (see D.3).

---

### D.2 Route of administration & pharmacokinetics

**Route:** All preclinical MOTS-c studies used intraperitoneal (IP) or subcutaneous (SC) injection in rodent models [3, animal]. The native peptide is not orally bioavailable; proteolytic degradation in the GI tract is expected for a 16-amino-acid peptide.

**Half-life / clearance (endogenous peptide):** No formal human PK study of exogenous native MOTS-c has been published, and published preclinical rodent data did not include formal PK characterization. The native peptide is understood to have a short circulating half-life / rapid clearance [4, anecdote_aggregate] — a qualitative inference consistent with the rapid post-exercise normalization of endogenous MOTS-c, and explicitly cited by CohBar as the rationale for engineering the stabilized CB4211 analog [5, open_label]. No specific human half-life value can be stated from a primary source.

**Stability problem:** The short half-life and susceptibility to proteolysis of the native peptide are precisely the engineering rationale for the CB4211 analog. CohBar's stated objective in developing CB4211 was to overcome the native peptide's limitations of rapid clearance and poor formulation stability [5, open_label]. (Reconstituted research-peptide stability is formulation-dependent and is not a clinical PK parameter; no primary-sourced clinical stability figure is available for the native peptide.)

**Preclinical dosing range (animals):** Mouse studies used IP doses of 0.5 mg/kg/day (foundational 2015 work) up to 5–15 mg/kg/day in later exercise-metabolism studies. These cannot be converted to human equivalent doses without formal allometric scaling and safety bridging studies, neither of which has been published for MOTS-c.

---

### D.3 CB4211 (MOTS-c analog) — Phase 1a/1b trial: safety and termination

CB4211 is a next-generation analog of MOTS-c engineered for improved stability and clinical feasibility. It is the **only MOTS-c-class compound to enter human clinical trials** (NCT03998514, CohBar, Inc.) [6, open_label].

**Trial design:**
- Phase 1a: Double-blind, placebo-controlled, single and multiple ascending dose in 65 healthy adults over one week.
- Phase 1b: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 25 mg SC once daily × 4 weeks in 20 obese adults with NAFLD (minimum 10% liver fat); 11 CB4211, 9 placebo.

**Safety and tolerability outcomes (2021 topline):** No serious adverse events were reported. Primary endpoints of safety and tolerability were met. The only adverse events occurring in >10% of CB4211 recipients during Phase 1b were **transient, generally mild-to-moderate injection site reactions** (CohBar company press release [7, open_label]; no peer-reviewed publication of Phase 1a/1b results exists; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03998514 shows the trial as completed with no results posted).

**Earlier suspension (2018):** The Phase 1 trial was temporarily suspended in November 2018 due to persistent subcutaneous nodules at injection sites. CohBar's Chief Scientific Officer characterized the nodules as non-tender, non-erythematous, non-purulent, freely mobile accumulations of undissolved drug ("CB4211 just sitting there"), with no evidence of local tissue harm. The suspension was used to refine injection technique [8, open_label].

**Program status — discontinued:** In post-Phase 1b disclosures, CohBar stated that "the formulation of CB4211 used in the Phase 1b stage of the trial is not suitable for further development" and that "efforts to develop an improved formulation have not been successful to date." The company suspended IND-enabling work for the next phase; clinical development has not advanced beyond Phase 1b. As of the 2023 SEC registration filing, CohBar described exploring partnership opportunities rather than internal continuation [9, open_label]. The CB4211 program was discontinued in 2023 when CohBar resolved to commence liquidation and dissolution (see E.4).

**Bottom line for wiki readers:** CB4211's Phase 1 established short-term tolerability at one dose level in a small, controlled population per company press releases (no peer-reviewed publication); it does NOT constitute a safety profile for the native MOTS-c peptide, nor does it characterize dose-response across a range, nor long-term safety. The Phase 1b formulation was deemed unsuitable for further development; the program was discontinued in 2023. Phase 2 efficacy and safety data do not exist.

### D.4 Contraindications and theoretical risks

**Hypoglycemia:** MOTS-c activates AMPK and promotes GLUT4 translocation to muscle cell membranes, increasing insulin-independent glucose uptake. In CB4211 Phase 1b, CohBar reported significant decreases in glucose as an exploratory endpoint (company press release [7, open_label]; no peer-reviewed publication). Co-administration with insulin, sulfonylureas, or other insulin secretagogues creates an additive glucose-lowering risk; hypoglycemia is a plausible pharmacodynamic interaction without a characterized safe threshold.

**Unknown long-term effects:** No chronic exposure data in humans exists. MOTS-c influences mitochondrial biogenesis, systemic metabolism, and potentially immune and inflammatory pathways [3, animal][4, anecdote_aggregate]. Long-term consequences of sustained supraphysiologic AMPK activation are uncharacterized in humans.

**Metabolic-modulator class concerns:** As a class, AMPK activators can affect hepatic glucose output, lipid oxidation, and potentially suppress mTOR signaling. The clinical significance of these effects with exogenous MOTS-c dosing is unknown.

**Population-specific cautions:**
- Individuals with type 1 or insulin-treated type 2 diabetes: heightened hypoglycemia risk.
- Pregnancy/lactation: no data; theoretical risk given metabolic and mitogenic signaling.
- Concurrent NASH/liver disease: CB4211 showed ALT/AST reduction, but confounding with underlying disease and off-target effects are uncharacterized.

**Injection site reactions** are the best-documented adverse effect from human data (CB4211 Phase 1). Whether native MOTS-c carries the same formulation-dependent nodule risk is unknown.

### D.5 Regulatory status

#### FDA (United States)

MOTS-c is **not FDA-approved** for any indication and is not legally marketed for human therapeutic use in the United States.

**Compounding (503A/503B):** MOTS-c is not on the FDA 503A Category 1 (eligible for compounding) list. Regulatory-tracker sources (PeptideClarity, newtropin.com) report that MOTS-c was removed from the 503A Category 2 interim bulks list effective April 22, 2026; however, no Federal Register document specifically describing this removal has been identified — these sources provide no FR citation number for that action and are vendor-authored.

What is confirmed via primary source: Federal Register notice FR 2026-07361 (published April 16, 2026, 91 FR 20465) announces a forthcoming PCAC (Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee) public meeting and solicitation of comments, and explicitly names "MOTS-C (free base)/MOTS-C acetate" as a bulk drug substance nominated for inclusion on the Section 503A Bulk Drug Substances List, scheduled for PCAC evaluation on July 23, 2026 [10, regulatory]. This notice is consistent with MOTS-c having exited Category 2 (which would precede formal PCAC consideration for Category 1 eligibility), but the removal action itself is not documented in a separately identified FR notice.

Even a favorable PCAC recommendation requires subsequent FDA rulemaking before licensed 503A pharmacies may legally compound MOTS-c with a prescription. As of the date of this entry (June 2026), **legal compounding of MOTS-c is not authorized**.

#### WADA (Anti-doping)

MOTS-c is **prohibited** under the 2026 WADA Prohibited List, Section **S4.4 — Metabolic Modulators**, covering activators of the AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) [11, regulatory]. The Prohibited List PDF (wada-ama.org, effective January 1, 2026) was not directly fetchable for this entry; the S4.4 section enumerates AMPK activators including AICAR. Whether MOTS-c is listed **explicitly by name** in the PDF text or is covered only by the AMPK-activator class definition (which would encompass it by mechanism) could not be independently confirmed from a direct PDF read during research. The WADA citation [11] is to the issuing body's published list and is treated as tier 1; the prohibitory status under S4.4 is not in dispute. Prohibition applies **in- and out-of-competition** (S4 non-specified substance). S4.4 substances are non-Specified Substances, meaning no reduction in sanction period is available for contamination defenses. Athletes subject to WADA-compliant testing must consider MOTS-c prohibited regardless of prescription status or therapeutic intent.

### D.6 Monitoring parameters

A clinician supervising any investigational or off-label MOTS-c use (e.g., in a future approved trial context) would rationally monitor the following given the known mechanistic profile:

**Glucose metabolism:**
- Fasting plasma glucose (pre-treatment baseline and at intervals)
- HbA1c (baseline; at 8–12 weeks of sustained use)
- Post-prandial glucose or OGTT-derived insulin sensitivity index (as used in NCT07505745, the ongoing 2026 Phase 2a trial in prediabetes/obesity [12, regulatory])
- Signs/symptoms of hypoglycemia, especially if co-administered with glucose-lowering agents

**Liver enzymes:**
- ALT, AST (CB4211 Phase 1b showed reductions in NASH subjects; monitoring appropriate to detect either improvement or unexpected hepatotoxicity)

**Injection site:**
- Palpation for subcutaneous nodules at each visit (informed by the CB4211 2018 suspension experience)

**Body weight and metabolic panel:**
- Weight, lipids (given AMPK effects on fat oxidation)

**Stopping criteria (theoretical, not from completed trial data):**
- Confirmed hypoglycemic episode (blood glucose <70 mg/dL with symptoms)
- ALT/AST >3× upper limit of normal from baseline
- Persistent painful or expanding injection site nodules
- Systemic allergic reaction
