# Rubric — ferritin (serum ferritin, biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** **ferritin** = the major intracellular **iron-STORAGE protein** — a ~450 kDa 24-subunit shell (**H [heavy, ferroxidase] + L [light] chains**) sequestering up to ~4,500 Fe³⁺ atoms, keeping iron non-toxic + bioavailable; predominantly intracellular (liver/Kupffer cells, spleen, marrow macrophages); **serum ferritin is a small SECRETED fraction (mostly L-rich, relatively iron-POOR, glycosylated, actively secreted — NOT just leaked)** that, under steady non-inflammatory conditions, **correlates with total body iron stores** (the load-bearing concept: ~1 ng/mL serum ferritin ≈ ~8-10 mg storage iron). Regulation: iron-responsive (IRE/IRP system — ferritin synthesis ↑ with iron) AND **cytokine-responsive (IL-6, IL-1β → ferritin is a POSITIVE ACUTE-PHASE REACTANT — synthesis ↑ in inflammation independent of iron)**. The dual regulation (iron + inflammation) is WHY serum ferritin is the best single iron-stores test but is confounded by inflammation. Iron-handling context (transferrin/TSAT, hepcidin as the master regulator).
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & The Iron-Deficiency Threshold Debate (load-bearing):** units **ng/mL (= µg/L)** (1:1); wide reference (~**men 30-300, women 15-200** roughly, age/sex/lab-dependent — males higher); **THE LOAD-BEARING THRESHOLD DEBATE for diagnosing IRON DEFICIENCY:** the classic **<15 ng/mL** (high specificity, older WHO) vs the increasingly recommended **<30 ng/mL** (better sensitivity; many guidelines now use ~30) vs the **<45-50** in some contexts; the BMJ/WHO 2020 thresholds; **in the presence of INFLAMMATION the cutoff is RAISED** (e.g. ferritin <100 [or <300 with TSAT<20%] may still indicate deficiency in CKD/heart failure/inflammation — the functional-iron-deficiency framing). Lay out the cutoffs + WHY they disagree (specificity-vs-sensitivity + the inflammation adjustment). The **HIGH end / hyperferritinemia**: no firm "upper normal" for disease — markedly high values (>1000) widen the differential. Note ferritin is interpreted WITH **CRP** (inflammation), **TSAT/transferrin** (iron studies), and the clinical picture.
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** **immunoassay** (immunoturbidimetric / chemiluminescent / ELISA — antibody-based); the historically POOR inter-assay/inter-method agreement → the **WHO International Standard (the recombinant ferritin IS, e.g. WHO 19/118 / earlier 94/572) + harmonization efforts** (why a "30" differs across platforms — parallels the vitamin-D standardization story); the **HOOK EFFECT** (very high ferritin can paradoxically read falsely LOW in some one-step immunoassays — a key pitfall); heterophile-antibody interference; the H vs L isoform + glycosylation (assays measure mostly the secreted L-rich form); pre-analytic stability; specimen (serum vs plasma). Note ferritin is NOT a functional iron test — pair with TSAT/transferrin saturation + the reticulocyte-hemoglobin/CHr where available.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **LOW ferritin (the most useful direction):** **<15-30 ng/mL is the most SPECIFIC single test for IRON-DEFICIENCY** (depleted stores) — causes: blood loss (GI, menstrual — the leading causes; ALWAYS investigate GI source in men + post-menopausal women), inadequate intake/absorption (celiac, IBD, bariatric, PPI, plant-based diet), increased demand (pregnancy, growth, endurance athletes/"sports anemia" + foot-strike hemolysis + hepcidin-driven), chronic blood donation. A LOW ferritin is ALWAYS true iron deficiency (high specificity — nothing else lowers it). **HIGH ferritin (a broad, non-specific differential — the interpretive challenge):** **INFLAMMATION / acute-phase (the most common cause of a high ferritin — NOT iron overload)**, infection, **liver disease (hepatocyte release)**, **alcohol**, malignancy, **metabolic/MASLD/metabolic syndrome (dysmetabolic hyperferritinemia)**, **iron overload (hereditary hemochromatosis [HFE C282Y] — but ferritin + TSAT needed; transfusional overload)**, and the **EXTREME hyperferritinemia → HLH / macrophage activation syndrome / adult-onset Still's (>1000s, a red flag)**. The KEY teaching: **a high ferritin is far more often inflammation/liver/metabolic than iron overload** — confirm overload with TSAT (>45%) + genetics; **functional iron deficiency** (high ferritin + low TSAT in CKD/HF/inflammation — iron is sequestered, unavailable). Prognostic: ferritin as an inflammation/severity + cardiometabolic-risk marker. **Limitations:** the acute-phase confound is the dominant pitfall (a "normal" or high ferritin does NOT exclude iron deficiency when inflammation is present — use CRP + TSAT + soluble transferrin receptor); assay hook effect; interpret WITH iron studies + CRP.
