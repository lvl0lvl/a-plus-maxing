## Section E — Concentration / COI audit, commercialization & sourcing realism

### E.1 Inventor origin and patent estate

SS-31 (elamipretide) was invented by **Hazel H. Szeto** (Weill Cornell Medicine, Department of Pharmacology) and **Peter W. Schiller** (Institut de Recherches Cliniques de Montréal / IRCM), with the compound named for their initials. The foundational mechanism paper — establishing that SS peptides selectively concentrate at the inner mitochondrial membrane and inhibit oxidative cell death — was published in 2004 by Zhao, Szeto et al. in the *Journal of Biological Chemistry* [1, in_vitro]. Patent prosecution was handled by Weill Cornell Medicine Enterprise Innovation (Cornell Research Foundation), which holds the underlying IP. The compound was licensed exclusively to Stealth Peptides Inc. (later Stealth BioTherapeutics) in 2006 when Szeto co-founded the company [2, anecdote_aggregate].

Szeto's financial conflict is **first-class and explicit:** mechanism papers co-authored by her carry the disclosure "The SS peptides described in this article are licensed for commercial research and development to Stealth Peptides Inc., a clinical stage biopharmaceutical company, in which H.H.S. and the Cornell Research Foundation have financial interests" [3, mechanism_review].

No independent academic group has run a fully independent RCT of elamipretide in any indication.

At the preclinical/mechanistic layer, independent groups have replicated SS-31 effects in animal models — notably a Chinese PLA General Hospital / Beijing Institute of Pharmacology group (National Natural Science Foundation of China funding, no Szeto authorship, no competing interests declared) demonstrating amelioration of LPS-induced mitochondrial dysfunction in mice [15, animal] — and Szeto's own 2013 JASN cardiolipin-mechanism paper (Weill Cornell / NIH-funded, with explicit Stealth financial-interest disclosure) [3]. The preclinical replication is broader than the clinical, but the clinical evidence base is **entirely single-sponsor-dominated** with one exception.

### E.4 Commercialization trajectory

**2004:** Szeto discovers SS-31 at Weill Cornell [2, anecdote_aggregate].

**2006:** Stealth Peptides Inc. founded; Cornell license executed [2, anecdote_aggregate].

**2020:** MMPOWER-3 Phase 3 trial (primary mitochondrial myopathy, 218 patients, 24 weeks double-blind) fails both co-primary endpoints — 6-minute walk distance and PMMSA total fatigue score [6, rct].

**2022:** Stealth BioTherapeutics executed a going-private transaction and was subsequently delisted from Nasdaq. (Specific merger price, premium, and buyer identities are reported in secondary sources but could not be verified against a primary SEC filing or company press release retrievable at research time; these figures are omitted pending primary-source confirmation.)

**2023–2024 (exact CRL date not primary-verified):** FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (CRL) on the Barth syndrome NDA; the company subsequently undertook restructuring including staff reductions. (CRL issuance date and layoff details reported in secondary sources; primary FDA correspondence not publicly retrievable — CRL date asserted as "2023" in some secondary sources but not confirmed against an FDA document; the May 2025 CRL date cited elsewhere is also unverified against a primary source. Per the verified approval timeline: resubmission January 29, 2024; FDA accelerated approval October 30, 2025 [14, regulatory]. The exact CRL sequence is not primary-verified and is stated as such.)

**2023–2024:** NuPOWER Phase 3 trial (nPMD, nuclear DNA mutations, NCT05162768) ran its 48-week treatment phase; primary completion September 30, 2024; final study completion December 4, 2024; 102 participants enrolled [16, regulatory]. Results not yet published as of June 2026.

**2024:** NDA for Barth syndrome resubmitted; FDA accepted and granted priority review. (Timeline reported in secondary sources; FDA acceptance letter not publicly retrievable at research time.)

**2025 (Oct 30):** **FDA grants accelerated approval** to FORZINITY (elamipretide HCl injection) for Barth syndrome — indicated to improve muscle strength in adult and pediatric patients weighing ≥30 kg; 40 mg SC once daily. Basis: open-label extension knee-extensor strength improvement (+63 N at week 168) after the randomized phase missed primary endpoints. NDA 215244 [14, regulatory]. This is the **first FDA-approved mitochondria-targeting drug and the first approved treatment for Barth syndrome.**

**2025 (Q4):** Commercial supply via specialty pharmacy becomes available.

**2026:** Company operating under the name **Mighty Therapeutics** (rebrand date not confirmed against a primary source; reported in secondary sources). Pipeline includes Barth syndrome confirmatory post-marketing trial and nPMD program.

**Key caveat:** Barth syndrome affects fewer than 200 individuals in the United States. Forzinity's commercial market is ultra-rare. The MMPOWER-3 failure in the larger primary mitochondrial myopathy population (218 patients enrolled) remains the pivotal negative result; post-hoc subgroup analyses (nuclear DNA subgroup) generated the NuPOWER hypothesis. NuPOWER primary completion was September 2024; results not yet published as of June 2026 [16, regulatory].

### E.5 Sourcing realism — clinical-grade vs. grey-market

**Regulatory status as of June 2026:** Elamipretide is FDA-approved only for Barth syndrome (≥30 kg) via accelerated approval (Oct 30 2025, NDA 215244). It carries no approval for anti-aging, longevity, mitochondrial enhancement, heart failure, or athletic performance. Clinical-grade material (FORZINITY) is a subcutaneous injectable manufactured under pharmaceutical GMP, available only via specialty pharmacy with a valid prescription for the approved indication [14, regulatory].

**Grey-market landscape:** Multiple research-chemical vendors sell peptides labeled "SS-31 / Elamipretide" for "research use only" — including vendors with names incorporating explicit grey-market framing (e.g., "Grey Research Peptides"). At least one vendor (Novopro Labs) lists the product as "discontinued due to IP conflict," acknowledging Mighty Therapeutics' patent estate. Vendor-stated Certificates of Analysis (COA) and third-party testing claims are unverifiable from the outside; independent pharmaceutical-grade purity assurance cannot be assumed.

**The clinical-vs-grey-market distinction is material:** clinical trials used formulations manufactured under IND-compliant GMP with verified identity and sterility. Grey-market "SS-31" peptides synthesized by research-chemical vendors have no regulatory oversight, no chain of custody to authenticated reference standard, and variable risk of aggregation, oxidation, or impurities in a sequence-sensitive tetrapeptide. No published data exist on grey-market SS-31 bioavailability or safety profile in humans.
