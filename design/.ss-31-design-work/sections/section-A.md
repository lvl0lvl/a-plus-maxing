## Section A — Identity, mechanism & molecular pharmacology

### A.1 Identity and nomenclature

SS-31 is a synthetic, cell-permeable tetrapeptide with the sequence
**D-Arg–2′,6′-dimethylTyr–Lys–Phe–NH₂** (C₃₂H₄₉N₉O₅; MW 639.8 g/mol;
CAS 736992-21-5). The compound is known under multiple development names:
**elamipretide** (current INN), **MTP-131** (Stealth BioTherapeutics internal
code), and **Bendavia** (the cardiac-trial brand). Its approved brand name
(October 30, 2025, FDA accelerated approval for Barth syndrome) is **Forzinity**.

The peptide belongs to the **Szeto-Schiller (SS) family** of aromatic-cationic
tetrapeptides, developed by Hazel H. Szeto (Weill Cornell Medicine) and
Peter W. Schiller (IRCM, Montreal). The founding SS-peptide discovery, including
SS-31, was first described in 2004 [1, in_vitro]. Szeto's 2006 AAPS review
formalized the SS-peptide class and established the >1000-fold mitochondrial
accumulation benchmark [2, mechanism_review].

The C-terminal amide and the incorporation of D-arginine and the non-canonical
residue 2′,6′-dimethyltyrosine (Dmt) confer resistance to enzymatic degradation
and are load-bearing for free-radical scavenging activity [1, in_vitro].

### A.2 The broader SS-peptide class

SS-31 is one of several alternating aromatic-cationic tetrapeptides in the
SS series. Key family members:

| Peptide | Sequence | Notable property |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| **SS-31** | D-Arg–Dmt–Lys–Phe–NH₂ | Contains Dmt; free-radical scavenging; extended membrane-bound conformation |
| **SS-20** | Phe–D-Arg–Phe–Lys–NH₂ | Lacks Dmt; no significant free-radical scavenging; still mitochondria-targeted via CL binding |
| **SS-02** | Dmt–D-Arg–Phe–Lys–NH₂ | Dmt at position 1; alternate register |

A 2022 eLife structure-activity study of the full tetrapeptide pharmacology
class established that **all SS-peptide analogs except SS-31 form H-bonded
reverse-turn conformations** when membrane-bound; SS-31's extended conformation
is a direct consequence of steric constraints imposed by the two methyl groups
on Dmt [3, mechanism_review]. The same study confirmed that SS-20 retains
mitochondrial efficacy without antioxidant activity, demonstrating that
cardiolipin binding — not free-radical scavenging — is the primary mechanistic
driver for the class [3, mechanism_review].

### A.3 Mechanism of action

#### A.3.1 Selective concentration in the inner mitochondrial membrane

SS-peptides bear an alternating aromatic-cationic motif that drives passive
partitioning into anionic lipid bilayers. The inner mitochondrial membrane
(IMM) is uniquely enriched in **cardiolipin (CL)**, a tetra-acyl anionic
phospholipid not found in the outer mitochondrial membrane or plasma membrane
at appreciable concentrations. This CL enrichment — plus the large
electrochemical gradient (−180 mV) across the IMM — results in SS-31
accumulating >1000-fold within the IMM relative to the cytoplasm in cellular
models [1, in_vitro; 2, mechanism_review]. **(Evidence base: in-vitro cell
models; no direct human intramitochondrial concentration measurements
available.)**

#### A.3.2 Cardiolipin binding — the load-bearing mechanism

SS-31 binds CL through **dual electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions**:
the guanidinium group of D-Arg and the ε-amino group of Lys engage the
anionic phosphate head-groups of CL; the Dmt and Phe aromatic rings insert
into the acyl-chain hydrophobic domain. A 2020 JBC study (Mitchell et al.,
in isolated mitochondrial membranes and model liposomes) quantified this
binding as **saturable** (6.9–7.4 lipids per peptide for CL vs. 15.4 for
phosphatidylglycerol), entropy-driven, and demonstrated that the primary
consequence is a **controlled downward modulation of membrane surface
electrostatics** rather than simple antioxidant quenching [4, in_vitro].
**(Evidence base: biochemical model membranes and isolated mitochondria from
mouse; not a human study.)**

A 2020 PNAS cross-linking/mass spectrometry study (Chavez et al.) mapped
**12 mitochondrial protein interactors** of SS-31, all documented CL-binding
proteins, spanning Complexes I–V of the ETC and 2-oxoglutarate metabolism.
Cross-linked sites mapped proximal to known CL–protein interfaces,
establishing that SS-31's protein contacts are secondary consequences of
its CL co-localization [5, in_vitro]. **(Evidence base: aged mouse heart
mitochondria, n = 3 biological replicates.)**

#### A.3.3 Stabilization of cristae architecture and cytochrome c

Cardiolipin is required for the curvature geometry of mitochondrial cristae and
for anchoring cytochrome c to the IMM in its electron-carrier (not peroxidase)
conformation. During ischemia or oxidative stress, CL undergoes peroxidation
that disrupts heme iron–methionine coordination in cytochrome c, converting it
to a **peroxidase** that amplifies lipid oxidation and triggers apoptotic
release.

Birk et al. (2013, JASN) demonstrated in rat renal ischemia-reperfusion that
SS-31 pretreatment (1 mg/kg i.v., rat, n = 8 per group):

- Prevented cytochrome c conversion to peroxidase
- Preserved cristae membrane ultrastructure (electron microscopy)
- Enabled rapid ATP recovery upon reperfusion
- Reduced tubular apoptosis and preserved function

Binding affinity for CL was measured at 1.87 ± 0.64 μM [6, animal].
**(Evidence base: rat ischemia-reperfusion model; not a human mechanistic study.)**

Szeto's 2014 Br J Pharmacol review synthesized the mechanistic model across
pre-clinical studies: SS-31 protects CL's structural role → maintains cristae
organization → stabilizes ETC supercomplex assembly → improves oxidative
phosphorylation efficiency → reduces electron leak and mitochondrial ROS
production [7, mechanism_review].

#### A.3.4 Effects on ETC efficiency, ATP, and ROS

The net bioenergetic consequences, established in animal and cell models:

- **Improved electron flux** through ETC supercomplex stabilization →
  higher ATP yield per oxygen consumed (improved P/O ratio).
- **Reduced pathological ROS** — not through direct scavenging by SS-31
  (though Dmt has intrinsic free-radical scavenging capacity), but primarily
  by reducing electron leak at Complexes I and III.
- **No effect in young/healthy mitochondria** — the Chavez 2020 PNAS study
  found SS-31 improved respiration and reduced H₂O₂ production in aged mouse
  mitochondria but not young controls, consistent with a mechanism that
  rescues dysfunctional CL rather than augmenting normal function [5, in_vitro].

**(Evidence base: in-vitro and rodent animal models for the above three
points. Human clinical data address functional outcomes, not direct
intramitochondrial ETC measurements.)**

### A.4 Human evidence — regulatory context

Elamipretide received FDA **accelerated approval** (NDA 215244; **October 30,
2025**) to improve muscle strength in adult and pediatric patients with Barth
syndrome (BTHS) weighing ≥ 30 kg (40 mg SC once daily) — a rare X-linked
mitochondrial disorder caused by tafazzin mutations that impair CL remodeling.
This makes it **the first therapeutic directly targeting the mitochondrial
etiology of BTHS and the first FDA-approved mitochondria-targeting drug** [8,
regulatory]. The FDA label states that "continued approval for this indication
may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in a
confirmatory trial" [8, regulatory].

The pivotal TAZPOWER trial (Thompson et al., 2021, Genetics in Medicine;
n = 12 males age 12–35, crossover RCT) failed its pre-specified primary
endpoints (6-minute walk test and fatigue score at 12 weeks). The open-label
extension (OLE) over 168 weeks showed: cumulative 6MWT improvement of +96 m;
**median knee extensor muscle strength gain of +63 N** (range 38–78 N, from a
baseline of 124 N at OLE entry); and cardiolipin ratio improvement. The
accelerated approval was based on the OLE knee-extensor-strength finding as
an intermediate clinical endpoint — not the controlled-phase results — with a
confirmatory trial required [8, regulatory; 9, open_label]. A secondary
review (Zhao et al. 2026, Drug Discov Ther) discusses the approval pathway
in the broader context of mitochondrial therapeutics [10, regulatory].

**Clinical mechanism claim:** the human trial outcome provides functional
evidence in a CL-deficient disease; it does not directly confirm the IMM
concentration, cristae-stabilization, or ETC-efficiency mechanisms described
in A.3, which remain animal/in-vitro established.
