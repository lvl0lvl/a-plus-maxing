## Section B — Human clinical-trial evidence

The human clinical program for elamipretide (SS-31 / MTP-131) was conducted primarily by Stealth BioTherapeutics (the company's domain is now parked, suggesting it has ceased operations). The program spanned primary mitochondrial myopathy (PMM), Barth syndrome (BTHS), geographic atrophy (GA/dry AMD), and cardiac indications. Across the pivotal controlled trials, elamipretide was safe and well tolerated but failed to meet its primary efficacy endpoint in every randomized, controlled study with a prospectively powered superiority design. The one area of sustained functional signal is the TAZPOWER open-label extension (OLE) in Barth syndrome. Critically, the uncontrolled OLE knee-extensor-strength data — not the controlled-phase results — served as the basis for FDA accelerated approval (NDA 215244, October 30, 2025), making elamipretide (FORZINITY) the first FDA-approved treatment for Barth syndrome and the first approved mitochondria-targeting drug. This context is essential: the OLE is simultaneously uncontrolled (and therefore cannot establish causality) and regulatory-accepted as an intermediate endpoint warranting accelerated approval, with a confirmatory trial pending.

### B1. Primary Mitochondrial Myopathy (PMM)

**MMPOWER — Phase 1/2 (NCT02367014)**

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation trial. 36 adults with genetically confirmed PMM were allocated 9 per arm to three ascending IV doses (0.01, 0.10, or 0.25 mg/kg/hr over 2 hours/day) or placebo for 5 consecutive days. Primary endpoint: change from baseline in 6-minute walk test (6MWT) distance to Day 5. Results: mean 6MWT change was 13.5 m (low dose), 36.5 m (intermediate), 64.5 m (high dose), and 20.4 m (placebo). The high-dose arm showed a numerically larger gain (44 m over placebo), consistent with a dose-response trend, but the trial was not powered for statistical significance. Published in Neurology 2018 (PMID 29500292); the NCT record posts results without p-values, and the paper did not report a statistically significant difference [1, rct]. This Phase 1/2 supported dose selection for the Phase 3 program.

**MMPOWER-2 — Phase 2 crossover (NCT02805790)**

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover in 30 adults with PMM. Four weeks of daily subcutaneous elamipretide, 4-week washout, 4 weeks of the alternate arm. Primary endpoint: 6MWT distance at end of each treatment period (weeks 4 and 12). Results: mean distance 394.1 m (elamipretide) vs 378.2 m (placebo), a 15.9 m difference. P-values were not reported in the registry results section. Secondary endpoints including PMMSA fatigue score (9.1 vs 10.7) and Neuro-QoL fatigue T-score (50.9 vs 54.7) showed numerical trends favoring elamipretide; 3-timed up-and-go was essentially identical (35.3 vs 35.2 sec). No published peer-reviewed primary results paper for MMPOWER-2 was identified in the PubMed search. Results posted July 2020 to ClinicalTrials.gov [2, regulatory].

**MMPOWER-3 — Phase 3 pivotal (NCT03323749) — PRIMARY ENDPOINT MISSED**

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group trial. 218 adults with genetically confirmed PMM were randomized 1:1 to daily subcutaneous elamipretide 40 mg (n = 109) or placebo (n = 109) for 24 weeks (double-blind period), with an open-label extension planned. Co-primary endpoints: (1) change from baseline in 6MWT distance and (2) change from baseline in the Primary Mitochondrial Myopathy Symptom Assessment (PMMSA) total fatigue score, both at week 24.

Results at week 24:
- 6MWT: elamipretide +15.33 m (±61.49) vs placebo +17.39 m (±51.70) — placebo numerically superior
- PMMSA total fatigue: elamipretide −1.18 (±2.13) vs placebo −1.09 (±2.44) — near-identical

Neither co-primary endpoint was met. The trial was terminated early on the basis that "Part 1, double-blind portion of the trial did not meet the primary endpoints." Published in Neurology 2023;101(3):e238–e252 (Karaa A et al., PMID 37268435) [3, rct]. This is the pivotal Phase 3 failure that ended the PMM program.

### B2. Barth Syndrome

**TAZPOWER — Phase 2/3 crossover (NCT03098797)**

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover in 12 males with genetically confirmed Barth syndrome (BTHS). Part 1: two 12-week treatment periods (40 mg subcutaneous elamipretide daily) separated by a 4-week washout. Part 2: open-label extension (OLE) for up to 192 weeks. Co-primary endpoints: (1) 6MWT and (2) BTHS Symptom Assessment (BTHS-SA) total fatigue score, both in the Part 1 crossover.

Part 1 (double-blind crossover) results: 6MWT improved ~43 m in both arms (elamipretide: 400.1 → 443.1 m; placebo: 412.6 → 443.9 m) with no between-group difference. BTHS-SA fatigue improved ~1.4 points in both arms (elamipretide 7.67 → 6.31; placebo 7.43 → 6.24) with no between-group separation. The crossover phase did not demonstrate statistical superiority of elamipretide over placebo on either primary endpoint [4, regulatory]. Results were posted to ClinicalTrials.gov April 2024.

Part 2 (OLE — uncontrolled): 10 participants entered; 8 completed week 168. At week 168, key results from the OLE were:

- **Knee extensor muscle strength:** median change +63 N (range 38–78 N) from an OLE baseline of 124 N. *This is the endpoint on which FDA accelerated approval rested* (see FDA approval below).
- **6MWT:** cumulative improvement from OLE baseline +96.1 m (P = .003).
- **BTHS-SA fatigue:** scores remained below OLE baseline throughout.
- **Cardiac volumes:** 3D left ventricular stroke volume, end-diastolic volume, and end-systolic volume showed improvement trends.
- **Cardiolipin ratio (MLCL/CL):** improved and correlated with functional outcomes.

Safety was acceptable; injection-site reactions were the predominant adverse event. Published in Genetics in Medicine 2024 (PMID 38602181) [5, open_label].

**FDA accelerated approval (NDA 215244, October 30, 2025):** On the basis of the OLE knee-extensor-strength finding — median +63 N at week 168, accepted by FDA as an intermediate clinical endpoint — elamipretide (brand: **FORZINITY**) received accelerated approval for improvement of muscle strength in adult and pediatric patients with Barth syndrome weighing ≥ 30 kg, dosed at 40 mg SC once daily [12, regulatory]. This is the first approved treatment for BTHS and the first FDA-approved mitochondria-targeting drug. The controlled crossover phase (B2 Part 1 above) did not demonstrate statistical superiority on its pre-specified primary endpoints; the approval rested entirely on the uncontrolled OLE data. Continued approval is contingent on results of a confirmatory trial.

A natural-history control comparison (PMID 36056411, Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases 2022) compared 8 TAZPOWER OLE patients against 19 untreated controls; elamipretide-treated patients showed a 79.7 m 6MWT advantage at week 64 (P = 0.0004) and 56.7 N muscle strength advantage at the same timepoint (P = 0.0002) [6, cohort]. This is a non-randomized historical control analysis and is subject to selection bias given the small n and the lack of blinding.

### B3. Geographic Atrophy / Dry AMD

**ReCLAIM / SPIAM-101 — Phase 1 (NCT02848313)**

Single-center, open-label, uncontrolled Phase 1 study evaluating subcutaneous elamipretide safety and tolerability in subjects with intermediate AMD. Primary endpoint was safety and tolerability (met). Exploratory analyses suggested improvements in low-luminance visual function. Two publications in Ophthalmology Science cover the non-central GA cohort (PMID 36246181) [7a, open_label] and the high-risk drusen cohort (PMID 36246187) [7b, open_label]. These uncontrolled Phase 1 data should not be interpreted as efficacy evidence.

**ReCLAIM-2 — Phase 2 (NCT03891875) — PRIMARY ENDPOINTS MISSED**

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial. 176 subjects with non-central geographic atrophy (117 elamipretide, 59 placebo); 134 completed the study. Subcutaneous elamipretide 40 mg daily for 48 weeks. Co-primary endpoints: (1) change in low-luminance best corrected visual acuity (LL BCVA) from baseline to week 48 and (2) change in GA area by OCT from baseline to week 48.

Results at week 48:
- LL BCVA: elamipretide −2.8 letters vs placebo −4.6 letters (numerically favored elamipretide, not significant)
- GA area: elamipretide +0.328 mm² vs placebo +0.281 mm² (elamipretide showed numerically larger atrophy growth)

Neither co-primary endpoint reached statistical significance. A notable exploratory signal was a 43% reduction in macular ellipsoid zone attenuation/loss progression in the elamipretide arm, though this was not a prespecified primary endpoint. Published in Ophthalmology Science 2024 (PMID 39605874) [8, rct]. A Phase 3 trial (ReNEW, NCT06373731) is active but not yet reporting results; it uses photoreceptor loss area as the primary endpoint.

### B4. Cardiac Indications

**EMBRACE-STEMI — Phase 2a (NCT01572909) — PRIMARY ENDPOINT MISSED**

Prospective, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. 300 first-time anterior STEMI patients with proximal or mid LAD occlusion undergoing primary PCI were randomized to IV elamipretide (MTP-131 / Bendavia) or placebo; 243 completed the study (120 active, 123 placebo). Primary endpoint: CK-MB area under the curve (AUC) over 72 hours post-PCI, reflecting infarct size.

Results: CK-MB AUC at 72 h was 5570 ± 486 ng·hr/mL (elamipretide) vs 5785 ± 426 ng·hr/mL (placebo), p = NS. No significant improvement was detected in any prespecified secondary outcome including MRI infarct size (Day 30 infarct size ratio 242.3 vs 225.2 — numerically worse in the active arm), angiographic, electrocardiographic, or clinical outcomes (e.g., incident heart failure within 24 h: 14% vs 25%, a non-significant numerical difference). Treatment was safe and well tolerated. Published in European Heart Journal 2016 (PMID 26586786) [9, rct].

**SPIHF-201 — Phase 2 HFrEF (NCT02788747) — PRIMARY ENDPOINT NOT MET**

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial in 71 subjects with stable heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). Arms: elamipretide 4 mg (n = 23), elamipretide 40 mg (n = 24), or placebo (n = 24) subcutaneously for 28 days. Primary endpoint: change in left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV) by MRI from baseline to week 4.

Results: LVESV change −4.4 mL (4 mg), −1.2 mL (40 mg), −3.8 mL (placebo) — no dose-response, and numerical differences were small with overlapping distributions. P-values were not reported in the ClinicalTrials.gov results entry. *[Inference: no significant between-arm difference is inferred from the absence of a significant finding in the registry record; p-values were not published in any identified source.]* The trial did not demonstrate a statistically significant effect on LVESV [10, regulatory].

**Heart failure (congestion / NT-proBNP) — Phase 2 (NCT02914665)**

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial in 308 subjects hospitalized with congestion due to heart failure. Primary endpoint: change in NT-proBNP between baseline and Day 8/early discharge. Results have not been posted to ClinicalTrials.gov (hasResults: false as of June 2026); no peer-reviewed publication was identified. Outcome status cannot be confirmed from available sources [11, regulatory — no results posted].

### B5. Pooled / Meta-analytic Evidence

No published meta-analysis or pooled analysis of elamipretide RCTs was identified. The manufacturer cited exploratory pooled analyses in regulatory and investor communications, but these do not constitute independent peer-reviewed evidence.

**Summary (2 sentences):** MMPOWER-3 (NCT03323749; Neurology 2023), the pivotal Phase 3 trial of elamipretide in primary mitochondrial myopathy (n = 218), missed both co-primary endpoints — 6MWT and PMMSA fatigue score — with the placebo arm numerically outperforming on the walk test at week 24, leading to early trial termination. Across the full human program, every prospectively powered randomized controlled trial of elamipretide failed its prespecified primary endpoint (PMM Phase 3, Barth crossover, STEMI, HFrEF, geographic atrophy Phase 2); nonetheless, the uncontrolled TAZPOWER OLE knee-extensor-strength finding (median +63 N at week 168) was accepted by the FDA as the basis for accelerated approval of FORZINITY for Barth syndrome (NDA 215244, October 30, 2025) — making it the first approved BTHS treatment and the first mitochondria-targeting drug, with a confirmatory trial still required.
