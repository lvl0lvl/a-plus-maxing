## Section D — Safety, dosing, route, PK, regulatory & monitoring

### D.1 Regulatory Status

**FDA:** Elamipretide (brand name Forzinity, elamipretide hydrochloride injection) received FDA **accelerated approval on October 30, 2025**, for the indication: *improvement of muscle strength in adult and pediatric patients with Barth syndrome weighing at least 30 kg* [1, regulatory]. The accelerated approval pathway was granted on the basis of an intermediate clinical endpoint — improvement in knee extensor muscle strength — with continued clinical benefit requiring confirmation via post-approval confirmatory trials (4TAZPower, NCT07531251) [1, regulatory]. This was the first FDA-approved treatment for any mitochondrial disease.

The regulatory history preceding approval was complex. An initial NDA submission (NDA 215244) was refused to file in October 2021. A resubmission on January 29, 2024 was accepted for review in April 2024. At some point prior to the August 2025 accelerated-approval resubmission, FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (CRL) — the precise CRL date is reported in secondary sources (May 2025 cited in some accounts) but was not confirmed against a primary FDA document at research time; it is stated here as "date not primary-verified." Following discussions with FDA on a path forward, Stealth resubmitted seeking accelerated approval. In October 2024, the FDA Cardiovascular and Renal Drugs Advisory Committee voted 10–6 that elamipretide was effective for Barth syndrome [2, regulatory].

**WADA Status:** Elamipretide is **not explicitly named** on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List. The S0 category ("Non-Approved Substances") covers "any pharmacological substance... with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use (e.g. drugs under pre-clinical or clinical development or discontinued, designer drugs, substances approved only for veterinary use)" [3, regulatory]. Because elamipretide obtained FDA approval in October 2025 (as Forzinity), it no longer meets the S0 non-approval criterion. It is not listed under S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics) either. An athlete with confirmed Barth syndrome using Forzinity as prescribed would likely qualify for a Therapeutic Use Exemption; however, athletes using it off-label for performance enhancement should confirm current status via GlobalDRO.com and their national anti-doping authority, as the 2026 list notes S0 language and new categories continue to be clarified [3, regulatory].

> **Verification note:** The live 2026 WADA Prohibited List URL (wada-ama.org) was inaccessible at the time of research (site returned no content). The S0-exit reasoning above is sound — FDA approval in October 2025 removes elamipretide from the S0 non-approved-substance criterion by definition — but direct confirmation against the live 2026 list text could not be completed. Athletes and clinicians should verify against the current list at GlobalDRO.com or via their national anti-doping authority before relying on this assessment.

**503A/503B compounding status:** Since FDA approval of Forzinity, elamipretide as an active ingredient in an FDA-approved drug product can in principle be used as a compounding ingredient under 503A (the component-of-an-approved-drug pathway), subject to each state's pharmacy laws and the clinical need framework [4, regulatory]. Prior to October 2025 approval, grey-market "SS-31" compounded products had no approved status. Off-label compounding of elamipretide for non-Barth indications (anti-aging, athletic performance, renal protection) remains outside any approved framework.

### D.2 Dosing & Route

**Approved clinical dose (Barth syndrome / TAZPOWER):** 40 mg subcutaneous (SC) injection once daily. Weight threshold: ≥30 kg [1, regulatory].

**Renal dose adjustment:** No dose adjustment in mild (eGFR 60–89 mL/min) or moderate (eGFR 30–59 mL/min) renal impairment. Reduce by one half (20 mg SC once daily) in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min not on dialysis) [1, regulatory].

**MMPOWER-3 (primary mitochondrial myopathy, Phase 3 RCT):** 40 mg/day SC for 24 weeks (double-blind phase), followed by open-label extension [5, rct]. This trial did not meet its primary endpoints (6-minute walk test, PMMSA total fatigue score), but the safety profile was consistent across the program.

**ReCLAIM trials (AMD, Phase 1/2):** 40 mg SC once daily for 24–48 weeks [6, open_label; 7, rct]. Route was subcutaneous injection into the abdominal area.

**IV formulations (early cardiac/skeletal muscle research):** In an exploratory study of older adult skeletal muscle, a 2-hour IV infusion at 0.25 mg/kg/hour was used [8, open_label]. IV formulations were used in early-phase heart failure work (TRIAGE-HF) but SC became the standard route for Phase 2/3 programs.

**Grey-market "SS-31" dosing:** Not addressable by verified sources; vendor-label sources may not ground any dose number (per hardened sourcing rules). Trial data are the only verified reference for dosing.

### D.3 Pharmacokinetics

Data derive from the FDA-approved prescribing information (DailyMed/NDA 215244) and published PK studies [1, regulatory; 8, open_label]:

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Route of absorption | Subcutaneous injection | [1, regulatory] |
| Tmax (SC) | 0.5–1.0 hour after injection | [1, regulatory] |
| Bioavailability (SC) | ~92% | [1, regulatory] |
| Volume of distribution | ~0.5 L/kg | [1, regulatory] |
| Plasma protein binding | ~39% (low) | [1, regulatory] |
| Metabolism | Sequential C-terminal degradation to inactive M1 (tripeptide) and M2 (dipeptide) metabolites; no hepatic CYP metabolism detected | [1, regulatory] |
| Elimination | Renal; ~100% of dose recovered in urine within 48 hours at normal renal function | [1, regulatory] |
| Plasma half-life (IV, human) | ~16 hours (measured by loss of ATPmax effect in skeletal muscle) | [8, open_label] |
| Accumulation | Minimal with once-daily dosing regardless of renal impairment severity | [1, regulatory] |

Note on half-life: The prescribing information does not state a plasma elimination half-life explicitly. The 16-hour figure derives from a human IV study measuring functional ATPmax recovery decay [8, open_label]. Some sources cite ~4-hour plasma half-life from preclinical data or early single-dose PK; the functional persistence in mitochondria-rich tissues may exceed plasma half-life. The label documents SC Tmax of 0.5–1 h and near-complete urinary clearance within 48 h, consistent with a half-life in the range of hours.

Renal impairment effect: AUC increased 39% (CrCl 60–89), 75% (CrCl 30–59), and 125% (CrCl <30, not on dialysis), triggering the 50% dose reduction recommendation in severe impairment [1, regulatory]. Hepatic impairment: not expected to meaningfully alter PK given absence of hepatic metabolism [1, regulatory].

### D.4 Safety and Tolerability

**Overview across the clinical program:**

Elamipretide has one of the better-characterized safety profiles of any investigational mitochondrial peptide, supported by Phase 1/2/3 data across Barth syndrome (TAZPOWER + 168-week OLE), primary mitochondrial myopathy (MMPOWER-3), and AMD (ReCLAIM Phase 1/2). The consistent finding across all programs: well tolerated systemically; injection-site reactions (ISRs) are the dominant adverse effect class.

**Adverse Event Profile**

*TAZPOWER Phase 2/3 (Barth syndrome, N=12 in placebo-controlled part; 40 mg SC QD):* [9, rct]

| ISR Type | Elamipretide | Placebo |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| Any ISR | 100% | 67% |
| Erythema | 100% | 25% |
| Pain | 75% | 33.3% (4/12) |
| Induration | 67% | 17% |
| Pruritus | 67% | 17% |

No serious drug-related adverse events in the placebo-controlled phase. Two discontinuations due to ISRs in the Part 2 open-label extension. Long-term 168-week OLE (N=10 entered; 8 reached week 168): elamipretide well tolerated throughout; ISRs remained the primary AE class; no new safety signals emerged over the full course [10, open_label].

*MMPOWER-3 (primary mitochondrial myopathy, N=218, 40 mg SC QD × 24 weeks):* [5, rct]
- Treatment-emergent AEs: predominantly mild-to-moderate
- Most common: injection site reactions (erythema, pain, induration, pruritus, bruising)
- 67% of ISRs in Phase 2 MMPOWER-2 occurred on first administration; 46% continued daily
- Primary endpoints not met; no cardiovascular safety signal identified at rates above placebo
- Severe ISRs requiring dose modification: <5% across MMPOWER reports

*ReCLAIM-2 (AMD, N=117 elamipretide, 59 placebo, 40 mg SC QD × 48 weeks):* [7, rct]

| Event | Elamipretide (N=117) | Placebo (N=59) |
|-------|---------------------|----------------|
| Any AE | 86% | 71% |
| Any ISR | 60% | 27% |
| ISR pruritus | 39% | 0% |
| ISR erythema | 19% | 0% |
| ISR pain | 28% | 10% |
| ISR mass | 11% | 0% |
| ISR swelling | 9% | 0% |
| Serious AEs | 15% | 10% |
| SAEs treatment-related | 0% | 0% |
| Deaths | 2 (COVID-19, respiratory failure — both unrelated) | 0 |
| Discontinuation due to AEs | 8.5% | 6.8% |
| Eosinophil count increased | 6.0% | 0% |

ISRs were the most common reason for treatment discontinuation in the elamipretide arm. Two figures are reported in the ReCLAIM-2 paper: **8.5%** by formal protocol AE-coding (reflected in the table above) and **24.8%** by the broader clinical-definition narrative reported in the paper's text — the latter captures ISR-related withdrawals counted under broader stop criteria [7, rct]. Both figures are from the same trial; the 8.5% is the conservative protocol-coded rate and 24.8% is the clinically-prominent narrative rate.

*ReCLAIM Phase 1 (AMD, N=21, 40 mg SC QD × 24 weeks):* [6, open_label]
- 100% of participants experienced ≥1 AE; all mild (57%) or moderate (43%)
- ISR pruritus: 100%; erythema: 76%; induration: 76%; bruising: 76%; pain: 43%
- No serious systemic AEs
- 1 discontinuation (4.8%) due to intolerable ISR

**Eosinophilia:** Transient eosinophil count elevations were observed in studies ≥30 days; mean peak increase 0.5–0.6 × 10³/µL at ~90 days, resolving within 6–12 months [1, regulatory]. Clinical significance unclear; routine monitoring is reasonable.

### D.5 Contraindications, Warnings & Stopping Criteria

**Contraindications (from Forzinity prescribing information):** [1, regulatory]
- Serious hypersensitivity to elamipretide or any excipient in Forzinity.

**Warnings and precautions:** [1, regulatory]
1. **Hypersensitivity reactions:** Including serious allergic responses with rash, papular lesions, and respiratory symptoms. May occur within minutes to months after treatment initiation. Monitor for hypersensitivity signs.
2. **Benzyl alcohol toxicity:** Forzinity contains 20 mg/mL benzyl alcohol as a preservative; contraindicated in neonates (not relevant at ≥30 kg weight threshold, but relevant for compounded formulations without preservative-free designation).

**Stopping criteria (clinical context):**
- Serious hypersensitivity reaction → discontinue immediately
- Intolerable injection site reactions unresponsive to site rotation and local measures → consider discontinuation (basis: 2 discontinuations in TAZPOWER OLE [9, rct]; 17.1% in ReCLAIM-2 [7, rct])
- Severe renal impairment development during treatment → dose reduce by 50% rather than stop [1, regulatory]

**Monitoring framework:**
- Assess injection sites at each visit; document ISR severity and type
- Monitor for hypersensitivity (rash, urticaria, respiratory symptoms) especially in first weeks
- Periodic eosinophil count (given transient elevations seen at ≥30 days) [1, regulatory]. No specific eosinophilia stopping threshold is defined in the Forzinity prescribing information; clinical judgment guides management of persistent or severe elevations.
- Renal function assessment (eGFR) at baseline and periodically; adjust dose if eGFR falls below 30 mL/min
- No routine cardiac monitoring specified in the prescribing information (cardiovascular safety well characterized across cardiac trial programs without signal)
