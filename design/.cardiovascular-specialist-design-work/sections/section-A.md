# Section A — Cardiovascular biomarkers, risk markers & CVD risk scoring

This section establishes the **validity discipline** a cardiovascular-specialist agent must carry when it reads, ranks, or reasons about CVD biomarkers and risk scores. For each marker the agent must hold two facts apart: what the marker *validly measures* (and on what evidence — causal genetic/MR vs. merely associational cohort), and what it *does NOT establish*. The recurring failure mode the agent must resist is treating an observational association (e.g. high HDL-C, low resting HR) as if it licensed a causal/interventional claim, and treating a guideline threshold as if it were an efficacy effect-size. Causation is reserved here for Mendelian-randomization (MR) and genetic evidence and for RCT-demonstrated effects of lowering the marker; everything else is association.

## 1. Blood pressure: measurement validity, thresholds, and the intensive-control question

**Measurement standards.** Out-of-office measurement is a *stronger* predictor of cardiovascular outcome than office measurement, not merely a confirmatory adjunct. In the IDACO meta-analysis of four populations (n=7,030, Belgium/Denmark/Japan/Sweden), when both conventional and ambulatory BP were entered into fully adjusted models, conventional (office) BP lost its predictive value for cardiovascular events (P≥0.052) whereas systolic and diastolic ambulatory BP retained prognostic significance (P≤0.007); category analysis showed risk rising progressively to sustained hypertension (elevated office *and* ambulatory BP, HR 1.80, 95% CI 1.59–2.03 vs. normotension) [1, cohort]. The agent-design payload: ambulatory BP (ABPM) and home BP (HBPM) measure the operative exposure; a single office reading is a noisy proxy whose prognostic content largely dissolves when out-of-office BP is known [1, cohort]. Both ACC/AHA and ESC/ESH endorse validated auscultatory or oscillometric devices; oscillometric devices estimate mean arterial pressure well but can be inaccurate for systolic/diastolic in individuals, so device validation (not just method) is the precondition for a usable number [2, regulatory].

**White-coat and masked phenotypes.** These are *defined by* the office-vs-ambulatory discordance and have distinct prognoses. In a meta-analysis of initially *untreated* subjects (n=7,961; 696 cardiovascular events), masked hypertension (normal office, high ambulatory) carried a cardiovascular-event hazard ratio of 2.09 (95% CI 1.55–2.81, P=0.0001) versus true normotension — comparable to sustained hypertension (HR 2.59, 95% CI 2.00–3.35, P=0.0001) — whereas untreated white-coat hypertension was *not* significantly different from normotension (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.65–1.42, P=0.85) [22, meta_analysis]. (Note: in *treated* white-coat hypertension and longer-follow-up cohorts, a meta-analysis still found elevated events, all-cause mortality, and cardiovascular mortality versus normotension — HR 1.36, 1.33, and 2.09 respectively [3, meta_analysis].) Payload: the agent must not read "white coat" as "false positive — ignore" — untreated WCH is roughly normotensive-risk, but masked hypertension is the dangerous miss that office-only screening cannot detect by construction, and the validity of any label is its measured out-of-office BP.

**Diagnostic-threshold divergence (a guideline disagreement, not a fact about biology).** The 2017 ACC/AHA guideline lowered the hypertension threshold to ≥130/80 mm Hg; modeled against US adults this raised hypertension prevalence to 45.6% (95% CI 43.6–47.6) versus 31.9% (95% CI 30.1–33.7) under the prior JNC7 ≥140/90 definition [4, regulatory]. ESC/ESH retain ≥140/90 as the office-hypertension threshold. These are threshold *conventions* (`regulatory`), not efficacy claims — the agent may cite them as definitions but must not treat the prevalence shift as evidence that treating 130–139 helps; that question is answered by trials.

**The intensive-control trial.** SPRINT randomized 9,361 high-CV-risk, non-diabetic adults to intensive (<120 mm Hg SBP) vs. standard (<140) targets [5, rct]. Intensive control reduced the primary composite (MI, ACS, stroke, heart failure, CV death) with HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.64–0.89, P<0.001) and reduced all-cause mortality (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.60–0.90, P=0.003) over a median 3.26 years [5, rct]. **Harms are part of the valid read**: intensive treatment significantly increased serious adverse events of hypotension, syncope, electrolyte abnormality, and acute kidney injury/failure [5, rct]. Payload: SPRINT establishes net benefit of a lower SBP target *in its enrolled high-risk population using its specific (largely automated office) measurement protocol* — it does not license a universal <120 target for low-risk individuals, and the agent must carry the measurement-protocol caveat and the harm profile alongside the headline HR.

## 2. Lipids and atherosclerosis: separating causal markers from associational ones

**LDL-C is causal — RCT + genetic.** The Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' meta-analysis of 26 statin trials (~170,000 participants) found each 1.0 mmol/L reduction in LDL-C reduced major vascular events with a rate ratio of 0.78 (95% CI 0.76–0.80, P<0.0001) — roughly a 22% proportional reduction per mmol/L, with the benefit proportional regardless of baseline risk [6, meta_analysis]. MR confirms causality: in the Voight analysis an LDL-C genetic score predicted MI (OR 2.13, 95% CI 1.69–2.69) concordant with the observational estimate (OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.45–1.63) [7, meta_analysis]. Payload: LDL-C is one of the few markers where lowering it *causes* event reduction (RCT) AND lifelong genetic exposure tracks risk (MR) — the agent treats it as a causal, modifiable target.

**ApoB measures particle number — the more proximate causal quantity.** Across the UK Biobank, Women's Health Study, and Framingham (14,994 CHD events), ApoB showed a consistent CHD hazard ratio of 1.24–1.33 per 1 SD, while the LDL-C/ApoB ratio (a particle-size proxy) was significant only in Framingham (HR 0.78) [8, cohort]. The authors could not confirm that cholesterol-depleted LDL particles are more atherogenic than cholesterol-replete ones — i.e. particle *number* (ApoB), not size, drives risk [8, cohort]. Payload: when LDL-C and ApoB are discordant (e.g. small dense LDL, high triglycerides, metabolic syndrome), ApoB is the more valid risk estimate because it counts atherogenic particles directly; LDL-C can under-state risk at a given cholesterol concentration. This is an observational/discordance argument (`cohort`), reinforced by the LDL-C causality above — not itself a hard-outcome RCT of ApoB-guided therapy.

**Lp(a) — causal by MR, ~20% prevalence, monogenic, and a current therapy gap.** Genetic LPA variants causally link Lp(a) to coronary disease: a 10 mg/dL genetically predicted Lp(a) reduction associated with 5.8% lower CHD risk (OR 0.942, 95% CI 0.933–0.951) [9, meta_analysis]. The MR analysis estimated that ~101.5 mg/dL (95% CI 71.0–137.0) of Lp(a) lowering is needed to match the CHD benefit of a 38.67 mg/dL LDL-C reduction — implying therapies that lower Lp(a) by only 25–35% are likely insufficient for meaningful event reduction [9, meta_analysis]. Lp(a) is largely monogenically determined by the *LPA* locus and elevated (>~30 mg/dL / >50 mg/dL by common cutoffs) in roughly 20–30% of the population [10, mechanism_review]. Payload: Lp(a) is a valid, causal, *largely unmodifiable-by-lifestyle* risk marker; the agent should treat a high Lp(a) as a one-time genetic-risk amplifier, flag that no approved Lp(a)-specific outcome therapy yet exists, and note the magnitude-of-lowering requirement [9, meta_analysis].

**HDL-C is associational, not causal (MR-negative).** Observationally a 1-SD higher HDL-C tracks ~38% lower MI (OR 0.62, 95% CI 0.58–0.66), but MR breaks the causal chain: the HDL-raising *LIPG* Asn396Ser allele predicted no MI reduction (observed OR 0.99, 95% CI 0.88–1.11) despite an expected OR 0.87, and a 14-SNP HDL genetic score showed OR 0.93 (95% CI 0.68–1.26, NS) [7, meta_analysis]. Payload: high HDL-C is a *risk marker* (associated with lower risk) but NOT a causal target — raising HDL-C pharmacologically is not expected to lower events, and the agent must not recommend "raise HDL" as a causal intervention.

**Triglycerides / remnant cholesterol are causal by MR.** In a Copenhagen MR analysis (73,513 subjects, 11,984 with ischemic heart disease), a 1 mmol/L genetically higher nonfasting remnant cholesterol carried a causal OR of 2.8 (95% CI 1.9–4.2) for ischemic heart disease, *independent of HDL-C* — and the HDL-C causal effect in the same framework was null (OR 0.7, 95% CI 0.4–1.4) [11, cohort]. Payload: triglyceride-rich remnant cholesterol is a causal, modifiable contributor; the agent treats remnant cholesterol as causal (MR) while keeping HDL-C in the associational-only bucket.

## 3. CVD risk scoring: what scores estimate and where they mis-calibrate

**Pooled Cohort Equations (ASCVD).** The PCE estimate 10-year risk of hard ASCVD (MI, CHD death, stroke). They systematically **over-estimate** in contemporary cohorts: in a modern multiethnic comparison (MESA-based), the PCE over-estimated 10-year ASCVD risk by ~20% on average, with misestimation especially extreme among Black adults [12, cohort]. A subsequent contemporary-multiethnic validation likewise reported substantial PCE over-estimation across every race/ethnic group, ranging from 67% (Hispanic men) and 72% (White women) up to 252% (Chinese women) and 314% (Chinese men) [13, cohort]. Payload: the PCE output is a *calibrated-population estimate that drifts high in lower-risk, contemporary, and certain racial subgroups* — the agent must present it as an order-of-magnitude triage tool, not a precise personal probability, and flag known over-estimation.

**SCORE2 / SCORE2-OP (Europe).** SCORE2 (derived from 45 cohorts, 677,684 individuals, ages 40–69) estimates 10-year *fatal and non-fatal* CVD, recalibrated to four geographical risk regions; external-validation C-indices ranged 0.67–0.81 [14, cohort]. SCORE2-OP extends estimation to persons >70 [14, cohort]. Payload: SCORE2's region-recalibration design directly targets the calibration problem that plagues the PCE; the agent must apply the *correct region* and note that discrimination (C-index ~0.7) is modest — scores rank populations, they do not finely separate individuals.

**Coronary artery calcium (CAC) — the strongest reclassifier.** CAC measures actual calcified atherosclerotic burden, so it adds information beyond risk-factor scores. In MESA (n=5,878; 209 CHD events) adding CAC to traditional risk factors gave a net reclassification improvement of 0.25 (95% CI 0.16–0.34, P<0.001) [15, cohort]. The dominant clinical use is the **power of CAC=0 as a de-risker**: roughly half of MESA had CAC=0 with very low event rates, making absence of calcium a strong negative predictor — though smoking, diabetes, and hypertension still confer residual risk even at CAC=0 [16, cohort]. Payload: CAC=0 is the validity-rich finding (it can safely down-classify many intermediate-risk people); a positive CAC up-classifies. The agent should treat CAC as the highest-yield reclassifier for intermediate-risk individuals, while noting CAC measures calcified (not soft/non-calcified) plaque and carries a small radiation dose.

## 4. Inflammation markers: the residual-inflammatory-risk hypothesis and its proof

**hs-CRP as a treatable-risk signal (JUPITER).** JUPITER randomized 17,802 apparently healthy adults with LDL-C <130 mg/dL but hs-CRP ≥2.0 mg/L to rosuvastatin vs. placebo; rosuvastatin cut the primary composite with HR 0.56 (95% CI 0.46–0.69, P<0.00001) [17, rct]. Payload: hs-CRP validly identifies a higher-risk, statin-responsive subgroup even at "normal" LDL-C — but JUPITER used a statin (which lowers LDL-C and CRP), so it does not by itself isolate inflammation as the causal lever.

**Isolating inflammation (CANTOS).** CANTOS randomized 10,061 post-MI patients with hs-CRP ≥2 mg/L to canakinumab (IL-1β antibody) or placebo; the 150 mg dose reduced the primary CV composite with HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.74–0.98, P=0.021) **without lowering LDL-C** — the cleanest proof that lowering inflammation alone reduces CV events [18, rct]. The valid read includes the harm: canakinumab caused more fatal infections than placebo [18, rct]. Payload: CANTOS converts "residual inflammatory risk" from hypothesis to a causal, druggable axis distinct from lipids.

**Low-cost confirmation (LoDoCo2).** Low-dose colchicine (0.5 mg/day) in 5,522 chronic-coronary-disease patients reduced the primary CV composite with HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.57–0.83) over ~28.6 months, with an unexplained numerical increase in non-cardiovascular death (HR 1.51, 95% CI 0.99–2.31) [19, rct]. Payload: LoDoCo2 reinforces the inflammatory hypothesis with a cheap, generic agent; the agent must carry the non-CV mortality signal as an open safety caveat, not bury it.

## 5. Autonomic markers: prognostic but observational

**Resting heart rate.** A meta-analysis of 46 cohorts (1,246,203 participants) found each 10 beats/min higher resting heart rate associated with all-cause mortality RR 1.09 (95% CI 1.07–1.12) and CV mortality RR 1.08 (95% CI 1.06–1.10) [20, meta_analysis]. **This is association, not causation** — RHR co-varies with fitness, autonomic tone, and subclinical disease; lowering RHR per se (outside specific indications) is not established to lower mortality.

**Heart-rate variability (HRV).** A meta-analysis of cohort studies in patients with cardiovascular disease found lower HRV associated with higher all-cause death (pooled HR 2.12, 95% CI 1.64–2.75) and cardiovascular events (HR 1.46, 95% CI 1.19–1.77) [21, meta_analysis]. Payload + caveats: HRV is a valid *prognostic* autonomic marker but is highly measurement-dependent (recording length, time- vs frequency-domain metric, posture, respiration, device) and is confounded by age, fitness, and medication. The agent must treat both RHR and HRV as observational risk *markers* — useful for stratification and trend-tracking, never as proof that moving the number moves the outcome.
