## Section D — Safety, dosing, route, PK, regulatory & monitoring

---

### D.1 Regulatory status

Bremelanotide (brand name Vyleesi) received FDA approval on **June 21, 2019** (NDA 210557, initial U.S. approval 2019) [1, regulatory]. The approved indication is: *treatment of premenopausal women with acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) as characterized by low sexual desire that causes marked distress or interpersonal difficulty, and is NOT due to a co-existing medical or psychiatric condition, problems with the relationship, or the effects of a medication or drug substance* [2, regulatory].

**Label scope limitations (verbatim):** "Not for postmenopausal women, men, or to enhance sexual performance" [2, regulatory]. These are explicit label exclusions — use in men and postmenopausal women is off-label; the evidence base for such use derives from earlier phase trials and grey-market/clinical practice, not the approved indication.

**Contraindications (verbatim):** "VYLEESI is contraindicated in patients who have uncontrolled hypertension or known cardiovascular disease" [2, regulatory].

---

### D.2 Dosing and route

**Label (Vyleesi):**
- Dose: 1.75 mg subcutaneously via single-use autoinjector [2, regulatory]
- Route: Subcutaneous self-injection (abdomen or thigh) [2, regulatory]
- Timing: At least 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity (on-demand dosing) [2, regulatory]
- Frequency cap: No more than one dose within any 24-hour period [2, regulatory]
- Monthly cap: Maximum 8 doses per month [2, regulatory]
- Response assessment: Discontinue after 8 weeks if no symptom improvement [2, regulatory]

**Grey-market / compounded injectable and nasal formulations:** Compounded bremelanotide is available through 503A pharmacies at substantially lower cost (~$50–$150/month); compounded nasal and sublingual routes are used off-label, enabled by the "different route of administration" carveout from the FDA's "essentially a copy" guidance [vendor_label/anecdote_aggregate — no number grounded from these sources]. No peer-reviewed dose-finding data exist for routes other than SC.

**503A/503B compounding context:** Bremelanotide is an FDA-approved drug, placing it under different compounding rules than unapproved peptides. Under FDA's "essentially a copy" doctrine, a 503A pharmacy may compound a preparation that a prescriber determines offers a significant clinical difference for an individual patient (e.g., different route). 503B outsourcing facilities face stricter constraints on compounding copies of commercially available drugs [8, regulatory].

The T_max of ~1 h aligns with the label's ≥45-minute pre-activity administration window. The short t½ (~2.7 h) means substantive plasma levels clear within 8–12 h, consistent with BP/HR returning to baseline within 12 h post-dose [2, regulatory].

### D.4 Adverse event profile

Data from two sources: (a) the FDA label [2, regulatory] which draws on the pooled RECONNECT phase 3 trials (n = 1,247 randomized); (b) the RECONNECT open-label 52-week extension (Simon et al. 2019) [4, open_label].

#### D.4.2 Nausea — dominant AE and primary discontinuation driver

Nausea is the single most clinically important AE [2, regulatory; 3, rct; 4, open_label]:
- Overall incidence: 40.0% (bremelanotide) vs 1.3% (placebo) [2, regulatory]
- Temporal pattern: Incidence highest with the first dose; median onset ~1 h; median duration ~2 h; attenuates with repeat on-demand use [2, regulatory]
- Severity: Nausea was the only severe treatment-emergent AE experienced by more than one participant in the 52-week open-label extension [4, open_label]
- Anti-emetic use: Required in 13% of bremelanotide-treated patients [2, regulatory]
- Discontinuation: Nausea drove premature discontinuation in 8% of bremelanotide-treated patients during the double-blind phase [2, regulatory]
- Open-label extension: 40.4% nausea incidence persisted; Study 301: 42.7%, Study 302: 37.7% [4, open_label]

The FDA label (DailyMed) notes nausea "improved for most patients with the second dose, but could occur intermittently with continued use" — consistent with a first-dose peak followed by partial tachyphylaxis [2, regulatory].

#### D.4.3 Focal hyperpigmentation — MC1R-mediated, potentially irreversible

Bremelanotide is a non-selective melanocortin receptor agonist; MC1R activation drives melanogenesis, producing pigmentary change in susceptible individuals [5, mechanism_review]:
- Incidence at label dosing (≤8 doses/month): 1% [2, regulatory]
- Sites: Face, gingiva, and breasts are the primary locations documented [2, regulatory]
- Risk modifiers: Darker baseline skin pigmentation increases risk; daily dosing greatly amplifies risk — 38% incidence observed with daily dosing for 8 consecutive days (a grey-market-adjacent pattern, not the label regimen) [2, regulatory]
- Reversibility: **Not confirmed in all patients after discontinuation** — the label explicitly states resolution cannot be assumed [2, regulatory]
- Mechanism: MC1R agonism stimulates melanocyte activity independently of solar UV exposure; the cyclic peptide structure makes receptor engagement persistent relative to endogenous α-MSH [5, mechanism_review]

Focal hyperpigmentation is the AE most uniquely tied to bremelanotide's mechanism and is the one AE that may not resolve. Patients should be monitored at each follow-up visit for new or expanding pigmented lesions on the face, gingiva, and breasts.

#### D.4.4 Cardiovascular effects — transient BP rise with compensatory HR decrease

The label carries a dedicated warning [2, regulatory; 4, open_label]:
- Systolic BP: Maximum mean increase ~6 mmHg (FDA label) / ~3 mmHg (52-week extension) [2, regulatory; 4, open_label]
- Diastolic BP: Maximum mean increase ~3 mmHg (FDA label) / ~2 mmHg (52-week extension) [2, regulatory; 4, open_label]
- Peak timing: 2–4 h post-dose [2, regulatory]
- Heart rate: Compensatory decrease up to ~5 bpm [2, regulatory]
- Duration: Returns to baseline within 8–12 h post-dose [2, regulatory; 4, open_label]
- Myocardial workload: Net myocardial workload did not meaningfully increase due to the BP–HR offset [4, open_label]

This transient pressor response is the basis for the contraindication in uncontrolled hypertension and known CVD. Blood pressure should be measured prior to initiation and at follow-up visits; patients experiencing sustained elevation, chest pain, or shortness of breath post-dose should not redose and should seek evaluation.

#### D.4.5 Hepatotoxicity (rare)

The LiverTox entry documents one confirmed case of acute hepatitis (ALT >1,000 U/L) with resolution after discontinuation; likelihood score D (possible rare cause) [6, mechanism_review]. No acute liver failure or chronic hepatic injury documented. Liver function monitoring is not required by the label but should be considered if symptomatic.

### D.5 Warnings, precautions, and monitoring summary

**Contraindications:**
1. Uncontrolled hypertension [2, regulatory]
2. Known cardiovascular disease [2, regulatory]

**Warnings (from label):**
- Transient BP increase / HR decrease: Do not use in patients with uncontrolled hypertension; check BP before initiating; limit to 1 dose/24 h and ≤8 doses/month [2, regulatory]
- Focal hyperpigmentation: Examine face/gingiva/breasts at baseline and each visit; counsel patients with dark skin about elevated risk; advise that reversal after stopping is not guaranteed [2, regulatory]
- Nausea management: Antiemetic pretreatment may be warranted in nausea-prone patients; use ≥45 min before activity allows nausea onset (T_max ~1 h) to occur and partly resolve before sexual activity [2, regulatory]

**Stopping criteria:**
- No symptom improvement after 8 weeks → discontinue [2, regulatory]
- Sustained or severe BP elevation post-dose → hold and evaluate [2, regulatory]
- New or progressive hyperpigmentation → consider discontinuation; no dose reduction strategy is established [2, regulatory]
- Symptomatic CV events (chest pain, syncope, dyspnea) → stop and evaluate urgently [2, regulatory]

**No REMS program** is required for Vyleesi [2, regulatory].
