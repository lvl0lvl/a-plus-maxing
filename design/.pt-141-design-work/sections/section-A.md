## Section A — Identity & molecular mechanism

### A.1 Identity and nomenclature

PT-141, the research designation used during development, is the INN bremelanotide, approved by the FDA in June 2019 under the brand name **Vyleesi** (Palatin Technologies / AMAG Pharmaceuticals) [1, regulatory]. It is a synthetic **cyclic heptapeptide lactam** with the sequence **Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-OH**, molecular formula C₅₀H₆₈N₁₄O₁₀ (acetate salt: C₅₀H₆₈N₁₄O₁₀ · xCH₃COOH, 1 ≤ x ≤ 2), molecular weight **1025.2 Da** (free base), CAS 189691-06-3, PubChem CID 9941379 [1, regulatory].

**Structural relationship to Melanotan-II (MT-II) and afamelanotide — three distinct compounds:**

- **MT-II** (Melanotan-II; cyclo[Nle⁴, D-Phe⁷]-α-MSH₄₋₁₀-NH₂) is the **C-terminal amide** cyclic heptapeptide — the non-approved research scaffold from which PT-141 was derived. MT-II has a terminal –NH₂ (molecular formula C₅₀H₆₉N₁₅O₉) [2, regulatory]; bremelanotide is its **des-amide (carboxylic acid) analog**, terminating in –OH, with "a free acid at the carboxyl terminus" per the FDA label [1, regulatory]. PT-141 is thus "the carboxylate derivative of MT-II" [5, mechanism_review]. This single C-terminal modification accounts for differences in receptor-selectivity profile and metabolic handling. MT-II was never approved and carries a broader receptor engagement, including prominent MC1R-mediated tanning.
- **Afamelanotide** (Melanotan-I; Scenesse, Clinuvel) is a structurally unrelated **linear 13-amino-acid analog of α-MSH** that is selectively MC1R-agonist; it is approved for the distinct indication of increasing pain-free light exposure in adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP) [4, rct]. Afamelanotide is not a cyclic peptide and does not carry the MC4R sexual-arousal pharmacology of bremelanotide.

### A.2 Receptor pharmacology — mechanism of action

Bremelanotide is a **melanocortin receptor (MCR) agonist** with an in-vitro potency order at human receptors of **MC1R > MC4R > MC3R > MC5R > MC2R** per the FDA-approved prescribing information [1, regulatory]. Despite MC1R ranking first in binding potency, clinically relevant pharmacodynamic effects at therapeutic doses are attributed predominantly to **MC4R** (sexual arousal/desire) and **MC1R** (melanocyte activation → flushing, transient hyperpigmentation), with MC2R (ACTH receptor) activity not observed at therapeutic concentrations.

**MC4R and the CNS sexual arousal pathway.** MC4R is highly expressed in hypothalamic nuclei (paraventricular nucleus, arcuate nucleus), limbic structures (medial preoptic area, nucleus accumbens, amygdala), and the ventral tegmental area [3, mechanism_review; 6, mechanism_review]. Activation of presynaptic MC4R in these regions modulates dopaminergic release, especially to the nucleus accumbens, providing a neurochemical substrate for increased sexual desire and arousal [3, mechanism_review; 6, mechanism_review]. Critically, this CNS pathway is **mechanistically distinct** from the peripheral vascular (NO/cGMP/PDE5) mechanism of sildenafil-class drugs; early male trials showed bremelanotide and PDE5 inhibitors can be additive [5, mechanism_review]. The FDA label explicitly states that "the mechanism by which VYLEESI improves HSDD in women is unknown" [1, regulatory] — the MC4R hypothesis is supported by animal and in-vitro evidence but has not been directly demonstrated by human mechanistic studies.

**Animal and in-vitro receptor evidence (population-annotated).** In male rats and mice, intrathecal administration of MT-II dose-dependently induced spontaneous penile erections, an effect blocked by melanocortin antagonists at spinal but not supraspinal sites, suggesting multiple CNS sites of action [5, mechanism_review]. In rodent mating models, MC4R emerged as "the principal effector of MC-induced erection," with MC3R involvement remaining unresolved [5, mechanism_review]. In vitro, PT-141's Ki values at cloned human receptors show stronger MC4R/MC3R binding relative to MC1R compared to MT-II, though both compounds engage all MCRs (MC1, MC3, MC4, MC5) with sub-nanomolar to low-nanomolar affinity [5, mechanism_review]. The claim of robust "100-fold MC4R over MC1R selectivity" that appears in vendor and commercial peptide-supplier sources is not supported by the FDA label's potency order or the peer-reviewed receptor data; the actual selectivity shift is modest and population-model-dependent.

**MC1R activity and adverse-event profile.** Residual MC1R agonism at Vyleesi doses underlies the most common adverse effects: nausea (40% vs. 1.3% placebo), flushing (20.3% vs. 0.3%), and headache (11.3% vs. 1.9%) in the RECONNECT phase 3 trials [7, rct; 8, open_label]. Transient hyperpigmentation (face, gums, breasts) was also observed with repeated dosing, consistent with melanocyte activation [8, open_label].

### A.3 Pharmacokinetics and dosing rationale

| Parameter | Value (from FDA label [1]) |
|---|---|
| Route | Subcutaneous injection (abdomen or thigh) |
| Bioavailability (SC) | ~100% |
| Tmax | ~1.0 h (range 0.5–1.0 h) |
| Elimination half-life | ~2.7 h (range 1.9–4.0 h) |
| Protein binding | 21% |
| Clearance | 6.5 ± 1.0 L/h |
| Excretion | ~64.8% urine, ~22.8% feces |
| Metabolism | Hydrolysis of peptide amide bonds; cellular peptidases [9, regulatory] |

The pharmacokinetic profile — short half-life (~2.7 h), fast Tmax (~1 h), near-complete SC bioavailability — supports the approved **on-demand dosing model**: self-administered 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity, not more than once per 24 hours [1, regulatory]. There is no approved chronic/daily dosing regimen; the on-demand design is intentional given the transient CNS activation required and the AE burden (nausea, blood pressure elevation) that resolves within 8–10 hours [8, open_label]. Blood pressure increases transiently (~3 mmHg systolic, ~2 mmHg diastolic) peaking 0–2 hours post-dose [1, regulatory; 8, open_label].

**Approved indication (Vyleesi):** Premenopausal women with **acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)** not attributable to a co-existing medical/psychiatric condition, relationship problems, or medication effects [1, regulatory]. It is not approved for postmenopausal women or men; the male erectile dysfunction application was discontinued after earlier intranasal trials raised blood pressure concerns and the SC formulation was not pursued in that population.
