## Section C — Off-label / male / grey-market use & melanocortin-sexual class context

### C.1 Off-label male use: what the evidence actually shows

PT-141 (bremelanotide) carries FDA approval only for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD). The label states explicitly: "Not indicated for treatment of HSDD in postmenopausal women or in men." [1, regulatory] Male use is therefore entirely off-label, supported only by pre-approval development-era trials that were never carried to the Phase III / NDA stage for this population.

The development-era evidence consists of three small early-phase trials conducted between 2003 and 2005, all in men:

**Diamond et al. 2004 (intranasal PT-141)** [2, rct] — Double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase I trial in healthy males and mild-to-moderate ED patients (n not reported in available sources; described as two cohorts — full text paywalled, PubMed abstract does not state enrollment). Intranasal PT-141 at doses >7 mg produced statistically significant RigiScan-measured erectile responses versus placebo, with onset at ~30 minutes. Flushing and nausea were the most common adverse events; no clinically significant vital-sign changes were reported at doses tested. No definitive maximum-tolerated dose was established. Published in Int J Impot Res (tier 2).

**Rosen, Diamond et al. 2004 (subcutaneous PT-141)** [3, rct] — Phase I/II randomized, crossover trial in healthy male subjects and ED patients with inadequate response to 100 mg sildenafil (n not reported in available sources; full text paywalled, PubMed abstract does not state enrollment). Subcutaneous PT-141 produced significant erectile responses at doses >1.0 mg in healthy subjects and at 4–6 mg in the sildenafil-failure cohort. "PT-141 was safe and well tolerated in both studies." Published in Int J Impot Res (tier 2).

**Diamond et al. 2005 (intranasal PT-141 + sildenafil combination)** [4, rct] — Randomized crossover trial, n = 19 men with ED who were responders to sildenafil or vardenafil. Three arms: 25 mg sildenafil + 7.5 mg intranasal PT-141 vs. sildenafil + placebo vs. double-placebo. The combination produced significantly greater RigiScan erectile response than sildenafil alone, with no new or increased-frequency adverse events. Published in Urology (tier 2).

A fourth trial, Safarinejad & Hosseini 2008 (J Urol) [5, rct] — a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of intranasal bremelanotide 10 mg in 342 married men aged 28–59 with sildenafil-unresponsive ED — reported a 33.5% response rate versus 8.5% placebo (p = 0.03). However, the Journal of Urology issued a formal Expression of Concern in January 2023 for this paper (integrity flag embedded in [5]), citing unresolved questions about the methodology, results, and statistical interpretation. Separately, a companion Safarinejad paper on bremelanotide in women (J Sex Med 2008) was fully retracted for the same class of concerns — authorial non-response to reviewer requests about methodology and data integrity. The J Urol male trial therefore carries a cloud of uncertainty and should not be treated as clean positive evidence.

**Net assessment for males:** The pre-approval male trials are small (Phase I/II, n = 10–342), used intranasal or subcutaneous formulations that were ultimately abandoned (the approved product is subcutaneous 1.75 mg), were published in 2003–2008 before the pivotal female program began, and were not taken forward to Phase III for men. No RCT of bremelanotide in men reaches the evidentiary standard of the approved female indication (two Phase III pivotal trials, RECONNECT-1 and RECONNECT-2, n > 600 per trial). The sole reasonably large male RCT carries an active expression of concern. The honest characterization is: modest short-term signal in early-phase male ED trials, no Phase III data in men, no regulatory approval, and the Safarinejad data under integrity scrutiny.

### C.2 The grey-market reality

Despite the narrow approved indication (premenopausal HSDD, women only), PT-141 circulates widely in grey-market channels as a libido/erectile booster for both sexes — sold as research-chemical injectable vials, nasal sprays, and lyophilized powders through online peptide vendors. The compound is positioned in this market as a "natural" or "central" libido enhancer superior to PDE5 inhibitors because it acts on desire rather than mechanics, targeting the brain rather than the vasculature.

The gap between label and market is substantial on multiple axes:

- **Population:** Approved for premenopausal women with diagnosed HSDD; marketed grey-market to men, post-menopausal women, and healthy individuals without any diagnosed dysfunction.
- **Formulation and purity:** The approved Vyleesi product is a pharmaceutical-grade 1.75 mg/0.3 mL subcutaneous autoinjector with controlled excipients and sterility validation. Grey-market peptides carry no such validation. Evans-Brown et al. 2009 documented this exact problem for the parent compound melanotan II: unregulated products obtained online had no quality assurance, with users self-injecting material of unknown purity and sterility, and reported adverse events including nausea, hypertension, and unintended skin darkening [6, mechanism_review]. The same structural issue applies to grey-market PT-141 — no pharmacopoeial standard, no batch release testing, no sterility guarantee.
- **Dosing:** The approved label restricts use to a maximum of one dose per 24-hour period and no more than 8 doses per month, with contraindications in uncontrolled hypertension and known cardiovascular disease. Grey-market nasal spray formulations are used at the user's discretion with no clinical monitoring.
- **Efficacy framing:** The approved evidence supports only a modest effect on the specific HSDD outcome measures (desire, distress) in premenopausal women. Grey-market marketing routinely positions PT-141 as a potent, broad-spectrum libido booster with near-universal effect — a representation unsupported by any regulatory-quality evidence in the claimed populations.

There is no peer-reviewed literature quantifying the volume of grey-market PT-141 use. The market reality is reconstructed from regulatory context (FDA approval scope), the melanotan grey-market precedent [6], and the structural availability of the peptide through research-chemical vendors — not from any controlled epidemiological dataset. Claims about the scale of off-label use therefore rest on anecdote aggregates, not primary evidence.

### C.3 The melanocortin-sexual class: MT-II, PT-141, and kisspeptin distinguished

PT-141 (bremelanotide) is a cyclic heptapeptide analog of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH) that acts as a non-selective agonist at central melanocortin receptors, with activity at MC3R and MC4R, both expressed in CNS hypothalamic circuits [7, mechanism_review]. The key mechanistic point established by the early Palatin/Molinoff program is that the relevant receptors are central, not peripheral — PT-141 works at the level of the brain, activating hypothalamic circuits involved in sexual arousal and desire, rather than acting on penile vasculature as PDE5 inhibitors do [7].

**Melanotan II (MT-II) — the non-selective parent.** MT-II is a cyclic lactam analog of alpha-MSH with broader receptor engagement. The Wessells/Hadley program at the University of Arizona established the human proof of concept: in a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial, n = 20 men with psychogenic and organic ED, subcutaneous MT-II (0.025 mg/kg) induced penile erection in 17 of 20 subjects without sexual stimulation, with approximately 41 minutes of RigiScan tip rigidity >80%, and increased sexual desire after 68% of active doses versus 19% of placebo (p <0.01) [8, rct]. A companion study in n = 10 men with organic ED showed subjective erections in 12 of 19 active injections versus 1 of 21 placebo doses [9, rct]. The adverse event profile with MT-II was substantially more prominent than what is reported for PT-141: severe nausea in 12.9% of subjects, frequent yawning/stretching (a hallmark MC receptor effect), and — critically not present in the approved PT-141 label — spontaneous (non-stimulation-dependent) erections. MT-II's broad receptor engagement (MC1R, MC2R, MC3R, MC4R, MC5R) accounts for its tanning effect (MC1R on melanocytes) and its more pronounced nausea/yawning burden (MC3R). PT-141 was developed specifically to retain the central erectogenic/arousal properties while reducing melanocyte activation, though it still carries nausea and flushing as common adverse events. The clinical evidence base for MT-II in men (small crossover trials, n = 10–20) is actually comparable in scale to the PT-141 male evidence — neither compound has been taken to a regulatory-quality Phase III program in men.

**Kisspeptin — a different mechanism, different pathway.** Kisspeptin is a neuropeptide that acts as the upstream orchestrator of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, driving GnRH release from the hypothalamus [10, mechanism_review]. In limbic and paralimbic brain regions implicated in sexual behavior, kisspeptin signaling modulates mood, anxiety, and emotional-behavioral responses to sexual cues. The mechanistic distinction from melanocortin agonists is fundamental: PT-141/MT-II act at MC3R/MC4R receptors on hypothalamic neurons to produce rapid, acute arousal and erectile responses independent of the HPG hormonal axis; kisspeptin acts upstream of GnRH to regulate the hormonal cascade that sets the tonic level of sexual drive and reproductive capacity. Kisspeptin does not directly agonize melanocortin receptors. The two pathways converge on sexual behavior via the hypothalamus but through entirely different receptor families, second-messenger cascades, and time courses (kisspeptin effects are hormonal/axis-level; PT-141 effects are acute central arousal). Evidence on kisspeptin's direct role in human sexual behavior is independent of — and should not be conflated with — the melanocortin literature.

**Attribution rule:** When citing erectogenic or libido effects, evidence must be attributed to the specific compound tested. MT-II evidence does not generalize to PT-141 (different receptor profile, different adverse event burden, different formulation). Kisspeptin evidence does not generalize to either melanocortin agonist. Grey-market products sold as "PT-141" may contain either compound, or neither — another dimension of the unregulated-purity problem.

### C.4 Blood pressure signal and label restrictions

Bremelanotide produces transient, statistically significant increases in blood pressure. The FDA-approved Vyleesi label quantifies the maximum effect as approximately 6 mmHg systolic and 3 mmHg diastolic increase, peaking 2–4 hours post-dose and typically resolving within 12 hours, accompanied by a decrease in heart rate of up to 5 bpm [1, regulatory]. The label imposes two hard frequency caps grounded in this signal: no more than one dose per 24 hours, and no more than 8 doses per month.

The label carries hard contraindications for uncontrolled hypertension and known cardiovascular disease — two conditions that are substantially more prevalent in the male population most likely to use PT-141 off-label for erectile dysfunction (middle-aged and older men with metabolic risk factors). Clayton et al. 2022 reviewed safety data across 43 studies comprising ~3,500 subjects in the full clinical development program and confirmed the BP increase pattern, characterizing it as "not deemed clinically important" in the screened, normotensive trial population — but explicitly noted that bremelanotide "should be used with caution in patients at risk of cardiovascular disease, and blood pressure should be well controlled during treatment" [11, meta_analysis].

The public health implication for grey-market off-label use is direct: individuals self-injecting unregulated PT-141 for libido or erectile purposes are typically not pre-screened for hypertension or CVD, are not monitored during the 2–4 hour BP peak window, are not limited to 8 doses/month, and may be combining the peptide with PDE5 inhibitors (as the Diamond 2005 combination trial demonstrated). The BP elevation is modest in magnitude but occurs reliably and without clinical oversight in the grey-market setting.
