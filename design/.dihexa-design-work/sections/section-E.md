## Section E — Concentration / origin audit, commercialization & grey-market realism

### E.1 Research-origin concentration audit

The Dihexa procognitive literature originates overwhelmingly from a single academic group: the **Harding / Wright laboratory at Washington State University (WSU), Pullman, WA**. A complete enumeration of primary research papers indexed on PubMed under the search terms "dihexa" and "Harding JW dihexa" (verified June 2026) yields 9 papers with direct mechanistic or efficacy relevance to Dihexa or its immediate angiotensin IV (AngIV) analog series:

| 25187433 | 2014 | Benoist, …, Harding JW | Washington State University / M3 Biotechnology | YES (retracted 2025) |
| 25455861 | 2015 | Wright JW, Kawas LH, Harding JW | Washington State University / M3 Biotechnology | YES |

**Concentration figure:** 5 papers are purely WSU/Harding-Wright (PMIDs 23055539, 25187433, 25455861, 25649658, 25674052); 1 additional paper (34703584) is a multi-institutional study with Harding as co-author. Three papers (29733881, 34827486, 38489193) originate from fully independent groups at USC, Chinese institutions, and Whitworth/OHSU respectively. **The WSU/Harding-Wright group is the primary investigator in 6 of 9 Dihexa-relevant studies (~67%) and the sole or lead group in 5 of 9 (~56%).** Including M3 Biotechnology (the WSU spinout co-founded by the same investigators; all three M3 co-founders — Wright, Kawas, Harding — held dual WSU/M3 affiliations on their papers [1, mechanism_review]) the effective single-origin share across the efficacy literature rises to ≥67%. This meets the HIGH-CONCENTRATION threshold [1, mechanism_review].

**Verdict: CONCENTRATION FLAGGED.** The foundational efficacy and mechanism literature for Dihexa is substantially single-group in origin. The three independent replications (USC systematic review, Chinese animal study, Whitworth AngIV-analog HD model) provide partial independent corroboration but none constitutes a full independent replication of the original procognitive claims in the same rodent Alzheimer model. Importantly, one of the two cornerstone mechanistic papers (PMID 25187433, the "procognitive and synaptogenic effects" paper) was retracted in April 2025 [2, animal].

### E.2 Integrity concerns in the originating literature

In September 2021 the *Journal of Pharmacology and Experimental Therapeutics* (JPET) issued expressions of concern against **four WSU papers** from the Harding/Wright/Kawas group — three of them directly in the Dihexa/AngIV-HGF program [3, anecdote_aggregate]:

- PMID 25187433 (Benoist et al. 2014) — expression of concern September 2021; **retracted April 2025** [2, animal]
- PMID 23055539 (McCoy et al. 2013) — expression of concern September 2021
- The Kawas et al. 2012 paper "Development of Angiotensin IV Analogs as HGF/Met Modifiers" (the paper itself is PMID 22129598) — its **2021 Expression of Concern is a separate notice, PMID 34551990 (issued September 2021)**, and the paper was **retracted in 2025** (note: the EoC-notice year [2021] and the retraction year [2025] are both distinct from the 2012 paper-publication year)

The expressions of concern followed a special committee investigation convened by Athira Pharma's board of directors in 2021, which concluded that **Leen Kawas — then Athira's CEO and a former Harding lab PhD student — had altered images in her 2011 doctoral dissertation and in at least four co-authored WSU research papers** [3, anecdote_aggregate]. Kawas departed as CEO in June 2021 pending the investigation. In January 2025 **Athira Pharma (by then renamed LeonaBio) agreed to pay $4 million to settle allegations of data falsification** brought by the U.S. Department of Justice, with the allegations centering on NIH grant applications supported by manipulated figures from the WSU papers [3, anecdote_aggregate].

This integrity record is directly load-bearing for the concentration audit: the primary evidentiary foundation for Dihexa's mechanism of action (activation of HGF/c-Met, synaptogenesis) includes at least one retracted paper and two additional papers under expressions of concern, all from the same single originating group.

### E.3 Commercialization pathway: M3 Biotechnology → Athira Pharma → LeonaBio

The Dihexa intellectual property was commercialized through a WSU spinout formed by the originating researchers:

**M3 Biotechnology, Inc.** (Seattle, WA) was co-founded by Wright, Kawas, and Harding, all of whom listed dual WSU/M3 affiliations on their mid-2010s publications [1, mechanism_review]. M3 subsequently rebranded as **Athira Pharma**, which conducted a NASDAQ IPO in 2020 and raised approximately $250 million.

**Critical distinction: Dihexa itself was NOT Athira's clinical candidate.** Athira's lead clinical compound was **fosgonimeton (ATH-1017)**, a structurally distinct small-molecule positive modulator of HGF/c-Met signaling — not the Dihexa peptide. Athira advanced fosgonimeton through Phase 2 (ACT-AD, NCT04491006) and a Phase 2/3 pivotal trial (LIFT-AD, NCT04488419) in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease [4, regulatory]. The LIFT-AD trial completed enrollment of 554 participants and reported topline results in 2024; fosgonimeton failed to demonstrate a statistically significant benefit on the composite Global Statistical Test primary endpoint [4, regulatory]. Fosgonimeton was also evaluated in a Phase 2 SHAPE trial in Parkinson's disease. Neither program advanced to Phase 3 registration. Athira subsequently rebranded as **LeonaBio**, which has pivoted its lead programs to lasofoxifene (breast cancer) and ATH-1105 (ALS) [5, vendor_label].

**Dihexa (the peptide) has never entered clinical trials in humans.** The grey-market compound is therefore entirely without clinical pharmacokinetic, safety, or efficacy data in any human population.

### E.4 Grey-market realism

Dihexa circulates on research-chemical and nootropic vendor markets in oral, topical, and injectable formulations, typically at prices of $30–$200 per gram and marketed primarily on the strength of the originating literature's claim that the compound is "100 million times more potent than BDNF" in inducing synaptogenesis [1, mechanism_review]. Several structural hazards apply:

**1. No human data.** Every efficacy and safety claim for Dihexa rests on rodent animal studies (intracerebroventricular, subcutaneous, or oral dosing in transgenic mice or scopolamine-impaired rodents) and in vitro mechanistic work, all from the same largely single-group literature described above. The "7 orders of magnitude more potent than BDNF" marketing claim derives from in vitro synaptogenesis assays, not from dose-response comparisons in cognitive outcome models; the paper most cited for this claim (PMID 25187433) was retracted in April 2025 [2, animal].

**2. c-Met oncogenic concern.** The HGF/c-Met signaling axis is a well-characterized oncogenic pathway. HGF/c-MET signaling drives tumor proliferation, invasion, and metastasis across multiple cancer types including gastric, lung, hepatocellular, and renal carcinomas; c-MET inhibitors are approved oncology drugs [6, mechanism_review]. Chronic systemic activation of this pathway by an unmonitored exogenous ligand-mimetic carries a plausible — if unquantified — cancer-promotion risk that is entirely unstudied in any chronic toxicology program for Dihexa itself. This is not a remote or theoretical concern: the therapeutic rationale for Dihexa depends on persistent c-Met agonism, which is exactly the mechanism multiple approved cancer drugs are designed to block.

**3. Purity, identity, and sterility unverifiable.** Grey-market Dihexa is sold as a research chemical; it is not subject to GMP manufacture, certificate-of-analysis identity confirmation, or sterility testing. Injectable preparations carry direct infectious and endotoxin risk. Oral bioavailability of the intact peptide is unknown and likely low; vendors offering "orally active" Dihexa are making a claim unsupported in the human-data record.

**4. Dihexa-vs-fosgonimeton confusion risk.** Buyers and online community members frequently conflate grey-market Dihexa with Athira's/LeonaBio's clinical fosgonimeton program, treating the existence of a Phase 2/3 trial as validation of Dihexa's safety and potency. This conflation is wrong in two directions: (a) fosgonimeton is a structurally different compound, not Dihexa; and (b) the fosgonimeton LIFT-AD pivotal trial failed to meet its primary endpoint [4, regulatory]. The clinical program provides no validation of Dihexa.
