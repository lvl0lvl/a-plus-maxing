## Section B — Cognitive / neuroprotective efficacy evidence

### Overview

Dihexa's (PNB-0408) cognitive efficacy evidence rests almost entirely on ANIMAL studies — primarily rodent behavioral pharmacology — conducted at Washington State University by the Harding/Wright group between 2011 and 2015, and a subsequent independent replication in 2021 by a Chinese group. There is no published human clinical trial of Dihexa for any indication. The evidence base carries a significant integrity caveat: the laboratory that generated most of the foundational behavioral and mechanistic data was also the site of a confirmed research misconduct finding that resulted in one retraction and three expressions of concern across four JPET papers in this program.

---

### B.1 The foundational behavioral work — scopolamine and aged-rat models

The primary behavioral evidence derives from McCoy et al. (2013) [1, animal], which introduced Dihexa as an orally bioavailable, metabolically stable hexapeptide. **In male Sprague-Dawley rats (n = 8 per group)**, oral Dihexa at 2.0 mg/kg/day reversed scopolamine (1.0 mg/kg i.p.)-induced deficits in the Morris water maze (MWM) — restoring escape latency to levels statistically indistinguishable from unimpaired controls (p < 0.001 by day 7). Doses of 1.25 mg/kg were also effective; 0.05 mg/kg i.p. was not. In a separate cohort of **aged (24-month-old) mixed-sex Sprague-Dawley rats (n = 6 per group)**, oral Dihexa at 2.0 mg/kg/day significantly improved MWM performance on most training days versus vehicle (p < 0.05, Mann-Whitney U). The paper also reported hippocampal neuronal culture data showing increased dendritic spine density with Dihexa exposure.

**Integrity status:** JPET issued an Expression of Concern for this paper in September 2021 (PMID: 34551989) following a Washington State University investigation that confirmed image manipulation in figures by co-author Leen Kawas (then CEO of Athira Pharma) [1, animal]. The paper has not been retracted as of mid-2026 but must be treated as under integrity cloud.

These are ANIMAL data. No human scopolamine-reversal or aged-population trial exists.

---

### B.2 HGF/c-Met mechanism + synaptogenesis — retracted

The Benoist et al. (2014) JPET paper [2, animal] was the mechanistic cornerstone of Dihexa's cognitive efficacy narrative. Using **male Sprague-Dawley rats (n = 8–10 per group)** in the MWM scopolamine model, the authors demonstrated that Dihexa's cognitive rescue was blocked by the HGF antagonist "Hinge," establishing HGF/c-Met as the obligate pathway. In dissociated hippocampal neuron cultures (n = 50 neurons per group), Dihexa at 10⁻¹² M increased dendritic spine density from ~17 to ~26 spines per 50 µm dendrite; combined sub-threshold HGF + Dihexa reached ~29 spines. Presynaptic colocalization with synapsin reached 98%, and miniature excitatory postsynaptic current (mEPSC) frequency doubled — supporting functional synaptogenesis.

**Integrity status: RETRACTED.** On 29 April 2025, JPET formally retracted this paper (retraction notice PMID: 40312093) after the WSU investigation found Figures 1B and 2A/C contained falsified data [2, animal]. The HGF/c-Met mechanism hypothesis is scientifically plausible (supported by independent fosgonimeton pharmacology), but the specific quantitative spine-density data supporting Dihexa in this paper cannot be relied upon.

These are ANIMAL / in-vitro data. No human synaptogenesis evidence exists.

---

### B.3 Earlier analog work confirming the procognitive signal

Before Dihexa itself, Benoist et al. (2011) [3, animal] established the structural requirements for procognitive activity in C-terminal truncated Nle1-AngIV analogs in rats. Using the MWM scopolamine model in **male Sprague-Dawley rats**, tetra- and tripeptide analogs reversed scopolamine-induced spatial memory deficits and increased hippocampal dendritic spine number and head width. Dihexa is a further metabolic stabilization of this analog series; this earlier work is not retracted and provides context for the broader AngIV-cognitive pharmacology (though image-manipulation concerns emerged across the group's 2011–2014 work generally [7, anecdote_aggregate]).

These are ANIMAL data.

---

### B.4 Systematic review of AngIV cognition — broader context

Ho and Nation (2018) [4, meta_analysis] reviewed 32 experimental studies of brain renin-angiotensin system peptides (AngIV and Ang-1-7) and cognition. Of 9 cognitive-deficit model studies, 8 found AngIV or analogs improved spatial working memory or passive avoidance tasks. Dihexa was grouped with Nle1-AngIV and other analogs as the effective compounds. The review included only non-human experimental (primarily rodent) studies. The authors noted that intracerebroventricular administration at or near learning acquisition produced the most reliable effects — relevant when interpreting the translatability of oral rodent data.

All included studies are ANIMAL data.

---

### B.5 Alzheimer's-model preclinical replication — independent group

Sun et al. (2021) [5, animal] provided the only substantial independent replication of Dihexa's cognitive effects outside the Harding/Wright lab. Using **APP/PS1 double-transgenic mice (6-month-old males; n = 12 per group for MWM, n = 6 for molecular analyses)**, intragastric Dihexa at 1.44 or 2.88 mg/kg/day for 3 months reduced escape latency and increased platform crossings in probe trials versus untreated APP/PS1 controls. PI3K/AKT pathway activation was confirmed: wortmannin (PI3K inhibitor) reversed the behavioral improvement, and p-AKT expression was elevated with Dihexa treatment. Neuroinflammatory markers IL-1β and TNF-α were reduced; the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 was increased. Nissl-positive neuron density and synaptophysin expression were preserved.

**Journal:** Brain Sciences (MDPI, open-access; tier 2-3 specialty neuroscience journal). No expression of concern or retraction noted.

Importantly, this replication used a different species (mouse vs. rat), a different AD model (amyloid transgenic vs. scopolamine pharmacological), a different administration route duration, and a Chinese academic group with no institutional connection to the Harding/Wright lab — making it the most credible independent confirmation of the cognitive rescue signal, though still ANIMAL data.

---

### B.6 Parkinson's disease — review claims, no published in-vivo Dihexa study found

Wright, Kawas, and Harding (2015) [6, mechanism_review] — a Progress in Neurobiology narrative review — describes the rationale for AngIV analog use in Parkinson's disease, noting HGF/c-Met activation's potential to protect dopaminergic neurons and facilitate motor recovery. The review cites earlier AngIV (not Dihexa specifically) motor-function data and projects Dihexa as a candidate. No published primary in-vivo study of Dihexa in a dopaminergic lesion model (6-OHDA or MPTP) was identified in the literature search.

The PD claims in the secondary literature are extrapolated from: (a) general HGF neuroprotection literature, (b) the Parkinson's disease dementia rationale used by Athira for the SHAPE trial of fosgonimeton (which failed its primary endpoint), and (c) the narrative review by the Harding/Wright group itself. These are not independent experimental data for Dihexa in PD.

---

### B.7 Huntington's disease model — null result

Wells et al. (2024) [8, animal] tested PNB-0408 (Dihexa) in **male Wistar rats (n = ~13–14 per group)** with 3-nitropropionic acid (3-NP)-induced Huntington's disease-like neurochemistry and motor/cognitive deficits. Dihexa failed to protect rats from the spatial learning and memory deficits induced by 3-NP neurotoxicity; no cognitive or motor benefit was detected. This is a published negative result, and the most recent published in-vivo Dihexa behavioral study.

This is an ANIMAL study with a null outcome.

---

### B.8 Human clinical evidence — absent for Dihexa

**No human clinical trial of Dihexa has been published or registered on ClinicalTrials.gov.** No human pharmacokinetic data, safety data, or efficacy signal exists for Dihexa specifically. The cognitive performance claims circulating in the supplementation community are wholly unsupported by human data.

The closest human evidence comes from a related compound, fosgonimeton (ATH-1017), developed by Athira Pharma from the same HGF/c-Met program that spawned Dihexa. Fosgonimeton Phase 1 safety data were published (Hua et al., J Alzheimers Dis, 2022; n = 88, healthy elderly and mild-AD patients; dose-proportional PK, no serious adverse events; EEG gamma-power and P300 markers suggested target engagement). However, fosgonimeton is a distinct compound administered subcutaneously, not orally; its pharmacology cannot be transposed to Dihexa. Fosgonimeton subsequently failed its primary endpoints in the LIFT-AD Phase 2/3 trial (554 enrolled [actual per ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04488419 results posting, April 2025]; Global Statistical Test and key secondary endpoints missed; announced 2024), in ACT-AD Phase 2 (77 participants, primary ERP endpoint failed), and the SHAPE trial in Parkinson's disease dementia was halted early. Athira discontinued all three programs. These failures are important context: the HGF/c-Met activation strategy derived from the same rodent pharmacology as Dihexa failed to translate to human cognitive benefit in completed trials.

---

### B.9 Review summary by Harding/Wright group

Wright and Harding (2015) [9, mechanism_review] provide a J Alzheimers Dis narrative review of the HGF/c-Met system as an AD target, characterizing Dihexa as orally active, BBB-penetrant, and synaptogenic based on the preclinical work. This is a perspective/review article, not a primary study, and reflects the originating group's own interpretation of their (now partially retracted) data.
