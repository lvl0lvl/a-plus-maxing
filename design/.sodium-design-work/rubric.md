# Rubric — sodium (serum sodium, biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** **sodium (Na⁺)** = the principal EXTRACELLULAR cation (~135-145 mmol/L serum; intracellular ~10-15 mmol/L — the gradient maintained by the **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase**); the major determinant of **plasma osmolality** (serum osmolality ≈ 2×[Na⁺] + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8) + thus extracellular fluid volume; **THE LOAD-BEARING CONCEPT: serum sodium concentration reflects WATER balance, NOT total-body sodium / salt intake** — it is the ratio of total-body Na to total-body water; hyponatremia is usually a water-EXCESS problem (or relative), hypernatremia a water-DEFICIT problem; the regulation — **osmoreceptors → ADH/vasopressin (water reabsorption via aquaporins) + thirst**, and the volume/RAAS axis (aldosterone → renal Na handling, which controls VOLUME not concentration); tonicity + cell-volume effects (rapid Na shifts → brain cell swelling/shrinkage — the basis of the symptoms + the correction-rate danger).
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Interpretation:** reference **~135-145 mmol/L (= mEq/L)**; units mmol/L ↔ mEq/L (1:1 for monovalent Na); **hyponatremia <135** (mild 130-134, moderate 125-129, severe <125) + **hypernatremia >145**; the interpretation FRAMEWORK — hyponatremia worked up by **tonicity (serum osmolality) then volume status** (hypovolemic / euvolemic [SIADH] / hypervolemic) + urine Na + urine osmolality; **pseudohyponatremia** (a LAB ARTIFACT — severe hyperlipidemia/hyperproteinemia falsely lowers Na on INDIRECT ISE, normal osmolality — distinguish from true); **translocational/hyperosmolar hyponatremia** (hyperglycemia pulls water out → dilutional low Na — the **glucose correction: add ~2.4 mmol/L Na per 100 mg/dL glucose above 100**); the danger of OVER-RAPID correction (osmotic demyelination syndrome — correction limits ~6-8 mmol/L per 24h); hypernatremia ≈ free-water deficit.
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** **ion-selective electrode (ISE)** is the standard — **DIRECT ISE (whole blood / blood-gas analyzers, no dilution)** vs **INDIRECT ISE (diluted, on main chemistry analyzers)**: the load-bearing distinction — **indirect ISE is susceptible to the electrolyte-exclusion effect → pseudohyponatremia in hyperlipidemia/hyperproteinemia** (because the dilution assumes a normal plasma-water fraction ~93%; high lipid/protein lowers the water fraction → falsely low Na), whereas DIRECT ISE measures Na activity in plasma water + is NOT fooled; quantify; flame photometry (historical reference); the IFCC/standardization + traceability; other pre-analytic — the wrong-IV-line/contamination artifact (drawing above a saline drip → falsely high), specimen type (serum vs plasma vs whole blood small differences); reference-interval method dependence.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **HYPONATREMIA (<135 — the most common electrolyte disorder):** by volume status — **hypovolemic** (renal/GI Na+water loss with water retention: diuretics [esp. thiazides], vomiting/diarrhea, adrenal insufficiency, cerebral salt wasting); **euvolemic** — **SIADH** (the classic — drugs [SSRIs, carbamazepine, antipsychotics], CNS/pulmonary disease, malignancy, pain/nausea), hypothyroidism, glucocorticoid deficiency, primary polydipsia, beer potomania, low-solute; **hypervolemic** (water-retaining edematous states: heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, renal failure); **exercise-associated hyponatremia** (over-drinking in endurance events — relevant to athletes). **HYPERNATREMIA (>145 — a water deficit, usually impaired thirst/access):** inadequate water intake (elderly, impaired access), diabetes insipidus (central/nephrogenic — ADH deficiency or resistance), osmotic diuresis, GI water loss. **PROGNOSTIC:** both dys-natremias (esp. hyponatremia) independently predict **in-hospital + long-term mortality** across many settings (heart failure, cirrhosis, pneumonia, surgical) — cite NAMED cohorts/meta (VERIFY); note it is largely a severity marker. The symptoms (neurologic — from cerebral edema in acute hyponatremia / dehydration in hypernatremia) + the correction-rate danger (osmotic demyelination if hyponatremia corrected too fast; cerebral edema if hypernatremia corrected too fast). **Limitations:** a concentration, NOT total-body Na; interpret WITH volume status + osmolality + glucose + the pseudo-artifacts.
