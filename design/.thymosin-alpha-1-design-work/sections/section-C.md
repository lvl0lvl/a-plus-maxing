## Section C — Immunosenescence/immune-restoration, aging context & global approval landscape

### C.1 Immunosenescence: the mechanistic rationale

Aging is accompanied by thymic involution — the progressive, age-dependent atrophy of the thymus that begins in early adulthood and reduces naïve T-cell output by roughly 90% by the seventh decade. The consequence is a narrowed T-cell receptor repertoire, impaired responses to novel antigens (including vaccines), accumulating exhausted and senescent effector-memory cells, and a low-grade chronic inflammatory tone termed "inflammaging." Because Tα1 is a native thymic peptide, its serum levels decline in parallel with thymic involution, and this age-correlated fall has been proposed as both a biomarker of immune age and a pharmacological target.

A 2025 mechanism-focused review in *International Journal of Molecular Sciences* (IJMS) synthesized the evidence for Tα1 in aging [1, mechanism_review]. The authors document that Tα1 stimulates IL-7 secretion (a key thymocyte-survival cytokine), activates protein kinase C to prevent thymocyte apoptosis, upregulates HLA-DR and co-stimulatory markers (CD40, CD80, CD86) on dendritic cells, and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines TNFα, IL-1β, and IL-6 while elevating IL-10. These are precisely the deficits of the immunosenescent state, providing a coherent mechanistic rationale for Tα1 as an immune-restorative agent in older adults. The review is a narrative synthesis rather than a meta-analysis and is primarily mechanistic; it draws on both preclinical and clinical literature without independent generation of primary data [1, mechanism_review].

### C.2 Vaccine-response augmentation in the elderly: human RCT evidence

The most concrete human evidence for Tα1's immunosenescence-relevance comes from vaccine-adjuvant studies in elderly and immunocompromised populations.

**Gravenstein et al. 1989 — founding RCT (elderly men, influenza).** Ninety elderly men aged 65–99 years (mean 77.3 years) in a long-term care setting were randomized double-blind to Tα1 (900 µg/m² subcutaneously twice weekly × 8 doses) alongside trivalent influenza vaccination, or to influenza vaccine plus placebo [2, rct]. Antibody response at 6 weeks was significantly greater in the Tα1 arm (P = 0.023), driven by older subjects (≥77 years; P = 0.039). The trial was conducted by independent US academic groups (Ershler, Gravenstein), not by SciClone or any commercial sponsor, and was published in the *Journal of the American Geriatrics Society* before Zadaxin reached market. It is the cleanest positive human vaccine-adjuvant RCT in the Tα1 literature.

**Ershler, Gravenstein & Geloo 2007 — trial summaries (Ann NY Acad Sci).** A summary article in *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* — one of the acceptable tier-1 outlets for this literature — reviewed multiple completed Tα1 influenza-vaccine trials in elderly populations [3, mechanism_review]. The authors report that in a subsequent Phase 2 study, biweekly Tα1 given alongside influenza vaccination reduced confirmed influenza incidence from 19% (vaccine alone) to 6% (vaccine + Tα1; P = 0.002), a clinically meaningful reduction in a population where standard vaccination provides incomplete protection [3, mechanism_review]. The article explicitly calls for further large RCTs, acknowledging that the existing evidence base, while consistently positive, is small in aggregate subject numbers.

**Carraro et al. 2012 — hemodialysis patients, pandemic H1N1 vaccine.** Hemodialysis patients represent a secondary immunosenescence-adjacent population: chronic renal failure induces a uremia-related immune dysfunction that blunts vaccine responses similarly to aging. Carraro and colleagues published an Italian-group pilot study in *Vaccine* showing that Tα1 co-administration (at 3.2 mg and 6.4 mg doses) with the adjuvanted pandemic H1N1 influenza vaccine (Focetria) improved seroconversion rates; both Tα1 combination arms met regulatory seroprotection licensing criteria by Day 21 while the vaccine-only arm did not (P < 0.01) [4, open_label]. This is a small pilot — not a powered efficacy RCT — but it extends the vaccine-adjuvant signal to immunocompromised, non-elderly populations and was conducted by Italian academic groups independent of the US Ershler/Gravenstein collaboration.

**NCT06821100 — ongoing COVID-19 booster trial in older adults.** A randomized trial sponsored by SciClone-legacy interests (NCT06821100) is currently enrolling adults ≥60 years to receive thymalfasin as adjuvant to COVID-19 booster vaccination [5, regulatory]. This represents the contemporary extension of the vaccine-adjuvant hypothesis to mRNA-platform vaccines and will provide the most relevant aging-immune data when published; no efficacy results are yet available.

**Limits of the vaccine-adjuvant evidence.** The positive trials are real, replicated across independent groups, and published in credible journals. They are, however, modest in scale: the two key elderly trials are in the hundreds of subjects, not thousands. The clinical significance of augmented antibody titers (versus protection against disease endpoints) was only addressed in the Phase 2 influenza-incidence study. No completed large-scale RCT of Tα1 as a vaccine adjuvant in the modern high-dose or adjuvanted-vaccine era exists.

### C.3 Immune reconstitution in severe illness: the sepsis and critical-illness data

Beyond the healthy-aging vaccine context, a substantial body of evidence tests Tα1 as an immune-restorative agent in sepsis-induced immunoparalysis — a state mechanistically analogous to accelerated immunosenescence (collapsed monocyte HLA-DR, lymphopenia, exhausted CD8+ T cells).

**ETASS (Wu et al. 2013, Critical Care).** The first multicenter RCT of Tα1 for severe sepsis — 361 patients across six tertiary Chinese hospitals — found a non-significant reduction in 28-day mortality (26% vs. 35%; RR 0.74, P = 0.062) with significant improvements in monocyte HLA-DR on Days 3 and 7 [6, rct]. Monocyte HLA-DR is the same immune-competence marker depressed in immunosenescence, and its restoration by Tα1 in sepsis provides direct mechanistic corroboration of the aging-immune hypothesis.

**TESTS (Wu et al. 2025, BMJ).** The largest Tα1 RCT to date — 1,106 adults across 22 Chinese centers — found no significant reduction in primary 28-day mortality overall (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.77–1.27; P = 0.93) [7, rct]. However, a pre-specified age-stratified interaction was significant (P = 0.01): patients ≥60 years showed a directional benefit (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.61–1.09) while those <60 showed potential harm (HR 1.67). This age-interaction is consistent with the immunosenescence hypothesis — Tα1 may reconstitute immune function that is already depleted, while dysregulating immune responses that remain intact. The finding is hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory, given the CI crossing 1.0 in the older subgroup.

**Meta-analyses of sepsis RCTs.** A 2016 systematic review in *BMC Infectious Diseases* pooled 19 RCTs (1,354 patients, all in China) and reported a significant mortality reduction (RR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.77) [8, meta_analysis]. A 2025 *Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology* meta-analysis pooling 11 RCTs (1,927 patients, all China) found a significant mortality benefit overall (OR 0.73, 95% CI 0.59–0.90, P = 0.003), but high-quality trials and multicenter trials in isolation showed no significant effect, and publication bias was detected [9, meta_analysis]. The sepsis meta-analytic result should be read with caution: all contributing trials are China-sourced, quality is variable, and the TESTS phase 3 trial neutralized the signal.

### C.4 Global approval landscape

Thymalfasin (Zadaxin) occupies an unusual regulatory position: it is a fully approved prescription drug in over 35 countries, with substantial post-marketing clinical experience, yet it has never received full FDA marketing approval [1, mechanism_review] [3, mechanism_review].

**Approved jurisdictions (selected):** China is the largest market and grants approval for chronic hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and as an immunomodulatory adjuvant in cancer and critical illness. Italy holds a national approval covering hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and use in melanoma. India, the Philippines, Singapore, Argentina, Peru, Russia, and multiple countries across Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East have also approved thymalfasin, predominantly for hepatitis B and C [1, mechanism_review] [10, anecdote_aggregate].

**FDA (United States):** Thymalfasin is NOT FDA-approved for any indication. Per FDA orphan-designation records (accessdata.fda.gov was not primary-fetchable at research time), SciClone Pharmaceuticals obtained FDA Orphan Drug designations for malignant melanoma and hepatocellular carcinoma — both indications are listed in peer-reviewed clinical literature (Ancell CD et al., *Am J Health Syst Pharm* 2001;58(10):879–885, PMID 11381492) — but never submitted for full marketing approval. The FDA has classified thymalfasin as a compoundable Category 2 substance. In the US, it is accessible only as a compounded peptide or through research protocols.

**EU:** No European Medicines Agency (EMA) centralized approval exists. Italy's national procedure approval is the primary European regulatory foothold.

The regulatory contrast with unapproved grey-market research peptides is real and substantive: thymalfasin has undergone formal Phase 3 registrational review in multiple jurisdictions, has an established pharmacovigilance record spanning decades of post-marketing use, and carries an INN (international nonproprietary name) — markers of regulatory maturity absent from compounds like BPC-157, TB-500, or the Epithalon/Khavinson series.

### C.5 Evidence breadth and independence: an honest assessment

Tα1's literature is structurally different from the Khavinson peptide series (Epithalon, Thymalin, Cortagen), which were generated almost entirely by a single group at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation. Multiple independent threads converge on Tα1:

- **US academic thread (1980s–2000s):** Ershler, Gravenstein, and colleagues at the Medical College of Wisconsin and Brown University conducted the vaccine-adjuvant RCTs without commercial sponsorship. These studies pre-date SciClone's market entry and were motivated by independent gerontological research [2, rct] [3, mechanism_review].
- **Italian clinical thread:** Garaci, Romani, and Italian collaborators have run a parallel program on Tα1 in infectious disease, cancer immunology, and aspergillosis — distinct from the US aging work [published in Frontiers in Medicine, Expert Opinion on Biological Therapy, and other European journals].
- **Chinese clinical thread:** A large and growing body of sepsis, hepatitis, and oncology RCTs originating from multiple independent Chinese academic centers — not a single group — constitutes the largest segment of the modern clinical literature [6, rct] [7, rct] [8, meta_analysis] [9, meta_analysis].
- **SciClone-sponsored registrational work:** The hepatitis B/C registrational trials that supported non-US approvals were industry-sponsored, a potential bias source, but the HBV evidence was sufficiently robust to satisfy multiple independent regulatory agencies.

**Caveats on independence:** The Italian and Chinese threads overlap with jurisdictions where thymalfasin is an approved, commercially available medicine, which means investigator independence and industry relationships require scrutiny on a trial-by-trial basis. The US academic thread is the cleanest from a conflict perspective. For the sepsis literature, all primary RCTs are China-sourced, which limits generalizability and raises the possibility of publication ecosystem effects (publication bias was detected in the 2025 meta-analysis [9, meta_analysis]).

**Overall verdict:** Tα1's evidence base is genuine multi-group, multi-country, multi-indication — a level of independent breadth that distinguishes it from single-investigator peptide series. The vaccine-adjuvant signal in aging is replicated across independent US academic and Italian groups. The sepsis mortality signal is partially confirmed by a landmark Phase 3 trial but the latter was null on the primary endpoint. The honest characterization is: a well-evidenced, approved-ex-US immunomodulator with a credible mechanistic rationale for immunosenescence applications, but whose anti-aging/longevity use is an extension beyond its approved indications and beyond the evidence that directly supports regulatory approval.
