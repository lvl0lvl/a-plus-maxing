## Section A — Identity & molecular mechanism

### A.1 Identity

Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1; INN: thymalfasin; brand: Zadaxin, SciClone Pharmaceuticals) is a 28-amino-acid peptide bearing an N-terminal acetyl group on its first residue (serine). It was first isolated from bovine calf thymus as one of several bioactive polypeptides within thymosin fraction 5, a partially purified thymic extract; the 1977 isolation paper by Goldstein et al. established the sequence, named the thymosin family convention, and proposed a role in the regulation and differentiation of T lymphocytes [1, in_vitro].

The canonical sequence (residues 2–29 of the human prothymosin-α precursor, numbered by the mature processed peptide convention) is:

> Ac-Ser-Asp-Ala-Ala-Val-Asp-Thr-Ser-Ser-Glu-Ile-Thr-Thr-Lys-Asp-Leu-Lys-Glu-Lys-Lys-Glu-Val-Glu-Glu-Glu-Ala-Glu-Asn

N-terminal acetylation is required for full biological activity. Molecular weight of the acetylated mature peptide is approximately 3108 Da (identical to the ZADAXIN clinical standard) [1, in_vitro]. Thymalfasin is the INN for the synthetic form of Tα1, approved in more than 35 countries for chronic hepatitis B, chronic hepatitis C, and as an immune adjuvant.

**Precursor and processing.** Tα1 is a cleavage product of prothymosin-α (ProTα), a 113-amino-acid nuclear protein that functions independently as a chromatin-associated factor. The lysosomal asparaginyl endopeptidase legumain (mammalian AEP) cleaves ProTα at Asn28-Gly29 and Asn35-Gly36, generating Tα1 and the related fragment thymosin alpha-11; the process has been demonstrated in mammalian tissue extracts, confirming Tα1 is a genuine endogenous peptide rather than an extraction artifact [2, in_vitro].

### A.2 Molecular mechanism of immunomodulation

Tα1 exerts pleiotropic effects across innate and adaptive immune compartments. Because many of its activities were characterized in parallel across multiple cell systems and disease contexts, the receptor-level biology was clarified relatively late — and a single high-affinity receptor has not been identified. Garaci et al. (2024) describe Tα1 as a paradigmatic **phenotypic drug** whose development preceded full target deconvolution [6, mechanism_review]. The mechanistic picture that has emerged maps onto three partially overlapping nodes: (1) TLR-dependent innate sensing and dendritic-cell programming, (2) T-cell maturation and Th1/Th2 re-balancing, and (3) tolerogenic/regulatory immune tuning via IDO. An antioxidant/Nrf2-adjacent activity is documented in vitro but its physiological relevance to immunomodulation remains uncertain.

#### A.2.1 TLR signaling: TLR2 and TLR9 agonism

The strongest mechanistic evidence positions Tα1 as a TLR2 and TLR9 agonist on myeloid and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (DCs). This was established through gene-knockout and blocking-antibody approaches in murine models and corroborated in human DC cultures.

**TLR9/MyD88/IRF7 axis (in vivo murine + ex vivo human DCs).** Romani et al. (2006) demonstrated in murine bone marrow-derived DCs and human monocyte-derived DCs that Tα1 induces IDO expression via a TLR9-dependent mechanism requiring type I interferon receptor co-signaling; TLR9-deficient DCs did not respond to Tα1 with IDO induction [3, animal]. Bozza et al. (2007) confirmed the TLR9/MyD88/IRF7 axis in vivo using murine cytomegalovirus (MCMV) infection in wild-type, susceptible, and TLR-deficient mouse strains (BALB/c and C57BL/6 backgrounds, n = 8–12 per group): Tα1 protected susceptible mice from lethal MCMV by activating plasmacytoid DCs through the TLR9 → MyD88 → IRF7 cascade, driving IFN-α and IFN-γ production; protection was abrogated in TLR9-KO and MyD88-KO mice [4, animal]. These data establish the TLR9/MyD88 axis as load-bearing in Tα1's antiviral DC-priming activity in animal models.

**TLR2 and broader TLR engagement.** Garaci et al. (2024) summarize convergent evidence that Tα1 activates TLR2, TLR4, and TLR9, engaging NF-κB, p38 MAPK, TRAF6/IKK, and IRF3 downstream [6, mechanism_review]. This is consistent with findings in human monocyte-derived DCs and PBMCs, though many of these receptor-assignment studies are limited to reporter assays and cytokine-measurement endpoints; the binding stoichiometry and whether Tα1 acts as a direct TLR ligand or via an accessory co-receptor remains unresolved. In the mechanistic review by Tao et al. (2023), the authors describe TLR2 engagement activating the MyD88/NF-κB/p38 MAPK pathway → pro-inflammatory cytokine (IL-12, TNF-α, IL-6) secretion, while TLR9 additionally drives the MyD88/IRF7 branch → type I interferon induction [7, mechanism_review].

#### A.2.2 Dendritic-cell maturation and Th1 polarization

Tα1-stimulated DCs upregulate MHC class II (HLA-DR), CD80, CD86, and IL-12 p70. The Romani et al. (2006) Blood study used murine bone marrow-derived DCs and human monocyte-derived DCs: Tα1 primed both for antifungal Th1 resistance and increased IFN-γ, IL-2 and IL-12 while reducing IL-4 and IL-10 in Th1-promoting contexts [3, animal]. The downstream T-cell consequence is polarization toward Th1 and CD8+ cytotoxic T-lymphocyte (CTL) phenotypes. In chronic HCV patients, Tα1 treatment increased IL-2 and IFN-γ while decreasing Th2 cytokines IL-4 and IL-10 [5, mechanism_review]. The peptide also enhances NK cell cytotoxicity, an activity noted in clinical immunodeficiency contexts though mechanistically less well-characterized.

**T-cell maturation (thymic-independent pathway).** The original Goldstein et al. (1977) paper proposed a role in T-cell differentiation from precursors, and subsequent work has confirmed that Tα1 promotes differentiation of CD4+/CD8+ T cells from stem-cell precursors and rescues T-cell function in immunosuppressed states (radiation, chemotherapy, tumor burden, aging). Naylor et al. (2007) demonstrated in a Lewis lung carcinoma mouse model (C57BL/6, standard tumor implantation) that Tα1 combined with cytokine immunotherapy (IRX-2) produced synergistic survival improvements exceeding either agent alone, consistent with Tα1 potentiating cytokine-driven T-cell restoration rather than acting through an entirely distinct pathway [8, animal].

#### A.2.3 Tolerogenic arm: IDO, regulatory T cells, and bidirectional immunomodulation

A key distinguishing feature of Tα1 is its capacity to simultaneously support effector immunity and immune tolerance — the "bidirectional" or "immune-restorative" framing. The mechanistic substrate for this was identified as the IDO (indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase) axis. Romani et al. (2006) showed that Tα1-primed DCs that induced IDO also generated regulatory T cells (Tregs) and IL-10 production, while maintaining Th1-polarizing capacity in parallel — an immune balance phenotype rather than pure immune activation [3, animal]. This IDO-mediated tolerance induction requires TLR9 and type I IFN receptor signaling in DCs. In the context of pre-existing inflammatory or autoimmune conditions, Tα1's induction of the IDO/Treg arm may explain its anti-inflammatory effects; in immunodeficient or infection states, the TLR9/Th1/CTL arm predominates. The Romani et al. (2012) Ann NY Acad Sci review synthesizes this as Tα1 being able to "enhance T cell, dendritic cell (DC), and antibody responses, modulate cytokine and chemokine production, and block steroid-induced apoptosis of thymocytes" depending on immunological context [5, mechanism_review]. The state of knowledge as of 2024 acknowledges this bidirectionality as mechanistically real but context-dependent — the peptide's net immunological effect in any given disease state reflects the prevailing immune tone rather than a fixed directional program [6, mechanism_review].

#### A.2.4 Antioxidant effects (in vitro)

Tα1 reduces intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) and enhances catalase, superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase activity. This has been demonstrated in A549 human lung adenocarcinoma cells at concentrations of 3–12 µg/mL, where significant increases in SOD and GSH-Px activity and reduction in ROS production were observed compared with untreated controls [9, in_vitro]. The Garaci et al. (2024) phenotypic review notes antioxidant activity as one of Tα1's characterized biological effects in human cells [6, mechanism_review]. Whether this represents direct Nrf2 pathway activation or indirect ROS scavenging via TLR-downstream MAPK signaling is not established. No clinical trial has isolated antioxidant activity as a primary endpoint; this mechanism requires human-model corroboration before it can be considered established in physiological contexts.
