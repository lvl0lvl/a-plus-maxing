## Section D — Safety, dosing, route, PK, regulatory & monitoring

### D.1 Route of Administration

Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1; thymalfasin; Zadaxin) is administered exclusively by **subcutaneous (SC) injection** [1, regulatory]. The lyophilized powder is reconstituted with 1 mL sterile water for injection immediately before use, yielding a 1.6 mg/mL solution [1, regulatory]. Intravenous and oral routes are not established for clinical use. The SC route provides near-complete bioavailability with rapid absorption.

---

### D.2 Pharmacokinetics

The PK profile of Tα1 is well characterized from a randomized, three-way crossover study (n = 9 healthy volunteers) comparing three marketed formulations (Zadaxin, Timosina, Tal-HLR) at 900 µg/m² SC [2, rct]. Urinary excretion data are sourced from the Zadaxin prescribing information [1, regulatory]; Rost et al. [2] did not report urinary excretion:

| Parameter | Observed values |
|---|---|
| **Tmax** | 1–2 hours post-SC injection |
| **Cmax** | 30–80 µg/L (formulation-dependent) |
| **AUC(0–∞)** | 95–267 µg·h/L |
| **t½** | < 3 hours (label cites ~2 hours) |
| **Volume of distribution** | ~30–40 L |
| **Urinary excretion** | 31–60% of administered dose [1, regulatory] |
| **Accumulation** | None observed on repeat dosing |

Serum concentrations return to baseline within 24 hours [1, regulatory]. Dose-proportional kinetics were confirmed across single doses of 0.8–6.4 mg and multi-dose regimens of 1.6–16 mg over 5–7 days [1, regulatory]. Tα1 is not metabolized by hepatic CYP450 enzymes; renal clearance accounts for the majority of elimination. No clinically significant drug-drug interactions have been reported [1, regulatory].

**Prodrug status:** Tα1 is a synthetic 28-amino acid polypeptide corresponding to the naturally occurring thymosin alpha 1; it is the active form and is not a prodrug [3, mechanism_review].

---

### D.3 Approved Dosing — Label and Clinical Trial–Derived

**CRITICAL FRAMING NOTE:** All doses in §D.3 are label-derived (Zadaxin prescribing information) or from peer-reviewed trial protocols — not grey-market sources. These are citable, not inferred.

#### D.3.1 Chronic Hepatitis B and Hepatitis C (Approved Label)

- **Dose:** 1.6 mg (equivalent to 900 µg/m²) SC **twice weekly** [1, regulatory]
- **Duration:** 6 to 12 months [1, regulatory]
- **Special populations:** Patients < 40 kg receive 40 µg/kg SC twice weekly [1, regulatory]

This is the foundational approved regimen; it was the reference dose for the hepatitis B trials (9–53% HBV DNA clearance across studied cohorts) and hepatitis C combination trials (71% normalized liver enzymes with Tα1 + interferon vs. 35% interferon monotherapy) [3, mechanism_review].

#### D.3.2 Sepsis (ETASS Trial Protocol)

The ETASS trial — a multicenter, single-blind RCT (n = 361 ICU patients with severe sepsis, six Chinese tertiary hospitals, 2008–2010) — used an intensified protocol [4, rct]:

- **Dose:** 1.6 mg SC (Zadaxin™, SciClone) **twice daily × 5 consecutive days**, then **once daily × 2 days**
- **Initiation:** Within 4 hours of enrollment
- **Control:** Standard care without Tα1

28-day all-cause mortality was 26.0% (Tα1) vs. 35.0% (control); RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.54–1.02, P = 0.049 log-rank) [4, rct]. No Tα1-related severe adverse events were recorded; no treatment discontinuation due to intolerance [4, rct]. A 2016 meta-analysis of 19 RCTs confirmed significant 28-day mortality reduction (RR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.77, P < 0.001) with both once-daily and twice-daily 1.6 mg SC regimens, noting zero Tα1-related severe adverse events across all included trials [5, meta_analysis]. Sub-analyses spanned 530 patients (mortality endpoint), 489 patients (APACHE II), and 721 patients (HLA-DR); the 19 RCTs do not report a single pooled total enrollment figure in the published abstract.

#### D.3.3 Vaccine Adjuvant Context

Studies have used the same 1.6 mg SC twice-weekly dosing as a co-administration schedule with hepatitis B and influenza vaccines in non-responders and elderly subjects (mean age ~72); the dose was not modified for adjuvant use compared to therapeutic use [3, mechanism_review]. Clinical review documents indicate adjuvant studies used biweekly injections of Tα1 beginning at the time of vaccination [3, mechanism_review].

#### D.3.4 Grey-Market / Longevity-Community Conventions (Disclosure; NOT citable for numbers)

Communities discussing Tα1 for "immune optimization" or longevity use doses ranging from 0.5–1.6 mg SC twice weekly on various schedules (e.g., 5 days on / 2 days off). **No vendor, protocol document, or anecdotal aggregate grounds these as evidence-based.** These conventions are noted for context only; no number from grey-market sources is used in this section [vendor_label/anecdote_aggregate — NOT grounding any claim].

### D.4 Safety Profile

#### D.4.1 Overview

Tα1 carries a **genuinely exceptional clinical safety record** — this is an approved pharmaceutical with > 25 years of post-market use in > 35 countries. The profile is not speculative or extrapolated from animal models. Key markers:

- Over 2,000 patients in controlled hepatitis trials; 19 sepsis RCTs (sub-analyses covering 530–721 patients per outcome); > 4,400 patients cited across the broader trial program [1, regulatory; 5, meta_analysis]
- No drug-related severe adverse events in the ETASS RCT [4, rct]
- Zero Tα1-related SAEs across 19 pooled sepsis RCTs [5, meta_analysis]
- No treatment discontinuations due to Tα1 intolerance in any trial arm reviewed [5, meta_analysis]

This contrasts sharply with experimental peptides (e.g., TB-500, BPC-157) that lack approved-drug safety databases.

#### D.4.2 Adverse Events — Documented

**Common (mild, infrequent):**
- Injection-site reactions: erythema, mild discomfort, local tenderness — the dominant AE class [1, regulatory; 3, mechanism_review]
- Transient mild injection-site atrophy (rare, with repeated dosing)

**Less common:**
- Polyarthralgia with hand edema (rare, Zadaxin label) [1, regulatory]
- Rash (rare) [1, regulatory]

**Combination therapy (Tα1 + interferon):** The AE profile in combination studies included fever, fatigue, myalgia, nausea, vomiting, and neutropenia — attributed predominantly to interferon, not Tα1 monotherapy [3, mechanism_review]. When Tα1 is assessed as monotherapy, AEs are indistinguishable from local injection-site effects.

**Systemic immunological AEs:** No documented signal for cytokine storm, severe autoimmune disease induction, or immunogenicity-related systemic reactions has emerged from the controlled trial record. The bidirectional immunomodulatory mechanism (Th1 upregulation in immunodeficiency; regulatory T-cell promotion in hyperinflammation) is hypothesized to underlie its tolerability even in inflammatory conditions [6, meta_analysis; 4, rct].

#### D.4.3 Theoretical Concerns and Honest Limitations

- **Autoimmune induction:** Tα1 has a theoretical capacity to exacerbate pre-existing autoimmune disease via T-cell activation, though no clinical trial has documented this. The Zadaxin label does not list active autoimmune disease as a contraindication but notes caution in immunosuppressed contexts [1, regulatory]. Clinical guidance documents flag autoimmune conditions as a monitoring point without firm contraindication [7, practitioner_protocol].
- **Long-term carcinogenicity data:** Carcinogenicity studies were not completed as part of the approval program; this is acknowledged in the label [1, regulatory]. No signal has emerged in post-market use but the formal gap exists.
- **Immunogenicity (compounded products):** The FDA's December 2024 PCAC evaluation raised concerns about peptide impurities and immunogenicity risk in non-approved compounded formulations — distinct from the approved Zadaxin formulation [8, regulatory]. This concern applies specifically to compounded Tα1, not to Zadaxin.
- **Trial geography:** The bulk of sepsis RCTs were conducted in China; generalizability to Western ICU populations and non-Asian genetic backgrounds has not been formally tested.

### D.5 Contraindications and Monitoring

#### D.5.1 Absolute Contraindications

1. **Hypersensitivity** to thymalfasin or any excipient of the formulation [1, regulatory]
2. **Organ transplant recipients under deliberate immunosuppression** — Tα1 may worsen acute or chronic graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) and cause engraftment failure in HSCT recipients [1, regulatory; 7, practitioner_protocol]

#### D.5.2 Precautions / Relative Contraindications

| Context | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Active autoimmune disease | Use with caution; immune stimulation may exacerbate; no formal contraindication in label but clinical guidance advises monitoring [7, practitioner_protocol] |
| Pregnancy (Category C) | Use only if clearly needed; no adequate human data [1, regulatory] |
| Nursing mothers | Unknown excretion in breast milk; exercise caution [1, regulatory] |
| Pediatric use (< 18 years) | Safety and efficacy not established [1, regulatory] |
| Co-administered immunosuppressants | Tα1 effect may be attenuated or conflicting; avoid unless risk/benefit clearly favors use [1, regulatory] |

#### D.5.3 Monitoring Parameters

- **Injection sites:** Inspect for erythema, induration, atrophy at each visit
- **Liver function:** Standard monitoring in hepatitis indications per co-managing protocol; not Tα1-specific
- **Immune labs (if indicated):** CD4/CD8 counts and NK cell activity were monitored in sepsis/HIV studies but are not mandated by the label for standard hepatitis indications
- **Drug interactions:** No established pharmacokinetic interactions; no CYP450 involvement; do not mix Tα1 with other drugs in the same syringe [1, regulatory]

#### D.5.4 Stopping Criteria

The label does not define formal stopping criteria beyond hypersensitivity. Based on the trial record:
- Discontinue immediately on hypersensitivity reaction
- Reassess if signs of graft rejection or GVHD emerge in transplant-adjacent use
- No lab threshold–based stopping rule is established in the clinical literature

### D.6 Regulatory Status

#### D.6.1 International Approval (≥ 35 Countries)

Zadaxin (thymalfasin, SciClone Pharmaceuticals / PATHEON Italia) received its first international approvals beginning 1999 [1, regulatory; 9, regulatory]. Approved countries include **China** (the dominant market), **Italy** and other European nations, **Philippines**, **Singapore**, **India**, **Argentina**, **Peru**, **Russia**, and additional markets across South/Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East [9, regulatory]. Approved indications vary by jurisdiction; the core approvals are for chronic hepatitis B and chronic hepatitis C (as monotherapy or combination with interferon-alpha).

#### D.6.2 United States — NOT FDA-Approved

**Tα1 has no FDA marketing approval for any indication in the United States** [1, regulatory]. Key facts:

- Orphan Drug Designation was obtained for malignant melanoma and hepatocellular carcinoma (specific orphan designations, not full approval; per Ancell et al., *Am J Health Syst Pharm* 2001;58(10):879–885, PMID 11381492) — this confers exclusivity windows if approved but does not constitute approval; accessdata.fda.gov was not primary-fetchable at research time so these indications are corroborated by peer-reviewed clinical pharmacy literature and hedged accordingly
- SciClone elected not to pursue full FDA NDA/BLA for standard hepatitis indications, judging the US hepatitis B market insufficient to justify Phase 3 trial investment required for submission
- Tα1 is not on the FDA's list of approved drugs

#### D.6.3 503A Compounding Status — NOT Permitted (as of June 2026)

This is the high-stakes regulatory question for US-based practitioners:

- **Category 2 placement:** FDA placed Tα1 on the interim 503A bulk substances Category 2 list (substances of concern), restricting free compounding pending review
- **PCAC review (December 4, 2024):** The FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee reviewed thymosin alpha-1 free base and thymosin alpha-1 acetate. PCAC **voted against** adding Tα1 to the 503A Bulks List (i.e., recommended against permitting 503A compounding). The FDA's own position was that these substances should not be included on the 503A list [8, regulatory]
- **Effective status (2026):** Tα1 is **not legally compoundable under Section 503A** as of the current date. PCAC's vote is advisory; FDA has not yet issued final notice-and-comment rulemaking, meaning the situation could technically evolve — but the current operative position is off-limits for 503A pharmacies
- **503B outsourcing facilities:** The compounding landscape is evolving; some analyses suggest 503B outsourcing facilities (which operate under different standards) may retain pathways, but this should be verified with current FDA guidance before reliance
- **No approved compounded substitute:** Practitioners seeking to use Tα1 in the US must source the approved Zadaxin product through international channels or obtain it as a research-use item; neither route constitutes ordinary clinical prescribing

#### D.6.4 WADA Status — Nuanced; Requires Sport-Specific Verification

**Thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4; TB-500) is explicitly prohibited** under WADA 2026 Prohibited List S2.3 (Growth Factors and Growth Factor Modulators) [10, regulatory]. Tα1 and Tβ4 are **structurally and functionally distinct peptides** — they share the "thymosin" name but differ in sequence, receptor targets, and primary pharmacology.

**Thymosin alpha-1 is NOT explicitly named** in the WADA 2026 Prohibited List [10, regulatory]. It does not appear in S2.3 alongside Tβ4 and its derivatives.

However, two catch-all provisions require verification:

1. **S0 (Non-Approved Substances):** WADA S0 prohibits "any pharmacological substance not addressed by any other section of the List, with no current approval by any governmental regulatory health authority for human therapeutic use." Because Tα1 **is** approved in > 35 countries (though not the US), it has governmental health authority approval — and therefore S0 does not straightforwardly apply. This is not a settled interpretation.

2. **S2 broad language:** S2 includes catch-all language covering "other growth factors or growth factor modulators affecting muscle, tendon or ligament protein synthesis/degradation, vascularisation, energy utilization, regenerative capacity or fibre type switching." Tα1's primary mechanism is T-cell maturation / immunomodulation, not direct muscle/tendon/vascular growth factor activity — this catch-all is unlikely to apply, but sport-specific rulings vary.

**Operational guidance for athletes:** The WADA list is not exhaustive, and national anti-doping organizations may interpret catch-alls differently. Athletes should submit a query to their relevant national anti-doping authority or use the WADA prohibited list substance check tool before using Tα1. Do NOT assume it is automatically cleared solely because Tβ4 and Tα1 are different peptides.
