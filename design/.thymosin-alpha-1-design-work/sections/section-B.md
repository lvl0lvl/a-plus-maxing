## Section B — Clinical evidence (approved indications + major trials)

Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1; thymalfasin; Zadaxin) is approved in >35 countries for chronic hepatitis B (CHB), chronic hepatitis C (CHC), and as a vaccine adjuvant. It is NOT FDA-approved. The evidence base spans genuine RCTs with regulatory weight (CHB, vaccine adjuvant), mixed-signal RCTs (sepsis), and predominantly observational data (COVID-19). Cancer evidence rests on phase II RCTs and meta-analyses of small, largely Chinese trials. Evidence is assessed by indication below.

---

### B.1 Chronic Hepatitis B — registrational evidence (strongest tier)

The clearest RCT evidence for Tα1 is in CHB. Chien et al. (1998) published a three-arm randomized controlled trial in *Hepatology* (n = ~98 HBeAg-positive CHB patients, Taiwan) comparing 26-week Tα1 (SC injection), 52-week Tα1 (SC injection), and observation [1, rct]. At 18-month follow-up, complete virological response rates (HBV DNA clearance + HBeAg clearance) were 40.6% (26-week), 26.5% (52-week), and 9.4% (control); the 26-week arm was significantly superior (p = 0.004). This trial established the standard 6-month dosing regimen and formed part of the registration package. Sponsor: non-US government (Taiwan); no SciClone funding stated.

A separate phase III multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (Mutchnick et al., 1999; *J Viral Hepatitis*; n = 97; USA) administered Tα1 1.6 mg SC twice weekly for 6 months and found numerically higher response rates with Tα1 vs. placebo — 25% vs. 13% sustained HBV DNA loss with negative HBeAg — but the primary endpoint did not reach statistical significance (p < 0.11) [2, rct]. This is the cautionary data point: not all CHB trials hit their primary endpoint, and the Mutchnick trial population (anti-HBe, HBV-DNA-positive) may have differed meaningfully from the Chien cohort.

A subsequent meta-analysis of eight RCTs (n = 583 patients) comparing lamivudine + Tα1 versus lamivudine alone reported HBeAg seroconversion of 45.1% vs. 15.2% in favor of the combination — though this evidence comes from predominantly Chinese trials with varying methodological quality [12, meta_analysis].

**Evidence tier for CHB:** Moderate-strong. Multiple RCTs in registrational dossiers, but primary endpoint failure in the US phase III trial; approved in >35 countries, not by FDA.

---

### B.2 Chronic Hepatitis C — combination with interferon (mixed)

Sherman et al. (1998) conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (*Hepatology*; n = 109 CHC patients; USA) comparing interferon-alpha (3 MU SC three times weekly) + Tα1 (1.6 mg SC twice weekly) vs. interferon + placebo [3, rct]. End-of-treatment biochemical response (ALT normalization, primary endpoint) was 37.1% (combination) vs. 16.2% (interferon alone), p = 0.04. HCV RNA clearance (virological) was 37.1% vs. 18.9% in the same arms. Sustained biochemical response was only 14.2% vs. 8.1% — not statistically significant at sustained follow-up. Funding: Non-US government; SciClone involvement not stated.

A pooled analysis of available combination trials (Sherman & Sherman, 1998; meta-analysis of 2 RCTs + 1 open-label study with historical controls; n = 121 patients) found statistically significant biochemical end-treatment response (44.7% vs. 22%; p = 0.01) but non-significant sustained response (22.4% vs. 11.1%; p = 0.10), with the DARE critical commentary noting methodological limitations (weak search strategy, unassessed study quality) [4, meta_analysis].

**Contextual note:** The DAA (direct-acting antiviral) revolution has made interferon-based HCV regimens obsolete. Tα1's HCV indication reflects a pre-DAA approval era; it is no longer clinically relevant in DAA-accessible markets.

**Evidence tier for HCV:** Weak-to-moderate. End-of-treatment signal; sustained response not robustly demonstrated; clinical setting is now obsolete.

---

### B.3 Vaccine adjuvant — influenza, elderly and dialysis populations (moderate-strong)

Tα1 has a documented immunomodulatory role when co-administered with influenza vaccines in populations with blunted immune responses. Panatto et al. (2011) published a narrative review in *J Preventive Medicine and Hygiene* covering trials in elderly and immunocompromised cohorts, concluding that Tα1 enhances T-cell, dendritic cell, and antibody responses and improves seroprotection in at-risk groups [5, mechanism_review].

The most rigorous individual trial in this domain is Carraro et al. (2012; *Vaccine*; n = 94 hemodialysis patients; Italy) — a pilot trial with three parallel arms: pandemic H1N1v influenza vaccine (Focetria) alone, vaccine + Tα1 3.2 mg SC, or vaccine + Tα1 6.4 mg SC [6, open_label]. The Tα1 co-administration arms achieved CHMP licensing immunogenicity criteria (seroconversion, seroprotection, geometric mean ratio) that the vaccine-alone arm failed to reach in this dialysis population. Well tolerated; no hematological or biochemical adverse effects.

**Evidence tier for vaccine adjuvant:** Moderate. Biologically well-grounded, consistent signal across smaller trials in target populations (dialysis, elderly); not a large phase III registration trial.

### B.4 Sepsis — the two landmark RCTs and their divergent verdicts

**ETASS (2013):** Wu et al., *Critical Care*; n = 361 (Tα1 n = 181, control n = 180); multicenter, single-blind RCT in six tertiary hospitals in China; patients with severe sepsis; Tα1 administered SC [7, rct]. The 28-day all-cause mortality primary endpoint showed 26.0% (Tα1) vs. 35.0% (control) — RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.54–1.02), p = 0.062 by unadjusted analysis; a pre-specified log-rank survival analysis reached p = 0.049. In-hospital mortality hit significance: 28.7% vs. 39.4%, RR 0.73, p = 0.032. However, no significant differences were found in ICU mortality, ventilation-free days, ICU-free days, or duration of mechanical ventilation. The primary endpoint technically did not reach p < 0.05. Funding: Guangdong Natural Science Foundation and Guangdong Medical Scientific Research Foundation (academic/government); no commercial sponsor disclosed; no competing interests declared.

**TESTS (2025):** Wu et al., *BMJ*; n = 1,089 mITT (Tα1 n = 542, placebo n = 547); 22-centre, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in China, conducted September 2016–December 2020; Tα1 administered SC [8, rct]. This is the definitive, adequately powered trial. Primary outcome: 28-day all-cause mortality 23.4% (Tα1) vs. 24.1% (placebo), HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.77–1.27), p = 0.93. Null result. No significant difference in any secondary or safety endpoint. Subgroup signals in elderly and diabetic patients were hypothesis-generating only. Sponsor: SciClone Pharmaceuticals (listed alongside Sun Yat-sen University and Guangdong Clinical Research Center).

**Sepsis meta-analysis context:** A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (Gu et al., *Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology*; 11 RCTs; n = 1,927) reported pooled OR 0.73 (95% CI 0.59–0.90, p = 0.003) — apparently positive — but the authors explicitly noted that "analyses of high-quality and multi-center subgroups did not reveal a mortality benefit," and trial sequential analysis indicated insufficient sample size for definitive conclusions even after pooling [9, meta_analysis]. The pool is dominated by small, lower-quality single-center Chinese trials that preceded TESTS; the TESTS trial alone (n = 1,089) effectively halves the weight of the positive signal.

**Honest verdict on sepsis:** ETASS (2013) showed a suggestive in-hospital mortality signal in a subgroup analysis with marginal primary-endpoint significance. The large, double-blind, adequately powered TESTS phase 3 trial (2025) was negative on all outcomes. Current evidence does NOT support Tα1 for unselected sepsis. The SciClone-sponsored TESTS is a high-quality disconfirmatory trial — the sponsor result cuts against commercial interest.

### B.5 COVID-19 — observational data only (weak)

Liu et al. (2020; *Clinical Infectious Diseases*; n = 76 severe COVID-19 patients from two Wuhan hospitals; retrospective cohort) reported 30-day mortality of 11.1% in Tα1-treated patients (n = 9) vs. 30.0% in untreated comparators (n = 10), p = 0.044 [10, cohort]. This is a **very small retrospective study** with serious confounding risk: the Tα1 group had n = 9, selection of treated patients was non-random, and the comparison group was contemporaneous but not randomized. The finding is hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

Subsequent observational studies produced contradictory results: one analysis of 771 COVID-19 patients found no mortality association with Tα1 in critically ill patients; another propensity-matched retrospective study (Wang et al.) found immunomodulatory therapies including Tα1 were associated with *higher* mortality, likely confounded by severity-of-illness channeling bias (sicker patients received more immunomodulators).

No adequately powered RCT of Tα1 for COVID-19 has been published.

**Evidence tier for COVID-19:** Weak (observational only). Preliminary signal from a 9-patient treated cohort. Contradicted by other observational data. Cannot be interpreted as efficacy evidence.

### B.6 Cancer adjunct — phase II RCTs and small meta-analyses (moderate for NSCLC; exploratory for melanoma/HCC)

Costantini et al. (2019; *Frontiers in Oncology*; mechanism review + trial summary; no COI declared) synthesized the cancer evidence [11, mechanism_review]:

**NSCLC:** A meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (n = 724 advanced/stage III–IV NSCLC patients) comparing Tα1 + cisplatin-vinorelbine/gemcitabine vs. chemotherapy alone found OR 1.86 for overall response rate and OR 3.06 for tumor control rate. A phase II sequential chemoimmunotherapy study (cisplatin + etoposide + Tα1 + IFN-α2a; n = 56 patients) achieved 43% objective response and 12.3-month median survival. A separate analysis reported 14.3% higher 5-year survival in stage IIIA NSCLC with Tα1 co-administration (HR = 0.72, p = 0.032). All trials were small-to-medium, predominantly Chinese; independent replication outside China is lacking.

**Melanoma:** A phase II multicenter, dose-ranging RCT used dacarbazine ± IFN-alpha ± Tα1 and reported 50% objective response in the combination arm. Long-term follow-up showed 13.3-month median OS. Sequential Tα1-then-ipilimumab in a compassionate-use program (non-randomized) showed 38.4 months median OS vs. 8 months historical ipilimumab-alone — but this is uncontrolled retrospective data, not an RCT.

**HCC:** Evidence consists of retrospective evaluations of post-resection adjuvant Tα1 and combination TACE + Tα1 studies. No adequately powered RCT for HCC as a primary endpoint.

**Evidence tier for cancer:** Moderate for NSCLC (meta-analysis of multiple RCTs, consistent directional signal), exploratory/phase II for melanoma, retrospective for HCC. No practice-changing phase III trial in any cancer type. Population is largely Chinese; generalizability is uncertain.

### Honest synthesis — per-indication evidence tiers

| Indication | Best evidence design | Evidence tier | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic hepatitis B | Multiple RCTs + registration package | Moderate-strong | Genuine regulatory signal; not all trials hit primary endpoint; approved >35 countries, not FDA |
| Vaccine adjuvant (dialysis/elderly) | Small-to-medium RCTs | Moderate | Consistent immunogenicity signal in blunted-immunity populations |
| Chronic hepatitis C | RCT + small meta-analysis | Weak-moderate | End-of-treatment signal only; clinically obsolete (DAA era) |
| Sepsis | Two large RCTs (ETASS, TESTS) | Weak (net negative) | ETASS marginally positive in-hospital signal; TESTS phase 3 definitively null (HR 0.99) |
| COVID-19 | Retrospective cohort (n = 9 treated) | Weak | No RCT; contradictory observational data; cannot claim efficacy |
| NSCLC adjunct | Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs | Moderate (exploratory) | Consistent directional signal; all small/Chinese trials; no practice-standard evidence |
| Melanoma/HCC | Phase II RCT + retrospective | Exploratory | Hypothesis-generating only |
