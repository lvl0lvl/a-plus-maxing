## Section E — Concentration / COI audit, commercialization & sourcing realism

### E.1 Single-lab concentration audit

Humanin was identified in 2001 by Hashimoto, Niikura, and Nishimoto at Keio University School of Medicine in a PNAS paper screening for genes that protect neurons against familial Alzheimer's disease insults [1, mechanism_review]. Wikipedia's humanin entry (substantiated by the publication record) notes three independent discovery events in rapid succession: (a) the Nishimoto/Hashimoto lab (Keio), (b) Jae C. Reed's group (La Jolla/Burnham), which identified humanin via Bax interaction screening, and (c) Pinchas Cohen's lab (then at UCLA, now USC Leonard Davis School of Gerontology), which identified humanin via IGFBP-3 interaction screening.

The overall humanin literature is larger and older than MOTS-c (2001 vs. 2015 discovery), and by 2026 encompasses several hundred primary papers. Mapping primary author affiliations across the verified bibliography assembled here:

**Group A — Nishimoto/Hashimoto/Niikura (Keio University, Tokyo):** The 2001 PNAS discovery paper [1] and approximately 15–20 primary mechanistic and structure-activity papers published 2001–2006 (J Neurosci, J Neurochem, Eur J Neurosci, Peptides, Life Sci, J Neurosci Res, Neurosci Lett). Senior authors: Ikuo Nishimoto (deceased 2006), Yasuko Hashimoto, Takako Niikura. A 2011 PLoS One paper (PMID 22022459 / PMC3022031) by Niikura et al. on amyloid reduction in Alzheimer's mice also falls in this group. Estimated contribution to primary efficacy/mechanism papers: **~30–35%** of the total identified primary literature, concentrated 2001–2011.

**Group B — Cohen/Yen/Kim/Lee (USC Leonard Davis School of Gerontology):** Changhan Lee, Kelvin Yen, Pinchas Cohen, Su-Jeong Kim, Hemal Mehta, Brendan Miller. Core papers include the 2013 J Mol Endocrinol review establishing MDP biology [2, mechanism_review], a 2014 Aging Cell paper on IGF-I/humanin regulation [3, animal], the 2018 Sci Rep cognitive aging paper [4, cohort], the 2019 Metabolomics metabolic profiling paper [5, animal], and the 2020 Aging (Albany NY) lifespan/healthspan paper [6, animal]. USC affiliates also appear as co-authors on the RPE/eye papers (Sreekumar et al. 2016 [7, in_vitro]; Nashine et al. 2017 [8, in_vitro]). Estimated contribution to primary efficacy/mechanism papers: **~25–30%**.

**Combined share (Groups A + B): approximately 55–65% of identifiable primary efficacy/mechanism papers.** This is meaningfully below the 70% single-lab flag threshold, unlike the MOTS-c literature where USC dominates at ~80%+. Humanin's literature has genuine — if uneven — independent breadth.

**Independent replicating groups (verified):**

- **Muzumdar/Gong (University of Pittsburgh / Children's Hospital of Pittsburgh / Montefiore-Albert Einstein):** Cardiovascular focus. Muzumdar et al. 2010 (Arterioscler Thromb Vasc Biol, PMID 20651283) [9, animal] demonstrated HNG protection in murine MI/R through AMPK/eNOS pathways; Gong & Muzumdar 2022 (BBA Gen Subj, PMID 34896254) extended this to cardio-protective mechanisms. The 2020 JACC Basic Transl Sci porcine study (Sharp et al., PMID 32760857) [10, animal] was conducted at LSU Health Science Center and University of Pittsburgh — independent of USC and Keio — and holds a US patent on humanin/humanin-analogs for acute MI, underscoring both independent scientific interest and separate IP.

- **Gong/Muzumdar/Zheng (Chinese collaborations + Pittsburgh):** Cai et al. 2022 (BBA Gen Subj, PMID 34525397) "Circulating humanin is lower in coronary artery disease and is a prognostic biomarker" — multicenter Chinese + Pittsburgh group; independent observational confirmation in human coronary disease.

- **Soochow University (Ren et al., China):** PMID 32174022, J Cell Mol Med 2020 [11, in_vitro] — humanin/HNG inhibits platelet activation via tubulin acetylation; independent Chinese lab, no USC/Keio ties.

- **Chinese independent groups (Bioengineered series, 2021–2022):** Multiple groups at Harbin Medical University, Hainan Medical University, and Changzhi Medical College have independently published HNG/S14G-humanin efficacy papers (gouty arthritis, STZ-induced cardiac dysfunction, dental pulp cells, TLR4/NF-κB pathways). These papers appear in Bioengineered, a journal not on the rejected-host list, though the quality level is tier 3. They demonstrate the peptide is being studied independently across Chinese institutions.

- **Chongqing Medical University / West China Hospital (Zhao et al., China):** Heliyon 2023 (PMID 38045183) [12, animal] — HNG in heart failure mouse models; Chongqing Medical University, no USC/Keio ties.

- **University of Tartu (Estonia):** NCT03431844, a completed observational study measuring humanin isoforms in cardiac muscle and blood plasma around cardiac surgery — an independent European clinical observational program [13, cohort].

**Assessment:** Humanin's literature is NOT single-lab-dominated in the MOTS-c sense. The two historically dominant nexuses (Keio and USC) together account for approximately 55–65% of identified primary efficacy/mechanism papers, which is concentrated but does not meet the ≥70% single-lab flag. A meaningful body of independent primary research exists, particularly in cardiovascular protection (Muzumdar/Gong group, LSU/Pittsburgh porcine study), ophthalmic biology (UC Irvine / Doheny), and scattered Chinese independent groups. The caveat is that many of the independent papers use HNG (the S14G analog) introduced and characterized by the USC group — the analog tool itself originates from a concentrated source even where the application labs are independent.

### E.2 COI and commercialization

**CohBar Inc.** was a publicly traded biopharmaceutical company (NASDAQ: CWBR) founded around 2013 by Pinchas Cohen and Nir Barzilai (Albert Einstein College of Medicine), with scientific advisory board ties to Cohen's MDP research program. CohBar's pipeline centered on MDP analogs including both humanin-derived and MOTS-c-derived compounds. Cohen's COI disclosure appears in every USC humanin paper from at least 2013 onward: "PC is a consultant and stockholder of CohBar Inc." [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. Kelvin Yen has also disclosed CohBar consultancy [4].

CohBar's clinical programs were primarily MOTS-c analogs (CB5945 / CB4209 for obesity and NASH) rather than native humanin. No humanin-specific IND or clinical trial was identified in ClinicalTrials.gov as of this audit (zero NCT results for humanin as an interventional agent; ClinicalTrials.gov API search returned only observational biomarker studies [13, cohort] [14, cohort] [15, cohort]). CohBar's website is currently unreachable (ECONNREFUSED), consistent with corporate dissolution. Yahoo Finance shows no active CWBR ticker. The company reported Q2 2023 financial results (active as of mid-2023 per BioSpace records) but available evidence points to dissolution in late 2023 or 2024; no verified dissolution date was found in public filings retrievable during this audit (SEC EDGAR returned 403). The Sharp et al. 2020 JACC paper discloses a separate US patent held by Muzumdar and Lefer on humanin/analogs for acute MI — distinct from the USC/CohBar IP.

**USC/Cohen patent nexus:** Pinchas Cohen and collaborators hold USC-assigned patents on humanin and MDP analogs. These have not been independently verified by patent number in this audit (Google Patents returned no relevant humanin patents via the attempted search), but are referenced in multiple paper COI disclosures and the academic literature. Changhan Lee is a co-discoverer of MOTS-c and holds related intellectual property through USC.

### E.3 Native vs. analog (consumer "humanin" ≠ research HNG)

Consumer-facing products labeled "humanin" or sold as research chemicals deliver the native 24-amino-acid peptide (sequence from the mt-16S rRNA / MT-RNR2 open reading frame). The majority of efficacy papers — especially the potent cardiovascular, metabolic, and neuroprotective effects — used **HNG (S14G-humanin)**, a single-amino-acid substitution (serine→glycine at position 14) that confers 1,000-fold greater potency than native humanin [9, animal]. Equating consumer humanin with HNG-derived efficacy data is an analog-substitution error; the two are not the same compound.

The genomic situation adds identity complexity. The humanin open reading frame sits within the human mitochondrial 16S rRNA gene (MT-RNR2, Gene ID 4550, chrMT). At least 13 nuclear-encoded pseudogenes and paralogs exist in the human genome under the MTRNR2L family (MTRNR2L1 through MTRNR2L13 and related loci). These nuclear copies can confound transcript-level detection and raise questions about whether assays measuring "circulating humanin" capture the mitochondrial-derived peptide exclusively or include nuclear paralog products. Consumer "humanin" peptides sold as research chemicals cannot be traced to MT-RNR2 specifically; purity and identity are unverifiable by the buyer.

### E.4 Sourcing realism

No humanin product (native or HNG analog) is approved by FDA, EMA, or any comparable regulatory body as a therapeutic or dietary supplement. No IND has been publicly filed for native humanin as a clinical investigational agent. Consumer access is research-chemical / grey-market only; products exist from research peptide vendors (e.g., Bachem synthesizes humanin as a research reagent; grey-market vendors offer it alongside BPC-157 and MOTS-c). Purity and sequence identity are unverifiable without mass-spec confirmation. Subcutaneous injection at home (the route used in animal studies) carries infection risk with no sterility guarantee. No compounding pharmacy data sheets were found for humanin; this is a research-only compound with no established clinical dosing schema.
