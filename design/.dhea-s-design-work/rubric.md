# Rubric — dhea-s (biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** DHEA-S (dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate) = the **sulfated, circulating storage form of DHEA**, the most abundant circulating steroid; made almost entirely in the adrenal **zona reticularis** (a small amount gonadal) under **ACTH** (+ other factors); **why DHEA-S, not DHEA, is measured** — DHEA-S has a long half-life (~7–10 h vs DHEA minutes), high stable concentration, NO diurnal variation (unlike cortisol/DHEA) → a single random sample reflects adrenal androgen output; the DHEA⇌DHEA-S interconversion (SULT2A1 sulfation / steroid sulfatase); DHEA-S as a **prohormone / precursor reservoir** peripherally converted to androgens (testosterone/DHT) + estrogens (intracrinology); the dramatic **age curve — adrenarche** (rise ~age 6–8), peak in the 20s, then progressive decline (**"adrenopause"**) to ~10–20% of peak by old age.
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Thresholds:** the strongly AGE- + SEX-dependent ranges (men higher than women; both decline steeply with age) — give representative adult ranges (e.g. men ~**~80–560 µg/dL**, women ~**~35–430 µg/dL**, both age-banded) in µg/dL ↔ µmol/L (×0.02714); the age-banding is essential (a "normal" value is meaningless without age); diagnostic uses — a **marker of adrenal androgen production**: very HIGH → adrenal androgen excess (adrenal tumor/carcinoma, CAH); modestly high in PCOS; LOW → adrenal insufficiency / hypopituitarism / adrenal suppression; its use in the **hyperandrogenism workup** (localizing androgen source: adrenal [DHEA-S] vs ovarian [testosterone]); no single universal cutpoint.
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** immunoassay vs **LC-MS/MS** (immunoassays for DHEA-S have cross-reactivity + accuracy issues, esp. at low concentrations; LC-MS/MS more specific); the lack of a CDC-HoSt-equivalent full standardization for DHEA-S → inter-assay variation; the high stable concentration + no-diurnal-variation makes pre-analytics EASIER than cortisol (timing not critical) — a genuine advantage; interferences (biotin, heterophile, cross-reactivity with DHEA + other sulfated steroids); sample stability; the importance of age-/sex-matched reference intervals; conversion + units consistency.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **HIGH DHEA-S:** adrenal androgen-secreting tumors / adrenocortical carcinoma (markedly high, >700 µg/dL a red flag), congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH, esp. 21-hydroxylase deficiency), PCOS (modest elevation in a subset), premature adrenarche; **LOW DHEA-S:** adrenal insufficiency (primary + secondary — low DHEA-S supports AI), hypopituitarism, glucocorticoid/exogenous-steroid suppression, aging (adrenopause), chronic illness, anorexia; the hyperandrogenism localization role (adrenal vs ovarian); the **DHEA-supplementation evidence** — addressed honestly: supplemental DHEA raises DHEA-S, but evidence for benefit in healthy aging is weak/mixed (no strong support for "anti-aging"); a possible role in adrenal insufficiency / certain conditions but not established for general use; associations of low DHEA-S with age-related outcomes (cardiovascular, mortality, mood, bone — cite cohorts cautiously, note confounding by age + illness); limitations — extreme age dependence (must age-band), assay variation, it is a MARKER of adrenal output not a standalone diagnosis.
