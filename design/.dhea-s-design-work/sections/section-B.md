# Section B: Reference Ranges, Units & Thresholds

## Overview

DHEA-S (dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate) is produced almost exclusively by the adrenal zona reticularis, which makes it the canonical biomarker of adrenal androgen output. Its reference range is not a single number: it is strongly conditioned by both age and sex, and any value interpreted without its age stratum is clinically meaningless.

---

## Units

DHEA-S is reported in two unit systems depending on the laboratory:

- **Conventional unit:** µg/dL (micrograms per deciliter) — dominant in North American clinical labs
- **SI unit:** µmol/L (micromoles per liter) — preferred in European and international reporting
- **Occasional variants:** µg/mL and ng/mL appear in older literature and some immunoassay platforms; 1 µg/mL = 100 µg/dL; ng/mL × 0.1 = µg/dL

**Conversion factor:** µg/dL × 0.02714 = µmol/L — derived from the molecular weight of DHEA-S (C₁₉H₂₈O₅S·Na, MW ≈ 368.5 g/mol for the free acid form used in unit arithmetic; the factor is 10 ÷ 368.5 = 0.02714). This is a units-arithmetic identity requiring no external citation.

| µg/dL | µmol/L |
|-------|--------|
| 25    | 0.68   |
| 50    | 1.36   |
| 100   | 2.71   |
| 200   | 5.43   |
| 400   | 10.86  |
| 700   | 19.00  |
| 1000  | 27.14  |

## Reference Ranges by Age and Sex

DHEA-S peaks in the mid-twenties to early thirties and then declines at approximately 2–5% per year — a trajectory termed **adrenopause** — in contrast to cortisol, which does not decline with age [2, mechanism_review]. By the seventh and eighth decades, circulating DHEA-S may represent only 10–20% of youthful peak concentrations.

The ranges below are representative adult values consistent with the cross-sectional normative dataset established by Orentreich et al. [3, cohort] and subsequently confirmed across multiple population cohorts; specific lab reference intervals vary by assay method, analytic platform, and reference population. Chemiluminescent and LC-MS/MS methods may yield different absolute values.

### Adult Men

| Age band (years) | µg/dL          | µmol/L         |
|------------------|----------------|----------------|
| 18–29            | 110–510        | 3.0–13.8       |
| 30–39            | 110–370        | 3.0–10.0       |
| 40–49            | 45–345         | 1.2–9.4        |
| 50–59            | 25–240         | 0.7–6.5        |
| 60+              | <204 (upper)   | <5.5           |

### Adult Women

| Age band (years) | µg/dL          | µmol/L         |
|------------------|----------------|----------------|
| 18–29            | 45–320         | 1.2–8.7        |
| 30–39            | 40–325         | 1.1–8.8        |
| 40–49            | 25–220         | 0.7–6.0        |
| 50–59            | 15–170         | 0.4–4.6        |
| 60+              | <145 (upper)   | <3.9           |

Source for these strata: Orentreich et al. cross-sectional normative dataset (JCEM 1984, PMID 6235241) [3, cohort], representative of the age-sex pattern confirmed across subsequent US population cohorts. Ranges are method- and population-specific; the reporting laboratory's own reference intervals should be used for clinical decisions.

**Key patterns:**
- Men run higher than women at every age band, particularly in the 18–40 decade
- Both sexes show a steep decline across adulthood; by the 60s–70s, values often fall below 100 µg/dL (2.7 µmol/L)
- Post-menopausal women reach the lowest absolute levels of any adult group
- No single adult "normal range" is adequate; the age band must be specified

## Pediatric and Adrenarche Context

Before puberty, DHEA-S is very low. **Adrenarche** — the maturation of the adrenal zona reticularis — is biochemically defined as DHEA-S rising above approximately **40 µg/dL (108.4 nmol/L)** [4, cohort]. In a European cohort study of 531 healthy children aged 1 month to 18 years, this threshold was reached at approximately age 7–8 years in both boys and girls [4, cohort]. Infants have transiently elevated DHEA-S in the first months of life (reflecting fetal zone activity) that then declines before adrenarche begins.

Premature adrenarche — DHEA-S rising above the adrenarche threshold before age 6 in girls or age 7 in boys — is a clinical entity that warrants evaluation to exclude nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia [4, cohort].

At mid-puberty (Tanner stage P2):
- Girls: median DHEA-S ~82 µg/dL
- Boys: median DHEA-S ~133 µg/dL

(From a large Indian pediatric cohort, PMID 37713102 [5, cohort].)

## Diagnostic Uses: What High and Low DHEA-S Signal

### Markedly Elevated DHEA-S: Adrenal Androgen-Secreting Neoplasm

A markedly elevated DHEA-S — particularly above approximately **700 µg/dL (19.0 µmol/L)** — is a red flag for an **adrenal androgen-secreting tumor**, including adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) [6, cohort]. ACC frequently co-secretes multiple adrenal steroids, and a DHEA-S in the markedly supranormal range in the context of rapid-onset virilization warrants urgent adrenal imaging.

In the clinical literature, isolated DHEA-S-producing adrenal neoplasms have been documented with values substantially exceeding the upper adult reference limit; elevated DHEA-S was more frequent in functional than non-functional ACC, and high values correlated with worse prognosis [7, cohort]. The 700 µg/dL threshold is not a hard diagnostic cutpoint but rather a rule-of-thumb above which non-PCOS pathology — especially adrenal neoplasm — must be excluded [6, cohort; 8, regulatory].

### Moderately Elevated DHEA-S: CAH and PCOS

**Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)** — particularly 21-hydroxylase deficiency — leads to ACTH-driven overproduction of adrenal androgen precursors including DHEA-S, sometimes reaching 5–10× the upper reference limit. The 2018 Endocrine Society CAH Clinical Practice Guideline (PMID 30272171) identifies DHEA-S overproduction as a consequence of shunted steroidogenesis [9, regulatory].

In **polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)**, approximately 20–30% of affected women have elevated DHEA-S, reflecting an adrenal component to their hyperandrogenism. The prevalence is highest in non-classic PCOS phenotypes B and C [10, cohort]. Importantly, PCOS-associated DHEA-S elevation is typically modest — values are rarely extreme — and the hyperandrogenism is understood as a generalized exaggeration of adrenal steroidogenesis in response to ACTH, not an autonomous adrenal process.

### Hyperandrogenism Localization: Adrenal vs. Ovarian Source

DHEA-S is synthesized almost exclusively in the adrenal cortex. This biochemical specificity gives it a critical **localization function** in the evaluation of hyperandrogenism in women [8, regulatory; 11, regulatory]:

- **Elevated DHEA-S + elevated testosterone** → suggests mixed adrenal + possibly ovarian source
- **Elevated DHEA-S + normal testosterone** → points strongly to an isolated adrenal source
- **Normal DHEA-S + elevated testosterone** → points toward an ovarian source (ovarian hyperthecosis or an androgen-secreting ovarian tumor)

The 2025 Society for Endocrinology guideline on androgen excess in women recommends measuring DHEA-S in all patients with clinical or biochemical androgen excess as an adjunct to distinguish adrenal from ovarian etiology [8, regulatory]. The same guideline explicitly cautions against a fixed DHEA-S threshold above which investigations for non-PCOS pathology are automatically triggered, given the absence of robust calibration data for such a cutpoint [8, regulatory].

The 2010 French Endocrine Society recommendations (PMID 20096825) proposed that DHEA-S >600 µg/dL in women should prompt evaluation for androgen-secreting adrenal cortical adenoma — the marker's near-exclusive adrenal origin makes a highly elevated result a direct pointer to adrenal pathology [11, regulatory].

### Low DHEA-S: Adrenal Insufficiency, Hypopituitarism, and Glucocorticoid Suppression

Because DHEA-S production depends on intact zona reticularis function driven by ACTH, it falls in conditions that destroy or suppress the adrenal cortex:

- **Primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease):** DHEA-S is low or undetectable
- **Central (secondary/tertiary) adrenal insufficiency:** low ACTH → low DHEA-S
- **Hypopituitarism:** reduced ACTH → low adrenal stimulation → low DHEA-S
- **Exogenous glucocorticoid use:** suppresses the HPA axis, reducing ACTH and consequently DHEA-S

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (PMID 40909019) found that at a DHEA-S cutoff of 60–70 µg/dL, sensitivity and specificity for mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS) from adrenal incidentalomas were each approximately 82% [12, meta_analysis]. For the diagnosis of adrenal insufficiency, a DHEA-S above ~100 µg/dL (adjusted for age and sex) makes the diagnosis very unlikely, while a value below ~25 µg/dL supports it — with intermediate values requiring dynamic cosyntropin stimulation testing [12, meta_analysis].

DHEA-S is not a standalone diagnostic test for adrenal insufficiency. It is a screening adjunct: a normal age-adjusted value has high negative predictive value, but a low value requires confirmation with dynamic testing.

---

## No Single Universal Cutpoint

The central practical message of DHEA-S interpretation is that age-banding is non-negotiable. A value of 130 µg/dL is unremarkable in a 25-year-old man and frankly elevated in a 70-year-old woman. A value of 40 µg/dL is appropriate for a post-menopausal woman and represents profound adrenal androgen deficiency in a woman aged 25. Reference intervals should always be sourced from the reporting laboratory's population data or an age-matched normative dataset; the decade-banded pattern documented by Orentreich et al. [3, cohort] represents the foundational normative standard, but assay-specific ranges apply.
