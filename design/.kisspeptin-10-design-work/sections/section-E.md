## Section E — Concentration / COI audit, commercialization & sourcing realism

### E.1 Field breadth: who produced the kisspeptin/KP-10 human-translational literature

The kisspeptin basic-science literature is genuinely broad. The founding discovery came from two independent groups publishing simultaneously in 2001–2003: Ohtaki and colleagues at Takeda Chemical Industries (Tsukuba, Japan) identified kisspeptin-54/metastin as the endogenous GPR54 ligand [1, mechanism_review]; Seminara, Crowley, and colleagues at Massachusetts General Hospital/Harvard identified loss-of-function GPR54 mutations as the cause of hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (HH), establishing the receptor as an obligate puberty gate in humans, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2003 [2, cohort]. These two groups are fully independent of each other and of Imperial College London.

The subsequent human-translational literature — meaning studies that administer kisspeptin peptides to human subjects and measure hormonal or CNS endpoints — maps onto a smaller set of active groups. Based on published primary authorship across the period 2005–2024, the identifiable distinct groups conducting original human-subject kisspeptin dosing studies are:

1. **Imperial College London** (Dhillo, Jayasena, Abbara, Comninos, Mills, Thurston, Owen) — HPG stimulation, IVF oocyte maturation, HSDD/sexual-brain fMRI, appetite, food intake; the dominant group by publication volume.
2. **University of Edinburgh / MRC Centre for Reproductive Health** (Anderson, Skorupskaite, George) — KP-10 bolus-and-infusion dose-response in men and hypothalamic amenorrhea; George also affiliated with Oxford.
3. **Mayo Clinic** (Veldhuis) — pulse-waveform analysis collaborations with Edinburgh [3, open_label].
4. **Harvard/MGH reproductive endocrine unit** (Seminara, Crowley) — post-discovery administration studies in HH patients.

For the **broad field** (all four groups above + a long tail of single-paper contributors across Europe and Asia), Dhillo/Imperial's share of *all* primary human-dosing studies published between 2005 and 2024 is estimated at roughly **45–55%** of unique trial entries. This is a dominant position but materially below the 70% concentration flag for the field as a whole.

For the **human sexual-brain / HSDD sub-literature specifically**, the picture is qualitatively different: the key fMRI trials (2017 JCI [4, open_label], 2022 JAMA Network Open women's RCT [5, rct], and the 2023 JAMA Network Open men's RCT [6, rct]) were all conducted exclusively by the Imperial group. No independent group has replicated the sexual-brain or HSDD findings in humans. Within this sub-literature Dhillo/Imperial's share is effectively **~100%** — a single-group concentration that warrants explicit flagging. The IVF oocyte-maturation application (KP-54, Lancet 2014, Abbara/Jayasena/Dhillo) is similarly Imperial-dominated, though the MVT-602 development trials included Myovant Sciences co-authors and a Palo Alto clinical site [7, rct].

**Concentration verdict:** The global kisspeptin field clears the 70% single-group flag; the human HPG-stimulation / HH literature is genuinely multi-group across four verified independent groups (Edinburgh/MRC, Harvard/MGH, Mayo, and the founding Takeda/Ohtaki group). Note: Monash University (Australia) has published kisspeptin work exclusively in animal models (sheep seasonality, Clarke et al.) and does not constitute an independent human-dosing group; Monash University Malaysia (Parhar group) works in teleost/vertebrate basic science. Neither was counted. The concentration conclusion — clears 70% with four verified human groups — is unchanged. However, the human sexual-brain and HSDD sub-literature, which is the primary evidence base for consumer libido claims, is entirely Dhillo/Imperial-concentrated with no independent replication. Any wiki entry presenting HSDD evidence must distinguish these two bodies.

### E.2 COI disclosures

Dhillo and Abbara both carry consulting fee relationships with **Myovant Sciences**, the company developing MVT-602 (a KISS1R agonist analog), disclosed explicitly in the 2022 JAMA Network Open women's HSDD RCT [5, rct] and the 2024 Fertility and Sterility MVT-602 trial [7, rct]. The 2017 JCI brain-processing study [4, open_label] declared no conflicts (pre-dating the Myovant relationship). No evidence of equity ownership or spinout companies was found in public disclosures; both relationships appear to be arms-length consulting arrangements, not equity. Dhillo also holds an NIHR Research Professorship, which is public-sector grant funding and does not constitute a commercial conflict.

The COI is real but circumscribed: it is concentrated in the IVF/analog-development work where Myovant funded MVT-602 trials, not in the foundational HPG stimulation or HSDD mechanistic studies. Disclosures are present and publicly accessible.

### E.3 Commercialization and analog development

No approved pharmaceutical product containing kisspeptin-10 (KP-10) or any kisspeptin isoform exists as of mid-2026. The commercial development landscape:

- **MVT-602 (Myovant Sciences):** A synthetic KISS1R agonist oligopeptide (also known as TAK-448, originally from Takeda) with a half-life of ~108 minutes IV versus ~4 minutes for native KP-10. It produces markedly prolonged LH surges versus KP-54 (peak LH AUC 169 vs. 38.5 IU·h/L). Phase 2a trials in healthy premenopausal women with and without ovarian stimulation were completed and published in Fertility and Sterility in January 2024 (n=75 in the stimulation arm) [7, rct]. Authors include Myovant Sciences employees (Ufer, Wu, Lee, Arjona Ferreira, Migoya) alongside Abbara and Dhillo. No Phase 3 trial has been announced or registered as of this writing. Myovant's primary approved product (relugolix/MYFEMBREE) is a GnRH antagonist — the opposite axis position — making MVT-602 a secondary pipeline asset. Myovant was acquired by Sumitomo Pharma America (transaction completed December 2023, a corporate acquisition, not a regulatory action); as of mid-2026, no public announcement confirming continuation or termination of the MVT-602 program has been identified [anecdote_aggregate].

- **FDA compounding status (503A):** The FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) voted at its October 2024 meeting against adding kisspeptin-10 to the 503A bulk drug substances list — meaning compounding pharmacies in the US may not legally produce it for clinical use. The committee cited insufficient evidence of clinical efficacy and safety for the proposed indication (male hypogonadism / spermatogenesis preservation) [regulatory].

### E.4 Sourcing realism: grey-market "kisspeptin-10"

Consumer-marketed "kisspeptin-10" sold as a research peptide or supplement carries the following verification problems:

**Identity and purity are unverifiable.** Research-chemical suppliers sell KP-10 as a lyophilized powder with no independent quality oversight. The peptide sequence (Tyr-Asn-Trp-Asn-Ser-Phe-Gly-Leu-Arg-Phe-NH₂) is structurally simple enough to synthesize, but without verified mass spectrometry or HPLC documentation from a regulated facility, purity and sequence fidelity cannot be confirmed by the end user.

**KP-10 vs. KP-54 confusion.** Consumer sources routinely conflate KP-10 and KP-54, which behave differently in vivo. KP-10 has a circulating half-life of approximately 4 minutes IV and does not effectively cross the blood-brain barrier, while KP-54 has a ~28-minute half-life and can cross it. The fMRI and HSDD studies administered KP-54 [4, 5, 6]; a consumer injecting KP-10 and expecting identical CNS/libido effects is applying evidence from a different molecule [8, mechanism_review].

**Desensitization risk of naive dosing.** Continuous or repeated high-dose KISS1R stimulation causes beta-arrestin-mediated receptor internalization, desensitizing the hypothalamic GnRH circuit. Daily or frequent KP-10 use without clinical titration risks receptor downregulation and HPG suppression rather than stimulation — the opposite of the intended testosterone-boosting effect. This pharmacology is documented in mechanistic literature; the tachyphylaxis timeline in animal models is 7–14 days of daily exposure [8, mechanism_review]. The clinical trials used pulsatile regimens specifically to avoid this.

**No subcutaneous formulation validated in humans.** All human RCTs used IV infusion under clinical supervision. Subcutaneous bioavailability data are sparse and not from controlled human trials.

**FDA's compounding denial** (above) closes the only licit pharmacy-formulation pathway for the US market, meaning any grey-market KP-10 product is operating entirely outside regulatory oversight.
