## Section B — Reproductive / HPG-axis clinical evidence

### Overview and isoform note

> **Critical framing for this section:** The compound under research is **kisspeptin-10 (KP-10)**. However, the human clinical literature is dominated by **kisspeptin-54 (KP-54)**, a longer-half-life isoform (t½ ~27 min vs. ~4 min for KP-10) that binds the same KISS1R receptor. **KP-10's direct human clinical evidence is limited to a small number of physiology studies (n ≤ 10 per study: George 2011, George 2013, Jayasena 2015).** The substantial HPG-axis / IVF clinical evidence (Dhillo 2005/2007, all hypothalamic amenorrhea studies, both Abbara IVF trials) is for **KP-54, a related but pharmacokinetically distinct peptide.** KP-10 efficacy claims in those clinical contexts are **extrapolated from KP-54 data**, not directly established. This distinction is load-bearing and is called out explicitly at each subsection where KP-54 is the study agent.

---

### B.1 HPG-axis stimulation in healthy men (proof-of-concept)

**Dhillo et al. 2005** — the landmark first-in-human study of exogenous kisspeptin — enrolled 6 healthy adult men in a double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover RCT. Intravenous KP-54 infusion raised mean 90-min LH from 4.2 to 10.8 IU/L (p < 0.001), FSH from 3.2 to 3.9 IU/L (p < 0.001), and mean 180-min testosterone from 21.7 to 24.9 nmol/L (p < 0.001) versus saline. Estimated plasma half-life was ~27.6 minutes. This is proof-of-concept physiology; 6-man crossover RCT, healthy volunteers, KP-54 [1, rct].

**George et al. 2011** — the first dedicated KP-10 human dose-response study — enrolled 6 healthy adult men (IV bolus phase) and 4 (infusion phases). IV bolus KP-10 dose-dependently raised LH (peak at 1 µg/kg: ~4.1 → 12.4 IU/L). Continuous KP-10 infusion at 1.5 µg/kg·h increased LH pulse frequency from 0.7 to 1.0 pulses/h; high-dose infusion (4 µg/kg·h) raised testosterone from 16.6 to 24.0 nmol/L over 22.5 hours without tachyphylaxis at that duration. Findings confirm KP-10 is a potent LH secretagogue in men. Very small n; open-label infusion design; healthy men only [2, open_label].

**Jayasena et al. 2015** (PMID: 26089302) — a single-blinded crossover study in 10 healthy men compared IV infusions of KP-10, KP-54, and GnRH at three escalating doses. Both kisspeptin isoforms showed "broadly similar potencies" for LH stimulation despite KP-54's 37-fold higher plasma immunoreactivity at equivalent dose (reflecting KP-10's shorter half-life). GnRH was ~3-fold more potent than KP-10 and ~2-fold more potent than KP-54 at matched doses. This is the key head-to-head pharmacological comparison establishing KP-10 and KP-54 as effectively equipotent at the receptor. Small n (5 per group); single-blinded; healthy men; published in Human Reproduction [3, open_label].

### B.2 HPG-axis stimulation in healthy women (menstrual cycle effects)

**Dhillo et al. 2007** (PMID: 17635940) — the first human study of kisspeptin in women — used dose-escalation (n = 3–4 per dose) and a cycle-phase comparison (n = 8 healthy women with regular cycles). KP-54 IV produced dose-dependent LH increases (0.2–6.4 nmol/kg range). Crucially, the LH response was cycle-phase dependent: mean LH AUC increase was greatest in the preovulatory (late follicular) phase (peak ~20.64 IU/L) and near-absent in the early follicular phase (0.12 IU/L), demonstrating that oestrogen priming gates kisspeptin responsiveness. This cycle-phase gating is pharmacologically important for timing any therapeutic intervention. Small n; open-label clinical trial; healthy women with cycles; JCEM [4, open_label].

**Jayasena et al. 2013** (PMID: 24030945) — prospective, single-blinded, crossover controlled trial in 10 healthy women (5 per arm). Twice-daily subcutaneous KP-54 for 7 days during the mid-follicular phase (days 7–14) shortened mean cycle length from 28.6 to 26.8 days, advanced luteal-phase onset, and increased mean dominant follicle diameter on day 15 — without abolishing menstrual cyclicity. This established that intermittent pulsatile-ish dosing in healthy women does not suppress the axis, relevant to understanding tachyphylaxis patterns. Small n; JCEM [5, open_label].

### B.3 Hypothalamic amenorrhea / hypogonadotropic hypogonadism

**Jayasena et al. 2009** (PMID: 19820030) — in 10 women with hypothalamic amenorrhea (HA), twice-daily subcutaneous KP-54 acutely stimulated LH (day 1: +24.0 ± 3.5 IU/L) and FSH (+9.1 ± 2.5 IU/L), but chronic dosing caused complete tachyphylaxis by day 14 (LH response fell to 2.5 ± 2.2 IU/L). GnRH responsiveness was preserved, indicating KISS1R-level desensitization rather than downstream failure. This ruled out twice-daily dosing as a sustainable therapeutic schedule. Small n; JCEM [6, open_label].

**Jayasena et al. 2010** (PMID: 20980998) — in the same clinical setting, twice-weekly subcutaneous KP-54 for 8 weeks in women with HA produced only partial desensitization (in contrast to complete tachyphylaxis with twice-daily dosing). Reproductive hormone release was maintained across the 8-week period. This established the dosing-interval principle that less frequent administration preserves responsiveness — a critical finding for any future therapeutic schedule. Published in Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics [7, open_label].

**Jayasena et al. 2014** (PMID: 24517142) — single-blinded, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation trial in 5 women with functional HA. IV KP-54 infusion at escalating doses (0.01–1.0 nmol/kg/h) increased mean LH pulse number 3-fold (1.6 → 5.0 pulses/8 h; p < 0.01) and peak LH pulse secretory mass 6-fold (3.92 → 23.44 IU/L; p < 0.05) versus vehicle. Individual peak responses occurred at different doses in each patient. At the highest dose (1.0 nmol/kg/h), tachyphylaxis appeared after ~5 hours. This is the foundational proof-of-concept that kisspeptin can pharmacologically restore LH pulsatility in HA. Very small n; JCEM [8, open_label].

**George et al. 2013** (PMID: 23153270) — proof-of-concept clinical trial in 5 men with type 2 diabetes and mild biochemical hypogonadism (mean testosterone 8.5 nmol/L), compared with 7 age-matched healthy controls. IV bolus and infusion of KP-10 increased LH pulse frequency (0.6 → 0.9 pulses/h) and testosterone (8.5 → 11.4 nmol/L) in hypogonadal diabetic men. This demonstrates KP-10 specifically can engage the HPG axis in men with functional/central hypogonadism — not just healthy volunteers. Very small n; proof-of-concept; Clinical Endocrinology (Oxford) [9, open_label].

### B.4 IVF oocyte-maturation trigger (replacing hCG) — **KP-54 evidence; KP-10 not studied**

> **Note:** Both IVF trials below used **KP-54**, not KP-10. Application to KP-10 in an IVF context is not established; any extrapolation assumes receptor-level equivalence (supported by Jayasena 2015 pharmacology) but requires higher molar dose to compensate for KP-10's shorter half-life.

Mechanistic rationale: in IVF, the standard oocyte-maturation trigger (hCG) acts directly on the ovary and has a >7-day duration, causing excessive, prolonged LH-receptor stimulation that drives OHSS in susceptible women. Kisspeptin acts upstream at the hypothalamus to trigger an endogenous, self-limiting LH surge (~45 IU/L peak at ~5 h, returning to baseline within 12–14 h), potentially avoiding the sustained ovarian stimulation that precipitates OHSS.

**Abbara et al. 2015** (PMID: 26192876) — Phase 2, multi-dose, open-label, randomized (but unblinded) dose-finding trial at Hammersmith Hospital IVF Unit, London. 60 women aged 18–34 at high OHSS risk (antral follicle count >23 or AMH ≥40 pmol/L), assigned to single KP-54 injection at 3.2, 6.4, 9.6, or 12.8 nmol/kg as oocyte-maturation trigger. Oocyte maturation rate: 95% of women. Live birth rate per transfer (n = 51 transfers): 45% overall; 62% at 9.6 nmol/kg. Critically, **zero women developed moderate, severe, or critical OHSS** (only mild early OHSS in 3 women, mild late in 1). This is the pivotal dose-finding study establishing KP-54 as a safe and effective oocyte trigger in high-risk women. Open-label (unblinded), no direct hCG comparator arm in the same study; JCEM (Tier 1) [10, open_label].

**Abbara et al. 2017** (PMID: 28854728) — Phase 2 randomized, double-blind (patients, embryologists, and IVF clinicians blinded) placebo-controlled trial; 62 women at high OHSS risk (Hammersmith Hospital, August 2015–May 2016). Randomized 1:1 to single KP-54 injection vs. double injection (second dose at +10 h). **Primary endpoint (oocyte yield ≥60%):** double dosing improved the proportion achieving this threshold from 45% to 71% (p = 0.042). **Secondary endpoints:** live birth rate per cycle was 19.4% (single) vs. 39% (double) — a pre-specified secondary outcome; no increase in OHSS frequency. Human Reproduction (Tier 1) [11, rct].

### B.5 Diagnostic use: kisspeptin stimulation test for puberty

**Chan, Lippincott, Seminara et al. 2020** (PMID: 32232399) — prospective longitudinal cohort study at an academic medical center; 16 children (3 girls, 13 boys) with delayed or stalled puberty. A single kisspeptin administration test predicted ultimate pubertal outcome with 100% accuracy: all 8 children showing LH response ≥0.8 mIU/mL subsequently progressed through puberty; all 8 with LH response ≤0.4 mIU/mL reached age 18 without pubertal development (p = 0.0002). Performance exceeded GnRH-stimulated LH, inhibin B, and genetic testing. Proof-of-concept for kisspeptin as a functional probe of GnRH-neuron integrity. Small n = 16; single academic center; longitudinal cohort; JCEM (Tier 1) [12, cohort].

*The Chan 2020 study used kisspeptin-54 as the stimulation agent.

**Overall honest tier:** Kisspeptin has genuine human physiology data (multiple proof-of-concept studies) and two Phase 2 trials in the IVF setting showing safety + preliminary efficacy with KP-54. The HA/hypogonadism data are all small open-label studies (n ≤ 10) with no Phase 3 RCT yet. KP-10 has substantially less direct human evidence than KP-54; the 2015 Jayasena head-to-head supports similar potency per receptor occupancy but KP-10's shorter half-life means higher circulating dose is needed for the same duration of effect. No kisspeptin form is an approved drug anywhere.
