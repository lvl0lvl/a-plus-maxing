## Section D — Safety, dosing, route, PK, regulatory & monitoring

### D.1 Pharmacokinetics

Kisspeptin-10 (KP-10) has an exceptionally short plasma half-life in humans of approximately **3.8–4.1 minutes** following IV bolus administration, consistent across men and women in both follicular and preovulatory phases [2, open_label]. This rapid clearance is intrinsic to the unprotected decapeptide structure and distinguishes KP-10 from longer kisspeptin isoforms (KP-54 half-life ~0.5 h) and synthetic analogues such as MVT-602 (half-life ~1.5–2.2 h). The short half-life means biologically meaningful plasma levels persist for only 20–30 minutes after a bolus, requiring either repeated pulsatile administration or continuous infusion for sustained HPG-axis engagement.

### D.2 Dosing and route — academic trial data

All dosing figures below derive from published academic trials in healthy human volunteers or patients; grey-market numbers are not grounded here.

**IV bolus (men):** George et al. [1, open_label] administered IV boluses of 0.01–3.0 µg/kg to healthy men. Peak LH stimulation occurred at **1 µg/kg** (LH rose from 4.1 ± 0.4 to 12.4 ± 1.7 IU/L at 30 min, P < 0.001). The **3 µg/kg** dose produced a paradoxically blunted LH response compared with lower doses — an early signal of dose-dependent receptor desensitization [2, open_label] (see D.3).

**IV bolus and SC bolus (men and women):** Jayasena et al. [2, open_label] tested IV boluses of 0.3–10 nmol/kg (men) and 1–10 nmol/kg (women) alongside SC boluses of **2–32 nmol/kg** in women. Measured half-life was **3.8 ± 0.3 min** (men) and **4.1 ± 0.4 min** (follicular-phase women). IV bolus reliably raised LH and FSH in men at all tested doses and in women during the preovulatory (but not follicular) phase; SC bolus produced a qualitatively similar but attenuated peak due to slower absorption [2, open_label].

**IV infusion (men):** Continuous infusion at **1.5 µg/kg·h for 9 hours** raised LH pulse frequency from 0.7 ± 0.1 to 1.0 ± 0.2 pulses/h (P = 0.01) and increased testosterone (16.6 ± 2.4 to 24.0 ± 2.5 nmol/L, P < 0.001) at **4 µg/kg·h for 22.5 hours** in healthy men, with no desensitization observed at those infusion rates [1, open_label].

**IV infusion (women with hypothalamic amenorrhea, HA):** Jayasena et al. [3, open_label] infused kisspeptin-54 IV at 0.01–1.0 nmol/kg/h for 8–10 h in five women with HA. LH pulses rose ~3-fold and LH secretory mass ~6-fold vs vehicle. At the **highest dose (1.0 nmol/kg/h)**, LH peaked at 5 h and then declined ~50% — demonstrating dose-dependent desensitization during extended high-dose infusion [3, open_label].

**SC infusion (women):** Subcutaneous kisspeptin-54 infusion at 0.1–1.0 nmol/kg/h for 8 h in healthy women produced significant LH and FSH rises without tachyphylaxis at those doses and duration; baseline estradiol correlated with LH response magnitude [4, open_label].

**IVF trigger (KP-54, women):** A Phase 2 adaptive randomized trial (n = 60, high-OHSS-risk IVF patients) tested single SC kisspeptin-54 injections at **3.2–12.8 nmol/kg** to trigger oocyte maturation. This is KP-54, not KP-10, but it is the largest single human safety dataset for kisspeptin; the mechanistic pathway (KISS1R → GnRH → LH surge) is shared [5, rct].

**Grey-market conventions:** Vendor-circulating dose numbers and self-administration protocols exist (e.g., SC injection of "100–200 µg" KP-10 based on uncontrolled operator reports). These are tagged [anecdote_aggregate] and ground no number in this section. No vendor has published purity or pharmacokinetic characterization of their preparations [vendor_label].

### D.3 The desensitization/tachyphylaxis phenomenon — key dosing nuance

High or continuous kisspeptin exposure paradoxically suppresses the HPG axis through KISS1R downregulation, analogous to the GnRH agonist paradox. Evidence in humans:

- **Chronic SC pulsatile dosing (KP-54, HA women):** Twice-daily SC kisspeptin-54 (6.4 nmol/kg) for 2 weeks produced robust LH and FSH responses on Day 1 (peak LH 24.0 ± 3.5 IU/L) but markedly blunted responses by Day 14 (peak LH 2.5 ± 2.2 IU/L; FSH 0.5 ± 0.5 IU/L), indicating near-complete tachyphylaxis [6, open_label].
- **High-dose IV infusion (HA women):** At 1.0 nmol/kg/h IV, LH secretion began declining after 5 h despite sustained infusion; GnRH challenge confirmed preserved pituitary responsiveness, placing the desensitization at the KISS1R/kisspeptin-neuron level [3, open_label].
- **Dose dependence:** The 3 µg/kg IV bolus produced blunted LH compared to 1 µg/kg in healthy men, consistent with acute receptor saturation [1, open_label].

**Clinical implication:** Pulsatile, lower-dose administration is the pharmacologically rational approach; continuous high-dose exposure risks converting a stimulatory agent into an HPG-suppressive one. The therapeutic window is narrow and context-dependent (gonadal steroid milieu, dose interval, cumulative exposure). This is not a theoretical risk — it is directly demonstrated in human subjects [3, 6, open_label].

### D.4 Safety — academic trial data

Across published human trials in healthy volunteers and clinical populations (n totals in dozens, not hundreds):

- **No serious adverse events** have been attributed to KP-10 or KP-54 at study doses. Blood pressure, heart rate, and standard laboratory parameters remained stable in all reporting studies [1, 2, open_label].
- **IVF safety (KP-54, n = 60):** Only 3/60 women (5%) developed mild early OHSS; none developed moderate, severe, or critical OHSS. No participant required medical intervention for OHSS. Kisspeptin's ability to trigger oocyte maturation via the endogenous LH surge — rather than exogenous hCG — is hypothesized to reduce OHSS risk by limiting luteal-phase hCG [5, rct].
- **Immunogenicity:** No cases of immunogenic reactions reported in academic trials. The FDA's PCAC briefing noted theoretical immunogenicity risk via parenteral routes due to the peptide's structure, aggregation potential, and impurity profile in compounded preparations — a manufacturing-quality concern rather than an effect demonstrated in academic trials [regulatory].
- **Honest limits:** Studies are small (typically n = 4–60), conducted in controlled research settings, and of short duration (hours to weeks). There are no long-term or chronic-dosing safety data in humans. Endocrine effects beyond the study window are not characterized. Extrapolation to self-administration of uncharacterized grey-market preparations is not supported by the trial evidence.

**Contraindications:** Not formally established (no approved product). Hormone-sensitive conditions (hormone-receptor-positive malignancies, sex-steroid-dependent disorders, precocious puberty risk) are theoretical cautions given KP-10's capacity to raise LH, FSH, and downstream gonadal steroids. Pregnancy is a contraindication by mechanism (risk of LH surge triggering premature ovulation or luteal disruption).

### D.5 Regulatory status

**FDA:** Kisspeptin-10 is **not FDA-approved** for any indication. It is investigational only. No approved drug product containing kisspeptin-10 exists in the US, EU, or Japan. The peptide analog MVT-602 (a longer-half-life KISS1R agonist) has been evaluated in Phase 2 randomized trials for IVF triggering [regulatory]; no IND approval status could be independently confirmed from publicly fetchable FDA pages at the time of this research (FDA URL 404/503 errors; disclosed).

**503A/503B compounding:** The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) evaluated kisspeptin-10 for inclusion on the 503A Bulk Drug Substances List (October 29, 2024 meeting). The FDA recommended **not including** kisspeptin-10 on the 503A bulks list, citing: (1) insufficient physicochemical characterization in the public literature; (2) significant parenteral immunogenicity risk; (3) inadequate effectiveness evidence for secondary hypogonadism; and (4) existence of FDA-approved alternatives [regulatory]. As of the April 15, 2026 Category 2 revision (when 11 other peptides were removed from Category 2), kisspeptin-10's status remained in **Category 2** (bulk drug substances that may present significant safety risks, not eligible for 503A compounding) [regulatory]. The July 23–24, 2026 PCAC hearing covers BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-C, Semax, Epitalon, and Emideltide/DSIP — kisspeptin-10 is not on that agenda [regulatory]. Compounding pharmacies may not lawfully prepare kisspeptin-10 under current US rules pending any reclassification.

**WADA status:** Kisspeptin and its agonist analogues are **explicitly named** on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List under **S2.2.1 — Testosterone-Stimulating Peptides in Males**, alongside chorionic gonadotrophin (CG), luteinizing hormone (LH), and GnRH and its agonist analogues (buserelin, deslorelin, goserelin, histrelin, leuprorelin, nafarelin, triptorelin) [regulatory]. Prohibition is **in-competition and out-of-competition** (S2 is an "at all times" category). The prohibition in S2.2.1 applies specifically to males. The mechanism is direct: KP-10 activates KISS1R → GnRH release → pituitary LH/FSH secretion → gonadal testosterone synthesis, constituting an indirect androgen-pathway stimulator. Analytical detection methods for kisspeptin and analogues in serum and urine have been developed using LC-HRMS for doping control purposes, confirming assay feasibility [7, mechanism_review].

### D.6 Monitoring parameters (fillable framework)

For any research or supervised clinical protocol context:

| Parameter | Rationale | Suggested timing |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Serum LH, FSH | Direct pharmacodynamic targets; confirm axis response or detect desensitization | Baseline + 30–60 min post-dose; at interval during any multi-day protocol |
| Total and free testosterone (males) | Downstream endpoint; elevated confirms HPG engagement | Baseline; 24–48 h post-dose for bolus; end of infusion period |
| Estradiol (females) | Confirms ovarian response; required for IVF-trigger context | Baseline; pre-trigger + 12–36 h post |
| Vitals (BP, HR) | Monitored in all academic trials; stable to date | Each administration visit |
| Local injection-site assessment | Theoretical aggregation/immunogenicity concern at SC routes | At each SC administration |

**Stopping criteria (framework):** Unexpected LH/FSH suppression below baseline on repeat dosing (tachyphylaxis signal requiring dose interval reassessment); any systemic allergic or anaphylactoid response; unanticipated stimulation in hormone-sensitive conditions.
