# Rubric — sleep-efficiency (WEARABLE biomarker, standard mode)

**Judge threshold:** 92/100. route_fidelity + risk_floor_readiness = null (biomarker).
**WEARABLE-ADAPTED SHAPE:** `category: wearable`, `source: wearable`, device-derived (unit %). Section C is **device validity vs polysomnography (PSG)** (NOT assay standardization). The sharp tension: wearables estimate sleep/wake reasonably but **sleep STAGING is unreliable** — and sleep efficiency depends on accurate wake-detection, where wearables historically OVERESTIMATE sleep (low specificity for wake).

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — What Sleep Efficiency Is & What the Device Measures:** **Sleep efficiency (SE)** = **total sleep time (TST) ÷ time in bed (TIB) × 100%** — the percentage of time spent in bed that is actually spent asleep. Define the components: TIB (lights-out to final rising / the in-bed window), TST (TIB minus sleep-onset latency [SOL] minus wake-after-sleep-onset [WASO] minus any early-morning awakening). SE is a summary index of sleep CONTINUITY/consolidation — low SE means fragmented sleep or trouble falling/staying asleep. **What WEARABLES measure:** consumer devices estimate sleep/wake (and sleep stages) from **actigraphy/accelerometry (movement) + PPG-derived heart rate / HRV + sometimes temperature/respiration**, using proprietary classifiers; they compute SE from the device's estimated TST + its inferred in-bed/sleep window. **The polysomnography (PSG) gold standard** scores sleep from EEG/EOG/EMG (AASM rules); SE on PSG uses the true TST/TIB. Flag the key tension up front: SE depends on accurate WAKE detection, and movement-based wearables historically struggle to detect quiet wakefulness (lying still awake) → they tend to OVERESTIMATE sleep / SE. Distinguish SE from the related metrics (SOL, WASO, TST, sleep-stage %s).
- **B — Interpretation, Norms & The Personal-Baseline:** **POPULATION NORMS:** the conventional benchmark for "good" / "normal" sleep efficiency in healthy adults is **≥85%** (often cited ~85-95% for healthy sleepers); the National Sleep Foundation's sleep-quality recommendations endorse **≥85% SE as "appropriate"** for most age groups; SE **<85%** suggests impaired continuity (and is a diagnostic feature/criterion threshold in insomnia contexts). Note **SE DECLINES with age** (older adults have lower SE — more WASO) and varies with the in-bed window (spending excessive time in bed lowers SE — the basis of sleep-restriction therapy). Give the age context honestly. ALSO the personal-baseline-trend (the wearable use): a drop in one's own SE vs baseline flags acute disruptors (stress, alcohol, illness, environment). The interpretation caveat: SE is necessary-but-not-sufficient for good sleep (you can have high SE with insufficient TST, or with poor sleep architecture) — SE is one facet of sleep quality, read alongside TST + timing + how rested one feels. The "orthosomnia" caveat belongs more in D but mention SE-perfectionism isn't the goal.
- **C — Measurement & Device Validity vs PSG (load-bearing):** **PSG (the gold standard)** scores 30-second epochs as wake/N1/N2/N3/REM from EEG/EOG/EMG per AASM rules; SE = TST/TIB from that scoring. **Consumer wearables vs PSG (the load-bearing validity story):** modern accelerometry+PPG devices (Oura, Fitbit, WHOOP, Apple Watch, Garmin) detect **SLEEP vs WAKE with high SENSITIVITY (good at calling sleep "sleep," often >90%) but historically LOW SPECIFICITY for WAKE (poor at calling wake "wake" — they miss quiet wakefulness)** → the classic consequence is **OVERESTIMATION of TST + sleep efficiency and UNDERESTIMATION of WASO**, though newer PPG/HRV-enhanced multi-sensor models have improved wake detection + added stage estimation. **SLEEP STAGING is less accurate than sleep/wake** (4-class stage agreement with PSG is moderate, e.g. ~60-70%, best for deep/REM gross structure, worst for N1/N2 boundaries) — so device "deep sleep" / "REM" numbers are rougher than the SE summary. Cite NAMED independent validation studies + a systematic review/meta (epoch-by-epoch sensitivity/specificity, Bland-Altman bias for SE/TST/WASO). The **consistency-of-conditions + within-device-trend > absolute-cross-device-value** message; devices use proprietary algorithms (not interchangeable); the in-bed-window detection problem (auto vs manual bedtime affects SE). Most consumer sleep tracking is **WELLNESS, NOT a diagnostic test** (does not diagnose insomnia/apnea); disclose any vendor-funded-validation COI.
- **D — Determinants & Significance:** **What LOWERS sleep efficiency:** **insomnia** (the defining feature — difficulty initiating/maintaining sleep → high SOL/WASO → low SE); **aging** (more fragmentation); **excessive time in bed** (low sleep drive relative to opportunity — corrected by **sleep-restriction / stimulus-control therapy [CBT-I], which RAISES SE**); **ALCOHOL** (fragments the second half of the night → more WASO → lower SE despite faster onset); **caffeine, nicotine, evening stimulants**; **stress / anxiety / hyperarousal**; **sleep disorders — obstructive sleep apnea** (arousals fragment sleep), restless legs/PLMD, circadian misalignment (shift work, jet lag); **pain, nocturia, environment (noise/light/temperature), screens**; some medications. **What RAISES SE:** good sleep hygiene, CBT-I/sleep-restriction, adequate sleep pressure, regular schedule, dark/quiet/cool environment. **SIGNIFICANCE:** SE is a core **sleep-continuity / sleep-quality** index — a defining metric in **insomnia** assessment + the primary outcome that **CBT-I** improves; poor sleep continuity/short sleep is associated (observationally) with cardiometabolic + mental-health + mortality risk (cite cautiously — most epidemiology uses sleep DURATION or self-report, not wearable SE specifically; be honest that wearable-SE-to-hard-outcome evidence is limited). **LIMITATIONS (load-bearing):** consumer SE OVERESTIMATES true SE (the wake-detection problem) + sleep-staging is unreliable → don't over-interpret a device's exact SE/stage numbers; SE is necessary-but-not-sufficient (high SE ≠ enough sleep); the **"ORTHOSOMNIA" caveat — perfectionistic preoccupation with sleep-tracker data can itself worsen sleep/anxiety** (a documented phenomenon); wearable SE is a WELLNESS/trend metric, not a diagnostic test; interpret trends vs personal baseline alongside how rested one feels + TST + timing.
