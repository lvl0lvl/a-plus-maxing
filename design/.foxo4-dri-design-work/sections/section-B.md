## Section B — Senolytic efficacy evidence (preclinical)

### B.1 In vitro senolytic selectivity — the foundational mechanistic evidence

The Baar et al. 2017 Cell paper [1, in_vitro] established FOXO4 as a survival factor unique to senescent cells. In quiescent senescent cells, FOXO4 sequesters p53 in nuclear bodies, preventing its pro-apoptotic cytoplasmic role. The authors designed FOXO4-DRI as an all-D-amino-acid retro-inverso peptide to disrupt this FOXO4–p53 interaction.

In vitro, ionizing-radiation-induced senescent IMR90 human fetal lung fibroblasts were treated with 50 µM FOXO4-DRI alongside non-senescent (proliferating) IMR90 controls. FOXO4-DRI produced **11.73-fold greater reduction in viability of senescent versus proliferating IMR90 cells**, assessed by annexin V/PI staining — demonstrating that the selectivity mechanism requires the FOXO4–p53 axis that is active only in senescent cells [1, in_vitro]. Proliferating cells were largely spared at this concentration. A scrambled D-retro-inverso control peptide lacked this activity, confirming sequence specificity.

### B.2 The Baar 2017 Cell study — in vivo efficacy across three mouse models

All in vivo evidence for FOXO4-DRI as a senolytic comes from a single landmark publication: Baar et al. (2017), Cell, 169(1):132–147 [1, animal]. The last (senior) author is Peter L.J. de Keizer (Erasmus University Medical Center, Rotterdam), who is also the corresponding author; Jan H.J. Hoeijmakers is the penultimate author. **This study has not been independently replicated in its full in vivo design.** No human trial of FOXO4-DRI exists.

#### Model 1: Naturally aged mice

Male C57BL/6 mice aged approximately 24 months (physiologically old) received FOXO4-DRI at **5 mg/kg intraperitoneally (i.p.), 3 doses every other day** [1, animal]. Treatment produced:

- **Fur density restoration**: visibly improved coat density and reduced alopecia relative to vehicle-treated aged controls
- **Physical fitness**: increased spontaneous locomotor activity (open-field testing)
- **Renal function**: improvement in plasma urea/BUN and creatinine toward values more typical of younger animals, indicating reduced senescent-cell burden in kidney tissue (approximately 30% reduction in p16-positive cells in renal cortex)

The treated mice tolerated the dosing without overt toxicity.

#### Model 2: XpdTTD fast-aging (progeroid) mice

XpdTTD mice carry a homozygous Xpd^TTD/TTD mutation causing a nucleotide-excision repair deficiency that drives accelerated cellular senescence and premature aging features. These mice show early-onset alopecia, renal decline, and reduced fitness. Following FOXO4-DRI administration (5 mg/kg **intravenously (i.v.)**, 3 doses every other day — distinct from the i.p. route used in the aged-mouse arm), the XpdTTD mice demonstrated [1, animal]:

- **Fur regrowth**: marked recovery of coat density, one of the most visually striking results in the paper
- **Improved renal markers**: plasma creatinine and urea toward younger ranges
- **Restored fitness**: increased locomotor activity

The progeroid model is considered an accelerated-senescence context, not a standard aging model, so these findings are not necessarily predictive of outcomes in normally aged animals or in humans.

#### Model 3: Doxorubicin-induced chemotoxicity mice

Young male C57BL/6 mice received doxorubicin chemotherapy (2× i.p. at 10 mg/kg), which drives acute senescent-cell accumulation (a known mechanism of "chemo-senescence"). FOXO4-DRI was administered **intravenously (i.v.) at 5 mg/kg, 3 doses every other day** following doxorubicin exposure [1, animal]:

- Reduced the senescent-cell burden induced by doxorubicin
- Restored physical fitness and body weight
- Was well tolerated

This model is relevant to chemotherapy-induced aging phenotypes, a distinct context from natural aging.

**Group sizes note:** Per-group n values reported in Baar et al. (Cell, 2017) figure captions: n=7–8 mice per treatment group for the XpdTTD cohorts (Fig. 6K); specific n values for the aged-mouse and doxorubicin cohorts are reported in the primary paper's figure captions and supplementary material [1, animal].

### B.3 Independent replication and extension studies

Several groups, independent of the de Keizer/Erasmus Rotterdam group, have tested FOXO4-DRI in different contexts. The body of evidence is primarily in vitro and in single-disease rodent models; no study has replicated the full Baar 2017 protocol in naturally aged mice.

#### B.3.1 Leydig cell senescence and testosterone — Zhang et al. 2020 [2, animal]

Zhang C, Xie Y, Chen H, et al. (Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou, China) — independent of de Keizer — administered FOXO4-DRI (5 mg/kg i.p., every other day, three doses) to naturally aged male C57BL/6 mice (n = 6 per treated group, ages 20–24 months; young controls 3 months, n = 10). Outcomes: serum testosterone increased significantly; testicular p53, p21, and p16 protein levels decreased; interstitial SA-β-galactosidase activity was reduced; SASP factors IL-1β, IL-6, and TGF-β fell. This is a genuine independent in vivo replication in an aged male cohort, though limited to the gonadal compartment [2, animal]. Published in Aging (Albany NY), PMID 31959736.

#### B.3.2 Spermatogenesis — Li et al. 2024 [3, animal]

Li Y, Zhang C, Cheng H, et al. (Sun Yat-sen University) extended the Leydig cell work in a 2024 study in Experimental Gerontology (PMID 39025385; DOI 10.1016/j.exger.2024.112522). Aged male mice treated with FOXO4-DRI exhibited improved sperm quality and spermatogenesis, attributed to reduced SASP secretion from senescent Leydig cells reducing paracrine suppression of germ cell proliferation. This group overlaps substantially with the Zhang 2020 group (same institution, some shared authors) and should not be treated as fully independent replication; it is best described as an extension in the same domain [3, animal].

#### B.3.3 Human chondrocytes (in vitro, osteoarthritis context) — Huang et al. 2021 [4, in_vitro]

Huang Y, He Y, Makarcyzk MJ, Lin H (University of Pittsburgh / Central South University) — independent of de Keizer — treated in vitro expanded human articular chondrocytes from 8 non-osteoarthritic donors (ages 15–73) with 25 µM FOXO4-DRI for 5 days [4, in_vitro]. Extensively expanded (PDL9; senescence-enriched) chondrocytes showed significant cell number reduction and lower senescence markers (SA-β-gal, p16, p21). Minimally expanded (PDL3) chondrocytes were not significantly affected, confirming senolytic selectivity in a human primary cell type. Published in Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology (PMID 33996787). This is an in vitro study in human cells; it does not constitute a clinical finding.

#### B.3.4 Pulmonary fibrosis (mouse, bleomycin model) — Han et al. 2022 [5, animal]

Han X, Yuan T, Zhang J, et al. (Institute of Radiation Medicine, Peking Union Medical College — independent of de Keizer) tested FOXO4-DRI (5 mg/kg i.p., on days 14, 16, and 18 post-bleomycin) in male C57BL/6J mice aged 6–8 weeks (n = 5 per group), a model of bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis [5, animal]. FOXO4-DRI reduced senescent cell and SASP marker burden, decreased collagen deposition and myofibroblast numbers, and attenuated bleomycin-induced morphological damage. Published in Journal of Cellular and Molecular Medicine (PMID 35510614). Note: this is a young-animal, disease-model study; it does not reflect natural aging.

#### B.3.5 Endothelial cell senescence — Hu et al. 2025/2026 [6, animal]

Hu Z, Li F, Hu C, et al. (Wenzhou Medical University — independent of de Keizer) [6, animal]. In vitro: 50 µM FOXO4-DRI applied to oxygen-glucose-deprived HUVECs reduced senescence markers (p21, p16, γ-H2AX) and improved tube formation and migration. In vivo: naturally aged male and female mice (17 months, n ≥ 4–6 per group) and a D-galactose progeroid model received 5 mg/kg i.p. every 2 days for 1 month. Outcomes: reduced aortic p21/p16, decreased SA-β-gal activity, improved pulse-wave velocity (lower arterial stiffness), reduced ROS. Published in Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology (DOI 10.3389/fbioe.2025.1729166; PMID 41625068; January 2026). This is an independent study with both male and female aged animals — a meaningful extension. However, Frontiers is a lower-tier venue than Cell or Aging Cell.

#### B.3.6 Mechanistic extension: p53 transactivation domain — Bourgeois et al. 2025 [7, in_vitro]

Bourgeois B, Spreitzer E, Platero-Rochart D, et al. (Medical University of Graz / Utrecht, with de Keizer as co-author — NOT independent) published structural mechanistic work in Nature Communications (PMID 40593617; DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-60844-9) showing that FOXO4-DRI binds the disordered p53 transactivation domain 2 (p53TAD2) via synergistic folding, and that the HIV-TAT cell-penetrating sequence contributes directly to binding rather than being inert cargo. This is mechanism-only work, not a new efficacy study, and involves the de Keizer group [7, in_vitro].

### B.4 The critical honest distinction: no human trial exists

**All efficacy data for FOXO4-DRI is preclinical — mouse models and cell culture.** There is no published human clinical trial (Phase 1, 2, or 3) of FOXO4-DRI, no published human pharmacokinetic data, and no human safety database beyond anecdotal self-experimentation reports in online communities (not cited here as evidence). Cleara Biotech was founded in 2018 to develop FOXO4-DRI-class compounds, but as of mid-2026, no registered human trial results are in the published literature. The jump from mouse data to human efficacy is not established and involves species-specific differences in FOXO4 biology, senescent cell burden, tissue distribution, and peptide pharmacokinetics that remain untested.
