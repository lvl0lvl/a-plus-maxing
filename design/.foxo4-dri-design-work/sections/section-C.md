## Section C — Senescence-clearance paradigm, longevity context & senolytic landscape

### C.1 The senescence-clearance rationale

Cellular senescence is a state in which damaged or stressed cells permanently exit the cell cycle yet resist apoptosis. Rather than being inert, senescent cells secrete a pro-inflammatory mix of cytokines, proteases, and growth factors collectively called the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP). SASP drives chronic low-grade inflammation, disrupts tissue architecture, and is thought to propagate senescence in neighbouring cells [1, mechanism_review]. The hypothesis that pharmacologically removing these cells could reverse or slow aging-related dysfunction rests on two foundational genetic ablation experiments in mice.

**INK-ATTAC, 2011 — health-associated disorders.** Baker, van Deursen, and colleagues engineered the INK-ATTAC transgenic mouse, in which cells expressing the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p16^Ink4a^ (a canonical marker of cellular senescence) could be selectively destroyed by administering the small molecule AP20187, which activates a caspase-8 fusion protein under the p16 promoter. In male and female mice on a BubR1 progeroid background (a model of accelerated aging), continuous clearance beginning at 3 months of age "delayed the onset of age-related phenotypes" in adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and eye — and even late-life clearance attenuated the progression of established disorders. This was the first demonstration that senescent cells are causally linked to aging-associated tissue dysfunction in vivo, not merely coincident with it (INK-ATTAC progeroid mice, n = 21–32/group) [2, animal] [1, mechanism_review].

**INK-ATTAC, 2016 — lifespan extension in naturally aged mice.** A follow-up study used the same INK-ATTAC system in wild-type mice on two distinct genetic backgrounds (C57BL/6 and F1 hybrid), beginning clearance at one year of age (midlife). Elimination of naturally occurring p16^Ink4a^-positive senescent cells "extended median lifespan in both male and female mice" while also delaying spontaneous tumorigenesis and preserving renal, cardiac, and adipose function. This was the first demonstration that senescent-cell clearance can extend lifespan — not only healthspan — in non-progeroid animals (wild-type C57BL/6 and F1 hybrid mice; median lifespan increase ~25% in males) [3, animal].

Together, these two studies established the genetic proof-of-concept that motivated the search for pharmacological senolytics: small molecules, peptides, or natural compounds capable of mimicking AP20187-induced clearance without genetic engineering.

### C.2 The senolytic landscape and FOXO4-DRI's position within it

**Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q)** were the first pharmacological senolytics identified. Dasatinib (a tyrosine-kinase inhibitor originally approved for chronic myeloid leukemia) and quercetin (a dietary flavonoid) were independently discovered as senolytics through a bioinformatics screen of SCAPs (senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways); the combination was more effective than either alone across multiple cell types [1, mechanism_review]. Critically, D+Q are **the most translationally advanced senolytics** with published human data. Hickson and colleagues at Mayo Clinic conducted an open-label Phase 1 pilot in nine adults with diabetic kidney disease (mean age 68.7 years): three days of oral dasatinib (100 mg/day) plus quercetin (1,000 mg/day) followed by an 11-day washout reduced senescent-cell burden in adipose tissue and skin biopsies, lowered circulating SASP factors, and reduced macrophage infiltration. The "hit-and-run" intermittent dosing paradigm — tolerated without serious adverse events in this small sample — is now the model for human senolytic trials (humans, n = 9, diabetic kidney disease, mean age 68.7 yr) [4, open_label].

**Fisetin** is a naturally occurring flavone (found in strawberries, apples, and other foods) that shows senolytic activity with a particularly favourable profile in endothelial cells (Zhu et al. 2017; in vitro — human HUVECs, fibroblasts, and preadipocytes) [5, in_vitro]. Human clinical investigation is in early stages — a Phase 2 randomised placebo-controlled trial (TROFFi study, NCT05595499) is underway in breast cancer survivors to test fisetin's effect on physical function [10, regulatory], and a multicenter adaptive Phase 2 trial (STOP-Sepsis, NCT05758246) is randomising elderly patients with sepsis [11, regulatory]. Neither has yet published efficacy readouts in the peer-reviewed literature, meaning fisetin's human senolytic activity is inferred from mechanism and early Phase 1/pilot signals rather than demonstrated in a completed controlled trial. Fisetin is ahead of FOXO4-DRI in clinical pipeline terms, but only marginally so.

**Navitoclax / ABT-263** targets BCL-2, BCL-XL, and BCL-W, which senescent cells upregulate to evade their own pro-apoptotic microenvironment. In mice, oral ABT-263 rejuvenated aged haematopoietic stem cells and improved muscle stem cell function by depleting senescent cells in the bone marrow niche (C57BL/6 mice, aged 24–26 months, and irradiated young mice, n = 6–12/group) [6, animal]. A separate cell-based study confirmed navitoclax as a senolytic across preadipocytes, endothelial cells, and fibroblasts [7, in_vitro]. However, navitoclax causes dose-limiting thrombocytopenia in humans (platelet BCL-XL dependence), limiting clinical advancement as a senolytic in healthy or aging populations. No human aging trial of navitoclax as a senolytic has been completed. Second-generation BCL-XL inhibitors (A1331852, A1155463) with reduced platelet toxicity were identified in the same screen that characterised fisetin [5, in_vitro].

**FOXO4-DRI** operates via a distinct mechanism: it is a D-amino-acid retro-inverso peptide designed to competitively disrupt the intranuclear interaction between FOXO4 and p53. Senescent cells rely on nuclear FOXO4–p53 co-localisation to maintain p53-driven survival signals; the peptide causes nuclear p53 exclusion and cell-intrinsic apoptosis selectively in senescent (not proliferating) cells. In Baar et al. 2017 (Cell) — the sole peer-reviewed mechanistic study of the compound — three mouse models were used: XpdTTD/TTD fast-aging mice, p16∷3MR senescence-reporter mice, and naturally aged wild-type mice. Intraperitoneal injection 3× per week improved fitness, fur density, and renal function in accelerated-aging mice, and reduced frailty markers in naturally aged mice (XpdTTD/TTD, p16∷3MR, and wild-type naturally aged C57BL/6 mice; n = 8–15/group) [8, animal]. There is **no lifespan endpoint** from this or any subsequent peer-reviewed study — Baar 2017 assessed healthspan-adjacent markers only.

### C.3 Translation gap: where FOXO4-DRI stands

The translation gap for FOXO4-DRI is substantial. All evidence is from rodent models; there is no registered human clinical trial and no published human pharmacokinetics, safety, or efficacy data. The compound's D-amino-acid backbone improves proteolytic resistance but has not been characterised pharmacokinetically in humans, and the optimal dosing interval, systemic exposure, and tissue distribution in a human context remain unknown.

By contrast, D+Q have completed multiple human pilots demonstrating measurable reduction in senescent-cell biomarkers in tissue biopsies [4, open_label]. Fisetin has at minimum entered randomised placebo-controlled human trials [10, regulatory] [11, regulatory]. Navitoclax, while hampered by thrombocytopenia, has extensive human pharmacology data from its oncology programme.

**No claim that FOXO4-DRI produces senolytic effects or any health benefit in humans is supported by evidence.** The Kirkland/Tchkonia 2020 review of senolytics explicitly places the field in the early-translation phase and cautions that "it is too early for senolytics to be used outside of clinical trials" — a statement that applies with additional force to FOXO4-DRI, where not even Phase 1 human safety data exist [9, mechanism_review].

The broader longevity hypothesis — that periodic senescent-cell clearance will translate to lifespan or healthspan benefits in humans at scale — remains preclinical. Even the mouse lifespan-extension data (Baker 2016) use supraphysiological genetic ablation that no current pharmacological senolytic replicates precisely.
