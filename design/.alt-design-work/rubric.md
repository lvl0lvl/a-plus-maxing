# Rubric — alt (biomarker, standard mode)

## Section plan (4 sections, paired retrieval+judge)
- **A — Physiology & What It Measures:** ALT (alanine aminotransferase, formerly SGPT) = a cytosolic enzyme catalyzing the transfer of an amino group between alanine and α-ketoglutarate (→ pyruvate + glutamate, linking amino-acid + carbohydrate metabolism); concentrated predominantly in **hepatocytes** (highest tissue concentration in liver) with smaller amounts in kidney/muscle — so serum ALT is the **most liver-SPECIFIC of the routine aminotransferases**; released into blood when hepatocyte membranes are damaged (a marker of hepatocellular injury, NOT liver function per se); two isoforms (ALT1/ALT2); requires pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (vitamin B6) as cofactor (B6 deficiency can lower measured ALT); plasma half-life ~47 h; the AST/ALT relationship (the De Ritis ratio) introduced.
- **B — Reference Ranges, Units & Thresholds (load-bearing — the "healthy" upper limit debate):** conventional adult reference upper limits (~**up to ~40–55 U/L**, lab/sex-dependent — men higher than women); units U/L (IU/L); **the load-bearing controversy: the standard ULN is too HIGH** — population-based "healthy" thresholds (excluding metabolic/viral/alcohol risk) are LOWER (~**~30 U/L men / ~19–25 U/L women**, e.g. the Prati 2002 / AASLD-updated thresholds) — a "normal" ALT by old criteria can miss MASLD; the implication for screening; age/sex/BMI dependence; mild vs marked elevation interpretation (×ULN bands); ALT < AST patterns.
- **C — Measurement & Standardization:** the **IFCC reference measurement procedure** for ALT (37°C, with/without pyridoxal-5'-phosphate — the P5P supplementation affects results, a standardization issue); automated enzymatic spectrophotometric assay (NADH-coupled, lactate dehydrogenase indicator reaction); inter-method/inter-lab variation + the harmonization status; the P5P (B6) cofactor effect on measured activity (assays without P5P under-read in B6-deficient samples); sample stability (relatively stable); hemolysis interference (modest for ALT); the units/reference-method dependence; why reference intervals are method- and population-specific.
- **D — Determinants & Clinical Significance:** **what RAISES ALT** — the differential by magnitude: **mild-moderate chronic elevation** most commonly **MASLD/NAFLD** (the leading cause in developed populations), alcohol-related liver disease (though AST>ALT pattern), chronic viral hepatitis (B/C), drug-induced (statins, acetaminophen, many), hemochromatosis, autoimmune/Wilson/α1-antitrypsin, celiac, thyroid; **marked (>10–25×ULN)** acute hepatocellular injury — acute viral hepatitis, ischemic hepatitis ("shock liver"), acetaminophen/toxin, acute biliary obstruction; **non-hepatic** — muscle injury/strenuous exercise/rhabdomyolysis (ALT is in muscle too), so correlate with CK; **the AST:ALT (De Ritis) ratio** — >2 suggests alcoholic liver disease, <1 typical of MASLD/viral, >1 in cirrhosis/fibrosis progression; **what LOWERS ALT** — vitamin B6 deficiency (assay-dependent), chronic kidney disease/dialysis, low ALT associates with frailty/sarcopenia/aging/mortality in elderly (cite cohorts cautiously); ALT as a screening + monitoring marker (hepatotoxic drug monitoring); the association of even high-normal ALT with metabolic syndrome + incident T2D + cardiovascular/liver outcomes (cite named cohorts/meta); limitations — ALT is a marker of hepatocellular INJURY not function (normal ALT does not exclude significant liver disease incl. cirrhosis), non-specific, the ULN problem, muscle source.
