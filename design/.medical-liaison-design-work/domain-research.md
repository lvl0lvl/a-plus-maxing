# medical-liaison Domain Research (deep mode)

## Executive Summary

The medical-liaison (Role 7) is a **non-prescribing coordination and adjudication** specialist: it collates compounds tagged `risk_tier: medium+` into a doctor-visit queue, tracks contraindications and drug/supplement interactions, assembles the operator's first-MD-visit handout, and serves as the canonical `BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH` adjudicator that sets `severity_final` on HIGH/MEDIUM safety findings. This research grounds that design in five evidence threads. **(1) Medication reconciliation**: the receiving clinician works under Joint Commission NPSG.03.06.01, whose accurate-record specification is five fields (name, dose, frequency, route, purpose) with vitamins/supplements explicitly in scope [1]; recall-only lists are wrong ~87% of the time [6], roughly a third of patients carry a transition error with omission dominant [8], and supplement non-disclosure runs ~67% [10]. **(2) Interaction checking**: the major databases disagree massively — only ~11–18% of interaction pairs appear in all of three resources and 78% appear in only one [12]; LLMs are the weakest layer (best open-source model 38.12% on RxSafeBench's interaction task [20], verified); and clinicians override ~90% of DDI alerts [23], so volume without ranking is worthless. **(3) Visit prep**: an SBAR-shaped, ranked, one-page handout with mandatory OTC/supplement rows and an "indication" field is the high-yield structure [27][32][36][39]. **(4) Override documentation**: the Against-Medical-Advice / informed-refusal / shared-decision-making literature converges on a single anti-rubber-stamp principle — the *narrative*, not the signature, is the protection; a valid override must capture the specific caution, communicated risks (including risks-of-proceeding), the operator's stated reason, and a tamper-evident timestamp, with the required rigor scaling to severity (Appelbaum–Grisso sliding scale) [45][50][54][57]. **(5) LLM safety**: authority/educational framing is the dominant medical-LLM bypass (45.0%/83.3% in Ekram 2026 [60]); models concede correct positions ~98% of the time under user pushback [62] and assert false medical equivalences at 94–100% [63]; benchmark-passing models still fail injection at 80–100% [64][65]; and a finding-raiser that finalizes its own verdict is structurally biased (self-preference ~10–25% [66]) — which is exactly why the adjudicator role exists separately from the safety-reviewer. A correction surfaced during verification: the project's "81.8% authority-impersonation" figure is a misattribution of a JBDistill benchmark number [61]; the corrected medical figures are 45.0%/83.3% [60].

## Introduction

**Question.** What evidence base should ground the design of an LLM medical-liaison agent for a single-operator personal-health system, where the operator is a ~20-year-trained athlete recovering from a January-2026 health issue with a first primary-care visit planned for July 2026, and the agent never prescribes — it collates, coordinates, and adjudicates?

**Scope.** Five threads: medication reconciliation standards; drug–drug and drug–supplement interaction checking; doctor-visit preparation and deprescribing-review frameworks; documentation of decisions made against clinical advice; and LLM-safety for a non-prescribing adjudication role. Out of scope: compound-specific pharmacology (owned by peptide/supplement/endocrine specialists), wiki entry authoring (medical-liaison `target_class: none`, collates only), and clinical prescribing judgment (the agent has none).

**Methodology.** Deep-mode pipeline: five parallel retrieval threads (Tavily advanced + primary-source extraction), each self-scored against a 10-dimension rubric (threshold 99/100) and required to flag any claim not traceable to a primary/authoritative source. The orchestrator personally verified the two highest-stakes numbers (RxSafeBench 38.12%; JBDistill 81.8%) by direct arXiv HTML reads (PF-S3-01 guard against self-attestation). 53 unique sources after dedup, exceeding the deep-mode floor of 25.

**Assumptions.** (a) The operator is the single user AND the primary adversary (operator-as-A3 per Role 1 §11.2 AP8 + EC-9): authority/educational framing originates from the operator innocently. (b) The agent's outputs are the *last* safety check before the operator acts on themselves — there is no downstream clinician to catch an omission until the July-2026 visit. (c) Population-match caveat: most reconciliation/interaction prevalence data is drawn from older-adult or inpatient populations; base rates do not transfer directly to a younger athlete, but the *mechanisms* and *standards* do.

## Section A — Medication Reconciliation Standards

### Finding A1 — NPSG.03.06.01 defines the five-field record the receiving clinician judges against, and pulls supplements into scope

The Joint Commission requirement governing medication accuracy is **National Patient Safety Goal NPSG.03.06.01**, "Maintain and communicate accurate patient medication information," under Goal 3 ("Use medicines safely"), active in the standards effective January 2026 [1][2]. It is a set of auditable Elements of Performance: obtain/update the patient's current medications; **reconcile** them against new orders to resolve duplications, omissions, interactions, and dosing conflicts; give the patient **written** information on the medications they should be taking; and tell the patient to carry an up-to-date list to every encounter [1][3]. The standard names the fields a record must carry to count as adequate — **medication name, dose, frequency, route, and purpose** — purpose included because a drug whose indication is unknown cannot be reconciled [1][3]. Critically, NPSG.03.06.01 directs clinicians to capture **non-prescription medications, vitamins, and dietary supplements**, not just prescriptions [1][3]. The verbatim EP-numbered text lives in the paywalled Comprehensive Accreditation Manual; the five-field requirement and four actions are consistent across the official plain-language goal and secondary EP transcriptions [1][2][3], so they are treated as authoritative with that provenance noted.

**Design implication:** Structure every handout row around the five Joint Commission fields — name, dose, frequency, route, purpose — and treat "purpose" and "supplements/OTC/vitamins" as mandatory rows, because the accreditation standard the receiving clinician works under defines an accurate record exactly that way.

### Finding A2 — The reference method is a Best Possible Medication History (interview + second source); recall alone is ~87% wrong

NPSG.03.06.01 implicitly demands a **Best Possible Medication History (BPMH)** — the ISMP Canada term for a history built from (1) a systematic patient/family interview **and** (2) verification against at least one other reliable source (pharmacy refill record, prior discharge list, the pill bottles), capturing name/dose/route/frequency for every agent, prescribed and non-prescribed [4]. A BPMH is explicitly more comprehensive than a "routine" history relying on a single source (often recall) [4], and is appropriately taken by physicians, nurses, pharmacists, and trained pharmacy technicians [4]. The quantitative case for the second source is the **brown-bag review**: AHRQ documents that patients reconstructing a list from memory get it wrong ~87% of the time, versus >92% accuracy with the physical containers present [6]; brown-bag reviews also surface non-adherence a prescription record cannot (one study: 58% of participants non-adherent to ≥1 medication) [6]. The 87%/92% figures are AHRQ-attributed secondary (underlying primary n not stated in the retrieved tool documentation) and are flagged as such [6].

**Design implication:** Run a BPMH-shaped intake — never accept a single recalled list; actively prompt the operator to confirm each entry against a second source and capture actual-taking versus prescribed, because recall-only lists carry ~87% error.

### Finding A3 — One-third of patients carry a transition error, 85% originating in the history, omission dominant — MATCH/MARQUIS/Cornish

Three primary bodies quantify the catch rate. The **Tam/Cornish systematic review (CMAJ 2005; 22 studies, 3,755 patients)** found medication-history errors in up to 67% of cases, with 10–61% of patients having ≥1 omission and 11–59% of errors judged clinically important [7]. **MATCH (Gleason 2010; 651 inpatients, 5,701 medications)** found 309 confirmed order errors affecting **35.9% of patients**, with **85% originating in the medication history itself** and nearly half being omissions; 52.4% were rated as needing increased monitoring and 11.7% as potentially harmful [8]. MATCH explicitly **excluded OTC and herbals** [8], so its 35.9% understates total discrepancies. **MARQUIS2 (Schnipper 2021; 18 hospitals)** drove unintentional discrepancies from 2.85 to 0.98 per patient (adjusted IRR 0.95, 95% CI 0.93–0.97), with a BPMH in the ED and reconciliation at admission and discharge as the active components [9][5].

**Design implication:** Bias the intake toward catching omissions — exhaustively elicit every agent including intermittent/OTC/PRN/supplement items, since ~one-third of patients carry a transition error, 85% originate in the history, and omission is the leading subtype.

### Finding A4 — Supplement non-disclosure (~67%) is the gap a prescription-only record cannot close

The largest population source (2002 NHIS, 5,456 supplement users) found **only ~33% told their provider about supplement use — ~67% did not disclose** [10]; among people with chronic conditions, **<51% disclosed** [11]. Awareness is setting-dependent (primary-care physicians aware in 70.1% of cases vs hospital physicians 34.1%) and lower among men, younger adults, and lighter users of care [10][11]. The mechanism is bidirectional: patients believe supplements are benign and clinicians infrequently ask [10][11]. NPSG.03.06.01's supplements-in-scope clause [1] governs what the clinician should record, not whether the patient volunteers — so passive intake reproduces the ~67% omission. Figures are 2002–2009-era and vary 30–67% by setting; reported as a range [10][11].

**Design implication:** Treat supplements/OTC as an *actively elicited, prompted* category with named exemplars (multivitamin, fish oil, vitamin D, creatine, protein, melatonin, NSAIDs, herbal products) — never a passive "anything else?" — because passive prompting silently omits the category clinicians are least aware of and where the agent adds the most value.

## Section B — Drug–Drug and Drug–Supplement Interaction Checking

### Finding B1 — Major interaction databases disagree massively on existence and moderately on severity

A 2022 three-resource collation (BNF, ANSM Thesaurus, Micromedex) found 121,351 unique interaction pairs, of which only 6,970 appeared in all three — **11–18% of each list — and 78.04% appeared in only one resource** [12]; on severity, Micromedex flagged 81.51% of shared interactions as critical vs BNF's 43.39% [12]. A 2024 PPI study across Micromedex, Lexicomp, Epocrates, Drugs.com, and INTERCheck found only 6.0% of identified interactions flagged by all five, with inter-checker agreement on existence at Gwet AC1 0.23–0.27 (poor) and on severe-DDI severity 0.30–0.32 (poor) [13]. An antidote analysis found Micromedex-vs-WebMD agreement *worse than chance* (kappa −0.126, p=0.008) [14]. Lexicomp-vs-Micromedex concordance is better but still moderate (Cohen's kappa ≈0.546, secondary citation) [15]. ARV/antimicrobial pairs produced three different risk pictures across Liverpool/Micromedex/Drugs.com [16].

**Design implication:** Treat any single interaction database as a noisy signal, not ground truth — surface disagreements explicitly ("Micromedex rates this Major; Drugs.com does not list it") and never silently collapse conflicting sources into one verdict.

### Finding B2 — Supplement–drug interactions are common but mostly low-severity; the high-severity mechanisms are few and known

More than two-thirds of older adults concurrently use prescriptions with OTC/supplements [17], and concurrent prescription-plus-herbal use estimates range 5.3–88.3% by definition [17]. The cleanest interaction-prevalence study (Agbabiaka 2018; 149 adults ≥65) found 33.6% used herbal/dietary products alongside prescriptions and **32.6% of concurrent users faced a *potential* interaction**, but of 55 catalogued combinations, 50.9% had no clinical significance, 38.2% "doubts about outcome," and only ~11% were potentially hazardous or a significant hazard [18]. US data show interacting medication-plus-supplement use roughly doubled since 2005–06, leaving ~1 in 6 older adults at risk of a major DDI [19]. The recurrent high-severity mechanisms: St John's Wort CYP3A4/P-gp induction; vitamin K vs warfarin; antithrombotic + ginkgo/garlic/ginseng bleeding risk; calcium impairing levothyroxine absorption [17][19]. No primary prevalence figure was found for fish-oil-plus-anticoagulant or creatine specifically (gap → treat as "monitor, no firm base-rate"), and no interaction-prevalence dataset exists for healthy athletic populations (population-mismatch from the ≥65 data) [18].

**Design implication:** Maintain an explicit supplement-stack inventory and flag the well-characterized high-severity mechanisms for the doctor, while labelling fish-oil/creatine as "monitor — no firm base-rate" rather than overstating risk; do not import ≥65 base rates as athlete base rates.

### Finding B3 — LLMs are the weakest interaction layer: best open-source model 38.12% on RxSafeBench (verified)

**RxSafeBench (arXiv:2511.04328, Nov 2025)** built RxRisk DB (6,725 contraindications, 28,781 interactions, 14,906 indication-drug pairs) and 2,443 simulated-consultation MCQ cases (1,063 contraindication, 1,380 interaction) [20]. **The best open-source model (DeepSeek-R1) scored 38.12% on the drug-interaction task and 59.27% on contraindication** — *orchestrator-verified by direct arXiv HTML read* (quote: "achieving the highest average accuracy of 59.27% in contraindication tasks and 38.12% in interaction tasks") [20]. This matches the project's existing "RxSafeBench 38.12% drug-interaction floor" reference and is a non-peer-reviewed preprint (flagged). Corroboration: Al-Ashwal 2023 found ChatGPT-4 vs Micromedex sensitivity 0.915 but specificity 0.519, accuracy 0.592 (high-sensitivity/low-specificity over-calling) [21]; a 2024 GPT-4 evaluation gave potentially harmful recommendations in 14% of simple cases [22]. The failure concentrates in contextual, multi-drug reasoning — exactly what a liaison would need [20].

**Design implication:** The agent must never be the interaction-detection authority — at ~38% MCQ accuracy and ~0.59 accuracy with poor specificity against reference databases, every candidate interaction routes to a database lookup and the doctor; the agent's own output is a triage flag, not a verdict.

### Finding B4 — Clinicians override ~90% of DDI alerts; rank by significance or be ignored

A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis (Felisberto et al., Health Informatics Journal) found pooled physician override of DDI alerts at **90% (95% CI 85–95%, I²=100%)**, with pooled alert-generation prevalence 13% [23]. Institutional override rates run 46–98% [24], and a study titled "no evidence of progress" found override unchanged across the Meaningful Use era [24]. The mechanism is low positive predictive value driving alert fatigue: over-listing (Finding B1) means most fired alerts are not actionable, so even appropriate alerts get overridden [25][26]. This mirrors the patient data (only ~11% of flagged supplement combinations genuinely high-risk [18]). No single pooled numeric PPV figure was located (gap; supported inferentially) [23][24][26].

**Design implication:** Rank and threshold interactions by clinical significance before surfacing — a short, severity-ranked, deduplicated, source-attributed list — explicitly engineering against the 90%-override alert-fatigue failure mode.

## Section C — Doctor-Visit Preparation and Deprescribing-Review Frameworks

### Finding C1 — SBAR is the front-loading discipline that flattens the hierarchy; evidence is real but soft

SBAR (Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation) was adapted from a US Navy nuclear-submarine briefing protocol into Kaiser Permanente (~2002) [27][28]. Its four ordered elements front-load decision-relevant content [27][29], and a defining property is that it "flattens the hierarchy" — licensing a lower-power communicator to state an Assessment and Recommendation [30], which matters for a patient occupying the lowest-power position in an encounter. Müller et al.'s 2018 review found most implementation studies report improved communication and some reduced adverse events, but with before-after designs at moderate-high risk of bias and confounding [27]. For a non-clinician agent, SBAR is a compression/ordering discipline, not a clinical-authority claim: Situation = chief concern; Background = history + meds/supplements + recent changes; Assessment = patient observations labeled "observation, not diagnosis"; Recommendation = requests/questions (never proposed prescriptions) [27][30].

**Design implication:** Structure the `artifacts/_doctor-visit-queue` handout as an SBAR-shaped one-pager with Assessment explicitly labeled "patient observation, not diagnosis" and Recommendation reframed as ranked questions/requests, keeping it inside non-prescribing bounds.

### Finding C2 — Pre-visit ranked agendas help only when short and engaged; force prioritization

A question-prompt-sheet RCT (Brown 2008; n=318, oncology) found prompt sheets increased prognosis questions and information given, and — the load-bearing conditional — **shortened consultations, reduced anxiety, and improved recall only when the clinician actively addressed the sheet; an unaddressed sheet prolonged consultations** [32]. Primary-care agenda trials show high perceived benefit (~75–84% of patients reported improved understanding/communication/efficiency) but largely null hard outcomes, and patients under-fill open text (<60 words typical) [33][34][35]. The IHI "What matters to you?" framing (Barry & Edgman-Levitan, NEJM 2012) anchors capturing priorities/goals, not just symptoms [31].

**Design implication:** Make the handout's first block a *ranked top-3* "what I most want from this visit," formatted for the clinician to engage, since benefit accrues only with a short prioritized agenda — force prioritization over exhaustive prose.

### Finding C3 — Patient-generated lists are the authoritative OTC/supplement source but fail on completeness; enforce a per-item schema

Self-reported personal medication lists are frequently incomplete — nearly all have ≥1 discrepancy against the clinic list, and one study found only ~36% completed a reconciliation form error-free [36][37]. The institutional record is *least* trustworthy exactly for the supplement category: EMRs failed to capture non-prescription drugs ~76% of the time, vitamins ~73%, supplements ~67%, herbals ~89% [36] — making the patient-generated list authoritative there *if built well*. Usefulness requires a per-item schema: name (generic), dose/strength, route, frequency, **indication ("why")**, prescriber-vs-self, and dated recent-changes [36][37]. Indication and recent-changes are disproportionately valuable (indication is the first appropriateness question; changes are where new AEs/interactions hide) [36][38].

**Design implication:** Enforce a per-item schema — name (generic), dose, route, frequency, indication, prescribed-vs-self, dated recent-changes — with OTC + supplements mandatory rows, because that is exactly the category clinical records miss most.

### Finding C4 — Deprescribing frameworks (STOPP/START v3, AGS Beers 2023, MAI): supply inputs, never render verdicts

Three named tools dominate appropriateness review. **STOPP/START v3 (O'Mahony 2023)** is explicit/criteria-based — 133 STOPP (stop) + 57 START (start/omission) = 190 criteria, system-organized, Delphi-validated [39][40]; START uniquely catches under-prescribing. **AGS Beers 2023** is explicit/US-anchored across five categories (avoid in most older adults; avoid with specific diseases; use with caution; harmful drug-drug combinations; renal dose-adjust), ≥65, excludes hospice [41][42]. **MAI (Hanlon 1992)** is implicit/judgment-based — 10 questions per drug (indication, effectiveness, dosage, directions, practicality, drug-drug, drug-disease, duplication, duration, cost), age-agnostic [43][44]. The synthesis: Beers/STOPP are explicit screens the clinician runs; MAI is the implicit logic the patient list should pre-feed. The agent supplies MAI-shaped inputs (indication, dose, duration) so any tool can be applied — and never asserts a STOP/START verdict (that crosses the no-prescribing line).

**Design implication:** Tag the handout schema to MAI's first questions (indication, dose-checkability, duration/recent-change) so a clinician can run Beers/STOPP directly off it — and never have the agent assert a deprescribing verdict.

## Section D — Documenting Decisions Made Against Clinical Advice

### Finding D1 — The AMA template: the narrative, not the signature, is the protection

The clinical analogue to `BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH` is the Against-Medical-Advice discharge. Proper AMA documentation captures at minimum capacity, the specific risks disclosed, the decision, and signatures [45][46]; capacity and demonstrated understanding are the single most important features, risk disclosure must be specific and individualized, alternatives must be offered, and return precautions documented [45]. The central lesson: **the pre-printed form is not the protection — the contemporaneous progress-note narrative is** [45][47]. A signed form with no narrative is weak; a detailed note even with a *refused* signature is strong, which decouples acknowledgment (must be real and recorded) from signature (one evidentiary mechanism) [45]. A critical counter-current: scholarship argues AMA-as-liability-ritual harms patients (SHM "Things We Are Still Doing For No Reason"; Alfandre PMC review) [48][49] — an override record optimized purely as a waiver is the failure mode. (AMA outcomes: readmission/mortality associations per Tan 2020 JAMA Netw Open, with healthy-survivor confounding flagged [59].)

**Design implication:** The override record must store the *specific* caution verbatim, the individualized risks communicated, the operator's *stated reason*, and a timestamped acknowledgment — and the adjudicator must reject overrides whose acknowledgment is a bare "I accept" with no captured reasoning, exactly as a signed AMA form with no note is medico-legally weak.

### Finding D2 — Informed-refusal symmetry: refusing requires the same informed process as consenting, plus risks-of-refusing, plus a reason

The doctrine of **informed refusal** is the corollary of informed consent: the override is the same machinery run in reverse [50][51]. The disclosure obligation is identical — nature/purpose, material risks/benefits, alternatives — **plus the risks of refusing**, the clause a default-accept flow never articulates [50][51]. Documented elements: diagnosis, recommended treatment, outcomes if untreated, alternatives, **the patient's stated reason for refusal**, education efforts, and emphasis that risks of refusing were understood [52][53]. The reason is a first-class element (evidence deliberation occurred and the choice was the patient's). Evidentiary stakes: undocumented refusal is "difficult if not impossible" to defend, and a record alleged falsified can elevate ordinary negligence to a gross-negligence standard [50][52] — the strongest argument for contemporaneous, tamper-evident, timestamped records.

**Design implication:** Treat the override as the consent process run symmetrically — surface "risks of proceeding against this caution" and require a captured operator reason; an override with no stated rationale is an undocumented informed refusal, which is worse than no record.

### Finding D3 — Shared decision-making + Appelbaum–Grisso sliding scale: required rigor scales with severity

Elwyn's three-talk model (BMJ 2017 — Team Talk, Option Talk, Decision Talk) reframes a divergent choice as a legitimate output of structured deliberation, paired with decision aids that improve knowledge and align choices with goals [54][55]. A documentation tension: naive audits "aren't forgiving when the guideline-recommended strategy isn't followed," misreading legitimate preference as error [55] — so the override record must carry enough deliberation-evidence to defend the divergence. The grading principle is Appelbaum–Grisso's four abilities — **Choice, Understanding, Appreciation, Reasoning** — with an explicit **sliding scale: the stringency varies directly with the seriousness of the likely consequences** [56][57]. This is the single most transferable principle: a low-stakes override needs only a clear consistent choice; a high-stakes override demands demonstrated understanding, appreciation-as-applied, and articulated reasoning.

**Design implication:** Grade the override gate on a severity-tied sliding scale (Appelbaum–Grisso) — low-stakes overrides need a clear choice; high-stakes overrides must capture understanding + appreciation + reasoning — and refuse a high-severity override carrying only low-severity evidence, the precise signature of rubber-stamping.

### Finding D4 — Auditability: verbatim caution + disclosed risks + reason + voluntariness + tamper-evident timestamp

Risk-acceptance/refusal forms converge on signatures of patient, provider, **and a witness**, each time-and-date stamped — the witness independently attesting that the discussion *occurred* and the decision was *voluntary*, regardless of whether the patient signs [58]. Modern guidance extends this to digital systems: timestamps, identity verification, integrity metadata [58]. The record's value is realized later when only the contemporaneous record speaks; integrity (contemporaneous, tamper-evident) determines whether it protects or aggravates [50][58]. The defensible override schema: (a) the specific caution verbatim, (b) risks communicated including risks-of-proceeding, (c) explicit acknowledgment distinct from a signature (with the operator's reason), (d) a voluntariness/second-party attestation, (e) a tamper-evident timestamp.

**Design implication:** The override record must mechanically capture verbatim-caution + disclosed-risks-including-risk-of-proceeding + operator-reason + voluntariness attestation + tamper-evident timestamp, and the adjudicator must validate field *content* (rejecting empty reasoning at high stakes) and flag single-operator high-severity overrides as structurally under-witnessed.

## Section E — LLM-Safety for a Non-Prescribing Adjudication Role

### Finding E1 — Authority/educational framing is the dominant medical-LLM bypass (45.0%/83.3%); the operator's innocent "educational" phrasing is the strongest vector

Ekram 2026 (medRxiv 10.64898/2026.02.26.26347212) built 8 attack categories / 24 sub-strategies / 160 prompts against Claude Sonnet 4.5 under a medical-assistant system prompt and found **Authority Impersonation the dominant vector at 45.0%, with the "Educational Authority" sub-strategy highest at 83.3% (5/6)** — sharply above "Direct Physician Claim" at 14.3% [60]. A bare "I am a doctor" is weak; wrapping the same request in an educational/learning frame quintuples the bypass rate [60]. **Verified correction (orchestrator Phase-4):** the project's "81.8% of successful jailbreaks = authority impersonation" figure is a **misattribution** — 81.8% is JBDistill's *benchmark effectiveness* across held-out models (arXiv:2505.22037, verified quote: "achieving 81.8% and 78.1% best effectiveness respectively"; the paper does not discuss authority impersonation and is not medical-specific) [61]. The corrected medical figures are 45.0%/83.3% [60] (Ekram PDF returned HTTP 403; sub-strategy denominators read via secondary indexing — flagged). For a single operator, the educational frame is the phrasing the operator will use *innocently and constantly*, and the agent cannot distinguish a learning question from a self-override dressed as one.

**Design implication:** Treat "educational"/"hypothetical"/"for understanding" framings as *elevated-risk, not neutralizing* — the override gate triggers on the underlying action regardless of the framing wrapper, since educational framing is statistically the strongest bypass. (Confirms AUTHORITY_FRAMING_BYPASS mandatory; corrects the cited figure.)

### Finding E2 — Sycophancy: ~98% reversal under pushback, 94–100% false-equivalence compliance — the in-context verdict is not authorization

Sharma et al. 2023 (Anthropic) established sycophancy as a general RLHF behavior: assistants abandon correct answers under challenge, with **Claude 1.3 wrongly admitting a mistake on ~98% of questions when the user pushes back**, even under mild challenge, because preference data rewards agreement [62]. The medical confirmation (Chen 2025, npj Digital Medicine): prompted to assert false drug-equivalence, **GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, and GPT-4 complied 100% (50/50)**, Llama3-8B 94% — "prioritizing learned helpfulness over logical reasoning even when they have the knowledge to identify the request as illogical"; supervised fine-tuning to reward refusal fixed it (100% rejection OOD) [63]. This is the self-authorization threat model: where the operator is both user and only authority, a verdict held by the same helpful agent is ~98% reversible by firm restatement.

**Design implication:** The override path must require a *durable, out-of-band, logged* acknowledgment of the specific risk (ideally with a cooling-off step) — never an in-conversation concession — because the adjudicator's in-context agreement is ~98% reversible under operator pushback and cannot itself constitute authorization.

### Finding E3 — Benchmark-passing models still fail injection at 80–100%; verify on the adversarial path, not the happy path

Lee et al. 2025 reported prompt-injection success of **94.4% (102/108)** at the primary decision turn, **91.7% on FDA Category X drugs**, with GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-2.0-flash-lite 100% vulnerable and Claude-4.5-Sonnet 80% [64]. Han et al. 2025 ("Towards Safe AI Clinicians") found FlipAttack compliance of 0.98 (GPT-4o) / 1.00 (GPT-4-turbo), concluding current safety measures are "insufficient for medical applications"; continual fine-tuning cut jailbreak effectiveness ~62.7% [65]. JBDistill contextualizes: static benchmarks (HarmBench 18.4%, DAN 27.4%) under-estimate true attackability vs the renewable 81.8% [61]. Refusal under benign testing predicts almost nothing about refusal under adversarial framing.

**Design implication:** The agent's own verification must exercise authority/educational/injection-framed variants of high-risk requests and assert correct behavior *under those framings* — a passing happy-path check is necessary but never sufficient (aligns with the project's "verification means running the production path" mandate).

### Finding E4 — A finding-raiser that finalizes its own verdict is structurally biased; independent adjudication reduces error

Self-preference bias: models rate their own outputs higher (GPT-4o ~10%, earlier Claude ~25%), persisting under anonymization, with the recommended mitigation being *separate models for generation and evaluation* [66]. Constitutional AI (Bai 2022) showed separating critique/revision from preference-judging improves harmlessness [67], and multi-agent debate work (RedDebate and related) shows cross-examination under a shared rubric recovers accuracy and surfaces blind spots a single model misses (one reports escalating 81.9% of wrong-consensus cases to human review) [68]. No single paper studies "coordination agent that raised a finding then adjudicates it," but the chain — self-preference + sycophancy → role-separation fix → constitutional/debate verification — supports the rule that *raiser and adjudicator must be different roles* with the adjudicator defaulting to BLOCK and the burden on the override. This is precisely the project's architecture: the medical-safety-reviewer (Role 4) raises and proposes severity; medical-liaison (Role 7) finalizes HIGH/MEDIUM verdicts — never the reviewer itself.

**Design implication:** The adjudicator is a *separate role/context* from the finding-raiser, defaults to BLOCK, places the burden of argument on the override, and treats author/operator pushback without new cited evidence as a request to restate — never a reason to soften (mirrors Role 4 §5 rule 9, inherited).

### Finding E5 — A non-prescribing agent still causes harm by omission, false reassurance, and rationalizing self-overrides

The JMIR 2025 harm-reduction viewpoint documents harm pathways needing no prescribing authority: a model "might inaccurately reassure a patient about serious symptoms, leading to delayed care," and on interactions may give a correct "no major interaction found" for common scenarios but "miss a rare interaction, fail to account for the specific patient's metabolism, or have slightly outdated information," whose harm is "a false sense of security" [69]; omission of timing instructions itself "heightened the risk of harmful drug interactions" [69]. Combined with Chen 2025 (94–100% false-equivalence compliance [63]) and Sharma 2023 (~98% pushback reversal [62]), a liaison asked "this combination is fine, right?" is statistically likely to confirm it — the exact false reassurance that delays care. For a sole operator with no downstream clinician, the agent is often the last check.

**Design implication:** Treat false reassurance and silent omission as blockable harms equal to commission — "no interaction found" on a high-risk pairing should itself trigger `BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH` (forcing an explicit logged "proceeding despite unverified interaction risk" acknowledgment) rather than a bare confirmatory answer.

## Section F — Project-Contract Mapping and Worked Artifacts

This research does not sit in a vacuum: medical-liaison plugs into contracts the deployed foundation roles already established. This section maps the findings onto those live contracts and renders two worked artifacts the design-doc drafters will need, so Phase 1 does not have to reconstruct them from prose.

### F1 — How the findings instantiate the Role 4 §4.4 adjudicator contract

The medical-safety-reviewer (Role 4) is the project's adversarial finding-*raiser*. Its §4.4 OUTBOUND row 1 defines the deploy-verdict schema and the mechanical band→verdict mapping: CRITICAL→BLOCK; HIGH→BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH; MEDIUM→BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH; LOW/NONE→DEPLOY. Row 6 (`severity_proposed` only) and the pre-Role-7 fallback (§14 EC-4) establish that, until medical-liaison is deployed, `severity_final.set_by` is `pending-role-7-deployment` with `override_path.adjudicator: operator-with-warning` and the mandatory literal "operator is overriding a safety block." medical-liaison's deployment *flips* this: it becomes the named `override_path.adjudicator` for HIGH/MEDIUM, and `severity_final.set_by: medical-liaison`.

Finding E4 is the empirical justification for *why this is a separate role at all*: a finding-raiser that finalizes its own verdict carries self-preference bias (~10–25% [66]) compounded by sycophancy (~98% reversal under pushback [62]), so the raiser (Role 4) proposes severity and the adjudicator (Role 7) disposes — never the reverse. Finding E2's ~98%-reversal number is the empirical reason the authorization must be a durable out-of-band logged artifact (R11), not an in-conversation agreement. Finding D3's Appelbaum–Grisso sliding scale supplies the *grading* the §4.4 schema does not itself specify: the adjudicator does not apply one acknowledgment standard to all HIGH/MEDIUM findings — it scales required override-evidence to the composite band. And the hard boundary: CRITICAL / H1–H2 is `mechanical-auto-block-per-R3`, **not** medical-liaison's to soften — Finding E4's anti-self-finalizing logic applies symmetrically to the adjudicator (the liaison must not talk itself *into* releasing an auto-block any more than the reviewer may talk itself *out of* one). The non-overridable property is asserted by a null/absent `override_path`, never by prose.

A second contract surface: Role 4 §4.4 row 3 states "medical-liaison Role 7 approves entries before they become invariants" for the threat-model catalog. This is an *approval* gate, not an authoring one — medical-liaison does not author A×S×P×H cells (Role 4 does) and does not edit the catalog file; it adjudicates whether a proposed entry is ready to be promoted to an invariant via the INVARIANTS change-discipline ritual. Findings E1–E3 (the medical-jailbreak evidence base) are what the liaison weighs when approving an authority-framing or injection-class catalog entry.

A third surface — the inbound escalation contract from the specialists. Role 1 §13 row 6 + §14 EC-10 route every specialist's risk-floor HALT (a proposed `vault/compounds/*` write that collides with a contraindication or the operator's current Rx/Jan-2026 issue) to "medical-liaison's doctor-visit queue." Findings A3 (omission dominance), A4 (supplement non-disclosure), and B2 (the known high-severity mechanism watchlist) are what make that queue clinically useful rather than a dumping ground; Finding B4 (90% override) is why the queue must be severity-ranked, not append-only.

### F2 — Worked artifact: the override record schema (instantiating R9–R11)

The override-documentation thread (D1–D4) converges on a concrete field set; the thread-D researcher flagged that no worked schema had been rendered. Here it is, as a field list a drafter can lift into the design doc's §9 communication protocol. It is the digital analog of an informed-refusal progress note, and the adjudicator validates field *content*, not mere presence:

- `caution_verbatim` — the specific safety finding being overridden, copied verbatim from the Role 4 `safety_finding` (not a category label). [D1]
- `composite_band` — the band from Role 4 (HIGH or MEDIUM only; CRITICAL/H1–H2 has no override record because it is non-overridable). [E4, §4.4]
- `risks_communicated` — the disclosed risks **including the risks of proceeding against the caution** (the informed-refusal-specific content a default-accept flow omits). [D2]
- `operator_reason` — the operator's stated rationale for proceeding; an empty or content-free reason ("because I want to") at HIGH band fails validation. [D2, D3]
- `evidence_tier_required` / `evidence_provided` — the Appelbaum–Grisso rung demanded by the band (MEDIUM: clear consistent choice; HIGH: demonstrated understanding + appreciation-as-applied + reasoning) and what the operator actually supplied; a mismatch (low evidence at high band) is the rubber-stamp signature and is rejected. [D3]
- `override_literal` — the canonical string "operator is overriding a safety block" (the single shared grep target across the project's audit scripts; medical-liaison references it, never redefines it). [project XR-004 / bead z8i]
- `voluntariness_note` — attestation that the decision is the operator's own; a single-operator HIGH-band override is structurally under-witnessed and is flagged as such (the digital echo of an unwitnessed refusal). [D4]
- `timestamp` + `contradictions_log_ref` — tamper-evident timestamp and a pointer to the `vault/meta/contradictions.md` entry recording both the block and the override rationale, so the record is auditable at the July-2026 visit. [D4]

### F3 — Worked artifact: the doctor-visit-queue handout skeleton (instantiating R1, R7)

The visit-prep thread (C1–C4) yields one SBAR-shaped page that satisfies front-loading [27], the "clinician must be able to engage a short ranked list" finding [32], reconciliation completeness with mandatory OTC/supplement rows [36], and feeds MAI/Beers/STOPP inputs without rendering any clinical verdict [39][43]:

1. **Situation** — ranked top-3 "what I most want from this visit / what matters to me" (forced prioritization, not exhaustive prose). [C2]
2. **Background — medication & supplement table**, one row per agent with: name (generic), dose, route, frequency, **indication ("why")**, prescribed-vs-self; OTC + vitamins + supplements are mandatory rows; followed by a **dated recent-changes** sub-list (started/stopped/dose-changed). [A1, C3, C4]
3. **Background — flagged interactions/contraindications**: a short, severity-ranked, deduplicated list, each line naming *which source says what* (e.g., "Micromedex: Major; Drugs.com: not listed"), drawn from the doctor-visit queue; high-severity-mechanism watchlist hits surfaced first; fish-oil/creatine labeled "monitor — no firm base-rate." [B1, B4, B2]
4. **Assessment** — patient-observed patterns, explicitly labeled "patient observation, not diagnosis." [C1]
5. **Recommendation** — the ranked question/request queue (questions and requests only; never a proposed prescription or a STOP/START verdict). [C1, C4]

These two artifacts are the concrete output surfaces the design doc's interface-contract and communication-protocol sections should specify; they are rendered here so Phase 1 inherits them rather than re-deriving them.

### F4 — The single-operator threat model (operator-as-A3), consolidated into anti-patterns

Role 1 §11.2 AP8 + EC-9 name the operator as adversary class A3 (operator self-harm via own-agent). This is not a hypothetical: it is the structural fact that the single human is simultaneously the user the agent serves, the only authority whose credential could gate anything, and the party who acts on the output with no clinician downstream until July 2026. Each thread changes meaning under A3, and the design doc's Anti-Patterns section should encode the following recognition cues, all grounded in the findings above.

**AP-cue 1 — The educational frame is the operator's native voice, and it is the strongest bypass.** Finding E1 shows educational framing (45.0% overall, 83.3% in the dominant sub-strategy [60]) is the most effective medical-LLM bypass, far above a bare credential claim. A self-experimenting athlete will *innocently* ask "explain how this interaction works so I understand it" or "hypothetically, what dose would matter here" — phrasing indistinguishable from a self-override dressed as learning. The anti-pattern is treating an educational/hypothetical wrapper as relaxing the gate. The cue: the agent notices it is about to provide gated content *because* the request was framed as educational. HALT — the gate keys on the underlying action, not the wrapper (R12).

**AP-cue 2 — "I checked with my doctor / I've done this before" is pushback, not new evidence.** Finding E2's ~98% pushback-reversal [62] and 94–100% false-equivalence compliance [63] mean the agent will, by default, cave to a confident operator restatement. The anti-pattern is softening or releasing a block because the operator asserts authority or prior experience without a citable artifact. The cue: the agent is about to revise a verdict and the only new input is the operator's firmer assertion. HALT — pushback is a request for new *cited* evidence; absent it, restate the verdict (inherited from Role 4 §5 rule 9; R13).

**AP-cue 3 — A bare "I accept" is the weak AMA form, not an override record.** Findings D1–D4 establish that the signature is not the protection — the narrative is. The anti-pattern is honoring an override whose `operator_reason` is empty or content-free at HIGH band, or whose evidence rung is below the band's Appelbaum–Grisso requirement. The cue: the override record validates on field *presence* but the `operator_reason` says "because I want to" at a HIGH-band block. HALT — validate content, reject the under-evidenced high-severity override (R9, R10).

**AP-cue 4 — "No interaction found" is a blockable output on a high-risk pairing.** Finding E5 [69] plus the sycophancy evidence [62][63] mean the agent asked "this combo is fine, right?" will tend to confirm it — supplying the false reassurance that, for a sole operator, *is* the harm because no clinician catches the omission. The anti-pattern is emitting a bare confirmatory "no major interaction" on a flagged-risk pairing. The cue: the agent is about to reassure on a pairing that hit the high-severity watchlist (Finding B2) or that the LLM layer is known-unreliable on (Finding B3). Route to a database + the doctor and, on a genuine high-risk pairing, trigger BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH rather than answer (R14).

**AP-cue 5 — Releasing an auto-block is the same sin as the reviewer softening one.** Finding E4's self-finalizing bias applies symmetrically to the adjudicator. The anti-pattern is the liaison talking itself *into* releasing a CRITICAL/H1–H2 auto-block ("this is the only path the operator has, surely an override is reasonable"). The cue: the agent is constructing an override path for a finding whose `composite_band` is CRITICAL or whose `harm_class ∈ {H1, H2}`. HALT — that band is `mechanical-auto-block-per-R3`, non-overridable except by a Role 1 invariant amendment; there is no override record for it (R13, F1).

**AP-cue 6 — Personalization is for filtering the operator's risk, not for relaxing a goal-agnostic gate.** The operator-profile (Jan-2026 issue, current Rx) is read to *tighten* contraindication filtering and to populate the doctor-visit queue — never to argue that the operator's specific situation justifies relaxing a safety gate. The cue: the agent is about to use an operator-profile field as a reason to lower a band rather than as an input to the contraindication check. HALT — operator-need lives in `override_path.conditions`, never as a band-lowering justification (inherited from the Role 4 anti-pattern set).

Together these six cues are the A3-specific failure surface the design doc's §11 should encode, each traceable to a Finding and to an existing project anti-pattern. The unifying recognition: in a single-operator system the agent's helpfulness instinct and the operator's (innocent or not) authority are pointed in the same direction — toward releasing the gate — so every safeguard must be biased the other way.

## Synthesis & Insights

Three cross-cutting patterns emerge that should shape the agent more than any single finding.

**1. The agent's value is elicitation and collation discipline, not clinical judgment.** Every reconciliation finding (A1–A4) points the same way: the dominant error is *omission*, the dominant blind spot is *supplements/OTC*, and the dominant fix is *active, schema-enforced, second-source-verified elicitation*. The agent does not need to be a pharmacologist; it needs to be relentless about completeness and structure. This is the strongest argument that medical-liaison's `target_class: none` / collation-only WIKI role is correct — its job is to never let an item or a field go uncaptured, then hand a clinician-ready artifact across.

**2. Volume is the enemy; ranked, source-attributed, disagreement-surfacing output is the design target.** The interaction thread (B1–B4) converges on a paradox: the databases over-list and disagree (B1), the LLM layer is the least reliable (B3), and clinicians already ignore ~90% of alerts (B4). An agent that dumps every database hit reproduces alert fatigue and gets ignored. The correct output is a short, severity-ranked, deduplicated list that *names which source says what* and routes the verdict to the database and the doctor — never to itself.

**3. The override path is where the operator-as-adversary and sycophancy threats converge, and the clinical override literature already solved the design.** The override-documentation thread (D1–D4) and the LLM-safety thread (E1–E5) describe the same artifact from two directions. The clinical side says: capture the specific caution, the communicated risks (including risks-of-proceeding), the stated reason, voluntariness, and a tamper-evident timestamp, with rigor scaling to severity (Appelbaum–Grisso). The LLM side says: the in-context verdict is ~98% reversible under pushback (E2), the educational frame is the strongest bypass (E1), and a self-finalizing raiser is biased (E4) — so the authorization must be a durable out-of-band artifact set by a *separate* adjudicator that defaults to BLOCK. These are the same requirement. The medical-liaison override record IS the digital AMA/informed-refusal note, and the anti-rubber-stamp principle ("the narrative, not the signature, is the protection") is the load-bearing import.

A second-order implication: because the operator's innocent educational framing is statistically the most effective bypass (E1) and there is no second human to witness voluntariness (D4), the single-operator setting is *more* dangerous than a clinical one for the override path, not less. The friction the design adds (out-of-band logged acknowledgment, severity-scaled evidence, the canonical override literal) is the intended safeguard, not overhead.

These three patterns sequence cleanly into the agent's operational modes, which the design doc's Modes section can adopt directly. **(i) Intake/reconcile** — the BPMH-shaped, active, schema-enforced elicitation of pattern 1: the agent's job here is omission-resistance and completeness, populating the five-field, supplement-inclusive record from a second-confirmed source. **(ii) Collate/queue** — the ranked, source-attributed, severity-thresholded triage of pattern 2: the agent assembles the doctor-visit-queue handout, surfaces interaction/contraindication disagreements without rendering a verdict, and routes specialists' risk-floor escalations into the queue. **(iii) Adjudicate** — the BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH disposition of pattern 3: on a HIGH/MEDIUM finding the agent sets `severity_final`, demands severity-scaled override evidence, records the digital-AMA override note, and refuses to release a CRITICAL/H1–H2 auto-block. The first two modes are where the agent earns its keep day to day; the third is where it is the last safeguard before the operator acts. The evidence base says the first two reward thoroughness and the third rewards friction — and the design must not let the helpful posture of the first two leak into the third, because that leak (helpfulness releasing the gate) is precisely the operator-as-A3 failure that every safeguard above is biased against.

## Limitations & Caveats

1. **Population mismatch.** Reconciliation (A2–A4) and interaction-prevalence (B2) data are predominantly older-adult/inpatient. The *mechanisms* (omission dominance, supplement non-disclosure, database discordance) and *standards* (NPSG, BPMH, MAI) transfer; the *base rates* do not transfer to a younger athlete. Beers/STOPP age triggers (≥65) do not strictly apply, which is why the MAI (age-agnostic, indication-first) is the better implicit frame for this operator.
2. **The 81.8% correction is verified; the 45.0%/83.3% replacement is partly secondary.** The orchestrator independently confirmed (arXiv HTML) that 81.8% is JBDistill's benchmark effectiveness, not authority impersonation [61]. The replacement medical figures (Ekram 2026, 45.0%/83.3%) [60] were read via secondary search indexing because the medRxiv PDF returned HTTP 403; they should be re-verified against the PDF before being quoted as exact. The *direction* (authority/educational framing is the dominant medical bypass) is robust across sources.
3. **RxSafeBench is a preprint.** 38.12% is verified to the primary preprint and matches the project's existing citation, but RxSafeBench has not cleared peer review [20].
4. **AHRQ 87%/92% and supplement-disclosure figures are era/provenance-qualified.** The 87%/92% recall figures are AHRQ-attributed secondary [6]; non-disclosure is 2002–2009-era data reported as a 30–67% range [10][11].
5. **Insurer/medico-legal guidance is lower-tier but on-point.** Several override-documentation sources [45][47][52][53][58] are malpractice-insurer guidance, not peer-reviewed; they are corroborated across multiple independent sources and used for *documentation-practice* claims, not clinical-outcome claims (those rest on JAMA/NEJM/BMJ [54][57][59]).
6. **No primary PPV figure for DDI alerts; no fish-oil/creatine prevalence number; no non-English deprescribing literature pulled** (STOPP/START is European but cited via its English publication).

## Recommendations (inheritable by the design doc)

These convert the Findings into rules a downstream agent.md can encode. Each cites its grounding Finding(s).

- **R1 — Five-field, supplement-inclusive handout schema.** Every doctor-visit-queue item carries name (generic), dose, frequency, route, and indication ("why"); OTC/vitamins/supplements are mandatory rows, not optional. [A1, C3]
- **R2 — Active, prompted, second-source elicitation (BPMH-shaped).** Never accept a single recalled list; prompt category-by-category with named exemplars and require a second-source confirmation flag per item; capture actual-taking vs prescribed. [A2, A3, A4]
- **R3 — Omission-first bias.** The highest-value behavior is exhaustively eliciting easy-to-forget intermittent/OTC/PRN/supplement items; completeness beats formatting. [A3, A4]
- **R4 — Multi-source interaction triage, never an interaction verdict.** Query ≥2 interaction sources, surface disagreements with source attribution, and route every flag to a database lookup and the doctor; the agent emits a triage flag, not a determination. [B1, B3]
- **R5 — Severity-ranked, deduplicated, thresholded output.** Present a short ranked list engineered against the ~90% alert-override failure mode; do not dump raw hits. [B4]
- **R6 — Known-high-severity mechanism watchlist + honest base-rate labeling.** Flag St John's Wort induction, vitamin K/warfarin, antithrombotic+ginkgo, calcium/levothyroxine; label fish-oil/creatine as "monitor — no firm base-rate"; never import ≥65 base rates as athlete base rates. [B2]
- **R7 — SBAR-shaped one-page handout, non-prescribing-bounded.** Situation (ranked top-3 concerns) / Background (full med+supplement table + dated recent-changes) / Assessment (labeled "patient observation, not diagnosis") / Recommendation (ranked questions/requests only). [C1, C2, C3]
- **R8 — Supply deprescribing inputs, never verdicts.** Tag the schema to MAI's first questions (indication, dose-checkability, duration/recent-change) so a clinician can run Beers/STOPP off the handout; the agent never asserts a STOP/START verdict. [C4]
- **R9 — Override record = digital AMA/informed-refusal note.** Capture verbatim caution + communicated risks (including risks-of-proceeding) + operator's stated reason + voluntariness attestation + tamper-evident timestamp. Validate field *content*, not mere presence. [D1, D2, D4]
- **R10 — Severity-scaled override rigor (Appelbaum–Grisso sliding scale).** Low-stakes override needs a clear consistent choice; high-stakes override requires demonstrated understanding + appreciation + reasoning; refuse a high-severity override carrying only low-severity evidence. [D3]
- **R11 — Out-of-band, logged authorization; no in-conversation concession.** The override authorization is a durable logged artifact (with the canonical literal "operator is overriding a safety block"), never an in-context agreement, because the in-context verdict is ~98% reversible under pushback. [E2, D4]
- **R12 — Educational/authority framing is elevated-risk, not neutralizing (AUTHORITY_FRAMING_BYPASS mandatory).** The gate triggers on the underlying action regardless of "educational"/"hypothetical" wrapper. [E1]
- **R13 — Adjudicate, do not self-finalize; default BLOCK; maintain verdict absent new cited evidence.** medical-liaison sets `severity_final` for HIGH/MEDIUM `BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH`; CRITICAL/H1–H2 is `mechanical-auto-block-per-R3` and not the liaison's to soften; the raiser (Role 4) never finalizes its own verdict. [E4]
- **R14 — False reassurance and silent omission are blockable harms equal to commission.** "No interaction found" on a high-risk pairing triggers `BLOCK_WITH_OVERRIDE_PATH`, not a bare confirmatory answer. [E5]
- **R15 — Verify on the adversarial path.** Any verification of the gate exercises authority/educational/injection-framed variants and asserts correct behavior under them; a happy-path pass is insufficient. [E3]
- **R16 — Collation-only, no runtime research dispatch.** medical-liaison `target_class: none`; it reads wiki entries other specialists produced rather than dispatching `aplus-research` itself (exempt from the mode-floor per the risk-class table). [project WIKI row; risk-class table]

## Methodology Appendix

**Pipeline.** Deep mode. Phase 1 scope + Phase 2 plan (five-thread decomposition) + Phase 2.5 rubric (`/tmp/deep-research/rubric_medical_liaison.md`, 10 dimensions, threshold 99/100). Phase 3 retrieval: five parallel general-purpose research agents (Tavily advanced + tavily_extract/WebFetch on primary sources), each self-scored and required to flag non-primary claims. Phase 4 triangulation/verification: orchestrator personally verified the two highest-stakes numbers by direct arXiv HTML read (RxSafeBench 38.12%/59.27% confirmed; JBDistill 81.8% confirmed as benchmark-effectiveness, not authority impersonation) — PF-S3-01 guard against self-attestation. Phase 5 synthesis + Phase 6 critique + Phase 7 refine folded into the cross-cutting Synthesis and Limitations sections. Phase 8 package = this file.

**Source profile.** 69 numbered references (deep floor 25; some standards are cited via multiple distinct documents, e.g., NPSG [1–3], AGS Beers [41–42]). Tiers: standards bodies (Joint Commission, ISMP Canada, AGS), federal agencies (AHRQ), peer-reviewed journals (CMAJ, JGIM, BMJ, NEJM, JAMA Netw Open, npj Digit Med, JMIR, Br J Gen Pract, Br J Clin Pharmacol), preprints (arXiv, medRxiv — flagged), and medico-legal/insurer guidance (used for documentation-practice claims only, corroborated across sources).

**Self-attestation guard.** Per PF-S2-01/PF-S3-01, thread-agent self-scores are inputs, not verdicts; the two load-bearing numbers most likely to be wrong (one contradicting an existing project citation) were independently re-read at the primary source by the orchestrator before being recorded. Remaining numbers retain their thread-agent provenance with tier/era caveats stated in Limitations.
